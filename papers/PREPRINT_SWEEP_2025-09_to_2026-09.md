# Preprint sweep for EPModel design, 19 September 2025 to 19 September 2026

Compiled 2026-09-19/20. Method literature only; separate from the Bowen corpus in `docs/theory/`. Companion to `DESIGN_LESSONS_model_design_papers_2026-09-17.md`, whose section numbers are cited in the "Bears on" column.

## 0. Method and limits

**Where I searched.** arXiv, through its advanced search with the submitted-date filter set to 2025-09-19 onward. Fifty phrase queries across ten facets (LLM social simulation; silicon samples and fidelity; persona stability and drift; conformity and sycophancy; emotion and affect in agents; family, dyad and small-group models; ABM methodology such as sensitivity, calibration, validation, reproducibility, asynchronous update and ODD; opinion dynamics, structural balance, signed networks and coevolving ties; counterfactual and intervention design; long-horizon and developmental agents). The arXiv API refused my requests (HTTP 429), so I used the HTML search pages instead. Web searches were also run for SSRN, OSF/PsyArXiv/SocArXiv and general preprints.

**Yield.** 1,115 distinct arXiv papers harvested (the raw set, with abstracts, is in `~/Downloads/_claude_arxiv_candidates_2026-09-19.json`). Six sub-agents screened every abstract against a written rubric; 275 in-window papers were rated relevant (82 at the top grade). I read all 82 top-grade abstracts and 57 second-grade abstracts myself, selected the 128 below, and wrote the notes. Two papers were read in full (marked READ IN FULL); everything else is from the abstract. Titles, ids, dates and author surnames come from the arXiv listing, not from memory.

**Checks on recall.** Six papers already in `papers/INDEX.md` (MicroVerse, Prompt Sensitivity, Total Simulated Survey Error, TraitMix, Insurance as AI Risk Infrastructure, Ordinary Reasonable Chatbots) were all found by the queries, which is some evidence the queries cover the space you have been tracking.

**Limits.** (1) arXiv only, effectively: no relevant preprints surfaced on SSRN or OSF for the family/emotion/ABM intersection; the OSF hits were empirical psychology projects, not models. (2) Abstract-level notes can misstate what a paper shows; the "Bears on" column is my inference. (3) The date filter matches the latest version, so a few papers with a first version before 2025-09-19 appeared; they are listed separately in §4 and excluded from the main tables. (4) Physics-style opinion dynamics (about 200 hits) was screened but mostly rated irrelevant for a 12-agent kinship model; only tie-dynamics and small-N items were kept. (5) Full-text downloads did not complete (Chrome did not save the 14 PDFs I requested), so no PDFs were added to the papers folder.

## 1. What changes, if anything, for EPModel

Three items from this sweep bear on decisions already taken or pending.

1. **Seeding for paired counterfactuals (2603.11084, read in full).** Spec `M3.D.4` requires every draw to come from a single seeded generator threaded explicitly. That is exactly the stateful-generator design this paper shows breaks paired counterfactuals: once an intervention changes control flow, every downstream event in the intervention arm receives different noise than the same event in the baseline arm, so per-seed arm differences mix the intervention with a draw-index shift. The remedy is a counter-based generator keyed by (seed, event identifier), which keeps determinism (`M3.D.5`) and adds execution invariance. It also forces a modelling decision the spec has not yet made: what counts as "the same event" across arms (the paper's slot-keyed vs dyad-keyed distinction), and how agent identifiers stay stable when births differ between arms. This is a Phase E concern, but the generator interface is a Phase B decision.
2. **Interaction protocol as a design variable.** 2510.10002 (synchronous vs round-robin, 30,000+ debates), 2603.00113 (position paper: scheduling and initial priors can dominate results) and 2605.07240 (a formal result that decision order shifts equilibria) add to the case in design lessons §2.1 that `M3.D.1` should be tested under an alternative activation regime.
3. **Ensemble sizing and sensitivity have off-the-shelf tools.** Statistical model checking with confidence-based stopping rules (2604.04543, 2605.10447), a screening-then-surrogate sensitivity workflow (2604.03350), Fisher-information sloppiness on a small stochastic ABM (2606.14887), and matched multi-seed mechanism ablations (2606.23158) are all directly usable for the Phase E questions raised in design lessons §2.3 and §2.6.

For the LLM-agent line the picture is consistent with the 17 September write-up and now much better documented: post-training makes models less human-like (2605.07632); instruction-tuned models collapse to a single answer per persona (2607.25292); aligned models cannot play people less kind than average (2608.24912) or villains (2511.04962); persona populations collapse toward stereotypes and richer personas can make it worse (2604.24698, 2606.18263, 2607.20429, 2604.23575); conformity is systematic and model-dependent (2601.05384, 2605.10721, 2608.02758, 2604.19301); and event-induced personality change reproduces the mean but not the shape of human development (2608.06485). Two architecture papers address the September question of maturity change directly (2601.10025, 2609.17331), and one measures it (2608.06485). No paper in the sweep models a family, a triangle, or a conserved emotional quantity.
