## 1. What was asked

The plan's §1: if each family member is a language-model agent that sees only what it feels and what happens to it, do
conflict, distance, cutoff, over/underfunctioning, projection and fixed triangles appear as they do with the model's
learning agents, on the same readouts? The LLM chooses each family member's weekly act; the engine computes everything
else. The arms (plan §2): A0, the model's own policy; A1, opaque act labels (`K1`…`K16`, permuted per seed); A2, plain
labels; A3, plain labels shuffled among acts of the same legality group; A4, plain labels with no tension number shown.
Layer 1 compares directions against A0, with seed counts and no threshold (§6).

### 2.3 The three contamination tests (plan §3.4; report, "Contamination tests")

- **Label-following (A3 against A2).** A3's act frequencies follow A2's more closely by the label shown than by the
  mechanics run in 5 of 5 seeds. Under §3.4, A2's patterns are counted as driven by what the words mean, that is, by
  the model's prior.
- **Feedback removed (A4 against A2).** A4 keeps A2's direction against A0 in 5 of 5 seeds for every pattern except
  distance (4 of 5). Under §3.4, the patterns did not come from felt relief.
- **Opaque against plain (A1 against A2).** No pattern appears in A2 and not in A1. This test is weak here: A2 shows
  only cutoff, distance and fixed triangles at all, so there is little that could have been missing from A1.

### 2.4 Position readouts (§13.4)

Under the approved rule (same-id repeats above same-option repeats and position 1 above the mean `1/N`), the report
reads "no" position effect in every run: 0 of 5 seeds in each arm of the main phase, and 0 of 4 cross-check runs.
Position 1 ranges from 0.026 to 0.049 against a mean `1/N` of 0.036 to 0.039. The first tenth of the list takes 0.076
to 0.113 of choices in the main and cross-check runs, against 0.214 in the second pilot.

### 2.5 Cross-check (2026-11-07, `claude-sonnet-5-5`, A1 and A2 × seeds 1–2, 208 weeks)

4 runs `complete`, 5,820 calls, no re-ask, fallback, retry or failure, $20.526398. Every pattern and readout has the
same direction against A0 as the main phase on the same seed, in 2 of 2 seeds, except A2's distance: 1 of 2 (on the
second model A2 is higher than A0 in both seeds; on the main phase in one of them). No position effect in the 4 runs.

Two differences between the models in A1:

- **Hold share.** A1 holds back in 0.446 and 0.635 of its choices on Sonnet, against 0.916 to 0.965 on Haiku. A2 holds
  back in 0.001 or less on both.
- **Token `K1`.** On Sonnet, A1's choices fall on act token `K1` in 0.990 and 0.855 of choices; on Haiku's main phase,

  was designed to test, and it bears on T-201 (§4.2).

## 3. What the findings can and cannot claim

- **Descriptive only.** An LLM cannot be mutated to prove a mechanism (§1, reason 4). No finding here is evidence for
  or against a mechanism of the model.
- **Not about a real family.** These are readouts of language models choosing in an invented family (`M11.F.9`). A
  language-model agent is not a person.
- **A0 is not ground truth** (§6). A0 is a model too; the LLM arms are not expected to match it.
- **The arms differ from A0 in more than who chooses** (§6): A1–A4 get no cross-person credit, no two-channel mix
  (`M4.D.1a`), no competing urges (`M4.D.1d`), their last rounds in place of learned values, and the external agent
  stays on the policy in every arm.
- **What the contamination tests leave.** A3 follows the labels in 5 of 5 seeds, so A2's patterns are read as coming
  from the model's prior about the words. A4 keeps A2's direction, so they are not from felt relief. A1, the one arm
  where the words cannot guide the act, mostly holds back on Haiku, and on Sonnet mostly picks one token by name;
  neither is shown to respond to tension (T-201).
- **What can be said.** On these readouts, language-model agents in this family produce less conflict,
  over/underfunctioning, projection and fixed-triangle activity than the model's learning agents, and more cutoff;
  distance depends on the arm. The direction holds on a second model in the seeds compared, except A2's distance. The
  contamination tests are consistent with the patterns coming from the model's reading of the option words rather
  than from felt relief; they show that the words predict the acts and that hiding tension changes little, not what
  the agents respond to. If A1's choices also follow text (T-201), the arm that was meant to show a response to tension
  without the words' help does not show one either.

## 4. Open items, each with a recommendation

### 4.1 T-200: the remaining lean toward the top of the list

Main phase and cross-check: no position effect under the approved rule in any of the 24 runs (§2.4). The report
computes the approved rule only, not §13.5's stronger rule. Counted offline from the report's position table for this
review (the first script in the appendix; not a number the report generates): under the stronger rule, 21 of the 24 main and
cross-check runs read "not gone", failing on position 1, the last position or same id by small margins in both
directions (for example position 1 at 0.026 in `main-A3-s5`, 0.049 in `main-A1-s2`). The stronger rule has four
strict conditions and no tolerance, so a margin of a few choices either way, which a chooser blind to position would
also show, makes it read "not gone"; that is an inference from the rule's form, not something these runs show. It was
written to judge a 182-call pilot.

**Recommendation: close T-200.** The lean does not show at the main phase's scale under the approved rule, and the
first tenth is at or near 0.1 in every full-length run. Record that the stronger rule is not used for full-length runs,
and that the report computes the approved rule only. No code change.

### 4.2 T-201: does A1's holding back respond to tension, or to the words "hold back"?

A1's act tokens are opaque, but its withhold option reads "hold back from <option>" in plain English (§3.1). New
evidence since T-201 was opened: on Sonnet, A1 holds back in about half its choices, not nine in ten, and its choices
fall mostly on token `K1` by name (§2.5). Both suggest that A1's choices follow the text on the option (the hold words
on Haiku, the token's name on Sonnet) more than the tension shown. That is a hypothesis, not a result.

The no-cost check: from the committed `choices.jsonl.gz` of the seven A1 runs, A1's hold share by week over the 208
weeks (each record carries `week`, `person` and `hold`). A share that falls or rises with the weeks would point to a
response to experience; a flat share would not. Comparing it with the tension history needs the tension per week,
which is not in `choices.jsonl.gz`; it could be read from an offline replay from the cache (no call), which is a
second, larger step.

**Recommendation: run the hold-share-by-week check now, as part of this review** (offline, minutes, no call), record
the result in T-201, and keep T-201 open for the larger question: an A1 run with the withhold option given an opaque
token, or with tension hidden, which needs a plan change and the owner's cost go-ahead.

### 4.4 DeepSeek, or another model family, later

The owner deferred this at the cross-check. Two Anthropic models agree on direction in the seeds compared. A third
family would test whether the directions are a habit of one vendor's training. But the open question (T-201) is what
A1's choices respond to, and another model would not answer it; it would add a third set of habits to explain.

**Recommendation: not now.** Revisit only if T-201's checks show A1 responds to tension; then a different model family
is a fair test of whether that response is general.

### 4.5 Whether the experiment goes further

The plan's §6 left layer 2 (composite `M11.C` criteria rerun with `LLMSource` in both arms) to be decided after layer
1's results. Layer 1 found that the LLM arms' patterns are counted as driven by the model's prior (A3) and not by felt
relief (A4). A layer 2 run would then test what the language model believes families do, not the model's mechanisms,
and its findings could not speak to a mechanism in any case (§1, reason 4). Each criterion doubles the calls.

**Recommendation: no layer 2. Close the experiment at layer 1,** with T-201 kept open as the one question layer 1
leaves that bears on emotional process, and return to the model of record (T-202, the social cues build plan).

## Appendix: the scripts

### The stronger-rule count (§4.1)

Run from the repo root, 2026-11-07, over the report's position table; it skips the pilots and prints the number of
runs and the number that fail at least one of the four conditions (24 and 21), with the conditions each fails.

```python
import re
rows = [l for l in open('docs/llm_agent_experiment_report.md')
        if re.match(r'\| (main|cross_check|pilot)', l) and l.count('|') == 13]
n = 0; fails = {}
for l in rows:
    c = [x.strip() for x in l.strip().strip('|').split('|')]
    run, ch, fl, p1, last, m, sid, sidc, sop, pairs, holds, eff = c
    p1, last, m, sid, sidc, sop = float(p1), float(last), float(m), int(sid), float(sidc), int(sop)
    if run.startswith('pilot'):
        continue
    conds = {'so>sid': sop > sid, 'sid<=chance': sid <= sidc, 'p1<=m': p1 <= m, 'last<=m': last <= m}
    bad = [k for k, v in conds.items() if not v]
    n += 1
    if bad:
        fails[run] = bad
print(n, len(fails)); [print(k, v) for k, v in sorted(fails.items())]
```

### The hold-share check (§4.2)

Run as `python3 -I hold_by_week.py experiments/llm_agents/runs` from the repo root, 2026-11-07. The weeks 0–3 column
was counted with the same filter on `week < 4`.

```python
"""A1's hold share by 52-week block, from the committed choices.jsonl.gz (T-201's no-cost check)."""
import gzip, json, sys
from pathlib import Path

RUNS = Path(sys.argv[1])
for run in sorted(RUNS.glob("*-A1-s*")):
    if run.name.startswith(("smoke", "pilot")):
        continue
    rows = [json.loads(l) for l in gzip.open(run / "choices.jsonl.gz", "rt")]
    blocks = []
    for b in range(4):
        sel = [r for r in rows if b * 52 <= r["week"] < (b + 1) * 52]
        blocks.append((sum(r["hold"] for r in sel), len(sel)))
    per_person = {}
    for r in rows:
        per_person.setdefault(r["person"], [[0, 0], [0, 0]])
        half = 0 if r["week"] < 104 else 1
        per_person[r["person"]][half][0] += r["hold"]
        per_person[r["person"]][half][1] += 1
    up = sum(1 for (a, b) in per_person.values() if b[0] / b[1] > a[0] / a[1])
    down = sum(1 for (a, b) in per_person.values() if b[0] / b[1] < a[0] / a[1])
    eq = len(per_person) - up - down
    print(run.name, "weeks", min(r["week"] for r in rows), max(r["week"] for r in rows),
          "| by block:", " ".join(f"{h/n:.3f}" for h, n in blocks),
          f"| persons later half higher {up}, lower {down}, equal {eq} of {len(per_person)}")
```
