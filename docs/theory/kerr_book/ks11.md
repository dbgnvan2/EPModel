## ⭐⭐ KS11.2 — The transmission rule, stated precisely `[T]` `[M]`

> "**A parent with a good level of differentiation does not choose a marital partner with a moderate level and then produce offspring somewhere in between. A person with a good level chooses a mate at the same level, and they can produce children with basic levels a little higher, a little lower, or the same as their levels.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.E`'s transmission rule in one sentence, and it is a three-outcome distribution centred on the parental level** — higher, lower, or same, all "a little". Combined with `KS09.1`'s different-triangles mechanism, the model has everything it needs: the *parental level sets the centre*, and *which child gets which offset* is determined by the triangle that child develops in.

Note what it forbids: **no averaging of dissimilar parents** (they are not dissimilar), and **no guaranteed decline**. `M11.C.6` asserts decline in the projection line specifically, and this confirms that is the right scope — the projection *line* declines, the family does not.

## ⭐⭐ KS11.3 — The assessment method, and its explicit limits `[T]`

> "**No one question or piece of data accurately assesses a person's basic level of differentiation.**"

> "**A computer program or questionnaire does not yet exist that can estimate a person's basic level with sufficient accuracy. Estimating basic level using a three-, four-, or five-generation family diagram is the most accurate method at this point.**"

> "**Estimates of basic levels of differentiation necessarily include data for as many generations as possible. Assessing multiple people who are closely connected emotionally to each other to assess basic level is necessary because one family member's functioning can be enhanced or undermined by the reciprocal functioning of other family members.**"

The geneticist's parallel, from John Mulvihill (1976): he "**trusted looking at the pedigree chart as a whole, rather than looking at numbers on chart derived from those data.**" Kerr: "**It is commonly something you can see but difficult to put numbers on.**"

*Model consequence:* the **strongest and most current statement of `A7` and `Q-VALIDATION`**, and it directly shapes `M1.A.4a`. Three requirements:

1. The estimator is **multi-person and multi-generation by necessity**, not by preference — because reciprocity means a single person's functioning is uninformative about their own basic level.
2. The estimator is **gestalt over a structure**, not a score over features. Mulvihill's remark is precisely a warning against exactly what a naive implementation would do.
3. **No instrument exists.** Any number the model produces for `basic_level` is a model-internal quantity and **MUST NOT** be presented as measurable. `M11.F` clause.

Kerr records a live attempt — a Bowen Theory Academy project "**to computerize all the data from a cohort of people that have collected and verified data from the multigenerational study of their own families**". Worth noting in `_EXTERNAL_MEASURES.md` as an open effort, not a result.

## ⭐⭐ KS11.4 — **Sibling reciprocity**: one child's functioning is purchased from another's `[T]`

> "a sibling that the data suggest has a **fairly high level of functioning** compared to another sibling **does not necessarily have a different basic level**; this can be based on **a degree of reciprocal functioning between the siblings.** For example, **one child may function in reaction to a sibling's acting out by trying to do everything right to win the parents' approval. Both children may be equally relationship oriented and have very similar basic levels of 'self.' The functioning disparity of the siblings in this case reflects pseudo-self, not solid self.**"

The natural experiment:

> "One very successful entrepreneur attributed part of his business success to **his twin brother's death at age twenty-one.** He described his childhood as **living in the shadow of his dominant twin brother and underachieving. He said he was released from the process with his twin brother's death.**"

*Model consequence:* **the overfunctioning/underfunctioning reciprocity extended to the sibling tie**, which `M6.I.4` currently scopes to the marital and parent-child relationships. And `KS07.2`'s "whisper of nature" gets a **third** instance in a different relationship class — remove one party and the other's functional level moves, this time *upward* for the survivor.

That third instance matters: cousin's visit (`KS07.1`), wife's trip (`KS10.8`), twin's death (here). All the same shape, three relationship types, two directions. `M11.C.18`'s discriminator is now supported by four independent natural experiments including `KS10.4`'s shock wave.

**Also a readout trap**, the sixth: the *high-functioning* sibling may be running on pseudo-self, so a model that reads sibling divergence as basic-level divergence is wrong.

## ⭐ KS11.5 — Conley's sibling study, with a number `[#]`

Dalton Conley (2004) studied sibling variation by "**educational attainment, income, and net worth**" across many multigenerational families.

> Family members described the siblings who did well as "**responsible, disciplined, persevered, motivated, set goals, had a plan**"; those who did less well as "**bad attitude, poor emotional or mental health, lack of determination.**"

> "**sibling variation was much more predictive of moving up or down the socioeconomic ladder as adults than the particular socioeconomic status of the family in which the siblings grew up.**"

> "**Having a sibling or siblings that do not adapt well increases one's own chances of doing well threefold!**"

*Model consequence:* an **independent, published, quantitative** result consistent with the projection process concentrating impairment on one child — and the **threefold** figure is the only external effect size in the corpus that bears on `M11.C.2`. Record in `_EXTERNAL_MEASURES.md`.

**Grade with care.** Conley did not measure differentiation, and the direction of the threefold effect is ambiguous between Kerr's reading (projection spares the siblings) and a straightforward resource/comparison account. It corroborates *within-family divergence exceeding between-family variation*, which is a real and useful constraint, and nothing more.

## ⭐ KS11.6 — Emotional maturity ≈ differentiation, with three stated differences `[T]`

> "**Bowen (1966) suggested that emotional maturity and basic level of differentiation of self addressed the same phenomenon. However, differentiation is a broader concept for several reasons.**"

1. "**differentiation of self is a life force with deep evolutionary roots. It involves not just psychological maturation but programming of the emotional system and associated physiology as well.**"
2. "**the theory describes how differentiation or lack thereof plays out specifically in relationships and broadens thinking about what constitutes emotional immaturity, such as anxiety-driven overfunctioning. A bully is typically regarded as immature, but a parent who is too anxious to stop overly protecting a child is acting immaturely as well.**"
3. "**relating basic level of differentiation to the degree of unresolved attachment to the family of origin is an alternative conceptual framework** to ideas widely implanted in the public consciousness such as **abuse, neglect, and trauma during development as core causes.**"

The conventional maturity list, given for comparison: "**acceptance of responsibility, ability to make decisions, ability to function as a team member, development of stable and sustaining relationships, and a realistic sense of self-worth.**"

*Model consequence:* reason 2 is the two axes again — **the bully and the over-protector are equally immature** — and it is the clearest short statement of why `M1.A.9a` needs both. Reason 3 restates `KS06.14`.

## ⭐ KS11.7 — Which data, and how each is read `[T]`

⛔ **[2026-08-28] This entry is correct and was mis-cited downstream — recorded so the blame lands in the
right place.** The sentence below opens with *this file's own connective prose*, "The family diagram records,
per person:", followed by the genuinely verbatim list in quotation marks. `M1.A.4e` in the spec took that
connective prose **inside** the quotation marks and attributed it to Kerr as the source's own opening line.
The phrase appears nowhere in the book. **The extraction did not drift; the spec quoted the extraction's
framing as though it were the source.** Corrected at `M1.A.4e`.

The family diagram records, per person: "**birth date, educational history, occupational history, health history, and date of death**", plus dates met/engaged/married and places lived; for dependent children, "**birth date, school functioning, social functioning, health, and death date.**"

Kerr then gives the **interpretation rule for each class**:

- **Occupation** — "**Data about occupational experiences can be misleading. A high level of occupational achievement may indicate a fairly high basic level… but not necessarily so. The reverse, however, is predictive: a person with a high level of differentiation… will very likely have a responsible and productive life course.**"
- **Health** — valuable, and **how the family adapts to illness is itself a reading**: "**significant variation in how youngsters and their families adapt to a diagnosis of juvenile diabetes… a successful adaptation results in a more stable clinical course.**"
- **Longevity** — "**living a long life does not necessarily equate with being a person with a high basic level.** Two grandparents may live long lives and function well, but their children and/or grandchildren may significantly dysfunction. **This discrepancy suggests that a strong projection process played out.**"
- **Courtship length** — bidirectional: "**Very drawn-out courtships and very short courtships may suggest, respectively, people with such limited ability to maintain a 'self' that they are leery of the finality of tying the knot and people with a strong need to be stabilized by a marriage.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.4a`'s estimator gets its per-observable reading rules**, and every one of them is **asymmetric or two-sided**:

| Observable | Reading |
|---|---|
| Occupation | high → uninformative; **low despite opportunity → informative** (one-way implication) |
| Health | the *adaptation*, not the diagnosis |
| Longevity | **not** a proxy; a long-lived generation above a dysfunctional one is *evidence of projection* |
| Courtship | **both** tails informative, middle uninformative |

That is a materially better specification than `KS05.10`'s four-domain list, and it is the first place the corpus states *how to read* each observable rather than just naming it. The longevity rule is especially useful — it makes a *cross-generational discrepancy* the signal rather than any individual value.

## ⭐ KS11.8 — Family-level compartmentalisation as the mechanism for transmission `[T]` `[M]`

> "One hypothesis about why it works the way the theory describes is that **it is the nature of living systems to compartmentalize tension or anxiety in one part of the system to preserve the stability of the system as a whole. Homeostasis can be maintained in the family organism at the expense of some of its parts.**"

> "**all parents have strengths and weaknesses emotionally. It is inevitable that some children will pick up more of the strengths and others more of the weaknesses.**"

> "families that are commonly described as dysfunctional are **the outcome for a branch of a multigenerational family. The more functional branches have unwittingly played a role in fostering the development of the less functional branches.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS03.3`'s triangle rationale — compartmentalise so as to impair as few members as possible — restated as the **multigenerational** mechanism. So the same principle operates at three scales: triangle, nuclear family, multigenerational branch. That is a genuine unification and it argues for implementing compartmentalisation **once**, generically, rather than three times.

The branch claim is also a **scope requirement**: a dysfunctional family cannot be modelled in isolation, because its state is partly the product of its better-functioning relatives. Reinforces `KS10.10`'s two-scale requirement and bears on `M2.A`'s reference-family scope — the model may need collateral branches, not just a vertical line.

## ⭐ KS11.9 — Variation widens across generations `[T]` `[D]`

> "**every multigenerational family gradually produces the full continuum of basic levels of differentiation of self.**"

> "The **marked differences in shading in generation IV indicate a broader span of variation than the degree of variation that occurs between the two siblings in generation II.**"

> "**The essence of the multigenerational transmission process concept is that it describes a natural process that generates variation.**"

> "**The no-self end of the spectrum in some lines may include extinction or severe family fragmentation.** At the other end… the amount of 'self' that can develop is **limited by what biological and cultural evolution have determined thus far.**"

*Model consequence:* an assertable **variance** claim to sit alongside `M11.C.6`'s mean claim: over generations the **spread** of `basic_level` within a family widens, bounded below by line extinction and above by a species ceiling (`KS05.3`'s ~75). `M11.C.6` currently tests only the projection-line mean. A variance criterion is cheap to add and tests a different mechanism — and it is the concept's *essence* per Kerr, so a model that reproduces the mean but not the spread has missed the point.

The extinction clause is also the model's **absorbing state** at the bottom, matching `KS10.4`'s non-recovering system.

## KS11.10 — Level of functioning is not a diagnostic category `[T]`

> "**Level of emotional functioning is not a diagnostic category; it transcends all diagnostic categories. The lower the level of emotional functioning at a given period of time, the more the chronic anxiety associated with that lower level accentuates the obsessiveness of a person with a tendency to such traits or the antisocial acts of a person with that tendency.**"

*Model consequence:* differentiation is **orthogonal to symptom type** and acts as an **amplifier on whatever tendency exists**. That is a cleaner formulation than `M1.A.11`'s three substitutable channels alone: the channel is set by the person's own predisposition (`KS04.9`'s "genes, toxins, and pathogens"), and the *level* sets the amplitude. Worth stating explicitly, because it removes any temptation to derive symptom *type* from relational position.

## KS11.11 — Reactivity is the problem, not emotion `[T]`

> "**the problems related to too much or too little emotion are not related to the emotions themselves but to the internal and external emotionally competent stimuli to which people have learned to be anxiously overreactive or underreactive** (in the sense of automatically constraining emotional expression). **Anxiety-driven emotional reactivity is the core problem, not the phenomenon of emotional reactivity itself.**"

*Model consequence:* two-sided once more — **overreactive and underreactive are both faults**, and the parenthesis defines underreactivity as *automatic constraint of expression*, i.e. not a virtue. Supports `KS05.13`/`KS06.15`'s guard against modelling differentiation as damping, and gives `M1.A.1`'s derived reactivity a **target value rather than a minimum**.

## KS11.12 — Neutrality yields two abilities, not one `[T]`

> "**Neutrality is a basis for more tolerance for the nature of the human condition — less judgmental — but also the basis for more ability to stand thoughtfully firm when that is important to do in an anxious world. These two seemingly unrelated abilities both relate to being able to focus on facts and to reason in intense emotional situations.**"

*Model consequence:* the same quantity — `systems_perspective` plus solid self — produces **both** reduced reactivity **and** increased capacity to hold a position. The model should not need two variables for "calm" and "firm", and an implementation in which higher differentiation only damps is missing half the effect. This is §G7's *seeing* and *aiming* arriving from one source, and it is the reason `M4.D.4` forbids `I-POSITION` propensity from being monotone in `basic_level` while still requiring capacity to rise.

## KS11.13 — Vonnegut against Santayana `[T]`

Kerr sets the two epigraphs against each other deliberately: Santayana's "those who do not remember the past are condemned to repeat it" against Vonnegut's "**we're doomed to repeat the past no matter what.**"

> "**Vonnegut implies that it is extremely difficult to modify automatic emotional processes that have come down through the generations. The effort to consciously control intense emotional reactivity puts free will to the test!** However, Bowen theory's ability to help people become more objective about the past helps people shift from **how they see the world to how the world really is — within limits, of course.**"

*Model consequence:* the honest position on the model's own central question, from the source: change is possible, hard, and bounded. "**Within limits, of course**" is the same hedge as `KS02.1`'s ceiling and `KS05.2`'s floor at 25. Three independent bounds on `M1.A.4a`'s estimator, all pointing the same way — the model should be **pessimistic by default** about basic-level movement, and `M11.C.17`'s no-drift criterion is the right shape.
