# FE08 — Multigenerational Emotional Process

**Citation rule:** cite `FE08`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person; cites Warthin, Lynch, Woodyatt & Spetz as
other researchers. `[K]`.

> **The chapter that supplies magnitudes.** It gives the quantum-jump conditions with a rate, the
> typical per-generation step, the traversal timing in both directions, and a mechanism that turns
> `M7.E.2`'s stochastic rate from an invented parameter into a derived one. It also **contradicts
> `M1.A.11b`.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE08.1 — The **quantum-jump conditions**: three conjunctive mechanisms with a rate `[K]` `[T]` `[#]`

> "**Marked downward changes in differentiation (quantum jumps), although uncommon, can occur if the
> following conditions are present in two successive generations: (1) The primary mechanism for
> binding family anxiety is focus on one particular child** (a large portion of the family problem is
> absorbed in the impaired functioning of one child); **(2) the nuclear family is poorly connected to
> emotional support systems, especially to the extended family system** (the isolation increases
> family anxiety and intensifies the focus on the child); **and (3) there is at least an average amount
> of stress on the family** (unusually favorable circumstances do not ameliorate the effects of the
> first two conditions). **If these conditions are met, a 20-point difference in basic levels of
> differentiation between grandparents and a grandchild can develop (about ten points in each
> generation).**"

*Model consequence:* **the most specific mechanism-plus-magnitude statement in the whole corpus, and
it is directly implementable and directly testable.**

Three named conditions, **conjunctive**, held over **two successive generations**, producing **~10
points per generation**. Every one of the three is a quantity the model already carries:

| Condition | Model quantity |
|---|---|
| (1) anxiety bound primarily in **focus on one child** | `M1.D.1`'s sink allocation concentrated on the projection sink, and *fixed* rather than rotating (`FE07.6`) |
| (2) **poorly connected to the extended family** | `M1.D.8` and `M2.3` — the family is not the boundary; ties to families of origin are an input |
| (3) at least **average stress** | `M1.F.6`'s stressor spells at baseline |

**A three-way ablation test falls straight out.** Run the full-conjunction arm against three arms
each missing one condition; the full arm must show roughly double the per-generation decline. That
is a stronger criterion than `M11.C.6` currently states and it is the first place the corpus lets a
*rate* be asserted at all.

**⚠ And that needs care against `M10.C.3`.** The spec forbids implementing "any per-generation
`basic_level` decrement", because two figures previously treated as rates were manufactured from
illustrations Bowen explicitly bounded. `FE08.1` is a different kind of statement: it is a rate
**conditional on three named mechanisms**, offered as an uncommon case, not a constant applied every
generation. So the correct implementation is that **the rate is an output of the three mechanisms,
never a parameter** — which is exactly `M10.A.2`'s preference. `M10.C.3` should be amended to say
so explicitly rather than left to be read as forbidding this.

Condition (2) also gives `M1.D.8` and `M2.3` a **quantified consequence** they have never had: the
prohibition on a closed nuclear four is not a modelling nicety, it is one of three conditions that
doubles the transmission rate.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE08.2 — Traversal timing, **both directions** — and the `KS11.1` tension dissolves `[K]` `[#]`

> "**Marked discrepancies in functioning can occur in as few as three generations.** … **Such quantum
> jumps in functioning are uncommon, however. It is much more common for only mild to moderate
> discrepancies in functioning to exist after four or five generations. So a fairly stable nuclear
> family unit can have a descendant who has a chronic schizophrenic level of functioning in just three
> generations (a quantum jump), but it is more common for such a marked decrease in level of
> functioning to require five to ten generations to develop. Similarly, a fairly unstable nuclear
> family unit can, in three or four generations, have a descendant whose functioning is stable in most
> aspects, but it is much more common for such a pronounced increase to develop over five to ten
> generations.**"

And the calendar: a "no-self" is "**an outcome of a multigenerational process that is 75 (three
generations) to 250 (ten generations) years old**" — i.e. ~25 years per generation.

*Model consequence:* **`FE07.7`'s flagged tension with `KS11.1` is resolved, and `M11.C.6(a)` gets a
better constraint.**

`FE07.7` recorded that at ±5–10 points a generation, `KS11.1`'s "eight to ten generations **or as few
as three or four**" looked inconsistent. `FE08.2` states the same range in 1988 and explains it:
**three generations is the quantum-jump case (uncommon, requires `FE08.1`'s three conditions); five
to ten is typical.** At ~10 points a generation under the conjunction, three generations gives 30
points — exactly `FE07.7`'s stated maximum. **The two figures are the same claim.** No
reconciliation item remains.

Two further requirements:

- **The timing is symmetric.** The same three-to-ten range applies to *increases*. `M11.C.6` asserts
  decline in the projection line only; `FE08.2` requires the model to produce **upward** trends at
  the same rates. With `FE05.11` ("every family, given sufficient generations, spawns fairly high
  levels of differentiation as well") this is now stated twice, and a decline-only implementation is
  a failing one.
- **`M3.B.1`'s slow tick and generation length.** ~25 years per generation is a usable `[#]` for
  `M2.A`'s three-generation instance, whose ages (81/78 → 52/50 → 22/17/14) already imply ~28–30.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE08.3 — ⚠ Genes set the **symptom**; learning sets the **category** — against `M1.A.11b` `[K]` `[T]` `[X]`

Footnote 25:

> "**Genes are an important influence on the type of symptom that develops, but learning based on
> childhood experience appears to be the most important influence on the category of clinical
> dysfunction (physical, emotional, social) that develops.** The characteristics of family
> relationships that foster one category of dysfunction rather than another are still not well
> defined, but differences clearly exist… **Genes can influence the specific symptom that develops, but
> they seem to have less influence on the category of dysfunction.** For example, **if a person with a
> genetic predisposition to cancer (symptom) is 'programmed' during childhood to act out his anxiety
> (social dysfunction) rather than to internalize it, he may never develop cancer. Genetic
> predisposition to a disease, however, can be strong enough to override relationship programming.**"

`FE07` states the same from the relational side: "**The type of symptom that develops (physical,
emotional, or social) is connected both to the particular way an individual manages anxiety and to
what others in the system focus on in that individual when they get anxious.** For example, some
families focus on physical health, others focus on real or imagined deficiencies in character and
emotional well-being, and others focus on behavior."

*Model consequence:* **this contradicts `M1.A.11b`, and it is the most consequential divergence in
the run.**

`M1.A.11b` requires:

> "Which channel a person expresses **MUST** be exogenous constitutional data, and the model **MUST
> NOT** derive symptom *type* from relational position… Level sets amplitude and **sign**;
> constitution sets **channel**." → `KS22.14`, `KS21.5` (2019)

`FE08.3` inverts that assignment: **constitution sets the specific symptom within a category;
relationship process sets the category.** And `FE07` names the relational determinant — *what the
family focuses on when anxious* — which is a quantity the model already carries in `M9` and
`M1.B.8`.

**Three notes before this goes to pass 2:**

- The 2019 citations may be about the **specific symptom** rather than the category. `KS22.14`'s
  wording — "whether the chronic anxiety plays out as **schizophrenia rather than some other clinical
  dysfunction**" — is ambiguous on exactly this point, and `KS21.5`'s "certain predispositions… can
  become assets or liabilities" is about amplitude, not channel. If both are within-category, the
  two texts are **compatible** and `M1.A.11b` simply has the level wrong.
- `FE08.3`'s last clause preserves an override: "**Genetic predisposition to a disease… can be strong
  enough to override relationship programming.**" So constitution is not merely a tiebreak.
- The consequence for the model is large either way. If the category is relationally determined,
  then `M1.A.11a`'s internalise/externalise axis becomes an **output** of the family's focus rather
  than a constitutional input, and `M11.C.2`'s concentration criterion gains a second dimension:
  symptoms concentrate on the projection target **and in the channel the family attends to**.

**Carry to pass 2 and to the decision document. `M1.A.11b` must not be edited until it is resolved.**

## ⭐⭐ FE08.4 — Support-system availability **is** `M7.E.2`'s stochastic rate `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A nuclear family's ability to sustain contact with emotional support systems is a particularly
> important influence on the rate of downward or upward progression in basic differentiation.**"

The worked case:

> Two parents "**highly emotionally dependent on the husband's extended family**"… "**During the year
> after the birth of this couple's second child, however, the husband's parents both died suddenly and
> this couple's situation changed dramatically. As the husband said, 'My parents died and the castle
> crumbled.' The couple divorced two years after the husband's parents' deaths and became markedly
> estranged from one another. After the divorce, most of the mother's worries and fears were focused
> heavily on the children, both of whom grew up to have significant life problems. Had the husband's
> parents lived longer, the marriage might have lasted and the children's lives might have been more
> stable.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.E.2`'s "stochastic" rate becomes derived rather than invented — a genuine
`M10.A.2` win.**

`M7.E.2` requires the generational rate to be **stochastic** — "fast for a few generations, static
for one or two, then fast again" — and forbids a fixed decrement. It gives no account of *what
varies*. `FE08.1` and `FE08.4` together supply it: **the rate varies because the three conditions
come and go**, and support-system availability is the one that moves fastest.

So the model does not need a random rate at all. It needs the three conditions evaluated per
generation, and the observed pattern — fast, static, fast — falls out of their conjunction being
intermittently satisfied. That is strictly better than a stochastic parameter, and it makes the
apparent randomness a **readout** rather than an input.

The case is also a complete `M11.C.11` removal fixture with a **three-generation consequence**:
two structurally important members removed at once (`M1.A.13a`), the marriage fails, the sink
reallocates onto the children (`FE07.13`'s residual-sink rule), and the next generation's level is
lower.

## ⭐⭐ FE08.5 — Two sources of chronic stress — and one of them the model does not have `[K]` `[T]`

> "This predictable stress emanates from two main sources. **The first source is a type of
> psychological pressure individuals exert on themselves. The lower an individual's level of
> differentiation, the more his uncertainty about who he is and about what he stands for.** The
> uncertainty is reflected in a preoccupation with questions such as… What do others think of me? Am I
> acceptable to others? What do others want me to do? … **This preoccupation or rumination is a
> self-imposed psychological stress. A second source of stress that predictably increases as
> differentiation decreases is a pressure that people actually exert on one another to think, feel,
> and act in certain ways.**"

Footnote 7 adds the third source and, importantly, which are modifiable:

> "**people can be 'programmed' on biological and psychological levels to have a certain level of
> anxiety**… **Of the various sources of anxiety, however, self-imposed psychological pressure and
> pressure from the relationship system are particularly important to recognize because people's
> anxious responses to these stresses can be modified.**"

*Model consequence:* **a clean three-way decomposition that matches `M1.A.7a`, and a missing term.**

| Source | Model | Modifiable? |
|---|---|---|
| Programmed in childhood | `M1.A.7` `programmed_reactivity` | no |
| Pressure from the relationship system | `M4.A.1` standing load from every tie | yes |
| **Self-imposed psychological pressure** | **nothing** | yes |

The model has **no self-generated load**. `M4.A.1` takes a load from every tie; `M4.C.1` takes it
from delivered events; nothing generates load from the agent's own uncertainty. `FE08.5` requires
one, it scales inversely with `basic_level`, and it is therefore **derivable** under `M10.A.1`
rather than being a new parameter. **New requirement candidate**, and it matters for `M11.C.17`:
an isolated agent in the model currently generates nothing at all.

It also matters for `M1.E.7`: the two modifiable sources are exactly what a landed contact acts on,
and the unmodifiable one is why `M1.A.7`'s value is a disposition rather than a target.

## ⭐⭐ FE08.6 — The reserve, second chapter — and the retirement case is `M1.D.2a`'s fixture `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The more adjustments people make to reduce anxiety, the fewer options or less 'emotional reserve'
> they have for managing additional anxiety. The less the flexibility of an individual and of a family
> system, the less adaptive that individual and family system to life events that have emotional
> significance. So as differentiation decreases, adaptiveness decreases.**"

Case one: a couple whose reserve was consumed by mutual adjustment had "**little 'emotional reserve'
for adapting to a routine life event, the birth of a child**".

Case two, and this is the valuable one:

> "Over the years, the marriage had required considerable emotional distance to keep it in equilibrium…
> After the husband retired, the level of chronic anxiety in the family increased markedly. **While
> several factors contributed… a very important factor was that the mechanism of binding anxiety
> through emotional distance (reinforced by physical distance) was less available to the couple
> following retirement. Husband and wife were physically together much more than in the past and that
> physical proximity inundated the marriage with 'too much togetherness.'** … **Anxiety that was bound
> previously in emotional distance 'spilled over' into clinical symptoms.** The wife began to drink
> heavily and developed a duodenal ulcer."

*Model consequence:* **`FE03.10`'s emotional reserve carried by a second chapter with the causal chain
complete** — level → anxiety → adjustments → reserve consumed → adaptiveness falls — which is the
derivation `FE03.10` proposed (*capacity − accommodation stock*).

And the retirement case is **`M1.D.2a`'s requirement demonstrated**: distance binds; prevent the
distancing and the anxiety returns to the person as symptom. Here the prevention is structural — the
physical separation that had been supporting the emotional distance is removed by retirement — and
the return is immediate and clinical.

It is the exact mirror of `FE02.12`'s arthritis case, where physical separation was *added* and the
symptom remitted. **Two cases, opposite directions, same mechanism.** Together they are the best
`M11` fixture pair in the run for the distinction between standing load (`M4.A.1`, unchanged) and
delivered load (`M4.C.1`, changed).

## ⭐⭐ FE08.7 — Symptoms skip generations; **the process never does** `[K]` `[T]` `[#]`

> "**Psychiatric diagnostic categories that 'run in families' may 'skip' generations. Such 'skips' make
> it very difficult for researchers attempting to find genetic patterns in mental illness. While
> symptoms may skip one or more generations, the multigenerational emotional process never 'skips.'
> Basic levels of differentiation in one generation always limit the range of possibilities for basic
> levels of differentiation in the next generation.**"

With the typical step, tighter than `FE07.7`'s range:

> "even though basic levels of differentiation are similar from one generation to the next (**usually
> less than five points difference**)"

Three reasons a symptom skips: the **binding pattern** changes; the **life events** vary; the
**support systems** change.

And where the undifferentiation goes instead:

> "**while the illness itself may skip a generation, the family may still have less differentiation or
> adaptiveness in that generation than in the previous one. The undifferentiation in the 'skipped'
> generation may be absorbed, for example, by dysfunction in the spouse of the person who has the
> genetic vulnerability to the 'family disease.'**"

*Model consequence:* **a complete magnitude picture for `M7.E.1a`, and an invariant.**

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Typical per-generation step | **< 5 points** | `FE08.7` |
| Range that produces divergent life courses | **5–10 points** | `FE07.7` |
| Hard bound, parent to child | **never 30** | `FE07.7` |
| Quantum jump, three conditions | **~10 per generation, two generations** | `FE08.1` |

And the invariant: **`basic_level(child)` is always bounded by `basic_level(parents)` — the process
never skips.** That is assertable every slow tick, and it is a stronger statement than `M7.E.1a`'s
three-outcome distribution, which permits but does not require the bound.

The skipped-generation passage is also a **readout trap** for `M11.F.6`: a symptom-free generation is
not a differentiated one, and the model should be able to show the load sitting in a different sink
while the family's own account says the problem skipped.

## ⭐⭐ FE08.8 — **Anticipation**: earlier onset and greater severity down the generations `[K]` `[#]`

Warthin (1913), on high-incidence cancer families:

> "**In a family showing the occurrence of carcinoma in several generations, there is a decided tendency
> for the neoplasm to develop at an earlier age in the members of the youngest generations. In this
> case, the neoplasm often shows an increased malignancy.**"

Woodyatt & Spetz (1942), on diabetes, quoted at length:

> "**The whole course can be run in two generations, but it is more commonly completed in three or four
> and rarely in more.** … **those patients that develop the disease in later life appear as cases of
> first or second generation. They are offshoots of a vine that has been affected for only a limited
> time**… **juvenile diabetic patients appear as cases of following generations**… **They are shoots from
> a vine that has been diseased for a long time.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.6`'s decline made observable through a proxy the model can emit.**

The criterion currently asserts declining mean `basic_level` in the projection line — an internal
quantity with no instrument. `FE08.8` supplies an **observable** with the same direction: **age of
symptom onset falls and severity rises across generations in the affected line**, while the
unaffected line shows neither. That is directly assertable from the event log and it is a better
test than one on a hidden variable.

Record the evidential status honestly and prominently: these are a 1913 pathology paper and a 1942
medical-genetics paper, **read by Kerr as consistent** with the theory. Neither measured
differentiation. Kerr's own footnote 17 italicises the caveat — the connection "**has not been
proven**" — and he states that anticipation "**does not always occur**", with counterexamples. Cite
for the **shape**, never as validation.

## ⭐⭐ FE08.9 — Line extinction as the lower bound — with a two-sided caveat `[K]` `[T]`

Warthin's "cancerous fraternities": "**small two-or-three-generation families in which the majority of
family members die of cancer and some die of tuberculosis**… **The last members of the family line die
of cancer in their twenties, thirties, or early forties without leaving descendants.**"

And Kerr's generalisation:

> "**The 'cancerous fraternities' Warthin described, in other words, are only one of many types of
> 'fraternities' that can be associated with the extinction of a family line.** … **The type of
> dysfunction that is present appears to be less important for determining extinction than the
> intensity of the emotional process underneath that dysfunction.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.6(b)`'s variance requirement is bounded below by **line extinction** — the
spec cites `KS20.1` (2019); this is 1988 with two data sources. And the extinction is **channel-
independent**, which is `M1.A.11b`'s "level sets amplitude" half surviving `FE08.3`'s challenge to
its other half.

Footnote 23 carries the two-sided caveat and it must travel with the finding: "**while one
manifestation of a low level of differentiation may be a failure to reproduce, a failure to
reproduce does not necessarily indicate a low level of differentiation. A low level of
differentiation can also be manifested in uncontrolled reproduction.**" Both tails. `M11.F.6` again,
and it forbids reading fertility as a differentiation readout in either direction.

## ⭐⭐ FE08.10 — The anti-blame clause — the framing `M11.F` needs, verbatim `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 26:

> "**Linking diseases to impairments in emotional adaptiveness is not equivalent to saying that
> individuals or families, by virtue of 'wrong attitudes' or 'bad habits,' create their illnesses.
> Emotionality is not a process people 'should' be able to control. The processes that undermine
> adaptiveness are rooted in the instinctual nature of man and operate automatically. Every person
> inherits an emotional system calibrated by his ancestors and he must manage that emotionality as
> best he can. If his ability to manage it is exceeded, the symptom he is most vulnerable to is
> waiting to express itself.** … **Symptoms do not indicate people 'should' have been able to adapt
> better. They simply indicate that people were not able to adapt better.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.F` requirement candidate, quoted verbatim.**

Every symptom the model emits is a claim about a simulated person, and the last two sentences are
the exact distinction the spec's framing section lacks: **not able**, not **should have been able**.
`M11.F.1` covers authority, `M11.F.2` analogy, `M11.F.3` general systems, `M11.F.4` disease
mechanisms — none of them covers the reading a symptom output most invites.

It also completes the set `KS05.17` (describes what is, not what should be), `KS01.5` (no
culpability), `KS03.3` (no victim), `FE04.19` (parents are not the cause) and `FE08.18` below
(beyond blame is not exoneration). Five statements, one clause.

## ⭐ FE08.11 — Agency is **graded**, stated plainly `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The assumption that naturally occurring processes have shaped and continue to shape human behavior
> does not imply human beings lack control over their destinies. However, all people do not have the
> same degree of control. The lower the basic level of differentiation of self, the more automatic
> human behavior is and the more the human is directed, for better and for worse, by his phylogenetic
> legacy. The higher the basic level of differentiation, the more control people have over their
> behavior.**"

*Model consequence:* `M4.D.1a`'s mixing weight and `M10.A.1a`'s derivation of agency as the solid-self
fraction, stated as plainly as anywhere in the corpus, and better than the ASR sources the spec
currently leans on. "**for better and for worse**" is the two-sided clause: the phylogenetic legacy
is not a defect, and low agency is not the same as bad outcomes.

## ⭐ FE08.12 — A 0–10 child implies parents ≤ 20 — the bound run **backwards** `[K]` `[#]`

> "**The parents of someone in the 0-10 range of differentiation may not have clinical schizophrenia,
> but their basic levels of differentiation are unlikely to be higher than 20. (Their functional levels
> may be higher than 20.)** … **A family that produces a member with such a low basic level usually has
> other members who also have fairly low levels.**"

And a structural discriminator between the two routes to the same endpoint:

> "**When severe schizophrenia emerges after several generational 'quantum jumps' in functioning, the
> adaptiveness of close relatives is less impaired than when it results from a more gradual
> generational process.**"

*Model consequence:* two things.

- **An inference rule**, assertable: `basic_level` at 0–10 in generation *n* implies ≤ 20 in
  generation *n−1*. That is `M7.E.1a`'s bound applied in reverse and it is the estimator's
  multigenerational check (`M1.A.4f`, `FE04.7`) with a number attached.
- **The fast and slow routes leave different family-wide signatures.** After a quantum jump the
  *relatives* are less impaired; after a gradual decline they are more so. That is a genuinely
  multigenerational prediction — it cannot be checked in one generation and cannot be produced by a
  model that only tracks a single line. **A good `M11.C.6` addition**, and it is the one result in
  this chapter that only a simulation could test at scale.

## ⭐ FE08.13 — Adaptiveness is **predicted**; symptoms are only **probable** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 8:

> "**while it is predictable that adaptiveness will decrease as basic differentiation decreases, it is
> only probable that the incidence and severity of symptoms and other life problems will increase as
> basic differentiation decreases.**"

The other variables named: "level of stress, biological factors, and patterns of emotional functioning
in the family."

*Model consequence:* **the source's own justification for `M11.2` and `M0.4`.**

`M11.2` requires that `M11.C.1`–`M11.C.16` be **ensemble** property tests and forbids single-run
assertions; `M0.4` requires a direction of difference between two arms rather than a threshold.
`FE08.13` says why in the theory's own terms: the deterministic prediction is about **adaptiveness**,
which is not directly observable, and the observable — symptoms — is only probabilistically related
to it. Quote it at `M11.2`.

It is also the cleanest statement of what the model can and cannot claim: it can assert the
direction of a distribution, never an individual outcome.

## ⭐ FE08.14 — The symptom insulates in **two directions**, and the allocation rule `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Preoccupation with psychotic ruminations is a type of internal withdrawal that appears to have two
> functions. It reduces a schizophrenic person's social contact, which provides some protection from
> the anxiety associated with dealing with others, and it insulates him somewhat from his own
> feelings.**"

> "**When anxiety increases in a family system, it comes to rest disproportionately in the family
> member with the least adequate emotional boundary.**"

*Model consequence:* the first quote is `FE05.8`'s protective binding with its mechanism — the symptom
is a **withdrawal with two targets**, others and self — which means `M7.D`'s symptom load is not only
an output but a functioning distance channel, consistent with `FE02.9`'s substitutable channels.

The second is `M11.C.2`'s allocation rule stated in one line, and it names the quantity that decides:
**boundary adequacy**, which the model expresses as `functional_level` in `M4.C.1`'s divisor.

## ⭐ FE08.15 — Research on one's own family lands **only when it disconfirms** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**an extremely important component of the process of psychotherapy is an individual's doing the
> research and thinking necessary to convince himself as to whether this theoretical construct is
> consistent with the facts, particularly the facts in his own family.**" … "**A 'research laboratory'
> is available to every person — his own family.**"

And the failure mode:

> "**Simply collecting information about multigenerational families is not enough to change a way of
> thinking. People can collect information without challenging their basic assumptions about the human
> process. Sometimes people collect just enough information to confirm preexisting notions about
> others.**"

*Model consequence:* **a condition on `M1.E.7c.1`.** The spec's first form of a landed contact is
**instance supply** — specific functional facts from the recipient's own history, expensive, requires
the log. `FE08.15` adds that supplying them is **not sufficient**: information that confirms the
recipient's existing account does not land, and people preferentially collect exactly that.

That is `FE01.10`'s foreclosure applied to the coach's most expensive move, and it makes
`M1.E.7c.1` conditional: instance supply lands **only when it disconfirms** the recipient's
attribution. Combined with `FE04.17` (landing is a collision and feels wrong), the model now has a
consistent account: **a landed contact is one whose content the recipient's frame cannot absorb.**

## ⭐ FE08.16 — Beyond blame is **not** exoneration `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Getting beyond blame does not mean exonerating people from the part they play or played in the
> creation of a problem. It means seeing the total picture, acquiring a balanced view — not feeling
> compelled to either approve or disapprove of the nature of one's own and other people's families.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.18a`'s two-sided readout — blame **or** praise is a loss — with the
precise formulation, and it forecloses the misreading that systems thinking excuses. Worth pairing
with `FE08.10` in `M11.F`: the model does not assign fault **and** does not deny participation.

## FE08.17 — The unidisease, in 1988 — and the second measured widening `[K]` `[K-ext]` `[X]`

> "**If physical and emotional illnesses are accurately conceptualized as symptoms of a more basic
> process, then all 'diseases' are rooted in one fundamental process, a unidisease. The unidisease may
> be anchored in the emotional processes that govern the adaptiveness of individuals and families.**"

*Model consequence:* **the ancestor of the 2019 book's `KS23` unidisease chapter — which the ledger
grades `[K-ext]` throughout — located thirty-one years earlier, and hedged.**

Note the 1988 framing: "**may be** anchored", offered as a conceptualisation that follows *if* the
