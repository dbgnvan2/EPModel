# FE02 — The Emotional System

**Citation rule:** cite `FE02`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person — "**Bowen's** concept of the emotional system",
"While **Bowen** developed the concept of these three systems based largely on clinical
observation…". `[K]` throughout.

**One caution that applies to this whole chapter.** It is the corpus's densest concentration of
**animal research by other people**, cited by Kerr in support. Every such finding is `[K]` for
*Kerr's reading of it* and carries the original researcher's name. None of it is Bowen data, none of
it measured differentiation, and a corroboration from a sunfish is corroboration of a *mechanism's
plausibility*, not of the theory. Recorded that way throughout.

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE02.1 — The emotional system **is** the agent loop, defined in one sentence `[K]` `[T]`

> "the concept postulates the existence of a naturally occurring system in all forms of life that
> enables an organism **to receive information (from within itself and from the environment), to
> integrate that information, and to respond on the basis of it.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M3.D.1`'s update order is the theory's own definition of its central object,
not an engineering convenience.** Receive → integrate → respond is `M4.B` perceive → `M4.C` appraise
→ `M4.D`/`M4.E` select and act, in that order and with no step omitted. The parenthesis matters
too: information arrives **from within itself and from the environment**, which is `M4.A`'s standing
load and `M7.D.1`'s endogenous symptom event sitting alongside `M4.B`'s inbox.

This is worth stating in `M4`'s preamble. The loop's shape has until now been justified by the
model's needs; it is in the source.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE02.2 — Emotion is **not felt** and must be **inferred from what is done and not done** `[K]` `[T]`

> "People can be aware of feelings by virtue of feeling them. **This contrasts with emotions, which
> are not felt. The influence of emotions must be inferred by observing what people and other
> organisms do and do not do in a given situation.** Feelings appear to be an intellectual or
> cognitive awareness of the **more superficial aspects** of the emotional system."

> "Many other animals frequently act as if they experience similar feelings, but there is little
> evidence that many of them do. They are simply reacting emotionally. The assumption is that
> **humans are reacting emotionally too, but with a layer of feeling on top of it.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.0` in written primary text — replace the ASR citations.** The spec
currently sources *emotional = instinct* to `kb02`/`kb13`, both under a no-verbatim constraint.
This is quotable and it adds two things those do not:

1. **The inference rule for every readout in the model.** Emotion is recovered from the **event
   log**, and specifically from *acts and omissions*. That is the epistemic justification for
   `M4.D.1b` — `WITHHOLD` must be a first-class outcome that **changes tie state**, because "what
   people do **not** do" is evidence of the same quantity as what they do. A no-op withhold would
   discard half the observable.
2. **Feeling is a partial, superficial readout of the emotional system** — so a feeling-derived
   measure is systematically incomplete, which is why `M11.F.6`'s readout traps are traps.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE02.3 — Calhoun's rats: reciprocity **re-forms in a group selected to have none** `[K]` `[#]` (Calhoun 1963)

> "these rats, **even though bred to be almost genetically identical**, when placed in a group could
> be differentiated from one another in terms of level of activity and how much they interacted with
> other rats. **This differentiation phenomenon was a constant feature of every colony.** Even when
> Calhoun used the most highly inbred strain of rat he could obtain, the colony still spawned active
> dominant rats and less active submissive ones. **If a group is formed of rats who were all dominant
> in their previous groups, a new hierarchy will lead to some of the formerly dominant rats becoming
> less active and submissive. By the same token, if a group is formed consisting of rats who were
> all submissive in their previous groups, dominant rats will then emerge in the new group.**"

> "**It would appear that it is not necessary to breed for this relationship process, nor can the
> process be bred out of rats.**"

*Model consequence:* **a new acceptance criterion, and it is the cleanest falsifier in the corpus.**

`M11.C.24` tests that dissolving a discrepant pair makes the discrepancy *reverse*. `FE02.3` tests
the complement: **construct a family from agents all at the same pole and the reciprocity re-forms
anyway.** Two arms — an all-overfunctioner instance and an all-underfunctioner instance, matched
`basic_level`, no discrepancy at `t0` — must both develop a spread in `functioning_balance`
comparable to a mixed arm. If the model can only *maintain* a polarity it was initialised with, then
`M1.B.5` is a lookup table rather than a process.

This is a **stronger** test than `M11.C.24` because it needs no initial gap at all, and it kills a
whole class of degenerate implementation. **New requirement candidate — provisional `M11.C.25`.**

Record honestly what it is: an inbred-rat experiment from 1963, read by Kerr as consistent with the
theory. Calhoun did not measure differentiation and the model is not being calibrated to rats. What
transfers is the **shape of the claim** — that stratification is generated by the relationship
process rather than carried in by the individuals.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE02.4 — Greenberg's sunfish: remove the sink and **the residue redirects onto whoever arrives next** `[K]` `[#]` (Greenberg 1946)

> "Sunfish organize themselves in a social hierarchy. Greenberg found that **when subordinate,
> nonterritorial green sunfish are removed from aquaria, the remaining territorial residents become
> more aggressive toward one another. When a strange fish is then introduced, it becomes the new
> target for attacks. It is as if these subordinate individuals serve as an 'aggression sink.'**"

*Model consequence:* **`M6.I.6`, `M11.C.11` and `M11.C.13` are one mechanism, and here it is in a
controlled removal experiment.** Every element of `M11.C.11` is present: removal, no reduction in
total, redistribution onto the remaining members, and — the part the spec has least evidence for —
**retargeting onto a newcomer**. Note the two-stage shape: the load first raises conflict *among the
remainder* and only then finds a new target. `M11.C.11`'s three phases have the same profile.

It also supplies an independent, non-clinical use of the word **sink** for exactly the quantity
`M1.D.1` allocates. The term is Greenberg's and Wilson's, not Bowen's; note that in any writeup so
the convergence is not overstated as shared vocabulary.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.5 — Functioning position determines **biology**, not only behaviour `[K]` `[T]` (Robertson 1972)

> "Social groups consist of one male and a harem of females occupying a common territory. Robertson
> (1972) has shown that **the male suppresses the tendency of the females to change sex by
> aggressively dominating them. When he dies, the dominant female in the group immediately changes
> sex and becomes the new harem master!**"

And Kerr's reading:

> "the sex of a particular fish is **a product of its functioning position in the social group.**"

*Model consequence:* the extreme case of `M1.A.14b` — **no positional attribute may be read from a
label; every one is derived from observed function.** It is also `M7.D.2b`'s removal producing
opposite-signed, position-keyed effects, with the sharpest possible instance of the *positive* sign:
the removal is a promotion for exactly one member, determined by rank rather than by relationship to
the removed one.

For the ant colony the same point is made developmentally: "**The type of ant a larva grows up to be
is not programmed into him at the time of birth. When the young larvae emerge, they are the 'raw
material' for the present need of the colony**"; "a young larva is **born into a functioning
position** in the colony and his development is determined by that position." That is `M7.E.1c`'s
history-dependent, emergent target selection.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.6 — Conviction strength is generated **independently of truth** `[K]` `[T]` (MacLean)

MacLean, quoted:

> "**nothing could be more fundamental than the realization that the primitive limbic system has the
> capacity to generate the strong feelings of conviction that we attach to our beliefs, regardless
> of whether they are true or false.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M9.2` has a mechanism.** The spec requires that truth value and emotional
function be orthogonal — that a claim be able to be accurate and still serve a denial — and gives no
account of *why*. This is why: **conviction is emitted by a different system from the one that
evaluates the claim.** So a belief's *strength* in `M9` must be a separate quantity from its
*accuracy*, driven by the emotional system, and the two must be independently settable. An
implementation in which strongly held beliefs are more likely to be true, or less, fails `M9.2` in
either direction.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.7 — The three systems influence each other **in both directions** — `M9` is currently one-way `[K]` `[T]`

> "It appears that the emotional, feeling, and intellectual systems **mutually influence one
> another.** Emotional reactions can trigger feeling reactions and feeling reactions can trigger
> thoughts that are colored by those feelings. **The opposite process can also occur: Subjectively
> determined thoughts can trigger feelings and those feelings can trigger emotional reactions.**"

> "**It is inaccurate to consider any one of these systems as 'better' than the others.** They are
> all a product of evolution and have been retained in the evolutionary process."

*Model consequence:* **a real gap.** `M9` is a *record*: beliefs are written by events (`M9.3`) and
may differ from ground truth (`M9.1`), but nothing lets a belief **drive** an emotional reaction.
`FE02.7` says the reverse path exists and is ordinary. Combined with `FE01.10`'s foreclosure and
`M4.C.2`'s gain function, the missing edge is: **a held belief raises appraised intensity on events
that conflict with it.** That is one term, it is implementable at `M4.C`, and without it the belief
layer cannot participate in the loop it is part of. **New requirement candidate.**

The second quote is `M11.F` material: no readout may rank the three systems, and in particular the
model **MUST NOT** present the intellectual system as the good one. `M1.A.3`'s licence framing
already avoids this; the framing requirement should say so explicitly.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.8 — One reaction, **three simultaneous manifestations** — with a worked pair `[K]` `[T]`

> "someone reacts to disapproval by **withdrawing** (an emotionally rooted response observed in all
> forms of life), **feeling sad** (a feeling experienced only by higher forms of life), and becoming
> **preoccupied with a sense of inadequacy** (a subjectively determined attitude of self…). Another
> example is a person who reacts to perceived disapproval with **an aggressive facial expression**
> (emotion), **anger** (feeling), and **a self-righteous assertion of the 'correct' viewpoint**
> (subjective)."

*Model consequence:* two things.

- **These are three manifestations of one reaction, not three events.** An `Event` (`M1.F.1`)
  carries one move; the feeling and belief-level manifestations are *attributes* of it, not siblings
  in the queue. Worth stating so an implementation does not triple-count.
- **The two examples are `M1.A.9a`'s two axes.** The first — withdrawal, sadness, *a sense of
  inadequacy* — is the **inward-impingement** failure. The second — aggression, anger, *assertion of
  the correct viewpoint* — is the **outward** one. Same stimulus, disapproval; opposite counterfeit.
  This is a ready-made fixture for `M11.C.19`, which needs exactly a pair at equal magnitude
  separated by axis, and the second example is `M5.F.4`'s assertion form verbatim.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.9 — Blocking the distance channel forces it into **other channels** `[K]` `[T]` (Wilson 1975)

The porcupine fable, quoted by Wilson and by Kerr:

> "their spines made proximity uncomfortable, so they moved apart again and got cold. After
> shuffling repeatedly in and out, **they eventually found a distance at which they could still be
> comfortably warm without getting pricked.**"

And the mechanism:

> "the individual distance observed between animals is **the compromise struck by animals that are
> both attracted to other members of their own species and repelled by them at short distances.**"

> "**If animals are forced into abnormal proximity, they will seek distance through other means,
> such as hiding or averting direct gaze.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.D.2a` and `M6.I.6` in one observation, and the last sentence is the
important one.** `M1.D.2a` requires that anxiety return to the person if distancing is *prevented*.
`FE02.9` says what actually happens first: **the organism substitutes another distancing channel.**
So the model needs distance to be a *set* of substitutable channels — physical separation, topic
avoidance (`M1.B.10`'s taboo set), gaze/attention withdrawal, narrowing `areas_of_joint_activity`
(`M1.B.9`) — with `M1.D.2a`'s return-to-person as what happens when **all** of them are blocked, not
when one is. That is a meaningful sharpening, and it makes `M11.C.13`'s relocation scoring apply
inside the distance mechanism as well as across it.

It is also the **two-sided closeness band** (`M11.F.6`, `L12.4`) with a named optimum, and the fable
is worth quoting because "decency and good manners" names the set point as a *convention* — the
observable, not the force.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.10 — Behavioral scaling: an **ordered ladder driven by one scalar** `[K]` `[T]` (Wilson 1975)

Wilson's illustrative case, quoted in full by Kerr:

> "**At low population densities, all aggressive behavior is suspended. At moderate densities, it
> takes a mild form, such as intermittent territorial defense. At high densities, territorial
> defense is sharp, while some joint occupancy of land is also permitted under a regime of dominance
> hierarchies. Finally, at extremely high densities, the system may break down almost completely**,
> transforming the pattern of aggressive encounters into homosexuality, cannibalism, and other
> symptoms of 'social pathology.'"

*Model consequence:* **`M4.D.3a`'s complexity ordering, from the outside.** `M4.D.3a` derives the
ordering from evolutionary age — cooperation → conflict → dominant-adaptive → distance — and slides
selection down it with anxiety. Behavioral scaling is the same construction with **density** as the
driver and the ladder running the other way, ending in structural breakdown. Two independent
derivations of *one scalar indexing an ordered repertoire* is good evidence the shape is right.

It is also a second witness for `M1.D.7k` — the dial is **non-monotone with an optimum**, and both
tails are bad. Note the honest limits: Wilson labels this an "imaginary case", it is about
population density rather than anxiety, and the terminal list reflects 1975 categories. Cite it for
the **shape**, never for the contents of the last rung.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.11 — The phobia case: the full trajectory, including that **reciprocity is stress-gated, not pathological** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**If the level of stress on the family is not too great, this overfunctioning-underfunctioning
> reciprocity may aid more than detract from the functioning of the individuals involved.** The
> overfunctioning one derives a sense of well-being by virtue of doing for others, and the
> underfunctioning ones derive well-being from being the object of that ministration. **If stress on
> the family increases, however, these functioning positions are pushed toward the extremes.**"

Then the sequence, in order: pressure converges on the overfunctioner → "In time, the overfunctioning
one can **'absorb' a disproportionate amount of the family problem**" → "she feels increasingly
overloaded, overwhelmed, and unsupported, and may then begin to experience the onset of phobic
symptoms" → **and then the pole inverts:**

> "**Following the onset of symptoms, an interesting change in this overfunctioning and
> underfunctioning reciprocity may occur. The wife begins to increasingly depend on her husband…
> Her ability to function for herself becomes increasingly constrained because of her symptoms and
> because of the family's willingness to function for her in many areas.** As the 'healthy' ones
> increasingly function for the 'sick' one in this way, **an enduring type of family stability can
> develop, a stability that is accompanied by the presence of a chronic symptom. It is easier for
> family members to make accommodations that make it possible to live with the symptom than it is to
> address the underlying relationship process that fosters the symptom in the first place.**"

*Model consequence:* **the single most complete worked trajectory in the corpus for `M7.D`, and it
carries one requirement the spec does not have.**

- **Reciprocity is not a defect.** At low load it *raises* both parties' functioning. `M1.B.5` is
  written neutrally but nothing states the benign regime, and an implementation that penalises
  reciprocity at all loads will never reproduce the ordinary stable family. **New requirement
  candidate:** the sign of the coupling's contribution to functioning **MUST** be load-dependent —
  positive below a threshold, negative above it. This is `M11.F.6`'s two-sidedness applied to the
  model's most-used mechanism.
- **The pole inverts *at the symptom*.** The overfunctioner becomes the dependent one. `M1.B.6`
  requires the flip to be relative and immediate, but the *trigger* here is the symptom itself, and
  `M5.E.8`'s observed pursue/distance inversion is the same event in a different pattern.
- **`M4.D.5a`'s accommodation stock and `M7.D.2a`'s lock-in are one process**, stated in one
  sentence: accommodating is *cheaper* than addressing. That is `M4.D.6a`'s forbidden short-horizon
  gradient again, at family level.

## ⭐⭐ FE02.12 — The arthritis case: geographic separation, symptom remission, **coupling intact** `[K]` `[T]`

> "In December, the husband had to move temporarily to another city because of job circumstances.
> **Within three weeks after his departure, his symptoms dramatically improved.** The husband said
> he was a calmer person when not in the physical presence of his wife. **He was so oriented to
> reacting to the look on her face, her perceived mood, and what he thought she wanted him to do,
> that it was difficult for him to feel he had adequate 'space' when she was present. While he was
> away, he missed his wife terribly, but did not miss the way he felt when he was home.**"

*Model consequence:* **this case looks like a counterexample to `M1.B.2` and is in fact the fixture
that proves the architecture.**

`M1.B.2` forbids `conductance` from being a function of physical distance; `M1.B.4` forbids
`bond_energy` decay; `L06.3` records that physical distance does not attenuate coupling. Here
geographic separation produced dramatic remission in three weeks. The resolution is in the text:
**he missed her terribly** — investment and bond energy are untouched — while *what stopped* was the
**per-tick delivered load** from her face and mood. That is exactly the model's own split:

| Quantity | Spec | What the move did |
|---|---|---|
| `bond_energy`, `investment` | `M1.B.4`, `M1.B.8` | unchanged — "missed his wife terribly" |
| Standing load from the tie | `M4.A.1` | unchanged — it runs on bond energy |
| Delivered-event load | `M4.C.1` | **collapsed** — no face to read, no mood to appraise |

So `M1.B.2` survives intact and `FE02.12` becomes a **named fixture for the distinction**. Worth
adding to `M11` as a shape check: relocate one member, assert `bond_energy` flat, delivered-event
load down, symptom load down, and the standing load **unchanged** — the last being what stops
relocation reading as a cure. It is also the mechanism `M11.C.13` scores as *relocation*.

And the misattribution pair appears a second time, in a second chapter: "**She viewed their marital
difficulties as 'caused' by her husband, and he tended to fault himself**, saying, 'If only I was a
more adequate person, things would be better.' The process, although fostered by blaming and
self-blaming, **transcends blame**." With `FE01.1` that is **two independent instances of
outward-blame at one pole and inward-blame at the other** — enough to make the asymmetric belief
error a requirement rather than an observation.

## ⭐ FE02.13 — Functional sibling position, in 1988 `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 20:

> "A family's level of differentiation and the particular characteristics of parental attitudes may,
> within limits, alter the expectations for oldest and youngest children. **Parental attitudes and
> anxieties about an oldest child, for example, can result in that child's not meeting the
> expectations of a first born. In such instances, the second child will then develop some of the
> functional characteristics of a firstborn.**"

And the default, in the body:

> "the expectations of functioning for the various positions are **similar in all families**. These
> expectations are, to some extent, **built into the situation and not designed by the parents.**
> … **Even if parents try not to make the oldest child responsible for younger siblings, the process
> is so automatic that much of it will occur anyway.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.14a` gains a 1988 source.** The spec cites `KS12.2` (2019) for the
functional-oldest displacement; the same claim, with the same mechanism (projection onto the
firstborn), is here thirty-one years earlier. Two Kerr texts three decades apart stating it
identically is the strongest form of stability this project can measure on a single author.

The body quote is the complement and it is `[T]` support for `M1.A.14`'s *static* half: position
expectations are **structural, not parental policy**, and survive deliberate parental effort to
avoid them. That is the reason `M1.A.14` holds birth order as data at all.

## ⭐ FE02.14 — The underfunctioner's self-absorption is **not selfishness** `[K]` `[T]`

> "An underfunctioning person may get sick by withdrawing into a self-absorbed and helpless state.
> **This self-absorption is not the product of selfishness, but of having given up too much
> self-control.**"

> "The overfunctioning one **may not experience this degree of reliance as a burden.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.9a`'s two axes, named at the exact point where a scalar implementation
would get it wrong. Self-absorption *looks* like the selfish pole and is the **inward-impingement**
failure — having given up self-control, i.e. being acted on. `M5.F.2b` requires the counterfeit
detector to report which axis failed; this is the case that makes it necessary.

The second sentence is a warning for any readout keyed to distress: the overfunctioner may report no
burden at all while carrying the load, which is `M11.F.6`'s readout-trap list gaining another entry.

## ⭐ FE02.15 — Remission on the partner's illness or death `[K]` `[T]`

> "**An underfunctioning person who has been chronically ill may improve his functioning and
> experience a dramatic remission of symptoms if his overfunctioning partner becomes ill or dies.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.21` (reciprocity inverts when the overfunctioner's capacity falls) and

## ⭐ FE02.16 — The intellect **operates in the service of** the emotional process `[K]` `[T]`

> "**It is clear that much of the time man's intellect operates in the service of the feeling and
> emotional process.** When someone's imagination runs wild to the point of distorting reality, when
> someone plans a murder, or when a group of people justify their attempt to control others on the
> basis of having the 'right' political or religious viewpoint, **the intellect is functioning in
> support of the feeling and emotional process.**"

> "**emotionally governed thinking responses can be just as automatic and just as influential on
> behavior.** Values, beliefs, and attitudes that are often referred to as 'knee jerk' reactions are
> examples of automatic, emotionally determined 'thinking' responses… **The response may sound
> 'intellectual,' but it is primarily governed by emotion and feelings.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.3`'s licence framing is correct and this is why — the intellect is not
*suppressed* below the transition, it is **recruited**. An implementation that models low
differentiation as reduced cognitive capacity is wrong in a way that matters: the low-level agent
argues fluently, and `M5.F.4`'s assertion form is articulate. This should be said in `M1.A.3`'s
note, because "suppression of intellect" is the natural misreading and the spec only forbids it in
one clause.

## ⭐ FE02.17 — Nature is neutral; the **feeling system takes sides** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Nature is neutral. There is no right and wrong, good and bad. Nature is simply a process of
> interrelated events. But the feeling system and human subjectivity take sides in nature and impose
> on it what should be.** When human thinking is relatively free of subjectivity, **polarizations
> are seen for what they are: viewpoints fueled by passions, selfishness, defensiveness, dogmatism,

*Model consequence:* `KS05.17`'s "describes what is, not what should be" at the earlier date, and
`M4.C.4`'s polarity capture with its causal direction stated — polarisation is a *product of
subjectivity*, so `systems_perspective` falling and polarity capture rising are one event, not two.

## FE02.18 — The emotional **field**, and the gravity image `[K]` `[T]`

> "The emotionally determined functioning of the family members **generates a family emotional
> 'atmosphere' or 'field' that, in turn, influences the emotional functioning of each person.** It
> is analogous to the gravitational field of the solar system, where **each planet and the sun, by
> virtue of their mass, contribute gravity to the field and are, in turn, regulated by the field
> they each help create.** One cannot 'see' gravity, nor can one 'see' the emotional field. **The
> presence of gravity and the emotional field can be inferred… by the predictable ways planets and
> people behave in reaction to one another.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.7a` — chronic anxiety as a property of the field — with its clearest
statement.** The field is *constituted by* the members and *regulates* them; that circularity is the
requirement, and it is why `M1.A.7a` derives `chronic_anxiety` each slow tick rather than storing
it. Same image as `FE01.5`'s definition of `systems_perspective`, which is consistent: perspective
is the capacity to hold both directions of the field relation at once.

**Flag the tension, do not hide it.** This is an **analogy to a physical system**, and `FE00.1`
states that analogies "are not a sound basis on which to build a theory" and `FE01.14` names
physics-derived models as the general-systems error. Kerr uses it illustratively rather than as
support, and it should be quoted that way. `M11.F.2` and `M11.F.3` both bear on this and neither
currently notes that the corpus itself reaches for a physical analogy at its central concept.

## FE02.19 — The emotional system is claimed to sit **below genes** — and it strains `M1.A.11b` `[K]` `[T]` `[X]`

> "Family theory, in contrast, assumes that the functioning and behavior of all organisms are
> significantly influenced by an emotional system that is **anchored in the life process at a level
> probably more basic than genes.** This system is assumed to have its roots in protoplasm itself
> and **it may even influence the functioning of genes.**"

*Model consequence:* **record as contested and do not implement.** `M1.A.11b` requires that which
symptom channel a person expresses be **exogenous constitutional data**, and forbids deriving
symptom type from relational position. `FE02.19` claims the emotional process may reach into gene
expression, which points the other way.

The two are reconcilable — `M1.A.11b` is about the *channel*, and this passage is about *expression
levels* — but the reconciliation is the project's, not the source's, and it should be stated rather
than assumed. Note the hedges: "assumes", "probably", "may even". This is the chapter's most
speculative claim and Kerr marks it as such. `M11.F.4`'s prohibition is unaffected.

## FE02.20 — MacLean is a **parallel, not a mapping** `[K]` `[T]`

> "These formations are **not to be precisely equated** with the emotional, feeling, and intellectual
> systems described by family theory. Systems pertain to functions and interrelationships, and **it
> may never be possible to establish a one-to-one correspondence between functions,
> interrelationships and anatomical structures. Attempts to force such correspondence often lead to
> reductionism.**"

And on the prefrontal cortex, posed as a question rather than a claim:

> "**Are we the only animal capable of observing its internal emotional, feeling, and subjective
> states and, as a consequence, the only animal capable of some degree of choice about the influence
> of those states on its actions and inactions?**"

*Model consequence:* two notes for framing. First, no output may present the model's three-system
structure as neuroanatomy — the authors forbid the mapping in their own text. Second, the
prefrontal passage is the corpus's location of `M4.D.1a`'s **self-directed channel** and
`M10.A.1a`'s agency — *self-observation yielding some degree of choice* — and it is offered **as a
question**. `M4.D.1a` is graded correctly as `[M]`; this is the reason it must not rise to `[T]`.
