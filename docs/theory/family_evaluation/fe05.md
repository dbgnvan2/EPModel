# FE05 — Chronic Anxiety

**Citation rule:** cite `FE05`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person; cites Kandel (1983) and the family research in
the third person throughout. `[K]`.

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE05.1 — Symptom **exchange**: the corpus's clearest statement of "help relocates" `[K]` `[T]`

> "**symptoms are frequently 'exchanged' among family members. This exchange is a product of the
> borrowing and trading of 'self' (pseudo-self) or functioning that occurs among family members.
> Commonly, one person's symptom will stabilize or even disappear when his mate or another
> emotionally significant figure develops a symptom. Frequently, after a symptomatic child enters
> psychotherapy and improves, his parents will report that a sibling, previously functioning well,
> has developed problems. One spouse's previously labile hypertension may stabilize (without a
> change in medication) after his mate develops a chronic back problem.**"

> "**The occurrence of symptom exchanges means that the basic level of chronic anxiety or emotional
> problem in a family has not changed; the anxiety or undifferentiation has simply become
> 'agglutinated' or bound in a new place. People are not more differentiated or emotionally separate
> from one another; the focus has just moved from one person to another.**"

And the therapeutic warning, which is `M11.C.13` in its strongest form:

> "**This can be a 'side effect' of stress management approaches that ignore family process. After a
> symptomatic family member makes a firm commitment to biofeedback, extensive jogging, or even
> individual psychotherapy and his functioning begins to improve, another family member may develop
> problems. This results from a 'hot potato' approach (usually unwitting) to managing anxiety; the
> anxiety is unloaded from one person onto another. In these instances, the relationship process
> that contributes to symptom development is bypassed in favor of symptom relief. One person
> achieves comfort at the expense of another.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M6.I.6` and `M11.C.13` stated together with three concrete instances and a
name.** The sibling-develops-problems-after-the-child-improves case is a directly assertable
prediction and it is cleaner than anything the criterion currently rests on. `M11.C.13`'s
count-and-location scoring is exactly what "agglutinated in a new place" requires.

Note the honest hedge on mechanism — "**exactly how this exchange occurs is unknown**", though
"**assumed to be dependent on the transfer of information through the standard sensory
modalities**", which is `FE03.3`'s narrow observable channel and rules out anything the model would
have to invent.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE05.2 — The **discriminator** between relocation and real change — a new acceptance criterion `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Differentiation of self is a process that can reduce anxiety and symptoms in one person without
> symptoms resurfacing in another, except perhaps temporarily.** When one family member can become
> more aware of his own part in whatever problems exist, become more willing to assume
> responsibility for that part, and become more able to act on that basis, **improvements in his
> functioning will not be contingent on someone else 'absorbing' his share of the family's immaturity
> or undifferentiation. It is a change in functioning that is not accompanied by a seesaw effect.**"

> "When one person can work toward a slightly higher level of differentiation, he will reduce not
> only his own level of chronic anxiety but the level of chronic anxiety in the family as well.
> **Symptoms are reduced throughout the family, not just exchanged.**"

*Model consequence:* **the model's central distinction — reallocating the budget versus reducing it
— has no test, and this supplies one.**

**New criterion candidate.** Two arms, matched on the index person's improvement in
`functional_level`:

| Arm | Index person | Rest of family | Total bound anxiety |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Symptom-relief** (`M11.C.13`'s help) | improves | a symptom appears elsewhere | **unchanged** |
| **Differentiation** (completed `M5.D` sequence) | improves | transient disturbance only | **falls** |

The relief arm exercises `M6.I.6`'s conservation; the differentiation arm requires
`M1.D.1`'s `undifferentiation_budget` itself to shrink. Nothing in `M11.C` currently distinguishes
them, and an implementation that only ever reallocates would pass every existing criterion.

**And footnote 11 supplies the discriminator's fine structure, which is the valuable part:**

> "**When one person in a family can function as a little more of a self, other family members
> automatically oppose and test it. Progress disturbs the togetherness as much as regression does.
> There can be symptoms in another family member associated with someone's effort to change, but
> these symptoms will disappear when the system equilibrates on a higher level of functioning. If
> the symptoms do not disappear, this indicates that emotional distance has been a major component
> of the effort to 'change.'**"

Two requirements follow, and both are new:

- **"Progress disturbs the togetherness as much as regression does"** — `M11.F.6`'s two-sidedness
  reaching the change mechanism itself. A detector that only fires on deterioration misses half.
- **`M5.E.5`'s third-person symptom acquires a temporal signature.** The spec requires that push-back
  be able to surface as symptom in a third person; `FE05.2` says that in a **genuine** move the
  third-person symptom is **transient and resolves at the new equilibrium**, and that a
  **persistent** one is evidence the move was **distance in disguise**. That is a counterfeit
  detector (`M5.F.2a`) with a *time course* rather than a threshold, and it is computable from the
  event log with no new state.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE05.3 — Content is exogenous; **level sets amplitude and social consequence** — stated for everything `[K]` `[T]`

The chapter enumerates anxiety binders at length — relationships, drugs, over/undereating,
over/underachievement, health preoccupation, money, gambling, and a long list of traits
(obsessiveness/hysteria, impulsiveness/indecisiveness, passivity/aggressiveness,
shyness/obtrusiveness, procrastination, perfectionism, paranoia, grandiosity, optimism/pessimism)
— and then states the rule that governs all of them:

> "**The higher the level of anxiety, the more pronounced all these traits and behaviors become. The
> obsessiveness of a person at 25 on the scale will be more rigid and constraining than the
> obsessiveness of a person at 60 on the scale.** … At a scale level of 60, the obsessiveness may be
> confined to annoying ruminations and somewhat excessive perfectionism. At 25, a series of
> compulsions that are clearly irrational may be acted out. … **Temperance at 55 on the scale is
> unlikely to antagonize others; temperance at 35 on the scale can result in a polarization between
> 'reformers' and 'sinners.' Feminism at 50 on the scale is quite different from feminism at 30.**"

*Model consequence:* **this is `M1.A.11b`, `M1.A.14c` and `FE03.11` generalised to every content the
model carries, and it is the strongest statement of the gating rule anywhere.**

The same *content* — obsessiveness, temperance, a political commitment — produces different
*behaviour* and different *social consequence* at different levels. So:

- `M9`'s beliefs are **content**; their behavioural expression **MUST** be gated by
  `basic_level` (or its per-tie attenuated value, `M1.A.18d`).
- The gating is **not on holding the belief** but on **how it is enacted toward others** — "unlikely
  to antagonise" versus "polarization between reformers and sinners". That is `M1.A.9a`'s **outward
  impingement axis**, and it means the belief layer and the counterfeit detector read the same
  quantity.
- **`M11.C.9`'s mid-scale peak is a general prediction**, not a sibling-position quirk. Four
  mechanisms now share the gating shape.

Also here, and it belongs with `FE04.10`: "**Beliefs are an especially important anxiety binder. To
the extent that a belief is an anxiety binder, it is part of pseudo-self.**" That is a **definition
of the pseudo-self fraction of the belief store** — the anxiety-binding subset — and it sits
alongside `FE04.10`'s solid-self-as-internally-consistent-subset. Together they suggest `M9` can
carry both fractions without a separate scalar, which is an `M10.A.2` win worth evaluating.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.4 — Regression **defined** as the forbidden proxy `[K]` `[T]`

> "**In a regression, whether it is in a family or in a larger social group, actions are taken to
> relieve the anxiety of the moment rather than being determined by a long-term view.**"

*Model consequence:* **quote this at `M4.D.6a`.** The spec forbids a short-horizon anxiety-relief
reinforcement signal on the grounds that it rewards the reactive moves by construction. `FE05.4`
says that optimising for immediate relief **is what regression is**. So an implementation using the
forbidden proxy is not merely miscalibrated — it is a model of a permanently regressed family, and
`M11.C.16`'s two arms are testing exactly that.

The same passage names the two forms: "giving in to others and doing what they want… **to taking
over and functioning for others because it is easier**", which are `M4.D.5`'s fused default and
`M1.B.5`'s two poles, both derived from one gradient.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.5 — The regression has a **floor**, and it can reverse **without an external agent** `[K]` `[T]` `[X]`

> "**There is a limit to how deep an emotional system will go into a regression. At some point the
> discomfort associated with habitually taking the 'easy' way becomes greater than the discomfort
> anticipated if one tries to recover some semblance of individuality.** At the emotional nadir
> someone might say, '**We are getting nowhere here and I must do something about me. If I keep
> worrying about you, we will all go under.**' **Even if one person takes a dogmatic and overly
> authoritarian stance, if the emphasis is on 'I' and not 'you,' it can break the anxiety spiral and
> stem the regression.** If somebody runs away or dies, that can stop it too… **Regressions can end
> without anyone's leaving or dying, however, if one person can develop some direction not dictated
> by trying to relieve the anxiety of the moment.** … **Anxiety-driven regressions in families and
> other social groups can last from days to years to lifetimes.**"

*Model consequence:* **two things, and the first needs stating in the spec before it is misread.**

**1. This is not a counterexample to `M11.C.17`, and the distinction must be explicit.** `M11.C.17`
requires that a coach-free arm show **no upward drift in mean `basic_level`** — differentiation is
not self-generating. `FE05.5` describes a **regression reversing**, which is recovery of
`functional_level` toward baseline. Those are different variables and the spec already separates
them (`M1.A.5a`, `M7.A.1`). But the passage reads like a contradiction, and a reader — or an
implementer — could easily take it as licence for spontaneous basic-level rise. **`M11.C.17` should
carry a note saying that functional recovery without a coach is expected and required, and that
what must not drift is the estimator's output.**

Related: `M7.A.1` already forbids `functional_level` recovery from being "a symmetric restoring
force". `FE05.5` gives the asymmetry its mechanism — recovery happens when the **binder's cost
exceeds the change's anticipated cost**, which is a threshold crossing, not a spring.

**2. `M1.E.7`'s binder-failure condition has an endogenous route.** The spec conditions
`systems_perspective` rise on the person's binders failing and requires a *landed external contact*
to supply the frame. `FE05.5` says the binder-cost crossing happens **on its own** at the nadir. So
the two conditions separate cleanly: **exhaustion is endogenous; frame supply is not.** That is
consistent with `K10.6` ("societal attitudes change when society no longer has an option") and with
`M1.E.7`'s requirement that pain be necessary and **not sufficient** — without frame supply,
exhaustion produces symptom escalation, not insight. Worth stating that `FE05.5`'s reversal is a
*functional* recovery precisely because no frame was supplied.

**⚠ And one tension to carry to pass 2.** "**Even if one person takes a dogmatic and overly
authoritarian stance, if the emphasis is on 'I' and not 'you,' it can break the anxiety spiral**"
sits awkwardly against `M5.F.4`, which requires an `I-POSITION` at low `systems_perspective` to
execute as the assertion form and **raise** reactivity, and against `FE04.4`/`FE04.12`, which make
dogmatic self-assertion negative evidence. The likely reconciliation is **level of effect**: the
dogmatic stance raises reactivity **on the tie** (`M5.F.4`) while lowering the **family-level**
spiral, because what breaks the spiral is one node ceasing to route others' anxiety, regardless of
how gracelessly. If that holds, `M5.F.4` is fine and the model gets a non-obvious prediction. It
must be checked, not assumed.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.6 — The anxiety cascade, with a **sensitisation ordering** on the tie graph `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "**The first person infected by the anxiety of someone in distress will usually be the one who is
> most sensitized emotionally to that individual. A mother, for example, usually reacts quicker to
> her most problem child than does a sibling or father. Rarely, a sibling will be almost as
> sensitized as the mother to a brother or sister who has problems, but siblings are usually more
> sensitized to distress in their parents than they are to distress in other siblings. Certain
> children, usually ones most involved in the family problem, are more sensitized to distress in
> parents than others.**"

The worked cascade that follows is one of the corpus's most complete: mother distressed by an
outside event → most-involved child reacts → the child's changed behaviour troubles the mother →
**the mother's anxiety shifts off the original event onto the child** → the father holds separation
briefly, then loses it to "apprehension about being criticized for not being sufficiently
'supportive'" → the father acts on feeling against his own judgement, trying to make the child "be
happy" → "**relieve[s] some family anxiety in the short run… but it complicates the situation… in
the long run**" → siblings are drawn in → regression.

> "**Anxiety converts feared or imagined problems into real ones (the daughter does become quite
> depressed), the appearance of which is then used to justify all the worry.**"

*Model consequence:* **three requirements, one of them new.**

- **Propagation order is by sensitisation, and sensitisation is asymmetric.** `M4.C.1` scales
  arriving anxiety by `conductance / functional_level`, which is symmetric on the tie. `FE05.6`
  requires a **directed** sensitivity — siblings react more to parents than to each other — and it
  is not derivable from bond energy alone. `M1.A.12`'s `involvement_weight` is the nearest existing
  quantity. **Worth stating as a directed readout on the tie rather than a new field.**
- **The focus *shifts off the original stressor onto the reactor*.** This is `L06.2`'s retargeting
  and it is why `M1.F.7`'s `exogenous` flag matters: after the shift the family is responding to an
  endogenous event and the original stressor is no longer in the loop. `FE05.2`'s
  exogenous→endogenous conversion (`M1.D.7e`) generalised beyond the societal dials.
- **`M7.E.1d`'s self-fulfilling prophecy in 1988** — the spec cites `KS09.3` (2019) and Merton. Here
  it is stated with the retrospective-justification clause, which is the part that makes it
  self-sealing and which `M9`'s belief layer must reproduce.

Also, a fourth instance of the inward-blame misattribution: "**People are prone to personalize the
reactions of others**" — "What did I do to cause him to be unhappy?" and "What must I do to make him
happy?"

## ⭐⭐ FE05.7 — Non-response is worse than conflict `[K]` `[T]`

> "The process often begins when one person feels rejected or not listened to… The second person…
> may feel the first has an insatiable need for attention and confrontation and '**not want to get
> into it.' This reluctance increases the unhappiness of the first person, who then pushes for a more
> 'acceptable' response.** The second person, generally oversensitive to disharmony and
> confrontation, may attempt to withdraw or at least **show no surface reaction. This withdrawal or
> apparent lack of response angers or 'hurts' the first person**, whose next series of prodding
> statements can result in the second person's finally exploding. At that point, both people are out
> of control. **Although a wearing and energy-consuming interaction, an out-of-control response is
> considered preferable to 'no response,' to being 'ignored' or 'discounted.'**"

*Model consequence:* **a propensity requirement the spec does not have, and it constrains
`M4.D.1b`.** The pursuing party escalates until *any* response is obtained, and rates a hostile
response above none. Two consequences:

- **A withheld move is received as non-response and drives escalation.** `M4.D.1b` requires that a
  withheld move still change tie state; `FE05.7` says what the change is on the *other* side —
  the perceived error grows, and the corrective move (`FE03.3`) intensifies. This is also why
  `M4.D.1c` is right that `WITHHOLD` alone is insufficient: on its own it *feeds* the ladder.
- **It explains why conflict is a stable sink** (`FE03.8`): the interaction is costly and still
  preferred to silence, so there is no gradient out of it.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.8 — Binding in one channel **protects the others** — second, stronger statement `[K]` `[T]`

> "Psychotic level thought processes can be powerful anxiety binders; to the extent that they are,
> the person is less likely to act out or develop physical symptoms. **The binding of anxiety in one
> process or bodily system, in other words, protects other systems.**"

Footnote 5, with its own honesty note:

> "**it appears that the process of reciprocal functioning may be just as important in internal
> bodily processes as it is in the family relationship system. There is inadequate statistical
> support for this assumption**, but there are numerous case reports of people who have had a
> dramatic remission of a chronic physical illness at the point at which they became psychotic.
> **People accustomed to acting out their anxieties, who for some reason are forced to give up that
> outlet, sometimes develop internalized problems such as physical illness or neurotic symptoms.**"

*Model consequence:* **confirms and sharpens `FE01.6`'s new-requirement candidate.** `M7.D.2`
requires the three channels to be substitutable; `FE05.8` requires them to be **mutually
protective** — occupancy of one lowers the hazard on the others — and names the mechanism as
`M6.I.4`'s reciprocal functioning operating **within** the person rather than between people.

That is a strong architectural claim: **the same invariant applies at two scales.** `M10.A.2` would
prefer one implementation used twice. The second sentence also gives the **blocked-outlet
direction**, which is `M6.I.6` at the person level and matches `FE02.9`'s porcupines exactly.

Grade with care: Kerr states plainly that the statistical support is inadequate and the evidence is
case reports. `M11.F.4`'s prohibition on disease-from-emotion mechanisms is unaffected — this is
about **channel substitution within the model's own three-channel vector**, not about causing
disease.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.9 — Chronic anxiety becomes **independent of its trigger** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**It is most accurately conceptualized as a system or process of actions and reactions that, once
> triggered, quickly provides its own momentum and becomes largely independent of the initial
> triggering stimuli.** While specific events or issues are usually the principal generators of acute
> anxiety, **the principal generators of chronic anxiety are people's reactions to a disturbance in
> the balance of a relationship system.**"

With the worked comparison: after a child leaves home, "**The increased reactivity of the parents to
one another usually generates more anxiety than their reactions to the absence of the child.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.D.7e`'s exogenous→endogenous conversion is a general property, not a
societal-dial special case.** The spec requires a dial-driven stressor to be able to convert and
sustain itself through the chronicity integrator once internalised; `FE05.9` says **every** stressor
does this, and gives a direction that is assertable: **the reaction generates more than the event**.

That is a clean `M11` shape check — instrument a nodal event and compare anxiety attributable to the
event against anxiety attributable to the members' responses to each other. It also means
`M1.F.7`'s `exogenous` flag is load-bearing beyond `M11.C.8`: it is how the model keeps the two
countable while they are running in the same integrator.

## ⭐⭐ FE05.10 — Acute and chronic split by **what is feared** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Acute anxiety generally occurs in response to real threats and is experienced as time-limited.**
> People usually adapt to acute anxiety fairly successfully. **Chronic anxiety generally occurs in
> response to imagined threats and is not experienced as time-limited.** … **Acute anxiety is fed by
> fear of what is; chronic anxiety is fed by fear of what might be.**"

> "While there are inborn and learned elements in both… **learning plays a more important role in
> chronic anxiety.** While everyone experiences acute and chronic anxiety, **the difference between
> people in the amount of chronic anxiety they experience appears to be based primarily on learned
> responses.**"

*Model consequence:* the cleanest available definition for `M1.A.7`/`M1.A.8`, and it supports
`M1.A.7`'s requirement that `programmed_reactivity` be fixed in childhood **from that person's own
witnessed event history** — learned, not constitutional, and not a family average. It also supports
`M1.A.7a`'s split into a fixed disposition and a derived state.

The *what might be* clause is the model's justification for `M9`: chronic anxiety runs on
**anticipated** states, so a belief layer is not an optional readout — it is where the driver lives.
Third independent argument for `FE02.7`'s missing belief→appraisal edge.

## ⭐ FE05.11 — "The family is the unit of illness" — and outsiders own it too `[K]` `[T]`

> "**It appears that the family owns the symptom — not just the nuclear family but the
> multigenerational family as well. The family is the 'unit of illness.' People outside the family
> who are emotionally responsive to it can also contribute their anxiety, emotional reactivity, and
> subjectivity to the family cauldron. When they do, these outside people must 'own' the problem
> too.** Saying that the family is the unit of illness does not imply that the family is 'sick.'"

> "**Every family, given sufficient generations, spawns schizophrenia or an equivalent dysfunction.
> Every family, given sufficient generations, spawns fairly high levels of differentiation as
> well.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.D.8` (the family is not the boundary), `M1.E.6` (a costless observer is a
failing implementation) and `L05.2` (function absorbed by whoever will carry it, including the
professional) in one passage.

The second quote is **`M11.C.6(b)`'s variance requirement stated symmetrically in 1988** — the
spread widens in **both** directions, and the model must produce high-differentiation branches as
readily as dysfunctional ones. `M7.E.2`'s reversibility at both extremes is the mechanism. This is
also `M11.F.6`: a model that only generates decline is one-sided and wrong.

## ⭐ FE05.12 — Anxiety raises togetherness pressure **and fragments the group** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**When people become more anxious, the togetherness pressure increases. During high anxiety
> periods, human beings strive for oneness through efforts to think and act alike. It is ironic that
> this striving for sameness increases the likelihood that a group will become fragmented into
> subgroups. We-they factions are a product of the pressure for oneness and the intolerance of
> differences associated with it. Fragmentation and emotionally determined relationship alliances
> reflect the loss of differentiation in a group.**"

*Model consequence:* **a non-obvious, testable prediction the model should be able to produce**:
raise `ambient_anxiety` (`M1.D.7`) and the family should show **both** rising togetherness pressure
**and** increasing alliance structure — more active triangles (`M1.C.3`), more
`ALIGNED_WITH_MOVER` positions (`M8.6`). That the two co-occur is counterintuitive and follows from
the mechanism rather than being stipulated, which makes it a good `M11` candidate.

It also connects `M8.6`'s alignment penalty to the societal dials for the first time: factions are
alignment at group scale, so `M1.D.7`'s effect should be observable in `M8`'s predicate.

## ⭐ FE05.13 — Bossy and helpless seek support **with the same intensity** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Even though the bossy overfunctioning person is often perceived by himself and others as
> 'strong' and the helpless underfunctioning person as 'weak,' both people seek emotional support and
> acceptance with about the same intensity. They simply 'connect' with others in opposite fashions —
> one through directing others; the other through being directed.** The lower the level of
> differentiation, the more exaggerated the bossy-helpless polarization will become during stressful
> periods."

And the same claim for the loner:

> "**People who deny their need for attachment to others are just as relationship-dependent as those
> who constantly seek a relationship. Loners can bind just as much anxiety by avoiding people as
> people who constantly seek social contact can bind anxiety through that contact.** Poorly
> differentiated people who are loners usually get labeled '**schizoid**.' Poorly differentiated
> people who are consistently involved in tumultuous relationships usually get labeled
> '**hysterical**.' **Both schizoid people and hysterical people are dealing with the same basic
> problem: a high degree of emotional need for and reactivity to others.**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE03.2`'s equal-investment invariant, stated twice more in a third
chapter.** With `FE03.2` and `FE05.13` the claim now has three independent statements across two
chapters and applies to three different pattern pairs (pursuer/distancer, bossy/helpless,
schizoid/hysterical). That is enough to make it a requirement rather than an observation.

It is also `M1.A.9a`'s two axes with two more instance pairs, and `M11.F.6`'s trap list gains the
**loner** alongside the indifferent partner.

## ⭐ FE05.14 — The withdrawn person is **not** the non-reactive one `[K]` `[T]`

> "Reactivity is manifested along a continuum that ranges from **hyperactivity (the extreme is
> behavioral frenzy) to hypoactivity (the extreme is behavioral paralysis).**"

Footnote 1:

> "**Many seemingly inactive, low response people are called 'nonreactive' when, in fact, their
> inactivity is a way of managing a high level of emotional reactivity.**"

And in the same chapter: "**too much or too little reactivity reduces an organism's adaptiveness.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.F.6`'s readout traps again, and it is the same trap the explainer's §10.1
records as *overt emotionality peaks mid-scale*. `FE05.14` states the underlying reason: the
observable is **absolute deviation from a middle**, not a signed quantity. Any reactivity readout in
the model must be computed as `|deviation|`, and a linear one will rank the paralysed agent as the
calmest in the family. Fifth independent instance of the two-sidedness rule.

## ⭐ FE05.15 — Technique works through the **relationship**, not the technique `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Sometimes… the effectiveness of these approaches appears to be based more on the relationship
> with the therapist or teacher (or with other group members) than on any new awareness of anxiety
> and greater ability for self-control. The therapist and group become a support system. The problem
> with using a group in this way is that improvements in functioning may be dependent on maintaining
> the relationships. People doing biofeedback may derive more benefit from the relationship with the
> technician than from the technique itself.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.14` (management technique has zero independent effect) and `M1.E.7e`
(the coach as a lender of pseudo-self) meeting in one observation, with a *falsifiable* signature:
the gain is **contingent on the relationship continuing**, which is exactly `M11.C.20`'s
withdrawal test. `FE04.15`'s *addicted to love* is the same mechanism seen from the recipient's
side.

## ⭐ FE05.16 — Impact scales with **contact × functional importance** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 8:

> "**One person's impact on a relationship system is dependent on the adequacy of his emotional
> contact with people and on his functional importance to the system.** The emotional functioning of
> the head of an organization is, obviously, more influential on the level of anxiety in the
> organization than the emotional functioning of a manager lower in the administrative hierarchy…
> **The emotional functioning of the family matriarch or patriarch has more impact on family anxiety
> than the functioning of someone not very involved with the family.**"

*Model consequence:* a **two-factor** product for `M1.A.13`'s `structural_importance` — contact
adequacy **and** functional position — which matches `M1.A.13a`'s derivation from a functional-doer
position and, with 1979 Tape 6, gives the matriarch/patriarch example twice. It also bounds
`M11.C.17`'s coach arm and `FE05.2`'s new criterion: **who** does the differentiating determines how
far the effect reaches, so the criterion must name the mover's position, not pick arbitrarily.

Footnote 12 states the reach directly: "**How far out into the family this process extends depends
on the extent of the person's viable emotional contact with others.**"

## ⭐ FE05.17 — Protecting children is **how the problem transmits** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Parents cannot really 'protect' their children from the transmission of anxieties. The
> transmission process is too 'wired in' to the way parents function and, in addition, children are
> very prone to picking up anxiety. Trying to protect children from one's own problems is actually
> one of the main ways problems are transmitted from one generation to the next.**"

*Model consequence:* a sharp, counterintuitive, testable claim and the model should reproduce it
without being told. The mechanism is available: protecting is `M4.D.5a`'s accommodation — a
concession that raises the stock, narrows `areas_of_joint_activity` (`M1.B.9`) and grows the
`taboo_set` (`M1.B.10`) — and the child witnesses the *avoidance* (`M1.F.5`) rather than the
problem. **An arm in which parents actively conceal a stressor should transmit more, not less, than
an arm in which they do not.** Good `M11` candidate, and it is `M1.B.10a`'s desensitisation rule run
backwards.

## ⭐ FE05.18 — Distance lowers anxiety in self and **raises it in others** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**when people use distance or denial to manage anxiety, they may lower it in themselves, but
> raise it in others. One may achieve more comfort for oneself, but increase the anxiety in those to
> whom one is emotionally connected. Such an outcome is a mixed blessing.**"

Footnote 9 adds the recurrence claim: "**Abandoning a given relationship… may provide a short-term
solution to one's discomfort, but it will not alter the unresolved attachment to one's parents**…
they "**recreate in new relationships a version of the problems they thought they had escaped by
running away.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.4`'s cut-off trade has a **second, immediate** channel the spec does not
carry. The criterion defers the cost to the next nodal event; `FE05.18` says there is also a
**same-tick transfer to the connected others**. Both should hold, and they are distinguishable in a
run: an immediate rise in others, plus a deferred rise in the actor.

The recurrence claim is `M11.C.10`'s cutoff-begets-cutoff at the level of one person's successive
relationships rather than across generations — `unmodelled` in `M2.A`'s fixed membership, and a
Phase E item.

## FE05.19 — Media as a **feeling-channel amplifier** — grounding for `media_amplification` `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Societal reinforcement of a togetherness and feeling orientation is considerable. It is
> transmitted through radio, television, movies, novels, sermons, newspapers, magazines, and
> gossip.** If children are having problems, it is assumed their parents are not sufficiently
> 'caring' and 'involved.' **Symptoms and aberrant or selfish behavior are assumed to reflect a lack
> of togetherness rather than to reflect an anxious togetherness in which people have lost individual
> direction and are functioning in reaction to one another. Blame is irresponsibly affixed for most
> issues about which society is anxious. We are implored to have more feeling for one another, all
> the while being bombarded by feeling laden communications that stir up still more of the feelings
> we are supposed to lack.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.D.7a`'s `media_amplification` gains a mechanism, and it is one the model
already has.** The dial has been among the weakest-sourced parts of the societal layer. `FE05.19`
says what media does: it **directs attention at the feeling channel**, which under `M4.C.8`
*amplifies* it. So `media_amplification` is not a new force — it is `M4.C.8` applied at population
scale, which satisfies `M10.A.2` and makes the dial's effect derivable rather than stipulated.

It also predicts the dial's **direction of error**: the amplification is applied to the very quantity
the message claims is lacking, so the intervention is self-defeating in exactly `M11.C.13`'s shape.

And this, which is `M1.D.7j` in 1988: "**In an anxious environment, people who want to make
decisions based on a broad and long-term view are pushed aside by people who want quick answers and
immediate relief from problems.**"

## FE05.20 — The lens, named — the framing `M11.F` needs `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 14:

> "**One's lens, of course, can become a problem itself if it becomes confused with the actual
> processes one is attempting to observe. The history of science is replete with examples of
> observations being shaped by the lens (subjectivity) instead of the lens being refined by
> observations (objectivity). If one uses a theoretical lens, however, that is recognized as
> imperfect and not equivalent to the natural world, it can help one see things that otherwise would
> be lost in the mountain of information with which one is confronted.**"

*Model consequence:* **with `FE01.15`'s "as if" clause, this is the honest description of what this
project is building, in the authors' own words.** A theoretical lens, recognised as imperfect and
not equivalent to the natural world, whose value is that it makes some things visible in a mountain
of information — and whose danger is being mistaken for the process it observes.

That is a better statement of the model's status than anything currently in `M11.F`, and it answers
`FE00.1`'s general-systems objection in the source's own terms rather than the project's.
**`M11.F` requirement candidate**, paired with `FE01.15`.

Also relevant to `M10.C.2a`'s self-report caveat: "**Most people have only a limited awareness of the
influence of anxiety, reactivity, and subjectivity on their behavior. The influence of these
processes tends to be grossly underestimated, even by people who think of themselves as 'anxious.'**"
— the bias is systematic and scales with involvement (`FE01.12`).
