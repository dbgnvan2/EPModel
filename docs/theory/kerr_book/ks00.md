## ⭐ KS00.1 — Kerr answers G1's question directly, and his answer is "coupled, no priority" `[T]`

The exact question §G1 worked out from fragments, asked and answered in one paragraph:

> "Students of Bowen theory sometimes ponder the question of whether we need to **see the patterns to get less reactive and more thoughtful** or **get less reactive to get more thoughtful and see the patterns**. **No obvious answer exists for this question, but my opinion is that these two processes are intermixed, with each continuously having an impact on the other.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.18` is confirmed by the most direct statement available**, and the confirmation includes the *shape* of the coupling — bidirectional, continuous, no priority. §G1 assembled this from four indirect places in `KB07`/`KB02`; here it is stated as the question itself. It also **licenses the graded form over a hard gate**: "no obvious answer" and "intermixed" is not a gate, it is mutual coupling.

Marked `[K-ext]`-adjacent: he says "**my opinion**", so the *answer* is Kerr's judgement while the *question* is the field's. Grade the coupling `[T]`, the no-priority claim `[K-ext]`.

## ⭐ KS00.2 — Blaming **others and oneself** are the enemies of systems perspective `[T]`

> "**Blaming others and blaming oneself are the enemies of gaining a systems perspective.**"

And the neutrality definition:

> "we gain more emotional neutrality. **The essence of that neutrality is less blaming** and less of the blame-associated negative emotions such as guilt and anger."

*Model consequence:* direct confirmation of **`M1.A.18a`** — blame is the observable readout for `systems_perspective`. And it is **two-sided in a second way** the model did not have: not only blame-versus-praise (`KB07` · K07.1) but **blame-of-other versus blame-of-self**. `M1.A.18a` currently has one of these two axes. Self-blame must count as a loss.

## ⭐ KS00.3 — The two channels, named by their functions `[T]`

> "The cortical components comprise what Bowen theory defines as the **intellectual system**… The recently evolved cortical circuits enable **more self-regulation**, a key ingredient for maintaining 'self,' particularly in an anxious social context. **A well-thought-out principle and long-term view can guide decision making in preference to acting on the feelings and anxiety of the moment and a short-term view.**"

And the cleanest one-line statement of the architecture in any source:

> "**Cortical components of differentiation guide actions; subcortical components motivate actions.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M4.D.1a` confirmed**, and the objectives are named exactly as the spec states them — *a principle and a long-term view* against *the feelings and anxiety of the moment and a short-term view*. The "guide versus motivate" line also settles the **agency** question in `M10.A.1a`: the self-directed channel does not supply its own motive force, it **redirects** the motive force the emotional system supplies. Agency is a steering capacity, not a second engine.

## ⭐ KS00.4 — Feelings are cortical experience of subcortical activity `[T]`

> "**Feelings are subjectively experienced through cortical activity but reflect activity of the underlying and ancient subcortical circuits of the emotional system.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.0` confirmed from the latest source**, in mechanistic rather than definitional terms. This is also the precise answer to *"how is comfort/discomfort represented in agents with no feelings"* — the model carries the subcortical layer (`acute_anxiety`) which is what feelings are a report of. Modelling feelings would model the report, not the driver.

## ⭐ KS00.5 — "Acting calm" is a counterfeit, and the two poles are the two axes `[T]`

> "The idea of **managing self is very different than trying to act calm and nonreactive**. When tension rises in a relationship, often one person tries to act calm, tries not to get drawn into a fight, but this person's **seeming lack of response and engagement can send the others into a frenzy**. Many marriages polarize around one partner being '**rational/even-keeled**' and the other being '**emotional/volatile**.' **Both spouses are equally emotional in these situations** but cope with their emotionality in **mirror-opposite ways**."

*Model consequence:* **direct corroboration of `M1.A.9a`'s two axes and of `M5.F`'s counterfeit problem.** The even-keeled partner and the volatile partner are the two counterfeits, they are **mirror-opposite**, and they are at **equal** emotional intensity — which is exactly what `M11.C.19` asserts must be distinguishable *at equal counterfeit magnitude*. "Both spouses are equally emotional" is also `M1.A.0`'s consequence stated as an observation.

Note the mechanism in the middle: the calm-acting partner's **non-response raises the other's reactivity**. That is `M5.F.4`'s assertion-form dynamic on the *inward* axis.

## ⭐ KS00.6 — A successful move **solidifies** the relationship; ten years to get there `[T]` `[#]`

> "**A hallmark of a successful effort to define 'self' is that it does not disrupt a relationship but, rather, solidifies it. The change fosters more mutual respect.**"

The worked case, and the duration:

> "It took me **ten years** of working on resolving key aspects of the relationship with my mother for me to finally recognize my subjective view of her being a fragile person… When I was finally able to take Mother on and back her off, she responded the next day with, '**Finally, you told me what you think.**' … **it took me ten years to get there.**"

*Model consequence:* two things the spec lacks.

1. **`M5.E` has no end state.** The reaction ladder runs *You are wrong → Change back → consequences*, decays unless fed, and reverts if `FOLLOW_UP` is skipped. It does **not** say what a *successful* completion produces. This does: **a solidified tie and increased mutual respect** — a durable positive change in tie state, not a return to baseline. `M11.C.5` should assert it.
2. **A measured duration on a primary tie: ten years.** Bears directly on `M1.A.4c`'s estimator window and on `S2.6`'s "months". They are not in conflict — *months* is the system's re-equilibration after one held position; *ten years* is resolution of a primary attachment. Both belong, at different scales.

Note also the mechanism of the breakthrough: he had to see that his view of his mother as fragile was **"a feeling-driven bias, not a fact"** — a perception correction, which is `M4.C.5`'s perception-side readout.

## KS00.7 — Basic level is **not context dependent**; solid self is its measure `[T]`

> "People's position on the continuum of emotional functioning is regarded as their **basic level of differentiation**. This basic level corresponds with the ability to define 'self' in relationship systems. **It is not context dependent.** People higher on the continuum have a **more solid self**, and people lower on the continuum have a **less solid self**."

*Model consequence:* bears on the **open question at `S2.12`**. Basic level ⇔ solid self, stated as an identity, and **explicitly not context dependent** — while functional level is (people "can maintain more of a 'self' in some relationship systems than in others, such as work versus family"). This supports `M1.A.4a`'s breadth requirement directly: *context-independence is the defining property of the basic level*, so breadth across situations is not a proxy for it — **it is the thing itself.**

## KS00.9 — Basic level is set by **relationship transmission, not genetics** `[T]`

> "**Variation in this basic level results from a relationship transmission process, not a genetic one.**"

> "Siblings typically have **different interactions** within the family, which results in their developing **somewhat different levels of differentiation**."

> "the **basic levels of the parents establish the emotional climate** in the family, which has a significant impact on the development of all the children, **albeit on some more than others**."

*Model consequence:* `M7.E` and `M11.C.2`/`M11.C.6` confirmed. The third quote gives the mechanism a two-layer shape the spec states only in part: a **family-wide floor** set by the parents' levels, plus **per-child variation** from differential interaction. `M11.C.2`'s concentration claim is the second layer; the first is the constraint on all offspring.

## KS00.10 — Overfunctioning/underfunctioning is normal until anxiety drives it `[T]`

> "If a wife falls and breaks her ankle, her husband modifies his schedule to take over functions that his wife would normally do. When her fracture heals, **he stops overfunctioning, and she stops underfunctioning** and resumes her old roles. This type of interaction has emotional… and rational… components."

> "The pattern **becomes a problem if chronic anxiety intensifies the emotional reactivity**… These sorts of anxiety-driven interactions are based **not on the realities of people's capabilities but on anxiety and distorted perceptions.**"

*Model consequence:* the reciprocity is **not pathological by type** — it is pathological by **driver**. `OVERFUNCTION`/`UNDERFUNCTION` in `M5.A.1` must be selectable by *both* channels (`M4.D.1a`), with the same move type reading as adaptive from the self-directed channel and as symptom from the automatic one. Same shape as `KB14`'s life-stage finding (`M2.A.0d`), generalised.

The discriminator is named: **distorted perceptions of self and other** versus "the realities of people's capabilities" — again `M4.C.5`.
