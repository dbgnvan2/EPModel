# Ch22 — Toward the Differentiation of Self in One's Family of Origin

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** "This presentation to the Georgetown Family Symposium was made in October 1971. This paper is being rewritten in October 1974, three years since the original presentation. There is now more solid data to support this approach." The chapter also names its own originating event precisely: "It began with a paper at a national conference in March 1967. In it I described my effort toward differentiating myself in my own family of origin." That March 1967 paper is the material of the preceding chapter (referenced in this chapter's reference list as Anonymous 1972, "Differentiation of self in one's family").
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):**
  - **The originating cohort:** psychiatric residents and other mental health trainees in a Georgetown teaching conference, "usually attended by fifteen to twenty residents and other trainees," meeting once a week, spring 1967 onward. None of them, and none of their spouses, were "in any kind of 'therapy.'" The conferences "had no 'therapeutic' objective." Time per trainee: "no more than fifteen to thirty minutes every month or two," plus "no more than token 'private' time," usually a corridor exchange about how to answer a letter or phone call from home.
  - **The comparison group:** mental health professionals and their spouses in Bowen's own part-time private practice in formal weekly family therapy, a practice line running "for some eight years" from the early 1960s. Because of that volume he claims to know "the average amount of time they took for each step in the therapy process."
  - **A named control group:** "In the early 1960s there was one 'control' group in which the total focus was on the relationship in the marriage" — i.e. extended-family work deliberately not urged, to test whether it would emerge on its own.
  - **The 1971–74 replication effort:** the teaching method extended to "small groups of fifteen to twenty people who meet once a week" and "much larger meetings of fifty to seventy-five people who meet once a month," all "mental health professionals at the postgraduate level"; plus private-practice coaching of individuals, "Most are seen for one hour about once a month," some "about two hours every two or three months."
  - **Totals claimed:** "perhaps five hundred have had reasonable exposure to the ideas in teaching sessions"; "about a hundred of these have consistently presented their experience to teaching sessions under my direct supervision"; "about ninety-five families in my private practice since 1969 who have continued for more than a year and with whom the focus has been almost exclusively on the family of origin with no more than peripheral mention of the relationship in the marriage."
  - **Bowen's own family** is the first data point — "some twelve years" of trial-and-error work on his primary triangle before the breakthrough.
  - **Selection bias is acknowledged in the text:** "it is impossible to know the percentage who become interested enough to start serious work on their families of origin, since people have by now heard about this approach and meetings attract people already specifically interested."
- **Genre:** Both, and unusually balanced for this book. Roughly **60% theory-and-narrative / 40% technique** — but the technique fraction is exceptionally operational. Structurally: a history of how the method was found (~30%), a theory block on cut-off, life patterns and open systems (~30%), an explicit "Principles and Techniques" section giving four named assignments plus prohibitions (~25%), and a results/dosage/hedging section (~15%). It contains no case dialogue and no transcript; the technique is stated as instructions to a person, not as therapist maneuvers in a session. **This is the book's closest thing to a manual for one person differentiating alone**, and it is the primary source for a coaching agent.
- **[p2] Qualifier now that Ch21 is in hand.** This chapter explicitly *defers* technique detail elsewhere — "The process of differentiating a self has been presented in other papers and is too complex to review in detail here," "Other details of theory and technique are beyond the scope of this brief presentation," and on the dead-parent techniques "The details of this will ultimately appear as a full paper." Ch22 supplies the **principles, the prohibitions and the dosage**; Ch21 supplies the worked operation (planning interval, letters, follow-up, reaction ladder). Do not treat Ch22 as the complete manual.
- **[p2] Verbatim publication date from the volume's Contents page: 1974.** Consistent with the internal "rewritten in October 1974".

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Whole-family therapy relieved symptoms but did not resolve the underlying attachment** → the original research families. "That method was amazingly effective in relieving symptoms but it was not effective as a long-term method for resolving the underlying family 'stuck-togetherness.'"
- **Parents-plus-symptomatic-offspring was only marginally better** → "a little more effective at relieving the symptoms, but the young adult son or daughter had little capacity to separate a self from the parents, and neither parent had much ability to separate a self from the other parent."
- **The two-spouses-plus-therapist triangle worked far better** → so effective it had been "the one most consistent approach to family systems therapy since the early 1960s."
- **A single successful move on one's own primary triangle required first handling the interlocking triangles** → Bowen's own family, twelve years. "Each effort to extricate myself emotionally had always been blocked by the other interlocking triangles in the family of origin."
- **A change in Bowen's teaching content produced unrequested behavior change in trainees** → Georgetown residents, within weeks of March 1967. "the trainees who attended the teaching sessions began to use these concepts in visits to their parental families. This came as a surprise, as it occurred spontaneously, without any suggestion from me. Previous trainees had not done this."
- **That cohort became better clinicians than any previous cohort** → late 1967/early 1968. And the correlation was internal to the cohort: "it was precisely those residents who had done best in the effort with their parental families who were also doing best in their clinical work."
- **The same cohort stopped presenting their own nuclear-family problems** → "None of these residents ever mentioned emotional problems in their own nuclear families. In retrospect this was unusual, since residents are usually quick to seek consultation about their own emotional problems."
- **On direct questioning (late 1968/early 1969) their marriages and children had improved as much as those of matched residents in weekly formal family therapy** → "to my amazement, they had made as much progress in dealing with the problems as similar residents whom I had been seeing in once-a-week formal family therapy with their spouses." They had transferred the family-of-origin learning to spouse and children without being told to.
- **The control group did not spontaneously go to the extended family** → early 1960s, marriage-focus-only group. "About 25 percent of these families made some significant change with families of origin, but the average family in that group never made more than a token effort. Most never really got beyond either blaming their parents or benevolently forgiving them. Most tended to become overinvolved in the relationship in the marriage, and the therapy either terminated early or dragged on to an indefinite termination."
- **Two distinct symptom profiles by distance mechanism** → clinical population. Those who use physical distance repeat the running-away pattern in later relationships; those who use internal mechanisms "stay on the scene in periods of emotional tension but [are] more prone to dysfunctions within himself such as physical illness; emotional dysfunction such as depression; and social dysfunction such as drinking and episodic irresponsibility in relation to others."
- **Cut-off nuclear families are more symptomatic across the board** → families who "communicate infrequently with parental families and who return home about once a year for brief, superficial 'duty' visits" have "relatively low levels of adaptability to stress, and relatively high levels of anxiety and vulnerability to marital discord, problems with children, and the full range of human problems." More complete cut-offs are "extremely vulnerable to all kinds of human problems."
- **Therapy fails on cut-off families when aimed at their presenting problem** → "To attempt family therapy that focuses directly on problems within the family can be long-term and nonproductive."
- **A growing societal trend toward substitute families** → "There is a growing trend toward cutting-off from 'bad' parental families and finding 'good' substitute families." And the people who cut off "are the most vigorous in the effort to create 'substitute' families from social relationships."
- **Therapy with non-marital long-term partnerships never succeeded** → over twenty years, across "common-law marriages, single people who have shared apartments for many years, partners in long-term homosexual relationships, people who have been lifelong closest friends, and a wide spectrum of men and women involved in a wide variety of 'living together' relationships." "I have never had a result that I would be willing to call successful with any of them, not even with common-law marriages in which there have been children."
- **Adolescent turmoil is treated as normal by most psychological theories** → "Family Systems Theory does not support this view." Bowen treats the near-universality of adolescent turmoil as a phenomenon *about attachment levels*, not a developmental norm.
- **Outcome variance is not predicted by apparent difficulty** → "There are families in which the problem seemed too difficult to expect much change, but in which significant change took place, as well as those that looked relatively easy in which change was slow."
- **[p2] A small subgroup escapes the isolation spiral** → "A small percentage of people are able to manage marginally productive lives with numerous superficial relationships, in which no one relationship becomes intense." An explicit exception: keeping every tie below the intensity threshold avoids the cut-off cycle at the cost of a "marginally productive" life.
- **[p2] Internal-mechanism distancing is anxiety-modulated in both directions, not only upward** → "When emotional stress is low, such people can relate to each other more spontaneously and freely. When anxiety is higher, they become more reserved and more isolated from each other." Distance is a continuous function of current stress, recovering when stress falls.
- **Resistance to the family-of-origin premise was less than expected** → "But family resistance has not been as great as I expected," explicitly paralleled to his 1954 wrong prediction that only unemployed or retired fathers would participate in family research.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Undifferentiation = unresolved emotional attachment to the family of origin (one quantity, three names).** "the degree to which we all have poorly 'differentiated' selfs, or the degree to which we are 'undifferentiated,' or the degree of our unresolved emotional attachments to families of origin. These are all different descriptive terms to refer to the same phenomenon." Acts on: the person, for life.
- **Marital fusion is identical to family-of-origin attachment.** "It has long been recognized that the emotional attachment between the spouses is identical to the emotional attachment that each spouse had in his or her family of origin." Acts on: the marriage. This is the premise that licenses the whole method — working the family-of-origin attachment necessarily works the marital one.
  **[p2] correction:** pass 1 wrote "identical *in magnitude*". Bowen writes "identical", unqualified, and frames it as **long recognised background**, not a finding of this chapter. The magnitude reading is the modeller's.
- **The Differentiation of Self Scale.** Bottom = lowest differentiation; top = "a theoretical level of complete differentiation." Determines life style "in terms of the way they deal with the intellect and the emotions," vulnerability to stress, difficulty of life adjustments, and "incidence of human illness and problems." Acts on: the person's whole life course.
- **Basic vs functional level.** "People are responsive to others in the emotional field and there are frequent shifts in the functional level of differentiation of self, in favorable or unfavorable life situations. It is possible to estimate the basic level of differentiation over long periods of time and this permits the use of the scale for predicting over-all life courses." Two variables: a slow basic level and a fast, field-responsive functional level.
- **Triangles.** "the predictable ways in which people relate to each other in an emotional field. Triangle moves may be so toned down they are barely observable in calm emotional fields. As anxiety and tension increase, the triangle moves increase in frequency and intensity. Less differentiated people are moved about like pawns by emotional tensions. Better differentiated people are less vulnerable to tension." Acts on: everyone in the field; anxiety is the gain control on triangle activity.
- **Interlocking triangles as a blocking structure.** "It is not possible to differentiate a self in any single triangle without a method for dealing simultaneously with the interlocking triangles." Acts on: any attempted differentiation move — the adjacent triangles absorb and neutralize it.
- **The primary triangle as the pattern template.** The triangle between self and parents is "the most important primary triangle in life, and the one in which a person develops the triangle relationship patterns that remain relatively fixed in all relationships." Acts on: every later relationship.
- **Two classes of distance mechanism.** Internal (psychological, "operate within the person" — silence, refusal to speak, emotional isolation while physically present) and physical (leaving the room, leaving home, never returning). "Most people use combinations of internal mechanisms and physical distance, with a preference for one type of mechanism," and the same person escalates from internal to physical as anxiety rises: "a person might handle usual levels of anxiety with an internal mechanism such as silence or refusal to speak and only under higher levels of anxiety use physical distance such as walking out of the room."
- **Emotional cut-off.** The umbrella term for emotional distancing "whether the cut-off is achieved by internal mechanisms or physical distance." Critically: "The type of mechanism used to achieve emotional distance is not an indication of the intensity or the degree of unresolved emotional attachment." Mechanism ≠ magnitude. Its principal manifestation is "denial of the intensity of the unresolved emotional attachment to parents, acting and pretending to be more independent than one is."
- **Distance mechanisms are load-bearing for family equilibrium.** "These mechanisms are necessary for maintaining the emotional equilibrium of the family unit. To regard such mechanisms as pathological and to attempt to remove the 'symptom' without regard for the total family unit can stir anxiety and maladjustment in the family unit."
- **[p2] The intensity of the distancing mechanism is monotone in undifferentiation — a dependency, NOT a fourth synonym.** "The degree of unresolved emotional attachment is equivalent to the degree of undifferentiation. The lower the level of differentiation, and the greater the amount of unresolved emotional attachment to parents, the more intense the mechanisms to deal with the undifferentiation." Bowen's declared synonym set is **three** terms (poorly differentiated self / undifferentiated / unresolved attachment). Cut-off *intensity* is a consequence of that quantity; cut-off *type* is explicitly independent of it. Three names, one quantity, **plus two derived attributes**.
- **[p2] One member's detriangling pays the whole family.** "The family gains as one member is able to relate more freely without taking sides and becoming entangled in the family emotional system." Stated as a system-level gain, with no reaction phase named.
- **Early programming of attachment level.** "The child is 'programmed' into the emotional configuration very early in life, following which the amount of unresolved emotional attachment remains relatively fixed except for functional shifts in the parents." **[p2]** With two stated exceptions in *both* directions, which pass 1 omitted: "Under favorable circumstances and with good fortune in the central family unit, the amount **may become less**. Under disastrous circumstances, with a high level of parental anxiety, the amount **will be greater**." Four named determinants: (1) the degree of unresolved attachment each parent had in *their* family of origin, (2) "the way their parents handled this in their marriage," (3) "the degree of anxiety during critical periods in life," (4) "the way the parents handled this anxiety."
- **Anxiety-handling as an independent variable.** "There is a variable determined by the ways parents handle anxiety." Two named handling styles: parents who "are able to stay on a predetermined course in spite of anxiety" vs parents who "are more reactive and shift life courses in response to anxiety."
- **The open relationship system as an anxiety regulator.** "One of the most effective automatic mechanisms for reducing the over-all level of anxiety in a family is a relatively 'open' relationship system in the extended family... in which family members have a reasonable degree of emotional contact with one another." Crucially it is *not* a differentiation mechanism: "Relative openness does not increase the level of differentiation in a family, but it reduces anxiety, and a continued low level of anxiety permits motivated family members to begin slow steps toward better differentiation."
- **Sustained anxiety moves the level of differentiation, slowly, in both directions.** "In an emotional field of sustained high anxiety, the over-all level of differentiation will move slowly toward greater undifferentiation." Stated as the explicit converse of the openness claim ("The opposite is also true").
  **[p2] correction:** pass 1 headed this "erodes *basic* level." The text says "the over-all level of differentiation" of a *family / emotional field*. Bowen does not say whether basic or functional level moves. Do not attribute the basic/functional split to this sentence.
- **The transgenerational cut-off force.** "the powerful transgenerational emotional force that goes toward cutting-off from the past" — invoked to explain both the substitute-family trend and why a combined approach fails. It is a directional drive away from the family of origin, always working against the effort.
- **A present-generation solution suppresses past-generation work.** "There is much clinical experience to support the thesis that people are not motivated to work toward the past when they are involved in a process that offers a solution in the present generation."
  **[p2] correction — this is the chapter's most over-read sentence.** (a) It is a *thesis* supported by unquantified "clinical experience", not a measurement; (b) the mechanism Bowen offers is the **transgenerational cut-off force**, hedged twice — "**I believe** the powerful transgenerational emotional force… **may be** the most important determining factor" — i.e. a *directional drive away from the past*, **not** a finite budget divided between targets; (c) it is explicitly partial: "There are some people who appear to do well with a combination approach, but there are others whose effort… are little more than token efforts." The second named mechanism is **habituation, not scarcity**: the failing group "seem to become so 'addicted' to a continuation of their regular family sessions together." **The words finite, budget, competing and measurably do not appear.**
- **Substitute relationships reproduce the original.** "If social relationships become significant, the relationships are duplicates of their relationships to their parental families. When they encounter stress, and anxiety increases, they cut-off from the social relationship and seek another, better social relationship. After a few cycles of this, they tend to become more and more isolated."
- **Person-to-person relationship.** "one in which two people can relate personally to each other about each other, without talking about others (triangling), and without talking about impersonal 'things.'" It is a relationship state to be built, and building it is simultaneously the assessment instrument and the intervention.
- **The observation ↔ reactivity feedback loop.** "The effort to become a better observer and to learn more about the family reduces the emotional reactivity, and this in turn helps one become a better observer." A mutually reinforcing pair; either one can be entered.
- **Detriangling.** "to be constantly in contact with an emotional issue involving two other people and self, without taking sides, without counterattacking or defending self, and to always have a neutral response."
- **Reactivation of the pre-cut-off system.** The four assignments together "create a more 'open' relationship system and to reactivate the emotional system as it was before one's cut-off from it. Now it is possible to see the triangles in which one grew up, and to be different in relation to them." The old system is dormant, not gone, and must be switched back on before it can be worked.
- **Change propagates outward from the changed member.** "When the spouses can change in relation to each other, other family members at home automatically change in relation to them." And in the new method, change in the family of origin propagates automatically to spouse and children.
- **Nuclear-family fusion cancels effort.** "The spouses are emotionally 'fused' with each other and with the children, and it is difficult to get far beyond the fusion or to do more than react and counterreact emotionally. Efforts to gain objectivity and to control emotional reactivity in the nuclear family can remain for long periods on the level of emotional game playing in which the games of each spouse cancel out the potential gains of both."

## A3. Predictive claims

Flagged as **[E]** where Bowen offers observation/counts as evidence in this chapter, **[A]** where he asserts.

**[p2] Flag audit.** Pass 1 was too generous with **[E]**. *Nowhere in this chapter is any outcome counted.* There is no tally, no instrument, no rating, and no denominator on any claim. The two percentages given (25 percent of the control group; "About fifty percent of those in the small weekly group become sufficiently interested to be regular participants") both have unstated N, and the second is a *participation* rate, not an outcome. The strongest evidential statement Bowen makes about the central observation is "The observations appeared valid by the most searching criteria I could use at the time" — criteria unnamed. Read **[E]** throughout this chapter as "Bowen reports having observed it", never as "measured". Items 19, 20 and 26 are re-flagged **[A]** below.

**About the effort and the family's reaction to specific moves (the sharpest testable claims):**

1. **If** a person attempts to differentiate within a single triangle without simultaneously handling the interlocking triangles, **then** the effort is blocked. **[E — his own twelve years]**
2. **If** a person makes an emotional confrontation with the extended family, **then** the family reacts negatively and "it may require months, or even a year or two, to get beyond the family's cut-off with the confronter" — even though the move "may be briefly effective in making the confronter believe he has accomplished something." **[A]** Named as "probably the one biggest error that people make in working with the extended family."
3. **If** the person tells the family what he is trying to do, **then** (a) disagreements about the process arise, (b) "natural resistance" is built "into insurmountable hurdles," and (c) group activity results, which impedes differentiation. Stated as a flat impossibility: "It is impossible to tell a family what one is trying to do and still make it work." **[A]**
4. **If** the person brings spouse and children on the visit, **then** "the parental family relat[es] to the trainee's family as a group, which further impedes the effort toward person to person relationships." **[A]**
5. **If** the visit turns into "modified group therapy sessions to talk about 'problems,'" **then** it "either can make the emotional climate at home more congenial or can stir up anxiety. In either case it impedes the effort to establish person to person relationships." **[A]** — note both outcomes, including the pleasant one, count as failure.
6. **If** the person goes silent during an emotional issue, **then** "To go silent is perceived by the other as an emotional response" — i.e. it fails as neutrality. **[A]**
7. **If** the person is in the midst of the family during an emotional issue and is "more objective and less reactive than the others," **then** the family detects it: "The family 'knows' this when it happens." **[A]**
8. **If** the family is calm, **then** no emotional issue is available *and* "the family system works hard to prevent issues from surfacing." **[A]**
9. **If** there is no emotional issue, **then** differentiation cannot occur: "Differentiation is possible only around an emotional issue about which to relate." **[A]**
10. **If** the person is emotionally "locked into" the triangle with his parents, **then** continuing to work against the bloc is counterproductive; **but if** he instead focuses on a triangle of one parent, another emotionally important family member, and self, **then** operating latitude is usually available. Siblings sometimes work; sometimes the parents' generation or the generation before is the productive route instead. **[A]**
11. **If** a person works without a coach who has done it in his own family, **then** "one unwittingly makes critical decisions based on emotionality and can waste months in fruitless dead-ends." **[A]**
12. **If** appointments are as infrequent as once or twice a year, **then** "People tend to let the efforts lapse and to make some kind of visit with their families just before the appointments," and results "have not been rewarding." **[E]**
13. **If** a motivated person has "half a dozen one-hour appointments a year" of family-of-origin coaching, **then** this is "more productive than weekly formal family therapy appointments which focus on the relationship between spouses." **[E]**
    **[p2] The comparison is confounded and cannot be read as a frequency finding.** The two arms differ in **frequency** (6/year vs weekly) *and* in **focus** (family of origin vs the marital relationship). Nothing in this chapter compares six-a-year family-of-origin coaching against weekly family-of-origin coaching. The chapter's actual rate claim is a different one: "The frequency of appointments with the extended family approach is determined by the amount of work the family member is able to do between appointments."

**About outcomes of the approach:**

14. **If** the focus is exclusively on differentiation of self in the family of origin, **then** the family "automatically make[s] as much or more progress in working out the relationship system with spouses and children as families seen in formal family therapy in which there is a principal focus on the interdependence in the marriage." **[E — the central claim, from the 1967–69 cohort plus five years' replication]**
15. **If** the person lives geographically removed from the family of origin, **then** the approach applies; **if** the person lives in the parental home, or the parents live with them, or they are "intimately exposed to the parental family in day-to-day living," **then** it does not. **[A — an explicit scope boundary]**
16. **If** effort is split between the marriage and the family of origin, **then** for many people the family-of-origin work degrades to "little more than token efforts yielding little or no progress," because "people are not motivated to work toward the past when they are involved in a process that offers a solution in the present generation." Some do well on a combination; the ones who fail "seem to become so 'addicted' to a continuation of their regular family sessions together that urging more work with the extended family results in little more than another token effort." **[E]**
17. **If** therapy focuses only on the marriage and leaves extended-family work to emerge on its own, **then** about 25 percent make some significant change with families of origin, the average family makes no more than a token effort, most get no further than "either blaming their parents or benevolently forgiving them," and therapy "either terminate[s] early or drag[s] on to an indefinite termination." **[E — the control group]**
18. **If** effort is confined to the nuclear family, **then** objectivity and reactivity work "can remain for long periods on the level of emotional game playing in which the games of each spouse cancel out the potential gains of both." **[A]**

**About cut-off, contact and symptoms:**

19. **If** the frequency and quality of emotional contact with the extended family improves, **then** it "will predictably improve the family's level of adjustment and reduce symptoms in the nuclear family." **[A — corrected at p2 from [E]]** Stated with the word *predictably* but with no data. **[p2] The antecedent is near-tautological:** the sentence is "Any **successful** effort that goes toward improving the frequency and quality of emotional contact… will predictably improve…". Success is inside the *if*. This is the chapter's most-quoted causal claim and it is not falsifiable as written.
20. **If** the cut-off is more complete, **then** adaptability to stress is lower, anxiety higher, and the family "extremely vulnerable to all kinds of human problems"; and the *improvement* from reducing the cut-off is most striking in exactly those families. **[A — corrected at p2 from [E]]**
21. **If** the extended-family cut-off is reduced even partially, **then** it "will soften the intensity of the family problem, reduce the symptoms, and make any kind of therapy far more productive." **[A]**
22. **If** anxiety in the emotional field is sustained and high, **then** the over-all level of differentiation moves slowly toward greater undifferentiation. **[A]**
23. **If** the relationship system is relatively open and anxiety stays low, **then** motivated members can begin slow steps toward better differentiation — **but** openness by itself does not raise differentiation. **[A]**
24. **If** a person's cut-off with parents is more intense, **then** "the more he is vulnerable to repeating the same pattern in future relationships": when tension mounts in a marriage "he will use the same pattern of running away," possibly across serial marriages, living-together arrangements, or more transient relationships, and also in work relationships. **[A]**
25. **If** a person achieves emotional distance by internal mechanisms rather than physical distance, **then** he stays on the scene under tension but is "more prone to dysfunctions within himself" — physical illness, depression, drinking, "episodic irresponsibility in relation to others." **[A]** With a dose relation for depression: "The higher the anxiety in the environment, the more he isolates himself from others emotionally while still appearing to carry on normal relationships in the group."
26. **If** a person cuts off from the parental family and forms substitute social relationships, **then** anxiety drops and it "works well for a time"; but **if** those relationships become significant, **then** they duplicate the parental pattern, and under stress he cuts off again and seeks a better one; **after a few cycles**, "they tend to become more and more isolated." **[A — corrected at p2 from [E]]** **[p2]** with a stated exception: "A small percentage of people are able to manage marginally productive lives with numerous superficial relationships, in which no one relationship becomes intense."
27. **If** the relationship is a non-marital long-term partnership, **then** change cannot be sustained: "It is as if there is not enough stability in the marriage to sustain change," and "the nonfamily relationship may provide a reasonably comfortable existence as long as the relationship is calm, but it has a low tolerance for stress." **[E — twenty years of attempts, zero successes claimed]**
28. **In such couples,** therapy is usually initiated by the symptomatic partner; "It is common for the other to give lip service to the idea of family therapy, and then find reason to withdraw after a few appointments." Most continue a few appointments and separate, "following which one is often motivated to go on alone." A small percentage go on to marriage. **[E]**
29. **If** the substitute family is sought while one's own family still exists, **then** it is a poor substitute — with explicit exceptions "in disasters, fragmented families, and a number of other extreme social situations." **[A]**

**About development and adolescence:**

30. **If** a young person is better differentiated and began "an orderly process of growing away from his parents in early childhood," **then** he "will continue a smooth and orderly growth process through the adolescent years," and adolescence "becomes a challenge and an opportunity to begin assuming responsibility for self, rather than a fight against the unresolved emotional attachment to the parents." **[A]**
31. **The intensity of denial and pretending in adolescence** is "a remarkably accurate index of the degree of unresolved emotional attachment to the parents." **[A]** — an assertion of a near-linear measurement relation.
32. **If** the family has a higher level of undifferentiation, **then** it will be "a more disorganized family with higher levels of anxiety"; better differentiated families are "more orderly with lower levels of anxiety." "In broad terms, the amount of anxiety tends to parallel the degree of unresolved emotional attachment in the family." **[A]**
33. **If** parents can stay on a predetermined course in spite of anxiety, **then** the family "will turn out better than the families in which the parents are more reactive and shift life courses in response to anxiety." **[A]**
34. **General life-course law:** "All things being equal, the life course of people is determined by the amount of unresolved emotional attachment, the amount of anxiety that comes from it, and the way they deal with this anxiety." **[A]** — three named terms, no others.
35. **If** anxiety and tension increase in a field, **then** "the triangle moves increase in frequency and intensity"; and less differentiated people "are moved about like pawns by emotional tensions" while better differentiated people "are less vulnerable to tension." **[A]**
36. **If** a person is low on the scale, **then** he "may keep [his life] in emotional equilibrium and symptom free, but [is] vulnerable to stress, life adjustments are more difficult, and [he has] a high incidence of human illness and problems." **[A]** — symptom-free is explicitly compatible with low differentiation.
37. **If** distance mechanisms are removed as if they were pathology, without regard for the total family unit, **then** it "can stir anxiety and maladjustment in the family unit." **[A]**

## A5. Terminology

- **differentiation of self / undifferentiation / unresolved emotional attachment to families of origin** → explicitly declared synonyms for one quantity: "These are all different descriptive terms to refer to the same phenomenon."
- **defining a self / working toward individuation / differentiating a self** → "essentially synonymous with differentiation."
- **stuck-togetherness** → the phenomenon the concepts describe: "the range of ways family members are emotionally 'stuck to' each other, and the ways this 'stuck-togetherness' continues to operate in the background, no matter how much people deny it or how much they pretend to be separate from the others." Note the two clauses: it persists *and* it is denied.
- **emotional cut-off / cut-off** → introduced here as a term ("We have come to use the term..."). Covers emotional distancing by either internal mechanism or physical distance. Its principal manifestation is denial of the attachment plus "acting and pretending to be more independent than one is."
- **internal mechanisms** → distance achieved within the person (silence, refusal to speak, emotional isolation) while physically present.
- **physical distance** → distance achieved by leaving — the room, the house, the region, permanently.
- **open relationship system** → "the opposite of an emotional cut-off... one in which family members have a reasonable degree of emotional contact with one another." An anxiety variable, explicitly *not* a differentiation variable.
- **person to person relationship** → "two people can relate personally to each other about each other, without talking about others (triangling), and without talking about impersonal 'things.'" Note "triangling" is used here as a verb meaning *talking about a third party*.
- **detriangling** → staying in contact with an emotional issue between two others "without taking sides, without counterattacking or defending self, and to always have a neutral response."
- **primary triangle** → self and parents; "the most important primary triangle in life."
- **interlocking triangles** → the adjacent triangles that block a move made in any one triangle.
- **coaching / coach / trainee** → the supervisory relationship, named by analogy: "so similar to the relationship of a coach to an athlete who is working to improve his athletic ability." Bowen uses **trainee** throughout for the person doing the work, even for private-practice clients — a deliberate departure from "patient."
- **Differentiation of Self Scale** → the classification instrument; basic level estimable over long periods, functional level shifting with the field.
- **basic level** vs **functional level of differentiation** → the slow, life-course-predicting quantity vs the fast, situation-responsive one.
- **relationship "nomad"** → "who moves from one relationship to another, each time cutting off emotional ties to the past and investing self in the present relationship."
- **substitute families** → social relationships recruited to replace a rejected parental family.
- **emotional confrontation** → the named single biggest error in extended-family work.
- **emotional game playing** → the degraded state that nuclear-family objectivity work falls into, "in which the games of each spouse cancel out the potential gains of both."
- **fused / fusion** → used of the nuclear family: spouses "emotionally 'fused' with each other and with the children."
- **"programmed"** → the child is programmed into the emotional configuration very early; the level then stays relatively fixed.
- **duty visits** → the brief, superficial once-a-year home visit that marks a cut-off, not a contact.
- **research study on one's own family** → the framing given to the whole effort. The person is a *researcher* into his own family, not a patient.

- **[p2] emotional confrontation vs. "small emotional issues from the past"** → the two ends of one axis Bowen names in a single sentence: "it is necessary to introduce small emotional issues from the past, **without getting into emotional confrontation**." The axis is the *mover's* engagement, not the family's anxiety level.

**[p2] Position in the corpus timeline.** This chapter (rewritten October 1974) uses **"emotional cut-off" as an established named term** — "We have come to use the term emotional cut-off or simply cut-off" — earlier than Ch16's dating of cut-off's formal addition to the concepts (1975), and after Ch21 (1967/70), which describes cut-off fully without naming it. Ch22 is the earliest *named* use in the corpus. The mechanism/magnitude orthogonality ("The type of mechanism used to achieve emotional distance is not an indication of the intensity or the degree of unresolved emotional attachment") appears nowhere else in the twenty-two chapters.

**Jobs done by unusual terms here:** "cut-off" in this chapter does the work that "emotional divorce" and "distance" do in earlier chapters, and is explicitly generalized to cover *both* internal and physical mechanisms — a broadening. "Coaching" replaces "therapy" as the name for the helper's function, and Bowen puts scare quotes on "therapy" repeatedly in this chapter ("family 'therapy' as practiced at Georgetown") to mark the shift.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The person who moves first is a single motivated family member, working alone.** No other family member need agree, be told, or participate. "A differentiating effort is one that takes place in one self in relation to each other self."
- **Go alone.** "It is far better if people go alone to visit their families." Spouse and children stay home.
- **The primary triangle is self + the two parents.** It is where "a person develops the triangle relationship patterns that remain relatively fixed in all relationships."
- **Each parent is a separate target.** The minimum assignment is "a person to person relationship with each parent" — individually, not as "Mother and Dad." Bowen names the group-addressing habit as the observable symptom of the problem: "It is common for parents to write letters signed 'Mother and Dad' and for their children to write to 'Dear Mother and Dad' or 'Dear Family.'"
- **The full target set is every living person in the extended family.** "If you can get a person to person relationship with each living person in your extended family, it will help you 'grow up' more than anything else you could ever do in life."
- **Fallback positions when the parental triangle is blocked**, in the order Bowen lists them: (1) other family members who are emotionally important to the parents — forming a triangle of one parent, that member, and self; (2) siblings; (3) family members in the parents' generation; (4) "people in the generation before the parents if they are available."
- **Dead parents are not a stopping condition.** "Most people have more relatives than they believe they have. It is possible, even when there are only a few living survivors, to use this theoretical system to reconstruct an effective family emotional system for the differentiation of self."
- **Groups and cliques are the family's default unit of relating.** "It is common for family members to be identified as part of groups and cliques, and for people to relate to the groups rather than to individuals." The whole method is an attempt to force dyadic relating where the system defaults to group relating.
- **The coach must have done it himself.** "This is where it is desirable to have a 'coach' who has already had the experience with his own family."
- **The differentiating member acquires a distinct position in the family.** "one comes to have a unique role which is important to everyone, which is highly respected, and which helps to contribute to one's own individuality and responsibility."
- **In non-marital couples, the symptomatic partner is the initiator** and the other withdraws after a few appointments.
- **In formal family therapy shifted to this method, "Each spouse is seen separately for work on families of origin."**
- **[p2] The target set is explicitly uncompletable.** The every-living-relative admonition is immediately qualified: "This admonition is accurate **except no one can ever live long enough to complete the task**. Success also depends on the response of others." Treat it as a direction of effort, never as a terminating goal state.
- **[p2] Detriangling is stated as mandatory, not optional.** "This is an **absolute necessity** if differentiation of self is the goal." The other three assignments are described as helpful and preparatory; only this one carries an absolute.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

Bowen's four named principles, in his order, plus the surrounding coaching actions.

**Framing moves**
1. **Reframe the work as research, not treatment.** The effort "requires knowledge of the function of emotional systems in all families and the motivation to do a research study on one's own family." The person is a researcher; the family is the subject.
2. **Coach, don't treat.** "The initial goal is to get the trainee started. Most of the learning comes as the trainee works toward his goal. The trainee is aware that progress depends on him. This process is quite different from conventional concepts of therapy."
3. **Teach the theory in a group and let the person apply it.** The originating method was *teaching*: a weekly conference where members report visits home, "the successes and the inevitable impasses," and "suggestions were made for the next visit to parental families." Fifteen to thirty minutes per person every month or two was sufficient.
4. **Debrief each visit and plan the next one.** The unit of work is the trip home; the unit of coaching is the report-and-plan cycle after it.
5. **Give tactical clues on demand between sessions** — "a clue about how to respond to a letter or telephone call from the parental family" — brief, in passing, not a session.
6. **Set appointment frequency by the person's own between-session work.** "The frequency of appointments with the extended family approach is determined by the amount of work the family member is able to do between appointments."
7. **Steer away from dead ends.** "A coach who has had experience can at least guide the trainee away from unprofitable trial and error wandering."
8. **In formal therapy that shifts to this method, see each spouse separately** for family-of-origin work.

**Assignment 1 — Person to person relationships**
9. Assign a person-to-person relationship with each parent individually, then with each living member of the extended family.
10. Define it operationally for the person: talk to the other person, about each other, with no third parties in the conversation and no impersonal topics.
11. Warn that this will surface everything: "person to person relationships with their parents will reveal all the emotional problems the parents have had in their relationship, and that they had in their own families of origin."
12. Assign visits home "as often as indicated."

**Assignment 2 — Becoming a better observer**
13. Assign learning more about the family and observing it — presented together with reactivity control because "these two assignments are so interlinked."
14. Aim the observation at getting "a little 'outside' the family emotional system," which then produces "a different view of the human phenomenon."
15. Target the specific end state: "beyond blaming" and "beyond anger" — and note the target is emotional knowing, not intellectual assent: "It is fairly easy for most people to intellectually accept the notion that no one is to blame in family situations, but the idea remains intellectual until it is possible to know it emotionally in one's own family."

**Assignment 3 — Controlling one's own emotional reactiveness**
16. Assign reactivity control as the paired half of observation; each improves the other.
17. Set a realistic ceiling: "One never becomes completely objective and no one ever gets the process to the point of not reacting emotionally to family situations."
18. Describe the achievable skill precisely: "Most of the time he can live his life, reacting with appropriate and natural emotional responses, but with the knowledge that at any time he can back out of the situation, slow down his reactiveness, and make observations that help him control himself and the situation."

**Assignment 4 — Detriangling**
19. Assign staying in contact with an emotional issue involving two others and self, taking no side, not counterattacking, not defending, "and to always have a neutral response."
20. Note that part of the work needs no technique at all: "One part of the process is achieved merely by being in the midst of the family during an emotional issue, and being more objective and less reactive than the others."
21. **Time visits to natural emotional issues.** "Trainees are advised, whenever possible, to be home when there is a natural emotional issue in the family. Trips home when there is a serious illness or a death, or at homecomings or holidays, often provide the levels of family anxiety that are effective for relating to the family."
22. **When the family is calm, introduce a small emotional issue from the past** — "without getting into emotional confrontation."
    **[p2] This is an issue-*creation* move, and Bowen states it as necessary, not optional:** "In these situations, **it is necessary to** introduce small emotional issues from the past." So the live-issue requirement is **not a hard gate that idles the coach in a calm family** — it is a precondition with a stated way to manufacture it. Pass 1's model note ("an issue availability check before a move is permitted") captured only half the rule.

**When blocked**
23. **When locked into the parental triangle, stop pushing and reroute.** "Operating latitude is usually possible by focusing on other family members who are emotionally important to the parents. This focus is on a triangle consisting of one parent, the other family member, and self."
24. Try siblings; if unproductive, work the parents' generation, or the generation before it.
25. **When parents are dead or survivors are few, reconstruct the system.** "It is possible, even when there are only a few living survivors, to use this theoretical system to reconstruct an effective family emotional system for the differentiation of self."

**Where cut-off is the presenting problem**
26. **Bypass the nuclear-family problem entirely and work the extended family.** "to completely bypass problems in the nuclear family and to focus on working out relationships with the extended family."
27. In heavily cut-off families, aim first at any increase in extended-family contact — even a small one "will soften the intensity of the family problem, reduce the symptoms, and make any kind of therapy far more productive."
28. **In anxious symptomatic families, sequence the marriage first, extended family later** (the conventional sequence he still describes): "It is usual for the first part of this kind of therapy to be devoted to the relationship in the marriage and, after anxiety has subsided and there is more objectivity, to devote more and more of the later stages of therapy to the extended family."

**On the coach himself**
29. "For the therapist, the extended family approach requires far more skill, more continuing work on himself, and more attention to detail than does more conventional therapy."

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

*(Prohibitions on the person doing the work, and on the coach.)*

- **Do not make an emotional confrontation.** "Probably the one biggest error that people make in working with the extended family is emotional confrontation." Explicitly noted as feeling productive to the confronter and being destructive in fact.
- **Do not tell the family what you are trying to do.** "It is impossible to tell a family what one is trying to do and still make it work." Two named consequences: it builds resistance into "insurmountable hurdles," and it "results in group activity which impedes the differentiating result."
- **Do not take spouse and children on the visit.** It causes the parental family to relate to the trainee's family as a group.
- **Do not turn the visit into group discussion of "problems."** Both possible outcomes — a more congenial climate, or stirred anxiety — count as impeding the work.
- **Do not mis-hear the assignment as "getting to know one's family better."** Bowen flags this specific misreading by name.
- **Do not mistake existing congeniality for a person-to-person relationship.** "They mistake the congeniality in calm families in which people relate from the time-honored roles originally assigned them."
- **Do not take sides, counterattack, or defend self** during an emotional issue.
- **Do not go silent** — silence is read as an emotional response, so it does not count as neutral.
  **[p2] scope correction:** the whole of Bowen's text on this is one clause inside the detriangling paragraph — "To go silent is perceived by the other as an emotional response." It is a statement about **what silence fails to achieve during an emotional issue between two others**, not a general law that every behaviour is a signal. It is not named as an error, carries no stated consequence, and is not one of a closed set of three prohibitions.
- **Do not keep working against a bloc.** "When this occurs, it is counterproductive to keep working against the bloc."
- **Do not attempt the work without a coach who has done it** if avoidable — the cost is emotional decisions and months of dead ends.
- **Do not treat distance mechanisms as pathology to be removed.** "To regard such mechanisms as pathological and to attempt to remove the 'symptom' without regard for the total family unit can stir anxiety and maladjustment in the family unit."
- **Do not focus on the emotional issues in the nuclear family** while doing this work: "the most productive route for change, for families who are motivated, is to work at defining self in the family of origin, and to specifically avoid focus on the emotional issues in the nuclear family."
- **Do not run appointments too infrequently** — once or twice a year produces lapsed effort and a token pre-appointment visit.
- **Do not expect the effort to be completed.** "This admonition is accurate except no one can ever live long enough to complete the task."
- **(Coach's own restraint, by example)** Bowen declines to prescribe the exclusive family-of-origin focus unreservedly, and declines to litigate the travel-expense deduction with the IRS — both instances of not taking on a fight that would consume the work.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

**Preconditions before any move is available**
1. **Knowledge of how emotional systems work in all families.** Acquirable from teaching sessions — Bowen reports people doing significant work with "no more teaching ideas than could be acquired from sitting in on a dozen sessions of about three hours each."
2. **Motivation to do a research study on one's own family.** Named as a separate requirement from knowledge.
3. **A coach who has done it in his own family** (strongly desirable, not absolutely required — some large-group attendees worked alone).
4. **Geographic separation from the parental family.** The approach "applies to people who live geographically removed from their families of origin. It would not apply to people who live in the parental home, or whose parents live with them, or who are intimately exposed to the parental family in day-to-day living."
5. **Existing living relatives, or enough survivors to reconstruct a system from.**
6. **For anxious symptomatic families:** anxiety must subside and objectivity increase before the extended family can even be *heard* as a topic. "It is difficult for a family to 'hear' the idea of the extended family when there are anxiety and symptoms."

**The stated order of the work**
7. Bowen's own sentence gives the causal ordering: "The study requires that the researcher begin to gain control over his emotional reactivity to his family, that he visit his parental family as often as indicated, and that he develop the ability to become a more objective observer in his own family. **As** the system becomes more 'open' **and** he can begin to see the triangles and the part he plays in the family reaction patterns, **he can begin** the more complex process toward differentiating himself from the myths, images, distortions, and triangles he had not previously seen."
   - Stage 1: reactivity control + frequent visits + objective observation (all three concurrent, mutually reinforcing).
   - Stage 2: the system becomes more open **and** the triangles and one's own part in them become visible. These are the gating conditions.
   - Stage 3: only then, differentiating from the myths, images, distortions and triangles.
8. **Detriangling comes last of the four assignments** and is dependent on the other three: "All the work that goes into personal relationships, as well as into learning more about the family through observation and into controlling one's own reactiveness, helps both to create a more 'open' relationship system and to reactivate the emotional system as it was before one's cut-off from it. Now it is possible to see the triangles in which one grew up, and to be different in relation to them."
9. **Person-to-person work must precede or accompany everything** — it is called "a beginning effort."
10. **An emotional issue must be present before a differentiating move is possible.** Sequence within a visit: either arrive during a natural issue (illness, death, homecoming, holiday), or, if the family is calm, introduce a small issue from the past.
11. **The interlocking triangles must be handled simultaneously with the primary one** — not before, not after. "It is not possible to differentiate a self in any single triangle without a method for dealing simultaneously with the interlocking triangles."

**[p2] An unresolved tension inside the chapter's own sequencing**
- The chapter's "essential message… to **completely bypass problems in the nuclear family**" sits beside its description of the conventional order it has not abandoned: "It is usual for the first part of this kind of therapy to be devoted to the relationship in the marriage and, **after anxiety has subsided** and there is more objectivity, to devote more and more of the later stages of therapy to the extended family." Both are stated; neither is withdrawn; and Bowen closes with "I am not yet ready to prescribe this unreservedly" and "About half of my practice is still with both spouses together." The precondition list above should therefore carry **two alternative entry policies**, not one, and the model must not implement the bypass as though the chapter had settled it.

**Durations**
12. "This is a big order and a mission that cannot be accomplished quickly."
13. Bowen's own primary-triangle work: twelve years before the breakthrough, and the breakthrough came only after acquiring the interlocking-triangles method.
14. Recovery from an emotional confrontation: months to a year or two.
15. Working appointment frequency: monthly to a few times a year; six one-hour appointments a year suffices for a motivated person; once or twice a year is too little.
16. Large-group participants sometimes attend silently "for a year or more" before making a major effort.

**[p2] Two corrections to how this table was read.**
1. **These are not a closed set of three.** Pass 1's ledger entry names "three failing moves" (announce / bring family / go silent). The chapter names more, and ranks one of them above all: **"Probably the one biggest error that people make in working with the extended family is emotional confrontation."** Confrontation, not announcing, is the chapter's headline error. Also named: mis-hearing the assignment as "getting to know one's family better"; keeping on against a bloc; taking sides / counterattacking / defending.
2. **Only confrontation carries a stated recovery cost** ("months, or even a year or two"). Every other row's consequence is stated as "impedes" — no duration, no magnitude. Do not give the other failing moves a confrontation-sized penalty.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

Each prescription above, converted back into a claim about how families work. This is the section that matters most for the simulation.

1. **The extended family emotional system persists intact through decades of cut-off and can be switched back on.** The technique's goal is "to reactivate the emotional system as it was before one's cut-off from it." → The system stores state; distance suspends it rather than dissolving it. A model must keep dormant relationships alive with their historical structure, not delete them.
2. **A person's relationship patterns are a property of the person, not of the relationship.** Family-of-origin work transferred automatically into marriage and parenting with no instruction to do so; the primary triangle sets "the triangle relationship patterns that remain relatively fixed in all relationships." → Patterns belong on `Person` and are instantiated into each `Relationship`, not learned per-relationship.
3. **Change is unilateral and propagates outward without the others' cooperation or consent.** One member, working alone, unannounced, produces change in spouse and children who are not in the room. → The propagation rule must not require reciprocity, agreement, or even awareness.
4. **The family actively defends against issues surfacing when calm.** "the family system works hard to prevent issues from surfacing." → There is a homeostatic suppression mechanism that activates in low-anxiety states, distinct from the anxiety-driven triangle mechanism.
5. **A stated intention and an enacted move are different inputs, and the system reacts to them differently.** Announcing the plan triggers organized resistance; the identical behavior unannounced does not. → The family responds to relationship behavior, not to declared content. There must be no channel by which explaining the theory produces the effect of doing it.
6. **The family relates to a group as a group, and to an individual as an individual — and these are different modes.** Bringing spouse and children switches the family into group mode; group mode blocks differentiation. → The unit of interaction is a variable of the encounter, not a constant of the family.
7. **A live emotional issue is required, and the mover's engagement with it has a bad end at both extremes.** "Differentiation is possible only around an emotional issue about which to relate." Trips are timed to illness, death, homecomings and holidays because those "provide the levels of family anxiety that are effective for relating to the family." → A differentiation move needs a live emotional issue as an input.
   **[p2] CORRECTION — pass 1 put the inverted-U on the wrong axis.** It read the bad high end as *high family anxiety* ("above it (confrontation) the family cuts off"). Bowen never says a family is too anxious for the move. The high-anxiety natural events he names — **serious illness and death** — are the *enabling* cases. What backfires is a **manner of acting by the mover**: "emotional confrontation", which he contrasts directly with "introduce small emotional issues from the past, **without getting into emotional confrontation**". So the non-monotone axis is **the intensity of the mover's own engagement**, not the ambient anxiety of the family. This chapter's only claim that high anxiety is itself harmful operates on a different quantity and timescale: "In an emotional field of sustained high anxiety, the over-all level of differentiation will move slowly toward greater undifferentiation."
   **The non-monotonicity is real** — zero payoff at the low end (calm: no issue, *and* "the family system works hard to prevent issues from surfacing"), a long-lasting negative at the high end (months to a year or two of the family's cut-off with the confronter). It is not merely "too much is bad": the low end is a stated impossibility, not a small positive.
8. **~~There is no null move.~~ [p2] WITHDRAWN as written — this was an invented generalisation.** Bowen's entire text is one clause: "To go silent is perceived by the other as an emotional response." It says that *within a detriangling attempt* silence fails to read as neutrality. It does **not** say every behaviour is a signal, and it does not say abstention is unavailable. What survives: **neutrality is a produced state, not the residue of withholding** — the stated recipe is "without taking sides, without counterattacking or defending self, and to **always have a neutral response**", which requires a response. Any "no null move" axiom in the model must be sourced elsewhere (Ch10's expectation model, Ch13's abort branches), not here.
9. **Differentiation is directly perceptible to the family.** "The family 'knows' this when it happens." → The mover's differentiation level is an observable of the encounter, not a hidden trait — which is what makes a move without content possible at all.
10. **Observation and reactivity form a positive feedback loop.** Each improves the other. → Two coupled variables, either of which can be the entry point; small gains compound.
11. **Blocks are local and the network is routable around them.** A locked parental triangle is bypassed by any triangle containing one parent and another emotionally important person. → The family is a connected graph and influence transmits through nodes adjacent to the target; leverage on the target does not require direct access to the target.
12. **Anxiety and differentiation are separate state variables with different time constants.** Contact frequency moves anxiety fast and differentiation not at all; low anxiety merely *permits* slow differentiation gains. → Two variables, one fast-and-manipulable, one slow-and-gated-by-the-first.
13. **Symptom relief and structural change are dissociable outcomes.** The original whole-family method was "amazingly effective in relieving symptoms" and ineffective at the underlying attachment. Low-scale people can be "in emotional equilibrium and symptom free." → Symptom state must not be a readout of differentiation level; the model needs both.
14. **Distance mechanisms are functional, not defects.** They are "necessary for maintaining the emotional equilibrium of the family unit," and removing them raises anxiety. → Cut-off must have a stabilizing effect in the model.
    **[p2] CORRECTION — pass 1 conflated two different acts.** The caution attaches to a *third party* treating the mechanism as a symptom: "To regard such mechanisms as pathological and to attempt to **remove the 'symptom' without regard for the total family unit** can stir anxiety and maladjustment." The person's *own* increase in contact is described in the same chapter as purely beneficial and with no cost term at all: "any effort toward reducing the cut-off with the extended family will soften the intensity of the family problem, reduce the symptoms, and make any kind of therapy far more productive." So: **stripping a distancing mechanism = costly; increasing contact = beneficial.** Do not attach a transient anxiety penalty to `move:REDUCE_CUTOFF` on this chapter's authority.
15. **The rate-limiting resource is the person's own between-session effort, not therapist contact.** Six hours a year beats weekly sessions. → Coaching contact is a low-bandwidth input that sets direction; the state change happens in the family encounters.
16. **Motivation is finite and allocated competitively across generational targets.** A live present-generation option starves past-generation work. → The model needs a scarce effort budget, and offering an easier target should measurably reduce work on the harder one.
17. **Some attribute of the marital bond is what lets a relationship sustain change.** "It is as if there is not enough stability in the marriage to sustain change"; the nonfamily relationship "may provide a reasonably comfortable existence as long as the relationship is calm, but it has a low tolerance for stress." → Relationships carry a **stability** attribute independent of intensity or duration, gating whether change survives stress.
    **[p2] correction:** Bowen's word is **stability**, not "binding commitment" — commitment is the modeller's construct and should not be attributed to him. Note also that his own sentence says "in the marriage" while the cases are explicitly non-marital, and that the finding is an unquantified twenty-year negative ("I have never had a result that I would be willing to call successful") across a heterogeneous set of relationship types.
18. **Group membership is the family's default representation of its members** ("Mother and Dad," "Dear Family," cliques). Person-to-person relating is the exception that must be manufactured. → Default relating mode is coarse-grained; dyadic relating is a state that has to be achieved and can decay.
19. **Congeniality is produced by role-following and is not evidence of differentiation.** Calm families relate "from the time-honored roles originally assigned them." → A "good relationship" observable can be entirely role-generated; the model must not infer differentiation from low conflict.
20. **A differentiating member becomes more valued, not less.** "In the course of such an effort with the family, one comes to have a unique role which is important to everyone, which is highly respected, and which helps to contribute to one's own individuality and responsibility." → Differentiation raises standing in the system.
    **[p2] correction:** pass 1 added "after the resistance passes". **There is no resistance phase anywhere in this chapter** — no change-back reaction, no reaction ladder, no opposition-then-calm sequence. The only negative family reaction Bowen names here is the response to *confrontation*. The standing gain is stated as arising "in the course of" the effort, with no phase structure. Import the resistance phase from Ch13/Ch21 if wanted, but do not source it here.
21. **~~Daily physical presence prevents the objectivity the technique needs.~~ [p2] CORRECTION — both the mechanism and the conclusion were invented.** Bowen states a **scope boundary with no reason attached**: the approach "applies to people who live geographically removed from their families of origin. It would not apply to people who live in the parental home, or whose parents live with them, or who are intimately exposed to the parental family in day-to-day living." The fusion-blocks-objectivity argument in the next sentences is about the **nuclear** family (spouses and children), not about co-residence with parents; pass 1 transplanted it.
    **"There is an optimum contact rate" is contradicted by the chapter.** Bowen reports "urging spouses to visit their family of origin **as frequently as possible**", instructs the trainee to "visit his parental family **as often as indicated**", and states that improving "the frequency and quality of emotional contact with the extended family" predictably improves adjustment. Contact with the family of origin is monotone-good in this chapter; only *residence* is out of scope, and unexplained.
22. **The cut-off force is a persistent drive, not a one-time event.** "the powerful transgenerational emotional force that goes toward cutting-off from the past." → A standing gradient away from the family of origin that any contact-increasing move must work against continuously.
23. **The mechanism of distancing is uninformative about its magnitude.** "The type of mechanism used to achieve emotional distance is not an indication of the intensity or the degree of unresolved emotional attachment." → Mechanism and magnitude are orthogonal attributes; the model must carry both, and must not infer level from the observed behavior class.
24. **The mechanism does, however, determine the symptom class.** Physical-distance users repeat the running pattern in later relationships; internal-mechanism users get illness, depression, drinking, irresponsibility. → Mechanism selects *which* symptom appears; level selects *how much*.
25. **The primary triangle can be worked with a dead member.** The reconstructed system is "effective... for the differentiation of self." → The relevant object is the internalized/structural triangle; a node's death does not remove it from the structure.
26. **An open extended system lowers family anxiety automatically.** "One of the most effective automatic mechanisms for reducing the over-all level of anxiety in a family is a relatively 'open' relationship system in the extended family" — automatic, i.e. requiring no intent. The stated variables are **frequency and quality** of emotional contact ("there are wide variations in the frequency and quality of relationships in cut-offs and in openness").
    **[p2] correction:** pass 1 wrote "diluted by the **number** of live edges." Node-count dilution is Ch10's claim (anxiety dilutes over nodes), not this chapter's. Ch22 gives frequency and quality only. Keep the two sources separate.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **The paper's central claim explicitly contradicts Bowen's own prior premise.** "Here was an experience that contradicted a central theoretical and therapeutic premise" — namely his commitment "to the notion that the fastest and best change in psychotherapy came from working out the relationship between self and the one most important other person in one's life."
- **Extended-family work is promoted from supplement to primary.** Earlier: "Over-all, the work with the extended family was seen as supplemental to work on the relationship system between the spouses." Now: it may be the whole treatment.
- **The therapist-in-the-triangle claim is quietly undercut.** Bowen states there "was a sound theoretical basis for saying that 'differentiation of self,' as described by this theory, takes place only in a triangle, and the most effective method was in the triangle consisting of the two most important family members (the two spouses) and the therapist." The chapter then reports differentiation occurring with no therapist in any triangle, in a teaching group with "no 'therapeutic' objective," at fifteen to thirty minutes every month or two. The claim is not retracted — the *triangle* requirement survives (the primary triangle with parents), but the *therapist-in-that-triangle* requirement does not.
- **"Therapy" is demoted to a scare-quoted word.** "This work is rapidly changing the course of family 'therapy' as practiced at Georgetown." The helping role is renamed coaching and the recipient renamed trainee.
- **Family Systems Theory is put in explicit opposition to mainstream developmental theory** on adolescence: "most psychological theories... consider emotional turmoil to be 'normal' during adolescence. Family Systems Theory does not support this view."
- **His prediction about who would be willing to do this work was wrong, and he says so** — and pairs it with the same error in 1954 about fathers in research. "But family resistance has not been as great as I expected."
- **[p2] The chapter contradicts itself on entry sequence and never resolves it.** Its "essential message" is "to completely bypass problems in the nuclear family and to focus on working out relationships with the extended family." Its description of the usual course is the opposite ordering: "It is usual for the first part of this kind of therapy to be devoted to the relationship in the marriage and, after anxiety has subsided and there is more objectivity, to devote more and more of the later stages of therapy to the extended family." Both stand, unreconciled, in the same chapter.
- **[p2] The technique section is explicitly incomplete and defers to other papers** — "too complex to review in detail here", "beyond the scope of this brief presentation", "The details of this will ultimately appear as a full paper." This chapter is the *statement of principle*; Ch21 is the worked case.
- **He has not adopted his own finding.** "I still have not had the courage and conviction to focus exclusively on the families of origin, except in the teaching sessions." Half his practice is still both spouses together. The chapter recommends something its author has not fully implemented, and says so.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The method was discovered accidentally and by observation of a side effect**, not designed. Bowen changed what he *taught*; the trainees changed what they *did*, unbidden; the clinical improvement was noticed a year later and initially misattributed to "an unusually good group of residents." The correct attribution came only when the within-cohort correlation (best family-of-origin work ↔ best clinical work) became visible.
- **The absence of a complaint was the diagnostic signal.** "None of these residents ever mentioned emotional problems in their own nuclear families. In retrospect this was unusual." Bowen treats a *missing* help-seeking behavior as evidence of improvement — a negative observable.
- **The IRS anecdote quantifies how much home travel the method generated.** "there had been so much emphasis on the extended family that some asked if they could deduct the expense of home trips as a medical expense for tax purposes."
- **Cost asymmetry, stated in both directions.** For the family: "it is immeasurably easier to do a year or two or three of periodic family therapy visits than it is to undertake the time, expense, and emotional turmoil of visits to parental families." For the therapist: "far more skill, more continuing work on himself, and more attention to detail... On the other hand, the extended family approach requires far less direct time with the family."
- **Outcome variance is uncorrelated with apparent case difficulty**, and Bowen flags that he is reporting averages: "In this group I am talking more about averages than extremes."
- **A separate societal claim appears in passing:** the cut-off/substitute-family trend is "a product of the emotional force that moves people toward the emotional cut-off with the past. This is a powerful force in a fair percentage of families and in the total of society."
- **The coaching analogy is load-bearing, not decorative.** Athlete/coach: the coach does not perform, the athlete's progress depends on the athlete, and the coach's value is in avoiding wasted training.
- **The teaching format itself became the standard.** "This teaching format, started in spring of 1967, has since become a standard format for teaching family concepts to trainees." The intervention and the training curriculum are the same object.
- **Three separate trainee explanations for why it worked** — worth noting because they are the participants' own accounts, not Bowen's: (1) theory became "alive and real" once seen working in their own families; (2) the experience made it possible "to better understand and relate to families in the psychiatric clinic"; (3) it made it possible "to help families avoid doing things that were nonproductive and hurtful when you had the very same experience with your own family."
- **[p2] The chapter's only proposed measurement relation is about adolescence, and it is asserted, not derived:** "The intensity of the denial and the pretending in adolescence is a **remarkably accurate index** of the degree of unresolved emotional attachment to the parents." No procedure, no calibration, no cases. It is the nearest thing in the chapter to an instrument for the scale, and it is one sentence.
- **[p2] The only material/economic content in the chapter is the cost of travel.** For the family: "the time, expense, and emotional turmoil of visits to parental families", contrasted with how much cheaper conventional therapy is ("immeasurably easier to do a year or two or three of periodic family therapy visits"). Plus the IRS anecdote, and one geography→format effect: "Some who live in more distant cities are seen about two hours every two or three months." There is **no** per-person income, wealth, employment or resource variable. The one mention of employment ("fathers who would be willing to participate would be fathers who were unemployed or retired") is a 1954 belief Bowen cites only to say it was wrong.
- **The observation-plus-reactivity skill is described as generalizing beyond the family** — "an ability that is useful for life in all kinds of emotional snarls."

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

The chapter is the primary source for the coaching agent, so every rule in Stream B was re-checked
against whether Bowen **states** it or an example was converted into a mechanism. Fifteen findings
needed correction; five of them are load-bearing for the coach.

**P1.1 — The inverted-U was put on the wrong axis, and it propagated into `_CONVERGENCES` C9.**
Pass 1 (B5 #7, `M`, ledger L22.2, C9) reads the working window as a band of *family anxiety*:
calm at the bottom, confrontation at the top. Bowen never says a family is too anxious for the move.
The highest-anxiety natural events he names — **"a serious illness or a death"** — are listed among
the events that "provide the levels of family anxiety that are effective." What backfires is the
**mover's own manner**: "emotional confrontation," set in direct opposition to "introduce small
emotional issues from the past, *without getting into emotional confrontation*."
→ The non-monotone variable is **the intensity of the mover's engagement**, not ambient anxiety.
A model that gates I-POSITION on a mid-band of `family_anxiety` implements a rule this chapter
does not contain, and it would suppress the move at exactly the deaths and illnesses Bowen tells
the trainee to travel for.

**P1.2 — "There is no null move" is invented.** Pass 1 (B5 #8, `M`) elevates one clause — "To go
silent is perceived by the other as an emotional response" — into a general law that every
behaviour is a signal and abstention is unavailable to the agent. The clause sits inside the
detriangling paragraph and says only that silence fails to *read as neutrality* during an emotional
issue between two others. No consequence, no duration, no generalisation. What survives is weaker
and still useful: neutrality is a **produced response** ("always have a neutral response"), not the
residue of withholding.

**P1.3 — "Motivation is a finite budget, measurably starved" is the modeller's mechanism, not
Bowen's.** This is the most consequential invention, because `_CONVERGENCES` C1 cites Ch22 as
*supplying the mechanism* for the corpus's most-corroborated finding — "a finite motivation budget."
The words finite, budget, competing and measurably do not occur. Bowen writes: "There is much
clinical experience to support the **thesis** that people are not motivated to work toward the past
when they are involved in a process that offers a solution in the present generation," and offers as
cause a **directional force**, double-hedged — "**I believe** the powerful transgenerational
emotional force that goes toward cutting-off from the past **may be** the most important determining
factor." A second mechanism is habituation, not scarcity: the failing group "seem to become so
'addicted' to a continuation of their regular family sessions together." And the effect is partial —
"some people appear to do well with a combination approach." No count, no measurement, no
denominator.

**P1.4 — Ch22 does not support "relief opposes progress" and contains a counterexample.** C1 lists
Ch22 among eleven chapters. What Ch22 supports is **dissociation** — the first method was "amazingly
effective in relieving symptoms but… not effective as a long-term method for resolving the
underlying family 'stuck-togetherness'", and low-scale people can be "symptom free." But the
chapter's central prescription has relief and progress moving *together*: reducing the cut-off "will
soften the intensity of the family problem, reduce the symptoms, **and make any kind of therapy far
more productive**." What competes with family-of-origin work in this chapter is an alternative
*programme* offering a present-generation solution — not symptom relief as such.

**P1.5 — "There is an optimum contact rate / contact is non-monotone" is contradicted by the text.**
Pass 1 (B5 #21, `M`) derives a contact optimum from the residence scope-boundary, and supplies a
mechanism ("daily physical presence prevents objectivity") that Bowen does not give — it is
transplanted from his remarks about **nuclear-family** fusion two sentences later. Against it, the
chapter has Bowen "urging spouses to visit their family of origin **as frequently as possible**",
instructing the trainee to visit "as often as indicated", and asserting that improved frequency and
quality of contact "will predictably improve the family's level of adjustment." Contact with the
family of origin is monotone-good here. Only *co-residence* is excluded, and without a reason.

**P1.6 — Reducing cut-off was given a cost that belongs to a different act.** Pass 1 (B5 #14, `M`)
makes `move:REDUCE_CUTOFF` carry a transient anxiety penalty. Bowen's caution is about a **third
party removing a distancing mechanism as if it were a symptom** — "to attempt to remove the 'symptom'
without regard for the total family unit." The person's own increase in contact is described with no
cost term anywhere in the chapter.

**P1.7 — Cut-off intensity was folded into the three-name identity.** Pass 1's first `M` bullet

names." Bowen's declared synonym set is **poorly differentiated self / undifferentiated / unresolved

*intensity* is a stated **consequence** ("the lower the level of differentiation… the more intense
the mechanisms"), and cut-off *type* is explicitly **orthogonal** to magnitude. Fusing them also

**P1.8 — "Sustained anxiety erodes basic level."** The text says "the over-all level of

## P2. What is NEW in this chapter

Nothing in the twenty-one earlier chapters contains:

- **"Emotional cut-off" as a named term** — "We have come to use the term emotional cut-off or simply
  cut-off" — covering both internal and physical distancing under one head, with the
  **mechanism-≠-magnitude** rule: "The type of mechanism used to achieve emotional distance is not an
  indication of the intensity or the degree of unresolved emotional attachment." Ch21 describes
  cut-off without naming it; Ch16 dates the concept's formal addition to 1975. This chapter (Oct 1974

  work relationships); internal-mechanism users stay on the scene and get "physical illness…
  depression… drinking and episodic irresponsibility." Level sets magnitude, mechanism selects class.
- **Coaching vocabulary.** "Coach", "trainee", and the athlete analogy, with the division of labour
  stated: "The initial goal is to get the trainee started. Most of the learning comes as the trainee
  works toward his goal. The trainee is aware that progress depends on him."
- **The person-to-person relationship as a defined, buildable relationship state** — with an
  operational definition, a **stated duration limit** ("Few people can talk personally to anyone for

- **The non-marital-partnership negative result** — twenty years, every relationship type listed,

- **The substitute-family cycle** with progressive isolation, plus the exception: "A small percentage
  of people are able to manage marginally productive lives with numerous superficial relationships,
  in which no one relationship becomes intense."
- **The adolescence claim**, placed in explicit opposition to mainstream developmental theory, with
  denial/pretending as a "remarkably accurate index" of attachment.
- **A four-input initialisation function for a child's attachment level**, with parental
  **anxiety-handling style** as an input separate from anxiety level ("able to stay on a predetermined

- **The blocked-triangle reroute rule** with a fallback order (other member emotionally important to
  a parent → siblings → parents' generation → the generation before), and **reconstruction of the

- **The observation ↔ reactivity-control feedback loop**, and the whole-family payoff from one

- **Two constraints on using the scale**: uneven population distribution, and "it is not possible to

- **vs Ch16 + Ch21 (the scale).** Ch22 gives **no numbers** — no range, no quartiles, no population
  percentages — and adds a constraint neither has: day-to-day estimation of the level is ruled out.

- **vs Ch18 (L18.1, cut-off as an input).** Corroborated from an independent direction: cut-off

- **vs Ch15 (the death shock wave).** The same event with **opposite sign of use**. Ch15: a death

  are true and the model needs both roles on one `event-kind`.
- **vs Ch06 / Ch08 / Ch10 (`_CONVERGENCES` C7, coupling does not decay).** Consistent, and Ch22
  supplies the missing channel: contact frequency moves **anxiety**, not coupling. C7's rule ("access

- **vs Ch05 / Ch09 (calibration).** Ch05 gives outcome tallies with denominators; Ch22 gives none.

- **vs Ch13 / Ch21 (`_CONVERGENCES` C9).** C9's header — "three chapters independently describe the

## P5. Assigned open questions

**Q-LIVEISSUE — verified, with one relocation.** All four elements are verbatim: "Differentiation is
possible only around an emotional issue about which to relate"; "When the family is calm, there not
only are no emotional issues about which to relate, but the family system works hard to prevent
issues from surfacing"; "Trips home when there is a serious illness or a death, or at homecomings or
holidays, often provide the levels of family anxiety that are effective for relating to the family";
and confrontation "may be briefly effective in making the confronter believe he has accomplished
something, but the family reacts negatively and it may require months, or even a year or two, to get
beyond the family's cut-off with the confronter." **The relationship is genuinely non-monotone, not
merely "too much is bad"** — the low end is a stated *impossibility* with an active suppression
mechanism behind it, not a small positive, and the high end is a lasting negative rather than a
smaller positive. **But the axis is the mover's engagement, not the family's anxiety** (P1.1), and
calm is not a hard gate: "it is necessary to introduce small emotional issues from the past" is a
stated issue-*creation* move.

**Q-ANNOUNCE — the sentence verifies; the proposed resolution does not.** "It is impossible to tell a
family what one is trying to do and still make it work" is verbatim, and its object is "what one is
trying to do" — the programme. This chapter says **nothing whatever about announcing a specific act**
to the other party, so it is *compatible with* the different-objects resolution but supplies **no
evidence for it**. The resolution rests entirely on Ch10's side of the pair. Ch21's broader form
("anyone else knows anything about it") is the harder constraint, and it is Ch21, not Ch22, that
strains the resolution.

**Q-FAILING-MOVES — two of three verify as errors; the third does not, and the set is not closed.**
Announcing the plan and bringing spouse and children are stated prohibitions with stated
consequences. **Going silent is not a named error**: it is one clause explaining that silence fails
to read as neutrality inside a detriangling attempt, with no consequence attached. The chapter names
further errors — mis-hearing the assignment, group problem-talk, working against a bloc, taking
sides/counterattacking/defending — and ranks **emotional confrontation** first ("probably the one
biggest error"). **There is no null move: WITHDRAWN** (P1.2). Only confrontation carries a stated
recovery cost.

**Q-CONTACT — verified verbatim, and it is bidirectional.** The sentence is exact. Two additions
pass 1 under-weighted: Bowen states the converse in the next breath — "The opposite is also true. In
an emotional field of sustained high anxiety, the over-all level of differentiation will move slowly
toward greater undifferentiation" — and the beneficiary clause is gated on **motivation** ("permits
*motivated* family members"). Openness *permits*; it does not produce.

**Q-DOSAGE — quantities verify; the inference does not, and there is no denominator anywhere.**
Verbatim: "no more than fifteen to thirty minutes every month or two"; "None of the trainees or their
spouses were in any kind of 'therapy'"; "as much progress… as similar residents whom I had been
seeing in once-a-week formal family therapy with their spouses"; "as often as once a month"; "as
infrequently as once or twice a year. The over-all results with the very infrequent appointments have
not been rewarding"; "half a dozen one-hour appointments a year are more productive than weekly
formal family therapy appointments which focus on the relationship between spouses." **No sample
size, no outcome instrument, no denominator on any of it** — the outcome assessment is Bowen asking
the residents questions, and his stated warrant is "the most searching criteria I could use at the
time." The only percentages (25%, 50%) have unstated N and the second is participation, not outcome.
And the headline comparison **varies frequency and focus together** (P1.10). Treat the band as
directional; do not calibrate against it.

**Q-MOTIVATION — an inference, not a stated finding.** Not measured, not counted, hedged as a
"thesis" supported by "much clinical experience", partial in scope, and mechanised by a *directional
transgenerational force* plus *habituation* — never by a finite budget. See P1.3. The `_CONVERGENCES`
C1 line crediting Ch22 with supplying "a finite motivation budget" should be rewritten.

**Q-MOVEMENT — yes, and it lands on the movable side.** Ch22 puts the fixing point at early childhood
("'programmed'… very early in life"), not at marriage, and then states movement in both directions:
in childhood ("may become less" / "will be greater"), and in the emotional field thereafter (low
anxiety permits slow steps upward; sustained high anxiety moves the level slowly down). It does not
use the basic/functional vocabulary for those movements, so it does not settle whether *basic* level
moves — but it does contradict any reading in which the level is frozen at marriage. Against Ch17's
freeze-at-young-adulthood, this is a genuine divergence, not a rewording.

**Q-VALIDATION — none.** No instrument, no rating scale, no external measure, no blinding, no
follow-up procedure, no comparison group with a stated N. The scale is described qualitatively and
its short-horizon use is explicitly ruled out. The chapter's only proposed measurement relation is
one asserted sentence about adolescence ("a remarkably accurate index"), with no procedure. Bowen's
own case classification is conceded to be "a judgment."

**Q-MATERIAL — none per person.** The only economic content is the **cost of the visit** — "the time,
expense, and emotional turmoil of visits to parental families", contrasted against the cheapness of
conventional therapy, plus the IRS medical-deduction anecdote — and one geography→format effect
("Some who live in more distant cities are seen about two hours every two or three months"). No
income, wealth, employment or resource attribute exists on a person. The single employment mention is
a 1954 belief cited as an error.
