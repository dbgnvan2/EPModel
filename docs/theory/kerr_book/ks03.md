# KS03 — The Molecule of an Emotional System (Triangles)

**Citation rule:** cite `KS03`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

---

## ⭐ KS03.1 — The **outsider absorbs**; the insiders are calmed by the alliance `[T]` `[M]`

> "An important aspect of the dynamic equilibrium of a triangle is that **the person in the outside position absorbs the anxiety generated by the triangle.** The alliance, togetherness, or fusion of the insiders is **comforting to both insiders and calms them.**"

> "The anxiety generated by the triangle includes **the conflicts that could potentially arise between the insiders plus the anxiety the outsider has about being in that position.** … Outsiders **both generate their own anxiety and absorb anxiety from insiders** through their blaming rejection of them."

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.3` ("triangling relieves the pair and costs the third") confirmed, and **the accounting is specified in a way the spec does not have.** The outsider's load has **two** components — transferred insider anxiety *and* self-generated anxiety about the position — so the transfer is **not conservative**. `M6.I`'s conservation invariants must exempt the triangle: a triangle **creates** anxiety it did not previously contain. Check `M1.C.1`'s `bound_anxiety` transfer against this.

## ⭐ KS03.2 — Why the dyad is unstable: closeness is wanted **and** aversive `[T]`

> "human beings have a **profound need for emotional closeness but are adverse to too much of it**… **A threat to closeness triggers feelings of rejection; a threat of too much closeness triggers feelings of being intruded upon, overwhelmed, and out of control.** Threats of too much distance **and** too much closeness activate the stress response."

*Model consequence:* the two-sided pattern **for the fifth time**, and here at the most fundamental level in the theory — this is `M1.A.9a`'s two impingement axes given their origin. *Too much distance* = the inward-facing threat; *too much closeness* = the outward-facing threat. `M4.C.1` currently raises anxiety on event intensity alone; this says a tie has an **optimum** and deviation in *either* direction is a stressor. The general property flagged at `S2.8` is now established well enough to be stated as one.

> "**well-differentiated people manage the closeness-distance dilemma more effectively**… better at rising above the feeling reactions and **remaining present and accounted for** in a relationship system, even an anxious system; **poorly differentiated people are very prone to shut down and distance or to react aggressively.**"

Shut down and distance / react aggressively — the two axes again, with differentiation as the term that damps both.

## ⭐ KS03.3 — Triangles are **not pathological**, and the outsider is **not a victim** `[T]`

> "**Triangles are ubiquitous and not pathological.** They are ways for a relationship system to **compartmentalize anxiety in one part of the system, such that system anxiety impairs the functioning of as few people as possible.** However, **someone does pay a price** for that stability."

> "**My schizophrenic brother was not a victim of the triangles but an active participant in them.**"

*Model consequence:* confirms `M1.C.3b`'s framing — the triangle is structure, not disease — and adds the **function**: anxiety compartmentalisation that *minimises the number of impaired members*. That is a design rationale the spec does not state, and it makes the triangle a **protective** mechanism with a distributional cost, not a failure mode. `M11.F` should carry the no-victim reading alongside `KS01.5`'s no-culpability one.

## ⭐ KS03.4 — Two named routes for the outsider to become an insider — both implementable `[T]`

**Route 1 — the negative story.**
> "the outsiders **tell an insider a negative story about the other insider**. If the negative story is sufficient to persuade the insider to become negative about the other insider, the outsider can often then successfully form an alliance… **That puts the other insider in the outside position.** Predictably, this **drives the new outsider to reestablish an inside position**, and thus unfolds the dynamic process of a triangle."

**Route 2 — the uncomfortable insider defects.** Tension between insiders → they distance → "**Distancing works better for one insider than for the other**" → the blocked one "**absorbs the anxiety**… commonly feels isolated and out of control" → she initiates closeness with the outsider → "The outsider is **eager for closeness**… and jumps on the opportunity" → "The previously calmer insider now **inherits the anxiety** of being in the outside position."

*Model consequence:* **the triangle position-shift mechanism, fully specified, which `M1.C` currently leaves as "position shifts".** Two distinct transitions with distinct preconditions and a stated cycle-driver ("predictably, this drives the new outsider to reestablish an inside position"). This is directly implementable and gives `M3.A.1`'s *triangle position shifts* step a rule set. **High-value.**

Note the asymmetry named in Route 2: **distance works better for one party than the other**, and the one it fails is the one who "**has typically been the one making the most accommodations to preserve relationship harmony**". Also: "The insulating insider is usually **blaming the other**… The other insider is usually **feeling inadequate and self-blaming.**" — the two blame directions of `KS00.2` mapped onto the two positions.

## KS03.5 — Overload at three → a fourth is recruited; interlocking triangles spread anxiety `[T]`

> "The triangle **can contain more anxiety, but when it is overloaded, one member of the threesome will involve a fourth person**, which leads to the formation of interlocking triangles. **Anxiety can spread infectiously** in a group of people through a series of interlocking triangles."

*Model consequence:* a **capacity threshold** on the triangle with a named consequence for exceeding it. `M1.C` has triangles but no stated overload behaviour. Confirms `KB06`/1979 Tape 5 on capacity scaling and gives it a discrete transition.

## KS03.6 — Belief supports polarisation but does not cause it `[T]`

> "the beliefs group members hold about themselves and members of the other groups **support the polarization but do not cause it. It is an emotional process at its core.** It is the **coupling of emotion with belief** and the compulsion to preserve the alliances within one's own group that make it extremely difficult for either side to grasp the other side's perspective. **Any group member who attempts to step outside** what can become a highly malignant process **is typically labeled as disloyal and often shunned.**"

*Model consequence:* two things.
1. `M9` (beliefs) **MUST** be downstream of emotional process, never an independent driver. Corroborates `KB10` · K10.3's societal→pseudo-self loop from the other end.
2. **The last sentence is the change-back reaction at group scale** — disloyal, shunned — which is `M5.E` applied to a subgroup rather than a dyad. Supports extending `M5.E.5` (push-back can surface elsewhere) to group-level exclusion.

## KS03.7 — Low "self" ⇒ dependence on group affiliation; the cult case `[T]`

> "**The less 'self' people have, the more dependent they are on affiliation with a group** or groups to support their emotional functioning. **People with low levels of 'self,' suffering from depression, with no direction in their lives, may join cult groups and improve their functioning dramatically.**"

*Model consequence:* **a clean, falsifiable prediction and a strong `S2.12` data point.** Joining a high-fusion group *raises* functional level while basic level is unchanged — functioning improved by **borrowed** self. This is precisely the automatic-channel functional rise in which **pseudo-self goes up**, which is the reading proposed for `S2.12`. It also gives `M11.C` a candidate criterion: an arm in which an agent's functional level rises on group affiliation **MUST NOT** raise its estimated basic level (`M1.A.4a`), because the gain is neither broad nor load-tested.

## KS03.8 — Rejection has named forms, and talking-about is easier than talking-to `[T]`

> "The rejection can take many forms, such as the insiders **overly focusing on what is wrong with the outsider, judging and criticizing** that person, **whether face to face or behind closed doors.** **It is easier for people to talk about a third person than to talk personally to each other.** Rejection, of course, can also take the form of **actively excluding** the third person."

*Model consequence:* `M1.F`'s `route` field is doing real work — the same rejection delivered *face to face* and *behind closed doors* are different events with different witness sets. And "easier to talk about a third than to talk personally" is a **propensity statement**: `TRIANGLE` is cheaper than a direct move on the tie, which `M4.D.2`'s propensity function should reflect.

## KS03.9 — Two more triangle variants, both implementable `[T]`

**The exclusive dyad.** "two people who can keep their relationship comfortable **provided they are not interacting with a third person**… each of the insiders putting **maximum energy into the other**." A third party draws the wife's energy; the husband "gets upset and criticizes the wife for her inattention"; when the third departs, "the wife invests more energy in her mate again and that calms him." The countermeasure — pressuring her to cut off her friends — is "**an 'administrative' solution, not a grown-up solution.**"

**The four-step family sequence** (Figures 3.4–3.7), given as a worked case from Kerr's own family: calm insider pair (mother/brother, father outside) → tension between insiders → mother signals distress → father moves in on the brother → conflict between father and brother → **mother retreats to the now-comfortable outside position** ("Let's you and him fight!") → at the threshold of violence, father recruits a fourth (oldest brother) → interlocking triangle → anxiety subsides "over a day or two."

*Model consequence:* the exclusive dyad is `M1.A.10`'s life-energy budget in triangle form, and the "administrative solution" line is a **named counterfeit of a differentiating move** — a structural fix substituting for a self-management one, which belongs with `M5.F`'s counterfeit family.

The four-step sequence is a **second complete scenario fixture** (with `KS02.8`), and this one runs a full cycle including recruitment of a fourth and a stated settling time. **Candidate acceptance-test scenario for `M1.C` + `M5.E.5`.**

## KS03.10 — Gossip is the triangle's normal-range form `[T]`

> "**A gossip system is a seemingly essential element of all human groups.** It can serve a useful purpose. **Gossip spreads through triangles.** When the **emotional valence of the gossip content rises**, it becomes **more likely to create insiders and outsiders.**"

*Model consequence:* a **continuous** rather than binary trigger for triangle activation, keyed to valence of the content passing along the route. Supports making `TRIANGLE` a graded move rather than an on/off structural change, and it is the benign end of the same mechanism as `KS03.4`'s negative story.
