# Ch01 — Treatment of Family Groups With a Schizophrenic Member

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** The chapter body states no date and names no venue. It is dated **1957** in the volume's table of contents. The book's Introduction (same file) supplies the surrounding facts: the research study ran **1954–1959 at the National Institute of Mental Health**; "the first formal presentations to national meetings were in the spring of 1957"; those papers "were never published" and "one of these early papers is included in this volume for the sake of history." Internal dating markers in the chapter body are relative only: the project "was started two and a half years ago," the treatment change has yielded "14 months of clinical experience," and the project "is going into its third year."
- **Byline:** Bowen, "With the collaboration of Robert H. Dysinger, M.D., Warren M. Brodey, M.D., and Betty Basamania, M.S.W."
- **Clinical setting and data source:** An inpatient research ward on which **normal family members lived on the ward with hospitalized schizophrenic patients**. Year 1: **three chronic schizophrenic young women and their mothers**. Year 2: **two complete family units** (father, mother, patient, normal sibling) admitted, with all parents required to live on the ward and school-age normal siblings staying on the ward over weekends. **Five families total**, "voluminous data." Data sources named: daily one-hour open ward-lounge meetings, all **tape recorded**, plus three simultaneous written records — process psychotherapy notes, a **process sociogram**, and a summary of content.
- **Genre:** Both, weighted toward observation. Roughly **60% theory/observation : 40% technique**. Structurally: the hypothesis and the observations occupy the first two-thirds; the "Extension of Hypothesis and Change in Treatment Plan" section, which is mostly technique (rules, meeting design, staff procedure), occupies the last third, though it too is interleaved with clinical-course observation. The technique described is administrative and structural (who may talk to whom, where, in whose presence) far more than it is interpretive.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

Facts Bowen reports as having forced the hypothesis to change:

- **Closeness-distance cycles.** Left to themselves, mother and daughter would "get overclose, fight, separate, come back together again, and repeat the cycles over and over." Observed in the three mother-patient pairs on the unstructured ward.
- **Anxiety is a function of proximity, in both directions.** As closeness increased, anxiety rose to "intense incorporation anxiety"; they then fought and separated until "separation anxiety set up a reunion and a new cycle." Neither pole is stable.
- **In closeness phases, outside relationships go calm.** The pair "seemed interested only in each other, and their relationships to outside figures were calm." In distance phases each was hostile to the other and each tried to duplicate the symbiotic relationship with an outside figure, whom they found "difficult, demanding, and anxious."
- **Neither member can regulate the cycle from inside.** They "never seemed to have the inner strength to control the intensity of the cycles" and sought "outside rules, advice, or structure" to do it.
- **External structure damps and obscures the cycle.** "When staff unwittingly did furnish this outside structure, the clarity of the closeness-distance cycles was less plainly distinguishable."
- **Fluidity rather than fixity.** The original hypothesis predicted a rigid fused state; the surprising observation was "the marked mobility, fluidity, and pervasiveness of the process."
- **Anxiety moves from mother to patient on the mother's schedule, not the patient's.** Mother becomes anxious → her thinking focuses on the patient's sickness → she verbalizes the patient's sickness repeatedly → "very soon the mother's anxiety would be less and the patient's psychotic symptoms would be increased." The timing "seemed related to the mother's own functioning rather than to the reality of the patient's functioning."
- **The patient is a better instrument for reading the mother than the mother is.** "The psychotic patient in daily living contact with mother can be a more sensitive indicator of the functioning of mother than direct observation of mother herself."
- **Reverse transfer (rarer).** A newly admitted patient making firm gains out of chronic regression was told "You are weak, go slow"; each time she took a new positive step "the mother, within a few hours, would go to bed with a physical illness of several days' duration."
- **Overflow to siblings.** One mother could separate from the hospitalized daughter, "and within hours her anxiety would be less and a younger son would have disabling neurotic symptoms."
- **Overflow to staff and beyond.** In separation phases "the anxiety or the sickness or the problem could shift to a variety of receptive figures inside or outside the family" — staff, other relatives, social agencies, "the hospital community or even the outside community." During these staff-turmoil periods "the family would then be more free of anxiety" and "the patterns of the closeness-distance cycles disappeared."
- **Staff insight does not prevent staff capture.** "The staff had an excellent intellectual understanding of the phenomenon but the process continued on an unconscious level."
- **Distortion propagates through relays.** A mother could speak to a staff person and "within minutes relay a distorted version of the staff comment to the daughter who would then act on the distortion. One distortion added to another, involving multiple staff members, could become complicated."
- **The psychosis is addressed.** "The patients' psychoses seemed to be directed specifically at mother, almost point for point, at the old issues between them."
- **Under peak anxiety the psychosis spares its target.** "When the patients' anxieties would reach a high level, the psychoses seemed to 'spray' to everyone except mother, leaving mother free to stay close by."
- **The father changed first, in both complete families.** They were "the first to become interested in their own problems and their part in the family dilemma."
- **A father's move produces immediate family calm.** Even weak, authoritarian first attempts at having his own convictions "usually brought a period of calm to the family."
- **Symptom migrates to the well sibling.** At seven months the normal fifteen-year-old daughter "reverted to a very rebellious infantile demanding state," lay with her head on mother's lap threatening to run away and marry; the mother "immediately had a relationship with this daughter similar to the one with the psychotic daughter," and "the family fear[ed] she would become psychotic too." The father "immediately reverted to the weak, authoritative role."
- **The pair cannot separate itself.** "The results with psychotherapy also suggested the symbiotic pair to be a weak undifferentiated fragment of a larger group and to lack within itself the strength to differentiate into two autonomous people."
- **The observed person is context-dependent.** "A family member is different when in relationship to family symbiotic attachments than in relationship with other figures"; observation of the member with the family "is different than composite observations assembled from other sources."
- **Emotional impact does not habituate for observers.** Seeing the intensity of a mother's involvement "was always a new and surprising experience for the staff… it has the quality of an emotional experience that can be tolerated only for brief periods without re-repression."
- **[p2] The cycle has no internal stopping rule; it stops by depletion or interruption.** The mothers' preoccupation with the patients "had the pattern of something the mothers and patients had to continue to exhaustion or disruption." Two terminating conditions, both external to any set point.
- **[p2] The two phases are described as equal in magnitude and opposite in character.** "There was an intense cohesiveness in the closeness phases… and an equally intense disruptiveness in the distance phases. There was a quality of magnetic pulling together in the closeness phases and explosive fragmentation in the distance phases."
- **[p2] When the anxiety leaves the pair, the oscillation itself stops.** During periods of staff turmoil "the family would then be more free of anxiety" and "the patterns of the closeness-distance cycles disappeared during these periods." Not merely obscured — the cycles are stated to disappear when an outside sink is carrying the load. The oscillator is powered by anxiety the pair retains.
- **[p2] Actual impairment and displayed adequacy can be inverted.** The soliciting end of the transfer scale "occurred often in the family in which mother was **really more impaired** than the daughter" — while that mother held the strong, pronouncing role, and while the daughter "would work actively to be more irresponsible and helpless than the mother." Bowen distinguishes what a member *really* is from the position she occupies, in plain language and without any two-level vocabulary.
- **[p2] The change-back message, and its escalation when words fail.** The patient making gains was told "You are weak, go slow" and "Remember the last time you tried things too fast, you cracked up." She "showed little concern"; only then did each further step produce the mother's illness within a few hours. The verbal rung is tried first and the somatic rung follows its failure. The reaction is also stated to decay unfed: she "maintained this command of the situation for several months and then relinquished it."
- **[p2] The move lands when the mover's strength exceeds the counter-attack.** "For the first time his strength seemed greater than the mother's attack and within ten days she went out alone with him for the first time." An explicit inequality between two quantities, not a property of the move.
- **[p2] The patient's exclusion from the parental pair is the observable of the shift.** "Up to this point she insisted that the psychotic daughter accompany them." The marital outing without the patient is what marks the change; the daughter's psychosis then becomes "like a tremendous temper tantrum designed to win mother back to her," and in the same period "the daughter made some gains" — display and function moving in opposite directions at once.
- **[p2] Airtime in the group is scarce and contested.** "Family members press for time to be heard, and have competed in bringing up difficult subjects. There is rarely a meeting without vigorous emotion."
- **[p2] The clinicians' own capture is a function of the format they work in.** "The therapists have felt themselves to be in more of an objective relationship to schizophrenia than they had found possible in doing or supervising individual psychotherapy." The same variable as staff incorporation, with the opposite sign, moved by a structural change rather than by training.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Unresolved symbiotic attachment to the mother** → produces "a state of relative physical maturity along with a state of marked psychological immaturity" → acts on the child's development. Bowen explicitly equates it in kind with biological symbiosis: the mother "physically gives up her child in the birth process but… is unable to psychologically give up the child." Framed as a **developmental arrest** in what was once a normal state.
- **The mother's double bind on the child's growth** → she "covertly does things to block the child's development" while she "simultaneously tries to force the child to achievement" → acts on the child's growth trajectory in two opposite directions at once.
- **The child's own two-way effort** → once entangled, the child "tries to perpetuate the symbiosis along with the opposite effort to grow up" → the child is a participant, not a victim.
- **The father's passive exclusion** → he "passively permits himself to be excluded from the intense twosome and marries his business or other outside interests" → removes the third position and leaves the twosome unbuffered.
- **Closeness-distance cycling** → an oscillator with two opposing anxiety terms (incorporation anxiety rising with closeness; separation anxiety rising with distance) → acts on the dyad's distance; runs continuously with no internal damping.
- **Transfer of anxiety** → anxiety, weakness, sickness, or psychosis moves quantitatively out of one member and into another ("a quality about this that suggested almost a quantitative transfer of anxiety") → acts across the mother-patient pair primarily, and secondarily onto siblings, staff, and the wider community. Bowen is explicit that "both play active roles."
- **The activity scale of transfer.** Transfers occur "at points along the same scale," defined by who is doing the pushing:
  - *One end:* mother forces the anxiety/psychosis onto "a resisting, fighting, victimized, reluctant patient," pronouncing the patient sick/stupid/weak/incompetent/inadequate/confused/schizophrenic, seeking "medical and other authoritative opinion to support the pronouncement," and using the term *schizophrenia* frequently once it entered her vocabulary. Patient fights back — denial → counterattack ("You are the one who is sick") → psychotic hostility. Requires the patient to be "integrated enough to fight back"; rare in these very impaired families.
  - *Middle (most common here):* "the parent actively forced the pronouncement onto the patient member and the patient member actively reached out to accept it."
  - *Other end:* the patient "actively sought to be called sick and weak and the parent who was reluctant to go along with it." Occurred in the family "in which mother was really more impaired than the daughter," and when the mother tried to force the daughter to be responsible and to mother her — at which point "the daughter would work actively to be more irresponsible and helpless than the mother and to have this confirmed with a label of stupid."
- **Status-quo maintenance.** "An important point was the activity that went into the situation to maintain an emotional status quo. Either mother or patient could portray themselves as being victimized by the other when status quo was threatened." Also seen when the daughter exceeded the mother's functioning level: "the daughter seemed threatened by any further move that might make her responsible for the mother and the mother seemed threatened at permitting herself to depend on the daughter."
- **Projection of the weak self** (Bowen's "tentative dynamic formulation"): the mother "projects her own weak self into the child which results in a psychological orientation in which she is completely strong and the child is weak. With her adequate self she mothers her weak self which is perceived to be in the child."
- **Consequence: giving decouples from the child's needs.** "The giving is then governed by her own wishes rather than the reality needs of the patient." When her own wish for mothering is intense she can force attention on the child regardless of the child's wishes, citing "an authority who advocated unlimited giving to children"; when her need is low she can "neglect the child beyond any reality needs of the child as a person" and justify it with "another authority who advises parents to be firm." The mother's attention "was determined from inside herself rather than the reality of the situation."
- **The defense ladder under load.** All five mothers used denial, reaction formation, and projection prominently. "Under calm conditions, all five mothers seemed to do fairly well with the use of denial and reaction formation. In periods of stress, projection came into use." What is projected is "primarily the mother's own feelings of helplessness, weakness, and inadequacy."
- **Denial of the passive wish.** "Their passive wishes (wish to be mothered) are great, but equally great is their inability to accept help": *"It is weak to want mothering. I am not weak. I am strong. I only want to mother my child."*
- **No feeling/reality discrimination.** "None of the families had any sense of discrimination between feeling and reality. To them, to feel helpless is to be helpless." This is the mechanism that lets a projected feeling become a functional fact.
- **Combining with outside figures.** On fragmentation of the central closeness, "the symbiotic pairs attempted to form outside relationships which had the same qualities as the central symbiotic relationship," a process taking place "on an unconscious level in which both the family members and outside figures played active roles." One staff member's involvement propagates "through a chain of intrastaff relationships" to involve the whole staff in turmoil.
- **Family projection process** (named here): "a kind of family projection process in which the family weakness is projected to the patient who resists noneffectively and then accepts it."
- **The father as substitute self.** "When a father did get into the relationship, he seemed to become mother's agent, using her words, and the patient related to him the same as to mother." Getting in is not the same as taking a position.
- **[p2] Two alternative routes to getting the denied passive wish met.** "Three mothers had somatic mechanisms by which it was possible to permit themselves to have some attention. The other two were the only ones who were consistently dominating and dogmatic." A 3/2 split across the five: somatize, or dominate. The same need is discharged through either channel — a person-level instance of the substitutable-channel idea the chapter states at family level.
- **[p2] Physical illness as a position of control, not only as a symptom.** Of the mother who took to bed each time the patient advanced: she "maintained this **command of the situation** for several months and then relinquished it." Illness is described as commanding the field, and as relinquishable.
- **The father's firm position as the unlock condition.** "Both of these mothers and patients seemed to be in the same helpless bind as the symbiotic pairs and remained so until the father could firmly establish himself as a person in the family. Only then was it really possible for the mother to understand or accept psychotherapeutic interpretations."

## A3. Predictive claims

Each stated as a conditional, with the evidential status Bowen gives it.

1. **If mother's anxiety rises, then the patient's psychotic symptoms rise shortly after.** *Asserted as an operational regularity used prospectively:* "This mechanism was so common that any increase in mother's anxiety would alert the staff for an increase in the patient's psychosis." (Repeated ward observation; no counts given.)
2. **If anxiety transfers out of a member, that member's own anxiety falls.** Stated for mother→patient ("very soon the mother's anxiety would be less"), for mother→son ("within hours her anxiety would be less and a younger son would have disabling neurotic symptoms"), and for family→staff ("the family would then be more free of anxiety"). *Observation, several instances.*
3. **If the patient makes gains toward autonomy, the mother becomes symptomatic.** Evidenced by one striking case — physical illness of several days' duration, recurring for several months. *Single-case evidence, presented as "a most striking one."*
4. **If closeness increases past a point, incorporation anxiety produces a fight and separation; if separation persists, separation anxiety produces reunion.** *Asserted as the characteristic pattern from year-one observation of three pairs.*
5. **If outside structure is supplied, the cycles become less visible** (not necessarily resolved). *Stated as an opinion about staff effect.*
6. **If the central closeness fragments, the pair will recruit outside figures into symbiosis-shaped relationships.** *Observation; "anticipated in the hypothesis."*
7. **If a staff member becomes involved with a family, staff objectivity and therapeutic effectiveness are lost and the turmoil spreads through the staff.** *Observation, "constantly occurring situations."*
8. **Intellectual understanding of the process does not prevent being drawn into it.** *Asserted from direct staff experience.*
9. **If the patient is integrated enough, she will fight the sick-label; if not, she will accept it.** *Observation, with the note that the fighting-back examples were "few… in these very impaired families."*
10. **If the daughter's functioning rises above the mother's, both act to reverse it.** *Observation, "on one occasion."*
11. **If confidential channels exist, distortion multiplies and relationships become distant and hostile.** "Relationships can become distant and hostile when there are secrets." *Asserted; the rule change is offered as the corrective and its effect ("This worked satisfactorily") as the evidence.*
12. **If the father takes and holds a position of his own, the family calms — and only then can the mother use interpretation.** *Presented as "the striking point," from the clinical course of two families.*
13. **If the father reverts, the process resumes and recruits the next available child.** *Observation, one family, one cycle of five months.*
14. **If patient anxiety is high enough, symptoms disperse to everyone except the primary attachment figure.** *Observation in both complete families.*
15. **If the family is treated as a unit rather than as individuals, anxiety on the whole project falls and observation becomes more complete and accurate.** *Asserted as staff judgment after 14 months.*
16. **[p2] The more intense the symbiotic attachment, the greater the personality impairment, and the slower the therapeutic response.** "It was assumed that this degree of symbiosis would imply a maximum degree of personality impairment which in turn would also result in a slower therapeutic response." *Explicitly an assumption carried into the study design — it is never tested or revisited in the chapter.* The only stated monotone relation between attachment intensity and rate of change.
17. **[p2] If the mover's strength exceeds the opposing member's attack, the opposition yields within days.** "For the first time his strength seemed greater than the mother's attack and within ten days she went out alone with him for the first time." *Single case, stated as the turning point of the one completed sequence.*
18. **[p2] If anxiety discharges into figures outside the pair, the pair's closeness-distance cycling stops.** *Observation, "constantly occurring situations"; stated as a disappearance of the pattern, not a reduction.*
19. **[p2] If the verbal change-back message fails, the reaction escalates to the objector's own physical illness — and later relinquishes if the gains hold.** *Single case, presented as "a most striking one."*

- **[p2] Frequency changed with the format.** The precursor forum was **weekly** — "A weekly meeting, in use for some months, had been fairly successful in dealing with discordant issues between the families and staff." The family-unit meeting that replaced it is **daily**, one hour. This is the highest contact frequency stated anywhere in the corpus; recorded as a fact, not as a cause of anything.

**[p2] Sample composition, precisely**
- Year 1 was female-only by design: "Women patients were chosen because the ward would adapt to all women better than to all men or to mixed groups." The rule did not hold in year 2 — family 2's patient is a "catatonic son… twenty." Four of the five index patients are female, one male.
- **Only one of the two father-arcs completes inside the reporting window:** "The second father is still in this stage" — i.e. still at weak, authoritative first efforts, with no declaration of independence. All timings under "Clinical course of family 1" belong to that one family.
- The "fourteen months of clinical experience since the change" and family 1's admission "fourteen months ago" are the same clock.

**Training-conference figure (from the book's Introduction, not the chapter):** trainees had "no more than thirty minutes a month for discussion of their own families in conference."

## A5. Terminology

- **Symbiosis / symbiotic attachment** — the core unit of this chapter's theory: an unresolved, mutually maintained mother-child fusion held to be "the same order of phenomenon as biological symbiosis" and defined as a **developmental arrest**. It occupies the explanatory position that *fusion* / *undifferentiation* occupies elsewhere in Bowen; here the term is developmental and dyadic, not scalar.
- **Range of symbiotic attachments** — "a wide range… from the more intense attachments in which mother and child remain together, to the mildest nonclinical attachments," including "the large group in which the child disrupts the tie to mother only to duplicate it in marriage or other relationships throughout life." A continuum is asserted, but it is a continuum of *attachment intensity*, not a numbered scale. This project "was designed to deal with a very narrow range of symbiosis."
- **Closeness-distance cycles** — the named oscillation of the pair. "The most characteristic relationship pattern."
- **Incorporation anxiety** — the anxiety that rises as closeness increases.
- **Separation anxiety** — the anxiety that rises during distance and drives reunion.
- **Transfer of anxiety / "transfer anxiety"** — the movement of "anxiety, of weakness, of sickness, or of psychosis from one member to another," with "almost a quantitative" quality. Doing the job later done by *anxiety binding*, *projection*, and *symptom location*, but stated here as a conserved substance changing hands.
- **Fixed fusion** — the *superseded* description ("a rather fixed and rigid state with a high degree of personality fusion") that the observed fluidity forced Bowen to extend. Note that "fusion" appears here as the thing that turned out to be too static, not as the master concept.
- **Family projection process** — appears by name: "a kind of family projection process in which the family weakness is projected to the patient who resists noneffectively and then accepts it." Already the mature phrasing, though the projector is "the family," not specifically the parental dyad.
- **"Family ego mass"** — appears once, in quotation marks: support goes to "a family member who gets anxious in his effort to differentiate self from the 'family ego mass.'" The quotation marks suggest a term newly coined and not yet settled. **[p2] Verified: exactly one occurrence in the chapter body.**
- **[p2] "Undifferentiated"** — also appears exactly once, and as a plain adjective: the symbiotic pair is "a weak undifferentiated fragment of a larger group." So both halves of the phrase Ch05 reports as already discarded — *undifferentiated* and *family ego mass* — are present in 1957, in separate sentences, never joined.
- **Ego boundaries** — "Family members all have poorly defined ego boundaries, if that be a satisfactory way to conceptualize it." The hedge is explicit; this is borrowed psychoanalytic vocabulary Bowen is already uncomfortable with. "Lack of ego boundaries in the symbiotic pairs."
- **Differentiate self from others** — used as the therapeutic aim: "helping the individual define his own 'self' and to differentiate self from others." *Self* is in quotation marks. The word *differentiation* is present, but as a therapeutic activity, not as a level, scale, or inherited quantity.
- **"Combining with"** — the unconscious recruitment of outside figures into symbiosis-shaped relationships.
- **"Declaration of independence"** — the father's decisive self-definition move; delivered to the head nurse "but which seemed for his own mother."
- **"The honeymoon"** — the family's own name for the two-month calm following the father's move.
- **"Spray"** — high patient anxiety directing psychotic symptoms at everyone except mother.
- **Family unit therapy / "treat the family as a single unit"** — the method name; also "psychotherapy for the family as a unit."
- **Borrowed convictions** — what a father has instead of his own; his first efforts are "convictions of their own (instead of borrowed convictions)."
- **Not present in this chapter:** triangle/triangling, differentiation *scale*, nuclear family emotional system, multigenerational transmission, sibling position, emotional cutoff, societal emotional process, chronic anxiety, basic vs. functional self, over/underfunctioning reciprocity as named terms. The chapter's causal vocabulary is symbiosis + anxiety transfer + projection.
- **[p2] Absence verified by exhaustive search of the chapter body, front matter excluded.** *triangle / triangling / detriangling*: **zero** in the body — the file's two hits sit in the volume's Introduction, Bowen's 1977 retrospective, where he describes using "new knowledge about triangles" in August 1966. *multigenerational*, *generation(s)*: **zero** in the body; again Introduction-only. *cutoff / cut-off*: **zero anywhere in the file.** *nuclear family*, *societal*: Introduction and Contents only. *sibling* appears four times, always as a family role, never as a position concept. *chronic* appears three times, always of chronic schizophrenia or chronic regression — never of anxiety.
- **[p2] The differentiation root appears four times and is never a noun.** "undifferentiated fragment"; "the strength to differentiate into two autonomous people"; "differentiate self from others"; "differentiate self from the 'family ego mass.'" No instance of *differentiation*, no level, no degree, no number attached to any person.
- **[p2] "Scale" appears three times and is not this scale.** All three are in the transfer passage — "at points along the same scale," "at one end of the scale," "the middle of the scale," "at the other end of the scale" — describing who is doing the pushing in the sick-label transaction. The first "scale" in the corpus measures the direction of a transaction, not a person.
- **[p2] Qualifier on "basic vs. functional self."** The terms are absent, but the distinction is drawn once, descriptively: a mother "really more impaired than the daughter" while occupying the adequate role. Absence of the vocabulary should not be recorded as absence of the observation.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The symptomatic child is the terminus of the projection.** "The family weakness is projected to the patient who resists noneffectively and then accepts it." Both complete-family patients "were helpless, irresponsible babies."
- **The mother is the dominant executive.** In both complete families "the mothers were the active, decision making, dominant family members who assumed roles of strength and adequacy." Her strength is explicitly the reciprocal of the child's projected weakness, not an independent trait.
- **The father is peripheral by default.** Originally hypothesized to "passively permit himself to be excluded" and marry "his business or other outside interests." Observed: "Both fathers were weak, conforming, and devoted in the relationship to the mother." "The fathers sat helplessly on the periphery. They did not seem to have an identity of their own but saw the struggle in terms of siding with one or the other."
- **Three positions available to a father, only one of which helps.** Stay outside (helpless periphery) → nothing changes. Enter by siding → he becomes "mother's agent, using her words," and is related to as a duplicate of mother. Enter by self-definition → family calms, and mother becomes reachable. The distinction between *involvement* and *position* is the chapter's sharpest structural claim.
- **[p2] The patient occupies a position inside the marriage, and vacating it is the marker of change.** Before the father's move the mother "insisted that the psychotic daughter accompany them"; ten days after it she "went out alone with him for the first time." The symptomatic member is a standing third party to the parental pair, and her removal from it — not any change in her symptoms — is what the chapter treats as the turning point.
- **[p2] Only one father completes the arc.** Both were "the first to become interested in their own problems," but "the second father is still in this stage." The peripheral-member-moves-first claim rests on two fathers *starting*; the full sequence through to a change in the mother is documented for one.
- **The father moves first.** "The first members to change in both families were the fathers." Their first attempts are poor in quality but effective in result — "weak and authoritative in telling others what to do and say and believe. Even this much activity usually brought a period of calm."
- **The mother resists the father's move specifically.** Through the father's early phase, the mothers "continued their preoccupation with the patient, denying that they had problems, insisting their lives were devoted to the welfare of the patient, and particularly attacking the father at any show of strength."
- **The well sibling looks unusually mature and is next in line.** "Both normal siblings appeared more mature than their years." When the mother was freed from the psychotic daughter, the fifteen-year-old normal daughter took the vacated position within months.
- **Sibling substitution across the whole set.** The mother-son case: separation from the hospitalized daughter → younger son symptomatic within hours. Symptom location is a family-level variable, not a person-level one.
- **Staff occupy family positions.** Outside figures — "other relatives, staff members, social agencies, or other receptive figures" — are structurally interchangeable with family members as recipients of the transfer. The ward staff functioned as an extension of the family system, reenacting "intrafamily tensions… between staff and family or entirely within the staff group."
- **Admission criteria encode a structural assumption:** parents "were to have lived away from their own immediate families," one severely impaired child, "not more than two children" — chosen so the process "would be contained within four people without involving inlaws or multiple siblings." Bowen designs the family boundary he wants to study, implying he already believes in-laws and additional siblings are live participants.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **Treat the family as the unit.** "The shift from psychotherapy for the individual to psychotherapy for the family as a unit." Concretely: the newly admitted family "was asked to forego individual psychotherapy and to use the meetings to discuss individual problems."
- **Convene everyone, daily, in one room.** An hour each day in the ward lounge, about eighteen people, roughly half staff and half family; unstructured; "any member can introduce any subject at any time."
- **Enforce the everyone-present rule for consequential talk.** "No one would speak to either mother or daughter about these issues until mother, patient, and all staff on duty were in the same room together." (Adopted after two weaker rules failed — see B3.)
- **Abolish confidences.** "Any communication presented in confidence could be brought up in the group" (a rule "similar to one used by Jones in 'The Therapeutic Community'").
- **Deflect private approaches with a fixed line.** "The staff person could refuse to relate to a family member in reference to a personal subject by saying, 'Take it up in the group.'" If a personalized communication does occur, "the staff member could report it in the group."
- **Redirect the staff toward neutral subjects.** "The staff could encourage relationship on neutral subjects."
- **Point out and clarify relationship patterns — at three levels.** The psychiatrists and social worker took "a therapeutic responsibility to point out and clarify relationship patterns between family members, between families and staff, and between staff members when appropriate."
- **Name projection every time it occurs.** "Projection is consistently pointed out in the group."
- **Name the staff's own participation.** "Much time goes to defining intrafamily relationship patterns and especially defining the part played by staff when a staff member provokes a repetition of the family pattern in the staff."
- **Help the individual define a self.** "Considerable effort goes into helping the individual define his own 'self' and to differentiate self from others."
- **Support the member who is differentiating.** "There are many ego supportive moves such as support from a family member who gets anxious in his effort to differentiate self from the 'family ego mass.'"
- **Start reality, arrive at personal.** The meetings began as a forum for ward policy and nursing procedures; "discussion of reality problems led to discussion of personal problems." The concrete grievance is the entry point, not an obstacle.
- **Record everything.** Tape plus process psychotherapy notes plus a process sociogram plus a content summary.
- **[p2] Raise the frequency when the format changes.** The precursor staff–family forum met **weekly**; the family-unit meeting meets **daily**. Bowen gives no reason for the change and never revisits it.
- **[p2] The convener is the project's ranking figure, not the family's therapist.** "The project director presided."
- **[p2] Expect the format to change the clinician, and treat that as a reported result.** "The therapists have felt themselves to be in more of an objective relationship to schizophrenia than they had found possible in doing or supervising individual psychotherapy."
- **In the original design:** provide a supportive therapeutic relationship for the mother (a social worker was chosen as the relationship that "most nearly fulfilled the hypothesized criteria"), a medical psychotherapist for the patient, and "influence the natural course of psychological events between mother and patient as little as possible by the ward environment."

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not take sides.** Staff must "relate to the families without taking sides or becoming incorporated into the family process."
- **Do not accept a confidence.** The whole confidentiality-abolishing rule exists because "each staff and family member being the keeper of many secrets" produces pervading anxiety and tension.
- **Do not discuss the family outside the family's presence.** "Routine staff meetings, to discuss family problems, were discontinued. This included staff conferences and ward rounds." Where meetings were unavoidable, "staff was asked to report about these to the group."
- **Do not be the single privileged channel.** The rule that "only one person, the ward psychiatrist, would speak to either mother or patient about important issues" was tried and explicitly reported: "This structure did not work well either."
- **Do not supply external structure to control the pair's cycling** (in the research phase) — when staff "unwittingly did furnish this outside structure," the phenomenon under study was obscured. Note that the families actively solicit exactly this: "They attempted to get outside rules, advice, or structure to control the vigor of the cycles."
- **Do not rely on understanding as protection.** Explicitly demonstrated to fail: excellent intellectual grasp, unchanged unconscious capture.
- **Do not encourage practices that fragment the family.** The meeting plan was designed "to discourage practices which had been found to encourage fragmentation of the family."
- **Do not treat the individual while claiming to treat the family.** Individual psychotherapy was withdrawn, not supplemented.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **The rule sequence, in the order tried, with results:**
  1. Ordinary multi-channel staff contact → distortion relayed within minutes and compounded across staff.
  2. Single-channel rule (only the ward psychiatrist speaks to mother or patient about important issues) → "did not work well either."
  3. All-present rule (nobody speaks to mother or daughter about these issues until mother, patient, and all on-duty staff are in one room) → "This worked satisfactorily."
- **Open meetings preceded and enabled family unit therapy.** The all-staff/all-family meetings had been in use "for some months" for staff-family discord before being "adapted to the family unit therapy" after the first complete family was admitted. The discord forum was the precursor; the therapy was retrofitted onto working machinery.
- **Meetings succeeded where staff meetings alone had failed:** "It had not been possible to accomplish this with staff meetings alone."
- **Family members can speak in group before staff can.** "The family members had little problem in being able to speak in the group, and most important, in front of the other members of their families. The staff was more anxious about speaking in the presence of family members but the initial anxiety about this subsided in a short time."
- **The father's self-definition is the precondition for the mother's usability of interpretation.** "Both of these mothers and patients seemed to be in the same helpless bind as the symbiotic pairs and remained so until the father could firmly establish himself as a person in the family. Only then was it really possible for the mother to understand or accept psychotherapeutic interpretations." This makes the father's move a **gate**, not merely a good outcome.
- **Timeline of the one completed sequence:** admission → early months of "much disagreement, high emotion, defensiveness, blaming, and contradiction" → father's declaration of independence at 5 months → mother goes out alone with father within 10 days → 2-month "honeymoon" with daughter's psychosis as "a tremendous temper tantrum designed to win mother back" and the daughter making some gains → at 7 months the well sibling takes on the symptom and the father reverts → 5 more months → father advances again → "a second change in mother."
- **Change is cyclical, not monotonic.** The reversion at seven months is presented as part of the course, not as a failure of it.
- **[p2] The gate opens on a strength comparison, not on the act.** The declaration itself is not what moves the mother; what is reported is that "his strength seemed greater than the mother's attack," and only then does she change position within ten days. The same declaration made while the attack still dominated would, on this account, be one more of the "weak and authoritative" efforts.
- **[p2] The gate is asserted of both families and demonstrated in one.** "Both of these mothers and patients… remained so until the father could firmly establish himself as a person" — while the second father has not yet done so. Read as one completed case plus one case consistent with the claim so far.
- **Selection precondition for admission:** parents "as young and healthy as possible with a potential for major changes within themselves **for their own gains**." The stated capacity being screened for is the parents' self-interested motivation, not their concern for the patient.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Staff incorporation.** "This was the process which constituted the one most difficult operating problem for the staff." Cause: unconscious "combining with." Consequences named: staff turmoil spreading through "a chain of intrastaff relationships"; loss of "objectivity and therapeutic effectiveness"; anxiety reaching "the hospital community or even the outside community"; the family becoming "more free of anxiety"; and, notably, the research phenomenon itself vanishing — "the patterns of the closeness-distance cycles disappeared during these periods."
- **Secrets.** "Relationships can become distant and hostile when there are secrets." The distortion mechanism compounds: one distortion relayed to another across multiple staff "could become complicated."
- **The single-channel structure.** Tried, failed, reported as failed.
- **Staff supplying structure.** Damps the visible cycle without addressing it.
- **Insight without structural change.** Excellent intellectual understanding did not stop the process; only a rule about *where people are standing when they speak* did.
- **The father entering as an agent rather than a self.** He becomes a duplicate of mother and the patient relates to him accordingly — involvement without differentiation is worse than useless because it looks like progress.
- **Father reverting.** Followed immediately by symptom transfer to the well sibling and by mother reproducing the symbiotic relationship with her.
- **Prior treatment failure as the baseline.** Both complete-family patients had four years of intensive treatment — "massive shock and drug therapies" with "no remission" in one, "progressively worse during four years of constant treatment" in the other.
- **[p2] Airtime is finite and the group competes for it.** "Family members press for time to be heard, and have competed in bringing up difficult subjects." An hour a day divided among about eighteen people; the chapter records the competition without treating it as a limit on the method.
- **[p2] Enthusiasm and project-wide calm coexist with no change in the identified patients.** "Both staff and family members were enthusiastic about the meetings"; "There has been a marked decrease in anxiety on the entire project"; and yet the patients "are much quieter and calmer, but they are still regressed and infantile." Satisfaction and anxiety relief are reported on one axis and the target outcome on another, and they do not agree. Bowen does not draw the inference here — he simply reports both — but the divergence is on the page.
- **Bowen's own accounting of results (honest, mixed):** "there have been significant changes in the fathers and mothers **and the staff**. The patients are much quieter and calmer, **but they are still regressed and infantile**." He also flags "questions about the size of the group and the participation of so many people not trained for psychotherapy," and that "the group is difficult to assess as a psychotherapeutic agent."

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family
*(Each rule of technique, converted back into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.)*

1. **Rule: nobody speaks about an issue unless everyone concerned is in the room.**
   → *Assumption: information about a family member, transmitted through any private channel, will be distorted in transit — and the distortion is not random noise but is shaped by the emotional process to serve it.* A message's meaning is a property of the configuration of people present at its utterance, not of its content. This is the strongest technical claim in the chapter and it implies that **relationship topology is the causal variable**, not what anyone says.
2. **Rule: no confidences; anything told in confidence may be raised in the group.**
   → *Assumption: a secret is a structural object, not a private one. Holding one places the holder inside the family's emotional process on a particular side.* Corollary: **secrecy generates distance and hostility mechanically**, independent of the secret's content.
3. **Rule: abolish staff conferences and ward rounds about the family.**
   → *Assumption: the treatment staff is not outside the family system.* A conversation about the family conducted without the family is *itself an event in the family process*. The professional/family boundary is not real.
4. **Rule: staff must not take sides and must be trained to resist incorporation — and this training will not be sufficient.**
   → *Assumption: the pull into the family's positions operates below awareness and on anyone in range, regardless of expertise or intention.* Structural rules are needed precisely *because* individual competence is not a defense. In simulation terms: **capture is a property of proximity, not of the agent's insight parameter.**
5. **Rule: the therapist points out projection every time it occurs, in the group.**
   → *Assumption: projection is (a) observable in real time from outside, (b) invisible from inside while it is happening, and (c) reversible by being witnessed by the whole set of participants simultaneously.* Note the loading on *simultaneous witnessing*: naming it privately would not do the same work.
6. **Rule: name the pattern at three levels — within family, family↔staff, within staff.**
   → *Assumption: the same pattern instantiates at multiple levels of the system, and the staff-level instantiation is a legible copy of the family-level one.* The staff group is a usable readout of the family.
7. **Rule: withdraw individual psychotherapy from the newly admitted family.**
   → *Assumption: individual treatment of a member of a fused unit is not merely insufficient — it participates in the fragmentation.* You cannot get to the unit through a member.
8. **Rule: help the individual define a self and support him when doing so raises anxiety.**
   → *Assumption: differentiating raises anxiety in the differentiating person (and, from A1, in the rest of the family, which attacks it — "particularly attacking the father at any show of strength").* Self-definition is therefore **anxiety-generating before it is anxiety-reducing**, and needs external support across that trough or it reverts.
9. **Rule: work with the father first / expect the father to move first.**
   → *Assumption: the peripheral member is the most movable member.* He is least fused, has the least invested in the status quo, and his move changes the whole configuration. The system's leverage point is not the symptomatic person and not the dominant person, but **the one with the loosest attachment**.
10. **Rule: the target is the father establishing himself as a person, not the father helping with the patient.**
    → *Assumption: taking a position changes the system; helping does not.* Formally: what alters the field is a member becoming a distinct node, not a member adding effort in his existing position. It follows that **effort applied within a position is absorbed by the status quo** — which is precisely what the mother's total devotion to the patient demonstrates.
11. **Rule: wait for the father before expecting the mother to use interpretation.**
    → *Assumption: receptivity to insight is state-dependent and structurally gated.* An interpretation is not a fact delivered to a person; its usability depends on the configuration the person is currently in. Timing dominates content.
12. **Rule: admit families where the process is contained within four people, with in-laws and extra siblings excluded by design.**
    → *Assumption: every additional relative is an additional channel for the process to run through — the system is not bounded at the household unless you bound it deliberately.* Bowen is controlling a variable he already believes is causal.
13. **Rule: select parents with "potential for major changes within themselves for their own gains."**
    → *Assumption: change motivated by concern for the patient reinforces the projection (it is more of the mother's devotion); only self-directed change breaks it.* The therapeutic motive must not be the symptom.
14. **Rule (research phase): supply "maximum neutral emotional support" and "minimal influence on the relationship"; do not supply the structure the pair asks for.**
    → *Assumption: the family's demand for external regulation is itself part of the process — supplying it substitutes an outside regulator for the missing internal one and freezes the arrest.* The pair "never seemed to have the inner strength to control the intensity of the cycles"; giving them that control from outside removes the pressure to develop it.
15. **Rule: start on ward policy and nursing procedures, let it move to personal problems.**
    → *Assumption: emotional process is fully expressed in mundane logistical conflict.* Content is arbitrary; the fight about laundry rules carries the same information as a fight about the illness.
16. **Rule: family members speak in front of their own family — treated as "most important."**
    → *Assumption: what a person will say about a relationship depends on who is present, and the version said in the presence of the other party is the one with therapeutic value.* Consistent with A1's "a family member is different when in relationship to family symbiotic attachments."
17. **Rule: staff should "encourage relationship on neutral subjects."**
    → *Assumption: contact per se is not the problem; contact *about the emotional issue* through the wrong channel is.* Neutral contact is safe and possibly stabilizing.
18. **[p2] Rule: record every meeting four ways at once — tape, process psychotherapy notes, a process sociogram, and a content summary.**
    → *Assumption: the process is not recoverable from any single channel, and in particular **who spoke to whom** is a separate datum from **what was said**.* The sociogram exists because topology is held to be the causal variable (cf. rule 1); the content summary is kept as a fourth, subordinate record. This is the chapter's technique conceding that content alone is insufficient data.
19. **[p2] Rule: the clinicians do the family work inside the group instead of in individual sessions and supervision.**
    → *Assumption (and here it is reported as a result, not only assumed): a clinician's objectivity is a property of the format he is working in, not a stable professional attribute he brings with him.* Pairs with rule 4 — insight does not protect, and structure does — and supplies the positive half: change the structure and the same people become more objective.
20. **[p2] Rule: exclude the symptomatic member from nothing, but treat her removal from the parental pair as the outcome marker.**
    → *Assumption: the patient occupies a structural position between the parents, and what has to change is the position, not her behaviour.* The chapter's stated turning point is a car journey the daughter did not go on.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **The chapter's central revision, stated by Bowen himself:** the original hypothesis held symbiosis to be "a rather fixed and rigid state with a high degree of personality fusion." Observation of daily family living instead showed "the marked mobility, fluidity, and pervasiveness of the process." Bowen names the reason for the error precisely: "the hypothesis having been developed from clinical experience with individuals and the observations came from watching the daily living of the family as a unit." A methodological claim as much as a theoretical one — **the unit of observation determined the shape of the theory.**
- **Symbiosis is extended, not replaced.** "The previous thinking about the symbiosis was not changed except it was now seen as part of a larger, active, shifting unit." Bowen is explicitly conservative here — the dyad theory is nested inside the family theory rather than superseded by it.
- **The problem has no boundary.** "There was a striking lack of definiteness in the boundary of the problem"; the pair "appeared to be more a dependent fragment of a larger family group." This undercuts the entire mother-child framing the project was built on, and Bowen says so.
- **The strong mother is not strong.** The hypothesis's causal agent is "the emotional immaturity of the mother"; the observed mothers are "the active, decision making, dominant family members who assumed roles of strength and adequacy." Bowen reconciles these with the projection formulation — her strength is the reciprocal of the weakness she has placed in the child — but the chapter does not flag the tension explicitly. **[p2] The sharpest instance is a single clause pass 1 passed over:** the soliciting end of the transfer scale "occurred often in the family in which mother was **really more impaired** than the daughter." *Really* is doing work here — Bowen is asserting a fact about the mother that contradicts her displayed position, with no vocabulary for the difference and no comment on having done it.
- **[p2] The gate claim is generalised past its evidence, without a hedge.** "Both of these mothers and patients seemed to be in the same helpless bind… and remained so until the father could firmly establish himself as a person in the family." Three sentences earlier: "The second father is still in this stage." Bowen hedges freely elsewhere in this chapter (see X2) and does not hedge here. The strongest structural claim in the paper is the one carrying the least qualification.
- **Mother-blame is present and simultaneously being dismantled.** The hypothesis assigns initiation to the mother ("the process is initiated by the emotional immaturity of the mother"). The observations repeatedly insist on the child's active participation ("This implies the concept of activity on the part of both"; the patient "actively reached out to accept it"; the patient "actively sought to be called sick"). The unidirectional etiology and the bidirectional mechanism sit side by side, unreconciled.
- **The group's therapeutic status was revised mid-project.** "In the beginning the group was regarded as a preliminary therapeutic method which would probably be followed by individual psychotherapy. The staff now feels that the group may answer the family's total treatment program."
- **Non-influence became impossible.** Design point (3) was to "influence the natural course of psychological events between mother and patient as little as possible by the ward environment," and the ward was planned for "minimal influence on the relationship." By the time family unit therapy is running, the staff is a declared participant whose involvements are a standing agenda item. The observer-neutrality premise did not survive the project.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- **Generalization is explicitly disclaimed:** "The total number of clinical subjects and families is too small to know how the observations can supply [*sic*, apply] to a wider clinical group. The most that can be said at this point is that these are observations made on five families with chronic psychotic family members, and that the observations were made in terms of the specific theoretical orientation presented in the paper." The last clause is a candid admission of theory-laden observation.
- **The dynamics are provisional:** "There has been some tentative initial thinking about the dynamics of this relationship fluidity"; "A tentative dynamic formulation suggested by observations…"
- **Marked as opinion, not finding:** "It is an opinion that the unstructured ward setting was the most important factor in this"; "It is an opinion that the difference between hypothesis and observation resulted from…"; "It is suggested that a family member is different when…"
- **Conceptual vocabulary held at arm's length:** "Family members all have poorly defined ego boundaries, **if that be a satisfactory way to conceptualize it**." "A **kind of** family projection process." "'family ego mass'" in quotation marks. "There was **a quality about this that suggested almost** a quantitative transfer of anxiety."
- **Deferred topics:** "The details of staff involvements with the families are not the subject of this paper." "Clinical impressions of the dynamics of this shift are not the subject of this paper." "This presentation will permit only a brief mention of the therapeutic effort."
- **Open questions named:** "The group is difficult to assess as a psychotherapeutic agent. …There are questions about the size of the group and the participation of so many people not trained for psychotherapy."
- **Results honestly qualified:** the patients "are much quieter and calmer, but they are still regressed and infantile." The changes are in the fathers, mothers, and staff.
- **Closing posture:** "The change to the treatment of the family as a unit is considered to have advantages worth a more detailed exploration." A claim of promise, not of proof.
- **[p2] One assumption is imported into the design and never examined.** "It was assumed that this degree of symbiosis would imply a maximum degree of personality impairment which in turn would also result in a slower therapeutic response." Two linked monotone relations — attachment intensity → impairment, impairment → slower response — stated as a premise for selecting the most extreme cases, and never returned to. It gives Bowen a ready explanation for a poor patient outcome, which is exactly the outcome reported.
- **[p2] No quantity in the chapter is measured against anything.** The chapter has an elaborate recording apparatus and no instrument: no rating, no scale, no rater procedure, no comparison group, no number attached to any person's state. Anxiety, strength and impairment are all handled as observed magnitudes with no units.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The most intense case was selected on purpose, and the reason is methodological:** "A goal was to find the most intense possible clinical examples… it would be easier to first observe the characteristics of the symbiotic relationship in the most exaggerated possible form." And: "Intensity of symbiotic attachment was considered more important than the intensity or configuration of schizophrenic symptoms." The severity of the diagnosis is *not* the selection variable — the intensity of the relationship is. This is already a systems selection criterion applied to an individual-diagnosis population, and it is the seed of the entire later theory (per the Introduction: seeing the intense patterns first made "less intense versions of the same patterns in milder forms of emotional illness, as well as in normal people" visible).
- **Symptom as instrument.** "Just as the emotional calm or tension in an infant can be a most sensitive indicator of the functioning of a mother, so the psychotic patient in daily living contact with mother can be a more sensitive indicator of the functioning of mother than direct observation of mother herself." The symptomatic member is a *readout of another member's state*. This inverts the entire diagnostic frame: to know the mother's anxiety, measure the patient.
- **The mother chooses her own dose.** "The mothers were given a choice of participation which ranged from rooming with the patient to living outside the hospital… **The most impaired mother chose to room with the patient** and the other two chose to live outside." Chosen proximity tracks impairment. An unforced natural experiment Bowen records without dwelling on.
- **Authority is recruited to license the mother's swing in either direction** — an authority "who advocated unlimited giving to children" when her need to give is high, "another authority who advises parents to be firm" when it is low. Expert opinion functions as a post-hoc justification layer for a state determined internally. (Cf. her seeking "medical and other authoritative opinion to support the pronouncement" of the patient's sickness, and using the word *schizophrenia* freely "after it was part of the mother's vocabulary" — diagnosis absorbed as ammunition.)
- **Fusion visible in a reflex:** "when the patient belched while sitting at the dinner table the parent sitting alongside followed with, 'Excuse me.'"
- **The unreachable-separation formulation, in a patient's own words:** "The only way that we could be separated was for me to kill her and if she died, I would die too."
- **The psychosis has a target and a purpose.** Directed at mother "almost point for point, at the old issues between them"; during the honeymoon it was "like a tremendous temper tantrum designed to win mother back to her." Symptoms are read as *relationship moves*, not as noise.
- **The father's declaration was misaddressed.** Delivered to the head nurse "but which seemed for his own mother." The move that changes the current family is aimed at the family of origin — a fully formed multigenerational observation two decades before Bowen made it central (and, per the Introduction, nine years before the 1966 breakthrough).
  - **[p2] Retraction of the wording above.** "A fully formed multigenerational observation" reads later theory into a single clause. What is on the page is one man's decisive statement whose felt object was his mother; there is no transmission claim, no generational mechanism, and the word *generation* does not occur in the chapter. Accurate statement: the chapter records a cross-generational **phenomenon** and has no cross-generational **concept**.
- **[p2] The chapter's cross-generational observations, listed, because scattered they read as less than they are.** Four, all in a paper with no generational vocabulary: (i) Hill (1955) cited for "symbiotic marriages among the fathers and mothers of schizophrenic patients" — the parental generation already paired the same way; (ii) the hypothesis's own "large group in which the child disrupts the tie to mother only to duplicate it in marriage or other relationships throughout life"; (iii) the admission criterion that parents "have lived away from their own immediate families," which controls a variable only if in-laws are believed to be live participants; (iv) the declaration aimed at the father's own mother.
- **[p2] The sex composition was a ward-management decision, and it did not survive year two.** "Women patients were chosen because the ward would adapt to all women better than to all men or to mixed groups." Year 2's second complete family has a catatonic son. Worth recording because the corpus later insists these patterns attach to position rather than sex; ch01's sample is 4 female / 1 male index patients for a stated reason that has nothing to do with theory.
- **[p2] Illness as command.** The mother who went to bed each time her daughter advanced "maintained this **command of the situation** for several months and then relinquished it." Symptom as an occupied position that is held and then given up — not as an outcome that remits.
- **[p2] Competition for the floor.** "Family members press for time to be heard, and have competed in bringing up difficult subjects." Eighteen people, one hour, daily. A scarce resource introduced by the format itself, noted and not pursued.
- **Anxiety is conserved across the whole ward, family and staff together.** Family calm and staff turmoil co-occur. A single anxiety pool with a variable location is the implicit model.
- **Cost of exposure to the process.** For staff it "has the quality of an emotional experience that can be tolerated only for brief periods without re-repression."
- **Searles (1956) is cited as convergent** — "The Effort to Drive the Other Person Crazy" — for the observation that the sick-label is actively imposed. Hill (1955) is cited for "symbiotic marriages among the fathers and mothers of schizophrenic patients," i.e. the parental generation is already known to be symbiotically paired.

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

*Second read of the full chapter with the corpus's pass-1 findings in hand. Ch01 is the volume's
first paper (1957), so the comparative axis runs forward only: there is nothing earlier to compare
against, and the discipline that matters most here is refusing to read the later theory back into it.*

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

1. **The completed father sequence is n = 1, and pass 1 repeated Bowen's own overstatement.** A3 #12
   attributed the gate claim to "the clinical course of two families," and the ledger entry L01.2
   says the fathers changed first "in both complete families" and that mother and patient "remained
   stuck until the father established himself." Both fathers were the first to become interested in
   their own problems; only the first reached a "declaration of independence." Of the other the
   chapter says flatly, "The second father is still in this stage." Every timing in the clinical
   course — 5 months, 10 days, the 2-month honeymoon, the reversion at 7 months, the 5-month cycle,
   the second advance — belongs to one family. C5 survives this; ch01's contribution to it is one
   completed case and one case still in its first phase, not two.

2. **Pass 1 recorded the chapter's separation of real impairment from displayed adequacy and drew
   nothing from it — then asserted the opposite in A5.** "It occurred often in the family in which
   mother was **really more impaired** than the daughter," while that mother held the pronouncing,
   adequate role and the daughter "would work actively to be more irresponsible and helpless than the
   mother." Pass 1 transcribed the passage into A2 and then listed "basic vs. functional self" among
   the things not present. The *terms* are absent; the *distinction* is drawn, once, in plain words.
   Ch09 (1966) supplies the arithmetic; the observation is at the origin.

3. **The change-back reaction is in this chapter, and pass 1 filed it as a latency.** A newly admitted
   patient making firm gains is told "You are weak, go slow" and "Remember the last time you tried
   things too fast, you cracked up." She "showed little concern" — and only then does each further
   step put the mother in bed within a few hours. Pass 1 recorded this as "reverse transfer" with the
   time constant. It is three things it did not name: the corpus's earliest change-back message; a
   **two-rung escalation** in which the somatic rung follows the *failure* of the verbal one; and a
   reaction that **decays unfed** — she "maintained this command of the situation for several months
   and then relinquished it." C9's ladder is dated to Ch13/21/22; its first rung is here.

4. **Pass 1 missed the stated success condition for the differentiating move, which is an inequality
   between two quantities.** "For the first time his strength seemed greater than the mother's attack
   and within ten days she went out alone with him for the first time." Pass 1 kept the ten days and
   dropped the comparison. Efficacy is not a property of the act — the same declaration under a
   heavier attack is one more of the "weak and authoritative" attempts.

5. **The marital-investment seesaw is present, with a concrete observable, and pass 1 did not see it.**
   "Up to this point she insisted that the psychotic daughter accompany them." The turning point is
   marked by a journey the daughter is not on. What follows is the daughter's psychosis "like a
   tremendous temper tantrum designed to win mother back to her" — and, in the same two months, "the
   daughter made some gains." Symptom display and functioning moving in opposite directions in the
   same window, which is C1's shape appearing in 1957.

6. **Pass 1 quoted the sentence twice and missed the mechanism in it.** "The patterns of the
   closeness-distance cycles disappeared during these periods" — the periods when staff turmoil was
   carrying the anxiety and "the family would then be more free of anxiety." Pass 1 read this as the
   research phenomenon becoming hard to see. Bowen says the pattern disappeared. The oscillator is

## P2. What is NEW here

Ch01 is chronologically first, so every concept in it is a first appearance. The useful form of this
question is which of the corpus's load-bearing mechanisms are already present in 1957 and in what
form — and which are genuinely absent, since that is what the timeline rests on.

**Present at the origin.**
- Anxiety as a near-conserved quantity with a location: "almost a quantitative transfer of anxiety,"
  the source's level falling as the recipient's symptom rises. The root of C3.
- Substitutable sinks and a target queue: relatives, staff, social agencies, "the hospital community
  or even the outside community"; and the well sibling taking the vacated position.
- The peripheral member moving first, and his move as a precondition rather than a benefit. Root of C5.
- **Involvement versus position** — the father who enters by siding "seemed to become mother's agent,
  using her words," and is related to as a duplicate of the mother. This is the chapter's most
  original structural distinction and it has no successor term in the ledger.
- Topology beating insight, with both signs (P1 #8). Root of C6.
- Per-hop message distortion: a staff comment relayed "within minutes" in distorted form, "one
  distortion added to another."
- Stress-gated projection: denial and reaction formation under calm, "in periods of stress, projection
  came into use."
- Symptom as a readout of an adjacent member's state, proposed as an instrument.
- The change-back message and its somatic escalation (P1 #3).
- A family-level allocation of the symptom, named: "a kind of family projection process in which the
  family weakness is projected to the patient who resists noneffectively and then accepts it."
- Selection on **self-directed** rather than patient-directed motivation: parents with "potential for
  major changes within themselves **for their own gains**."
- The closeness–distance oscillator with two opposing anxiety terms. First and, on the ledger's
  evidence, last appearance (see P4).
- "Family ego mass" (once, quoted) and "undifferentiated" (once) — both halves of the phrase Ch05
  reports as already discarded, present here and never joined.

**Absent, verified by search of the chapter body with the volume's front matter excluded.**
- *triangle / triangling / detriangling*: zero. The file's two hits are in the 1977 Introduction.
  What ch01 *does* have is an excluded third (the father "passively permits himself to be excluded
  from the intense twosome"), siding as the mode of entry, and overflow to a fourth party. What it
  lacks is any rotation of positions, any preference for the outside position, and the term.
  One further contrast worth stating precisely because it is a *difference*, not an absence: here the
  third party is recruited **when the closeness fragments**, in the distance phase; Ch09 recruits a
  third **when tension exceeds a threshold**. Different trigger conditions, both stated explicitly.
- Differentiation as a level, scale or quantity: zero. The root occurs four times and is always a verb
  or adjective. No person is ever assigned a number.
- *multigenerational*, *generation(s)*: zero in the body.
- *cutoff / cut-off*: zero in the entire file.
- Sibling position, nuclear family emotional system, societal emotional process, chronic anxiety: zero.
- "Scale" occurs three times and always denotes the transfer-activity scale — who is pushing the sick
  label onto whom. The first "scale" in the corpus measures a transaction, not a person.

## P3. What CHANGED from earlier chapters

There is no earlier chapter. Two deltas can still be recorded honestly.

**(a) Internal to the chapter — the revision Bowen states himself.** Hypothesis: fixed, rigid, high
personality fusion; initiated by the mother; bounded at the mother–child dyad; the ward to exert
"minimal influence on the relationship." Observation: "marked mobility, fluidity, and pervasiveness";
"activity on the part of both"; "a striking lack of definiteness in the boundary of the problem"; and
a staff whose involvements become a standing agenda item. Bowen names the cause of the error and it is
methodological — the hypothesis came "from clinical experience with individuals," the observations
"from watching the daily living of the family as a unit."

**(b) The origin end of deltas the rest of the corpus completes**, stated as ch01's position, not as a
claim about later chapters. The peripheral position here is a pure deficit — the fathers "did not seem
to have an identity of their own"; nothing in the chapter treats being outside as an advantage. The
transfer scale is about who initiates the sick-label transaction, not about who ends up strong. The
patient's symptom is a location for the family's weakness, and its relief is the stated goal — the
chapter does not yet contain the idea that relieving it could cost anything.

## P4. What was DROPPED

Nothing can be dropped by the first paper. What can be recorded is what ch01 makes central that the
rest of the corpus stops using — visible only from the ledger, and a fact about the corpus rather than
about this chapter.

- **The closeness–distance cycle, incorporation anxiety and separation anxiety.** The chapter's central
  dynamic, with its own time constants (two cycles in a day to several months). No later ledger entry
  uses any of the three. The oscillator does not survive; the reciprocity seesaw takes its place.
- **Symbiosis as developmental arrest.** Still the master concept here; by Ch05 the ego-mass language is
  reported as already discarded and differentiation-as-process has replaced it.
- **Ego boundaries** — hedged even on first use ("if that be a satisfactory way to conceptualize it").
- **Local coinages:** "transfer anxiety," "combining with," "spray," "borrowed convictions,"
  "declaration of independence," "the honeymoon." None recur in the ledger.
- **The setting itself.** A daily hour with about eighteen people, half of them staff "not trained for
  psychotherapy," is the machinery this chapter invented. Ch10 separates family therapy from family
  group therapy, and Ch11 engineers deliberately *against* group process between families. What ch01
  built is later treated as the thing to prevent.
- **Dropped inside the chapter itself:** design point (3), "influence the natural course of
  psychological events between mother and patient as little as possible." It does not survive to the
  end of the paper.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Closeness-distance cycle with two opposing anxiety terms (incorporation ↑ with closeness, separation ↑ with distance) → `Relationship` → distance is a dynamic state variable with no stable equilibrium; needs an oscillator, not a set point. Period is highly variable (2 cycles/day to months) and should be driven by intensity, not fixed.
- Pair "never seemed to have the inner strength to control the intensity of the cycles" → `Relationship` / `Person` → an internal damping/regulation capacity that can be near zero; when zero, amplitude is bounded only externally.
- Externally supplied structure damps the visible cycle without changing the underlying state → `move:PROVIDE_STRUCTURE` → `coach` → a coach action with a *symptom-suppressing but non-differentiating* effect. Must be modelled as distinct from a differentiating move, or the simulation will reward the wrong thing.
- Transfer of anxiety, "almost quantitative," with the source's anxiety measurably falling → `Family` / `Relationship` → argues for a **conserved or near-conserved anxiety quantity with a location**, transferred along relationship edges, rather than independent per-person anxiety levels.
- Transfer latency varies by target (mother→patient "very soon"; mother→son "within hours"; patient→mother "within a few hours") → `event-kind` → transfer should be an event with a per-edge latency, not an instantaneous recomputation.
- Symptom intensity in the patient is a function of the mother's anxiety, not of the patient's own state ("timing… related to the mother's own functioning rather than to the reality of the patient's functioning") → `Person` → symptom expression must be readable as a function of an *adjacent* node's state.
- Reverse transfer: patient's autonomous gains → mother's physical illness within hours, sustained several months → `Relationship` → progress by one member is a stressor on the other. Any "improvement" must propagate as a perturbation, not only as a good.
- Activity scale of transfer (forcing / mutual / soliciting) → `Relationship` → the sick-label transaction needs a **bidirectional push-pull parameter**, since Bowen's middle case (both active) is the modal one and the endpoints are the same mechanism at different settings.
- "Integrated enough to fight back" gates whether the label is resisted or accepted → `Person` → a threshold on the receiving person's functioning below which the projection is absorbed without resistance.
- Status-quo maintenance: when the daughter's functioning exceeded the mother's, both acted to reverse it → `Relationship` → a restoring force on *relative* functioning within a pair, triggered by crossover. Suggests reciprocity should be modelled on the difference, not on absolute levels.
- Mother's defense ladder — denial/reaction formation under calm, projection under stress → `Person` → **projection should be stress-gated**, switching on above an anxiety threshold rather than running continuously.
- "To feel helpless is to be helpless" (no feeling/reality discrimination) → `Person` → a discrimination parameter that determines whether a projected attribution converts into an actual functioning decrement. This is the coupling constant between projection and symptom.
- Family projection process: family weakness → patient, who "resists noneffectively and then accepts it" → `Family` → symptom-bearer selection is a family-level allocation, with an ineffective-resistance phase before acceptance.
- Symptom migrates to the next available child when the primary is unavailable (mother separates from hospitalized daughter → son symptomatic within hours; well daughter takes the position at 7 months) → `Family` → the projection has a **target queue**; removing the current target does not remove the projection.
- "Spray" — at high patient anxiety, symptoms disperse to everyone *except* mother → `Relationship` → symptom direction is target-selective and inverts under load, preserving the primary attachment. Currently `unmodelled` in any simple contagion scheme.
- Father's three states (peripheral / entered-as-agent / entered-as-self) with different system effects → `Person` + `move:TAKE_POSITION` → the critical distinction is **involvement vs. position**; entering as agent duplicates the existing node rather than adding one. A model with a single "engagement" scalar cannot represent this.
- Father moves first, in both families, and even a weak first move calms the family → `move:TAKE_POSITION` → the peripheral/least-fused member should be the highest-leverage actor, and move *quality* should be decoupled from move *effect* on system anxiety.
- "Remained so until the father could firmly establish himself… Only then was it really possible for the mother to… accept psychotherapeutic interpretations" → `coach` → interpretation-receptivity is **structurally gated**. A coach agent must have moves that are unavailable/ineffective until a precondition holds. Highest-value single mechanic in the chapter.
- The mother attacks the father "at any show of strength" → `Relationship` → a differentiating move triggers active counter-pressure from the most-invested member. Differentiation must cost before it pays.
- Father reverts at 7 months → symptom transfers to well sibling → 5 months → father advances again → second change in mother → `test` → a regression fixture: an oscillating, non-monotonic course with symptom migration on the downswing. Any model that only produces monotonic improvement fails this.
- Staff/outside figures recruited into symbiosis-shaped relationships, with the family's anxiety falling as staff turmoil rises → `Triangle` (in embryo) / `Family` → the system boundary is permeable; **the anxiety sink can be outside the family**. Note: Bowen has no triangle concept here; the phenomenon is described without it, so the model should not smuggle triangles in at this chapter.
- Distortion compounds across private relay channels ("within minutes… one distortion added to another") → `Relationship` / `event-kind` → message fidelity degrades per private hop; needs a channel-topology term.
- All-present rule works where single-channel rule fails → `move:CONVENE` / `coach` → a coach move that changes *who is present*, with efficacy dependent on completeness of attendance, not on the coach's skill. Suggests the effective therapeutic variable is topology.
- Confidentiality-abolition rule ("Take it up in the group"; any confidence may be raised) → `move:DECLINE_PRIVATE_CHANNEL` → `coach` → `unmodelled` if the model has no representation of secrets. Secrets as edges that create distance/hostility independent of content would be a compact addition.
- Staff insight does not prevent staff capture → `coach` → **the coach must be capturable**, and insight must not be a defense parameter. A coach agent immune to the family's pull would be unfaithful to this chapter.
- Discussion of reality problems leads to discussion of personal problems → `coach` → mundane logistical conflict is a valid and possibly necessary entry point; content is interchangeable with emotional content.
- Anxiety of self-definition, with explicit "ego supportive moves" for the person differentiating → `move:SUPPORT_DIFFERENTIATOR` → `coach` → self-definition raises the actor's own anxiety before lowering the system's; support across that trough prevents reversion.
- Selection for parents with potential to change "for their own gains" → `Person` → motivation-source as an attribute: self-directed vs. patient-directed. Patient-directed effort should feed the projection rather than reduce it.
- Symbiosis modelled as *developmental arrest* on a continuum from intense-and-cohabiting to "mildest nonclinical," including reproduction of the tie in marriage → `Relationship` → a fusion intensity parameter that is heritable across the marriage boundary, without the later differentiation scale. `unmodelled` if fusion is currently only a family-level constant.
- "A dependent fragment of a larger family group… lack[ing] within itself the strength to differentiate into two autonomous people" → `Family` → a dyad's capacity to differentiate depends on resources outside the dyad. Argues against a purely pairwise model.
- Design constraint "not more than two children… contained within four people without involving inlaws or multiple siblings" → `Family` → each additional relative is an additional channel; system size should raise available paths for transfer.
- Father's declaration "delivered to the head nurse but which seemed for his own mother" → `unmodelled` → a move in the current family whose real object is the family of origin. No multigenerational structure exists in this chapter's theory, but the phenomenon is already recorded; flag for the ch14/ch22 comparative pass.
- Patient as a more sensitive indicator of the mother than the mother is → `test` → an observability assertion: the model should permit inferring node A's anxiety from node B's symptom level. Useful as a validation check, not a mechanism.
- Emotional exposure tolerable "only for brief periods without re-repression" → `coach` → an observer/coach fatigue or re-repression term. `unmodelled`.
- Outcome asymmetry: significant change in fathers, mothers, and staff; patients "still regressed and infantile" after 14 months → `test` → calibration target. Peripheral and parental nodes should respond well before the symptom-bearer does, and 14 months of family-unit work should not resolve a 4-year chronic regression.
