# KS06 — Emotional Regression

**Citation rule:** cite `KS06`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

---

## ⭐⭐ KS06.1 — Regression defined as a **shift to evolutionarily older behaviour** `[T]` `[M]`

> "**If chronic anxiety escalates in a relationship system, the system becomes dominated by less thoughtful and more reactive ways of interacting that are older in an evolutionary sense than the advanced complex behaviors of a well-functioning relationship system.**"

The worked example gives the ordering:

> "Two people are working **cooperatively**… As [anxiety] builds, one person may anxiously try to **control** the other, and the other may anxiously **defer**… **Cooperation is a complex behavior… that requires the intellectual and emotional systems to function as a working team. Dominant-adaptive (deferential) interactions are older evolutionarily, more primitive.** … the pressure of heightened anxiety drives system functioning **toward less complex behaviors.**"

*Model consequence:* **the model's move repertoire acquires a complexity ordering it does not have**, and rising anxiety **MUST** move selection down it. `M4.D.3` currently says only that rising anxiety raises the weight on the seven reactive moves — a binary split. This says the ordering is **graded**: cooperation at the top, then conflict, then dominant-adaptive, with `DISTANCE` oldest of all (`KS02.3`, "single-celled organisms could not survive without a distancing mechanism").

That gives `M4.D.2`'s propensity vector a principled shape rather than nine independent weights, and it is a **derivation** in the sense `M10.A.2` prefers.

## ⭐ KS06.2 — The two life forces, itemised `[T]`

> "**Individuality manifests in autonomy for self, goal-directed behaviors, productivity, and being governed by principle versus feelings of the moment.**"

> "**Togetherness manifests in pressuring for oneness, sameness, and agreement; seeking love, approval, closeness, and assigning positive value to thinking about the other before self; and holding others responsible for one's own happiness and/or holding self responsible for the happiness of others.**"

> "**Bowen theory does not assume that one life force is better or more important than the other.** Good basic levels of differentiation allow people to **choose** to act based on individuality or on togetherness **depending on the realities of the existing life situation.**"

*Model consequence:* two closed lists, directly implementable as move-classification criteria, and a **neutrality requirement**: neither force is the good one, and high differentiation is the capacity to *select between them by circumstance*. That last point matters for `M4.D.1a` — the self-directed channel is **not** "the individuality channel". It is the channel that can choose either.

Note the final togetherness item is **two-sided again**: holding others responsible for one's happiness **and** holding self responsible for others'.

## ⭐ KS06.3 — Anxiety's fundamental triggers are **threats in both directions** `[T]`

> "**Threats to adequate emotional contact (such as rejection) and to sufficient emotional distance (such as excessive expectations)** between people in an emotionally significant relationship system **appear to be the fundamental triggers of anxiety** that drives emotional regressions and ultimately fuel symptom development."

> "This interplay… may be related evolutionarily to the **approach-avoid** responses that are fundamental to all living organisms."

*Model consequence:* **`M4.C.1` should be re-derived from this.** Anxiety rises not on event *intensity* but on **deviation from a tie's contact optimum in either direction** — rejection on one side, excessive expectation on the other. That is `KS03.2`'s closeness dilemma stated as the appraisal rule, and it unifies `KS01.7`'s four cues: *approval* and *attention* are contact-side, *expectations* and *distress* are distance-side.

This is the sixth appearance of the two-sided structure and the most load-bearing one yet — it is now the **appraisal function itself**, not a readout.

## ⭐ KS06.4 — Conflicting urges within one person, on both sides of a dyad `[T]` `[M]`

> "one person might experience a **heightened sense of responsibility for the other person's distress**, which inclines the person to **move toward**… However, this may be coupled with a compelling urge to **avoid getting entangled in the other person's expectations**, which inclines the person to **withdraw.** The other person experiences conflicting urges as well, such as **wanting the person to soothe** the other's distress… which inclines the other to **move toward**… coupled with **guilt about acting helpless and expecting too much**, which inclines the other to **withdraw. The conflicting urges raise each person's anxiety**, which further infects their interactions."

*Model consequence:* **the ambivalence is intra-agent and simultaneous, and it is itself a source of anxiety.** `M4.D.1` has each agent draw exactly one move by softmax, which discards the conflict rather than representing it. This says the *unresolved competition between candidate moves* raises anxiety independently of whichever move is emitted.

That is implementable cheaply — an entropy or margin term over the propensity distribution feeding back into `acute_anxiety` — and it produces the observed vicious circle for free. **Candidate requirement, and a genuinely new mechanism for the model.**

## ⭐ KS06.5 — Anxiety raises **togetherness** by default `[T]` `[D]`

> "**As anxiety rises in an anxious emotional field, automatic intensification of the togetherness force is the default mode.** This makes it more difficult for people to balance contact and distance."

> "**If anxiety goes up, fusion increases. This represents a functional shift in individuality-togetherness balance, not a basic change.**"

> "**The less resistance a relationship has to being disturbed by anxiety, the greater the fluctuations in functional level tend to be.**"

*Model consequence:* a directional law the spec lacks — anxiety's **first** effect is a togetherness increase, and the regression follows from that rather than from anxiety directly. And the second quote is `S2.9`'s decomposition applied at the *relationship* level: relationships have a **basic** individuality-togetherness balance and a **functional** one, with anxiety moving only the functional one. "**Unlike functional shifts in relationship balance, anxiety does not affect basic relationship balance.**"

So the basic/functional split is **not** a property of persons only. `M1.B` (`Relationship`) needs the same two-level structure `M1.A` has.

## ⭐ KS06.6 — Fusion is **one process at two levels**, and they reinforce each other `[T]` `[M]`

> "**The degree of fusion in relationships parallels the degree of vulnerability to fusion of the intellectual and emotional systems in the two people.** Increased fusion in a relationship means the togetherness force is having more clout than the individuality force. **Increased fusion of the intellectual and feelings systems means that anxiety-driven feeling states are overwhelming the thinking system.**"

> "**Fusion is an apt term to describe both processes because functional separateness erodes in both cases.**"

> "**The reciprocal relationship togetherness-driven fusion process reinforces the reciprocal brain fusion process and vice versa.**"

*Model consequence:* **this is the coupling that makes `M4.D.1a`'s mixing weight a relational quantity, not only a personal one.** Intra-agent fusion (feeling overriding intellect) and inter-agent fusion (togetherness overriding individuality) are the *same variable at two scales*, and they drive each other. So the self-directed channel's weight depends on the state of the **tie the agent is acting on**, not only on the agent — which the spec does not currently say.

Kerr also resolves the terminology: "**Differentiation and individuality are attempting to conceptualize the same idea but in different contexts: one within the individual and the other within the relationship system.**"

## ⭐ KS06.7 — Work versus family, worked at length: the Madisons `[T]` `[D]`

James Madison, "Father of the Constitution", held his position against enormous pressure after the burning of Washington in 1814 — "**It would be dishonorable to send any deputation, and… we will defend the city to the very last.**" Kerr: "**If that is not acting on principle and defining a 'self' in face of tremendous pressure from the social/political system, I do not know what is!**"

> "**However, in their nuclear family system it was an entirely different story.**"

The son, Payne Todd: irresponsible, a serious drinking problem, debtor's prison twice, drained the family's money, and the debt was a major reason Dolley eventually had to sell Montpelier and "**lived out her life in poverty.**"

> "**James's functional level of differentiation in the triangle with Dolley and Payne seems to have been low.** Dolley was tremendously overinvolved emotionally with Payne, and she appears to have followed the pattern of '**I love you no matter what you do.**' **The permissiveness in this pattern typically fosters sociopathic traits in an offspring.** James seems to have completely supported Dolley's focus on Payne, perhaps thinking that she was just being a good mother and possibly also wanting to avoid disharmony."

Kerr names two other cases: "**Winston Churchill and Franklin Roosevelt also had similar well-publicized significant discrepancies in personal and public life functioning. I find this context-dependent contrast in emotional functioning a challenge to understand.**"

*Model consequence:* the strongest available demonstration that **`functional_level` is per-context** while `basic_level` is not (`KS00.7`), worked over a whole life course with an outcome. Also two specific claims:

- **Unconditional permissiveness → sociopathic traits.** A named parent-side input with a named offspring outcome; corroborates `KB10` · K10.7 ("permissiveness can be a breeder of violence") and `L18.4`.
- The oscillation: "**Such parents typically threaten harsh punishments periodically in desperate attempt to effect change in the child but routinely flip back to longer periods of permissiveness.**" — an *unstable* parental policy, not a lenient one. `M11.C` candidate: a **high-variance** parental response arm **MUST** produce worse offspring outcomes than a consistently lenient one at equal mean.

The Madison sequence is a third scenario fixture, with a nodal-event origin: Dolley's first husband and second son died of yellow fever on the same day when Payne was twenty months old.

## ⭐ KS06.8 — Personal life is where basic level is assessed; occupational success is **not** a measure `[T]`

> "**In terms of solid self, one's personal life is where the rubber meets the road.**"

> "how people manage themselves **in the intimate relationship with their spouses, and not how they manage themselves in their work lives, is the primary determinant of the basic levels of differentiation of their offspring.**"

> "**a good level of differentiation of self predicts that individuals will likely do well in whatever career they pursue but excelling in their occupation is not a reliable measure of their basic level of differentiation.**"

And a colleague's stronger form: James E. Jones "**to conclude that basic level of differentiation of self has little to do with occupational success.**"

*Model consequence:* **a weighting rule for `M1.A.4a`'s estimator, and it partially corrects `KS05.10`.** Ch 5 lists four assessment domains as if comparable; Ch 6 says the **intimate-relationship domain is the discriminating one** and occupational success is nearly uninformative. The estimator **MUST** weight contexts by emotional intensity — which is `M1.A.4b`'s load-weighting requirement, now with a named ordering of contexts rather than an abstract load term.

The middle quote is also a **transmission constraint**: only the *marital* functioning determines offspring basic level. `M7.E` should read the parents' functional level **on the marital tie**, not their global average.

## ⭐ KS06.9 — The child stabilises the triangle until symptoms appear `[T]`

> "part of the dynamic is **the mother meeting needs for emotional closeness with their son. It is not just about worry and protectiveness.** It is also about a special closeness that **renders her vulnerable to giving in to the child.** Another process present… is an **undercurrent of tension and distance in the marital relationship. The son functions to stabilize the parental triangle, at least up to the point that symptoms appear and the regression deepens.**"

And the outsider's dividend:

> "**As the outsider in the triangle, but with a fundamentally supportive connection to Dolley, James was likely freed to be all he could be in his political life.**"

*Model consequence:* two things `M1.C` does not represent.

1. **Child focus has a closeness-reward component**, not only an anxiety-displacement one. The mother's move is reinforced by met attachment need, which is why it is stable. Two reinforcement sources, matching `KS04.7`'s loop.
2. **The outside position can be a net benefit** when the triangle is stable and the outsider retains a supportive tie. `KS03.1` has the outsider absorbing anxiety; this says that at *low* triangle tension the outside position **frees life energy** (`M1.A.10`) for goal-directed activity. So the outsider's payoff is **non-monotone in triangle tension** — a real refinement.

## KS06.10 — Culture is content; differentiation is how the content is held `[T]`

> "**Culture might be best thought of as the content of a society's beliefs, principles, and values. Differentiation is about how people acquire those ideas, whether thoughtfully or passively, and their flexibility in living them (solid self).**"

> "principles such as duty, respect for elders, service to the group, and negation of self's desires would play out very differently in a poorly differentiated family system compared to a better differentiated one. In the former, **the principles may be regarded as dogma and adhered to rigidly (pseudo-self)**; in the latter, **the principles are open for thoughtful discussion about whether they apply in all circumstances (solid self).**"

> "**Both a culture of individualism and one of sociocentrism have their more and less differentiated members.**"

*Model consequence:* the **cleanest available separation of `M9` (beliefs) from `basic_level`** — the model **MUST NOT** encode any belief content as more or less differentiated. What varies is *how held*: rigid/dogmatic versus open to revision. That is exactly `KS05.7`'s two-compartment belief structure, and it makes the pseudo/solid distinction a property of the **holding**, not of the proposition.

Also a `M11.F` requirement: no readout may present a culture or value system as more or less differentiated.

Kerr distinguishes **individuality** from **individualism** explicitly and records Bowen's advice against naming a chapter "Individuality and Togetherness" because "**people will think it is a philosophy.**"

## KS06.11 — The I-position example, and "it does not help to tell others to calm down" `[T]`

> "One system member being calmer and more thoughtful is helpful, but **often that is not enough** for moderating the system's anxiety spiral. **It may also require an I-position**, such as '**I am no longer willing to believe and act as if our son's behavior is the cause of this family's anxiety. We all have a part in this.**'"

> "**This person is not telling others what they should do; just defining what the person is going to do.** Emotions such as **confidence, courage, and interest** accompany effective I-positions and communicate a firmness that others may react to strongly, but well-thought-out positions are ultimately calming."

> "**It does not help to tell others to calm down; the key is to live it by calming oneself.**"

> "One phrase to describe this ability… is being **in contact with, but outside of, an emotional system.**"

*Model consequence:* `M5.D`'s `I-POSITION` gets its **content constraint**: a move that states what the actor will do, never what others should do. A move addressed to another's behaviour is by definition not an I-position — which is a **structural**, checkable discriminator to sit alongside `M5.F.4`'s manner-based one.

And the first quote is a required **ordering**: calm alone is insufficient; the position is a separate and additional act. Confirms that `M11.C.17`'s no-drift result cannot be evaded by simply lowering anxiety.

Note the accompanying emotional states named as *positive* — confidence, courage, interest. `M5.F.5`'s objective is held through discomfort but is **not** affectively neutral.

## KS06.12 — Regression is a natural-systems phenomenon: the paper wasps `[T]`

Gadagkar & Joshi (1985) on an Old World paper wasp: a colony declined over two months; five workers left overnight and founded a new nest nearby; **both** colonies then thrived. The researchers found the colony had **split into two subgroups long before the fission**, each associating primarily with its own members, coordinating activities, and **actively avoiding** the other, with one subgroup attacking the other on encounter.

> "**the high rate of aggression… reduced the efficiency of brood rearing before fission and the low rate of aggression after fission… permitted efficient brood rearing.**"

> "**The fission permitted a decrease in tension and a consequent rise in functional level for both groups, a reversal of the regression.**"

*Model consequence:* a **non-human, independently published** instance of the regression/subgroup/fission sequence — the strongest external corroboration in the book so far, and a useful one because no cultural or psychological explanation is available for wasps. Record in `_EXTERNAL_MEASURES.md`.

Note the model-relevant shape: **the subgroup structure precedes and predicts the aggression**, and **fission raises functional level on both sides**. That last is a genuinely testable prediction about `CUTOFF` at group scale that runs *against* the usual reading of cutoff as costly — cutoff between **subgroups** relieved both. Reconcile with `M11.C.4` in pass 2; it may be that group-level fission and individual-level cutoff are different operations.

## KS06.13 — Tribalism has subcortical roots; beliefs are downstream `[T]`

Quoting E. O. Wilson (2014): "**people prefer to be with others who look like them, speak the same dialect, and hold the same beliefs. An amplification of this evidently inborn predisposition leads with frightening ease to racism and religious bigotry.**"

> "**Understanding tribalism as having subcortical roots rather than blaming it on, for example, shared common beliefs is a useful perspective. Common beliefs are part of the process but do not cause it.**"

*Model consequence:* second statement of `KS03.6` — belief supports but does not cause polarisation — now with an evolutionary account. `M9` stays downstream. Also supports treating **similarity-seeking** as a primitive drive rather than a derived preference, which bears on `M1.B`'s tie formation.

## KS06.14 — Alcoholism as symptom, not cause; and the "usual suspects" are secondary `[T]`

> "Payne Todd's alcoholism certainly complicated his life course, but it is an **inadequate explanation.** Bowen theory considers it **as much a symptom of a dysfunctional life course as its cause.**"

> "when it comes to factors that adversely affect psychological development, factors such as **child abuse, trauma, and neglect** are usually considered. **Situations like the Madison family show that those three usual suspects are not necessarily present.** Furthermore, **child abuse, trauma, and neglect are typically secondary manifestations of a family's basic level of differentiation.**"

*Model consequence:* confirms the mapping of substance use to a **symptom channel** rather than a move (noted earlier against §G2's four examples), and states that abuse/trauma/neglect are **downstream of differentiation** rather than independent inputs. That is a strong architectural claim: the model needs no exogenous trauma input — the same claim as `KS02.4`'s "the past does not cause the problem in the present", now with the usual candidate causes named and dismissed.

Kerr is honest about the research gap: "**The concept of differentiation and its relationship to chronic anxiety are not included in research studies. Granted, it is currently a difficult variable to assess.**"

## KS06.15 — Reining in feelings is not suppression — the mechanism `[T]`

> "**Reigning in feeling states generated by increased togetherness pressure does not mean suppressing the feelings. The feelings subside based on having an objective perspective on the reciprocal relationship process at work, which can make the interaction less threatening.** The objective system knowledge is available to process information about the interaction, and that renders the cause-and-effect driven feelings and subjectivity less overwhelming."

> "This counterbalancing… can also result from **adhering to a solid self-based principle, such as understanding that jumping in to help can undermine another person's capabilities.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS05.13`'s guard given its mechanism, and it is the same one as the chimpanzee/symbol experiment in the Introduction: **reappraisal reduces perceived threat**, which reduces anxiety, rather than anxiety being suppressed downstream. So `systems_perspective` acts at **appraisal** (`M4.C`), not at selection — it lowers the anxiety an event generates rather than resisting the move the anxiety produces. That is a cleaner and more testable placement than the spec currently implies, and it makes `M4.C.4` bidirectional: anxiety lowers `systems_perspective`, and `systems_perspective` lowers the anxiety each event produces.

Two named routes are given for the counterbalance — **objective perspective on the process** (mindware, `KS05.9`) and **adherence to a solid-self principle**. These correspond exactly to the *seeing* and *aiming* capacities of §G7's table.
