# KB15 — A New Concept of the Midbrain

**Citation rule:** cite `KB15`. ASR source — no verbatim quotation.

**Read this first.** Despite the coverage map showing 127 biology hits, **this interview contributes almost nothing the model can implement.** It is Bowen's own neuroanatomical proposal — that the midbrain is best understood as a functional extension of the spinal cord — and he presents it as speculation throughout: "that is a postulation", "I tried to look that up and I couldn't find it", "I don't know how one gets into this". It is graded `[I]` almost entirely, and recording that honestly matters more than mining it.

---

## K15.1 — The automatic / conscious division, with a locus `[M]`

The midbrain "presides over the **automatic functions** of the whole body"; the cerebrum has to do with "**conscious control** of self to one's environment". Some automatic functions are consciously overridable within limits — breathing, blinking — "there are certain functions you can control yourself."

*Model consequence:* this is the nearest thing in any source to a biological grounding for the model's two regimes — automatic reactivity versus deliberate, differentiated action. It supports the *shape* of `M4.D` (a policy that is mostly automatic with a narrow band of controllable response) and it supports nothing quantitative. **It must not be cited as evidence for any parameter.**

## K15.2 — Biofeedback is what made him think in terms of function `[T]`

> "thinking about function had to **wait on the family center becoming involved in biofeedback**, and then I got thinking about function."

Biofeedback works by exposing a measurable automatic variable — finger temperature is his example — which the cerebrum can then influence.

*Model consequence:* supplies the mechanism behind `KB04` · K04.16, where self-regulation (biofeedback, meditation, yoga, jogging) is a route to lowering anxiety **that does not run through the relationship system**. The mechanism is: make an automatic variable observable, and conscious control becomes partially possible. The model has no representation of this channel at all.

## K15.3 — Function over structure, as a method `[T]`

> "the more we can all think in terms of **function** with everything, I think we'll be farther along… if we can think of the function rather than the structure, maybe it'll be helpful to all of us."

Applied by Kerr to cancer, and accepted with a caveat: "I don't suppose you can think of cancer as anything but malignant."

*Model consequence:* a methodological preference consistent with `KB10` · K10.9 (measure the how, what, when and where; tune out the why) and with the parsimony rule. Not implementable; worth recording because the project has repeatedly had to correct its reading of Bowen's methodological stance.

## K15.4 — Paul MacLean, and where Bowen defers `[T]`

MacLean's triune brain is discussed, with Bowen deferring on the taxonomy — "I think Paul McLean has put in limbic system instead of midbrain… I don't know that that makes any difference, because that's maybe just a structural distinction."

*Model consequence:* none directly. It closes the thread opened in `KB12`, where MacLean is named as the person Bowen wanted to take the feeling-versus-intellect question to. **The question he wanted answered — how the feeling world came to dominate the intellectual — is not answered here or anywhere in the material reviewed.**

## Why this interview is thin, recorded so a later pass does not re-mine it

The 127 term hits are anatomical vocabulary, not mechanism. There is no claim here about differentiation, anxiety, triangles, symptom routing or family process that is not stated better elsewhere. The interview's value to this project is **negative evidence about the biological grounding**: Bowen wanted it (see `_KERR_INTERVIEWS.md`), pursued it personally for fifty years, and at the end had a structural conjecture he could not source and an open question he never answered.

That is worth knowing before anyone proposes a biologically-grounded parameter on his authority.
