# KB08 — Family Therapy with Schizophrenia

**Citation rule:** cite `KB08`. ASR source — no verbatim quotation.

---

## ⚠⚠ K08.1 — Intervening in a saturated system kills people, with three cases

Three named outcomes of forcing conjoint sessions on families already at capacity:

- a resident who brought a family together after hearing a lecture — "the patient became **acutely psychotic**", and the resident concluded family therapy was no good;
- a young therapist running on technique who required the family to meet — "the young woman who was a real disturbed, psychotic person, **took scissors and put out her eyes** as a response to this";
- a research protocol requiring conjoint meetings with "a real uptight family" — Bowen warned them in advance, "**I do not think this family can stand what you're going to get into**"; it "lasted two weeks before the patient… **got a pass to go out of the hospital and went and killed herself**."

The mechanism is stated plainly: "the **same process works in the therapy that works at home** — when the anxiety goes up at home, the parents focus on the patient, which makes the patient worse, and you bring them into a therapy session, and **they do the same thing**."

*Model consequence:* `KB04` · K04.13 said an overloaded system loses its weakest link. **KB08 is that finding with an intervention as the trigger and three outcomes.** It makes the family-type gate (`KB05` · K05.1, `KB06` · K06.4) a safety property rather than an efficiency one, and it is the strongest argument in the corpus that **a coach acting on a saturated system is not neutral**. `M11.C.11` and the coach's cost model both need it.

## ⚠ K08.2 — Schizophrenia is defined by treatability, not by symptoms `[T]`

> "schizophrenia would be that group of people for whom **this would not be possible**" — where *this* is anyone in the family differentiating. He ran "a **test of treatability, a test of differentiation**, to see if somebody had the ability to be a self in spite of the objections of the others, and **if one person had that ability, I'd stick with them**" and expected a good result. Families with no such member he called schizophrenia; the rest, "functional psychoses" — "**there I have been now for 20 years plus.**"

He notes the profession diagnoses the whole range as schizophrenia and that his usage is "confusing to the profession."

*Model consequence:* **a family-level capacity variable the model does not have.** Severity here is not a property of the symptom-bearer; it is *whether any member retains the capacity to differentiate*. That is computable in the model from per-agent state, it predicts treatability rather than describing symptoms, and it reframes `K12.10`'s scope limit — schizophrenia does not resist differentiation because of what it is, it is *named* for the absence of the capacity.

## K08.3 — Content to process, and the theory's actual focus `[T]`

> "I moved very quickly **from content to process**, which I still do follow… it is not the content of the conflict or whatever, but it is the **process through which these things are handled**."

*Model consequence:* with `KB02` · K02.2 (the theory is built on actions, not on what was said), this is the accurate and well-sourced replacement for the withdrawn "content is noise" argument (`L20.3`). The claim is a **focus** claim about where the signal is, not a claim that content is empty.

## K08.4 — Total openness is de-selfing; a self requires reserve `[T]`

He recommends, as an exercise, a couple communicating anything either thinks about self or the other — "not a bad model… it is workable." But:

> "almost like **to maintain a self, there has to be some self that self does not communicate to the other**."

And in the family context: full mutual disclosure "is a **de-selfing kind of thing**… you are de-selfing one person and sort of contributing to the group self."

*Model consequence:* a **floor** on `REDUCE_CUTOFF` and on the "open relationship with every living relative" goal (`KB05` · K05.5) that the model does not have. Openness is not monotonically good; past a point it converts self into group self. This is the same shape as `KB05` · K05.2's rate limit and `L12.4`'s two-sided closeness band.

## K08.5 — The technique with a severe family, stated as a procedure `[T]`

Go round the family looking for "the one who had the most stability, who had done the most thinking about the problem"; **avoid every conflictual issue**; "my goal is to **keep anxiety down, down, down**, and to keep it just as **thinking-oriented** as possible, and anytime somebody brings up a conflictual issue, to **bypass it, tone it down, go to somebody else, change the subject**."

Of the resulting session, and approvingly: "**I didn't do any therapy. I missed a chance to do good therapy.**"

*Model consequence:* an implementable coach policy for the high-anxiety arm — search for the highest-capacity member, suppress conflict surfacing, maintain a thinking orientation. It is the operational form of the anxiety-first ordering (`KB04` · K04.4) and it is the opposite of the conflict-externalising policy the same interview rejects.

## K08.6 — Differentiation was reached by exhausting the alternatives `[T]`

The sequence: family group therapy → facilitator of communication → externalising and discussing conflicts → "years and years of meeting again and again… by now it gets **cyclical** and you're going through the same thing over and over and **the process is not changing**."

> "these various approaches to therapy just did not have the potential… **Except with differentiation**, if one began to show some ability to be a self in it, then I gradually moved to trying to help that one."

He also forecloses the obvious objection: "you could say they were not workable because I was an inept therapist. But I knew my therapy from years before, and **I was good**."

*Model consequence:* the differentiating move is not one option among the nine — it is the **only one that changed the process** after the alternatives were tried to exhaustion. That is a strong prior for the model's outcome axes, and it is the reason `M11.C.16`'s repertoire-collapse test matters.

## K08.7 — The three-legged stool `[M]`

In a family with schizophrenia, "all the family members would **peel off**, go away, and go their own ways, **except for the father and the mother and the patient**"; a three-legged stool that "can't exist without all three legs."

*Model consequence:* the primary triad is what **remains** when the rest of the family disengages — a selection effect rather than a designated structure. It corroborates `L02.6`'s single distinguished triad and gives the mechanism by which it becomes distinguished.

## K08.8 — Severity is a spectrum, and the method does not change along it `[T]`

> family therapy "could apply with **the more severe emotional problems, all of them**… It's about the **same** whether it be with schizophrenia or not. Schizophrenia is sort of, as I see it, at the **extreme end of that spectrum**."

*Model consequence:* one mechanism across the range, with severity as position rather than kind. Consistent with the model's continuous symptom load, and it means no separate machinery is needed for severe cases — only the family-type gate on technique.
