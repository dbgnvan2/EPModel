# Ch21 — On the Differentiation of Self

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** Presented at the "Family Research Conference," chaired by Paul Watzlawick, with panel discussants Rubinstein, Weakland, Weiner, Whitaker, Bell, Minuchin, Levinger and Ackerman. The text states "Since the 1967 conference…" and "This final version is written in 1970, three years after the conference." The conference presentation ran thirty minutes: about fifteen minutes of family history and background principles, then about fifteen minutes of the clinical experience. Conference participants heard **only** the final section ("The Family Experience"); the theory sections were added for publication. A routine didactic paper had been prepared and mailed to discussants beforehand and was deliberately not delivered. Original publication was **anonymous** — "Anonymous authorship helped to resolve the issues" raised by the editor's and publisher's anxiety about publishing personal material.
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):** Four sources, layered.
  1. **Bowen's own family of origin**, the chapter's principal clinical example: a cohesive family in the same small town for several generations; five children plus parents, an uncle, a grandmother, spouses, nieces and nephews, five separate households. "The step by step sequence of events in this family emotional field covers a period of over fifty years." His own active work on it: a twelve-year effort to understand the family within family theory, with an active effort to modify himself in relation to it during the last seven or eight years of that period, culminating in the reported breakthrough. Follow-up covers the almost three years since.
  2. **The family research programme** (mother–patient symbiosis in schizophrenia, extended to fathers, then to families with lesser problems and to families with no clinical problem).
  3. **A large well-known psychiatric clinic** — the staff and employee "family," used explicitly as a "control" emotional system for the same phenomenon.
  4. **Multigenerational family-history research**: "family tree knowledge about twenty-four families of origin," one traced back 300 years, another 250 years, several traced back 150 to 200 years, plus microscopic study of a few families back 200 or 300 years and numerous families back 100 years or more.
- **Genre: theory / technique / both — and roughly what proportion.** Both, weighted to technique and worked example. Roughly **35% theory** (the six-concept exposition and the therapeutic-system overview) and **65% technique** — but almost all of that technique is delivered as a single extended first-person worked example rather than as a rule list. The final ~15% is the verbatim conference discussion, which is data about how the field received the method rather than theory or technique.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Objectivity is lost on physical re-entry to an emotional system, and regained on exit, on a measurable time course.** Observed on himself at the psychiatric clinic where he worked: objectivity would arrive "by the time the plane was an hour away"; it would be lost "as I went through the front door returning to work," as if the emotional system "closed in." Returning to the city on a Saturday, objectivity would hold until Monday morning. On one occasion it was lost during a *telephone* conversation with a staff member before returning to work; on others, when greeting a staff member in the parking lot before entering the building. The fusion "operated most intensely with those most involved in the gossip system at work."
- **The same loss of objectivity, at the same speed, in a work system re-entered after years away.** On visits back to the old work system after two or three years' absence, "it would take no more than thirty minutes to meet someone important to the system, and immediately 'fuse' into taking sides in the emotional issues of the system."
- **The same phenomenon on visits to his family of origin, modulated by family tension.** When the family was calm it was possible to go several hours or a day before fusing into taking sides; if the family was tense, fusion could occur on first contact with a key person. Objectivity would usually return within an hour or two after the visit, en route home.
- **Every technique tried for extricating himself mid-visit failed.** Leaving wife and children at home and retreating to them to "de-fuse" failed — and his wife would report something terrible a sister or sister-in-law had said, showing the nuclear family had also fused despite being relatively isolated from the larger family. A two-day visit followed by a two-day "subvacation" 100 miles away, then a second visit, also failed. Visiting alone before and after a professional meeting worked somewhat better but he "never really regained objectivity until an hour or two after the second visit had ended."
- **Talking openly about problems made the fusion worse, not better.** "Discussions about family issues seemed to make the family system calmer, but they made the fusions more intense and it was more difficult to get back to objectivity later."
- **Emotional distance and silence do not register as neutrality.** During the ten years after the business reorganisation he believed he was "detached" and "objective" and "staying out of the family problem." "Actually, I was almost as emotionally involved as ever, and I was using emotional distance and silence to create an illusion of nonresponsiveness. Distance and silence do not fool an emotional system." Likewise: "Listening to such communications without response, pretending that one is not involved, does not fool an emotional system."
- **Confrontation about childhood grievances produced short-term relief and long-term worsening.** During his psychoanalysis, angry confrontations with his parents, considered at the time to be emotional emancipation, gave "some short-term gain from knowing my feelings a little better and learning to 'sound off'," but "the long-term result was an intensification of previous patterns," ending in the conviction that his parents would never change and that formal distance was justified.
- **Removing a symptom's focus makes a system behave as if the problem is solved.** When his uncle sold his half of the business and retired, "the family tended to see the new arrangement as the final solution." Bowen: "When the focus of the symptom is removed from the system, the system acts as if the problem is solved… it would be only a matter of time until the symptom surfaced elsewhere."
- **Detriangling from one triangle displaces the tension into another.** "In the past I had done fairly well in detriangling myself from one triangle, only to have the tension slip into another triangle; this pattern had been my undoing." He had assumed differentiating from his parents would settle the matter; the problem re-emerged displaced onto the triangle of mother, second brother, and self.
- **A death in one family branch was followed by a chain of apparently unrelated problems across the whole connected system.** The "shock wave" phenomenon, investigated in earlier research: the sudden heart-attack death of his second brother's wife's brother was followed (in observed sequence) by his oldest sister's anxious upset, then overt conflict in the central family triangle over stock, then the second brother's several-week immobilisation with a herniated vertebral disc.
- **One family member functions as an early-warning indicator of system tension.** Of the oldest sister: "She is so attuned to emotional forces in the family that a symptom in her is often an early indication of tension in the family system," and the indications were that she was responding to pressure in the larger family system rather than to her own nuclear family.
- **Gossip clumping at social gatherings.** "People would clump in small groups, each talking about someone outside that clump, and each apparently unaware that all the clumps were doing the same 'triangling' gossip about them."
- **Emotional fusion is universally present.** "I have never seen a family in which the 'emotional fusion' phenomenon is not present." Most people are not aware of it; some can become aware; some are too intensely fused ever to know emotional objectivity with their parents.
- **The relationship patterns first seen in schizophrenia turned out to be present, less intensely, everywhere.** "Once it was possible to finally 'see' the patterns in families with schizophrenia, it was possible to see the same patterns in a less intense form in all levels of people with less emotional impairment. We could see the patterns even in 'normal' families, and in the staff, and in ourselves."
- **Patterns of all emotional systems are identical across setting.** "whether they be family systems, work systems, or social systems, the only difference being one of intensity." Including the "'undifferentiated family ego mass' that exists among the prominent family therapists" — who, in a conference room discussing relationship patterns in sick families, "do the same things to each other while talking about what they do to each other."
- **In chaotic disturbed families, one member's move to define a position calmed the whole family almost immediately.** After a family had been paralysed and unable to arrive at a joint decision, one member would stop speaking for the whole family and define where he stood and what he intended to do and not do — "Almost immediately the entire family would become calmer. Then another family member would begin a version of the same process." Those families were too impaired to hold the position for long.
- **The same happened in the research staff.** Chaotic staff upsets, complaints about each other, unsuccessful group discussions; Bowen as director defined his own role, plans and intentions and terminated the "togetherness" group meetings; "Almost immediately the staff tension subsided and then another and another of the staff members began to define their responsibilities." Thereafter upsets settled within hours instead of days.
- **In the course of that self-definition he found he had infantilised the staff** by instructing them and functioning for them, while being irresponsible about things in his own area.
- **Parents in improving families spontaneously drop the united front.** "Observation of families in family psychotherapy indicates that parents tend to develop more individual relationships with the child as the family improves."
- **All families arrive at the united-front principle on their own** — sophisticated families present it as a modern child-rearing principle, less sophisticated families as a culture-bound principle about children obeying parents.
- **Nuclear families out of contact with families of origin do worse.** "a nuclear family out of meaningful emotional contact with families of origin is more vulnerable to intense symptoms, and the problems tend to be more chronic than in families that maintain contact with parental families."
- **[p2] The fusion loss is reversible, and the reversal transfers to a system it was not worked on.** After the family-of-origin breakthrough he returned to the old work system "for a long visit and was able to relate intimately to those important to the system without a single episode of 'fusion.'" Before the breakthrough the same system fused him in thirty minutes. Pass 1 recorded the 30-minute figure and omitted the outcome, which is the chapter's cleanest evidence that the gain is a per-person property rather than a per-system one.
- **[p2] Gossip volume rises as direct contact falls.** "As the distance between my second brother and me increased, the stories about him increased. I was hearing all about him and he was probably hearing all about me from the family network, but I could not see him." Closing the direct edge did not reduce traffic about the node; it relocated it onto indirect (triangling) channels.
- **[p2] The cool distance dated from a structural event.** "One of the first changes in the postorganizational period (from my standpoint) was a cool distance between my second brother and me, initiated by him. He and I had always been close and this, I realized in retrospect, continued until the business was reorganized." The cutoff was created by the reorganisation and initiated by the other party.
- **[p2] A repeating stress cycle that never resolved, terminated only by a structural change.** "With each period of stress there would be discussion about dividing the business, some new recognition of my brother's contribution, and a new period of calm. This sequence continued until the time came when, in a new period of stress, my uncle sold his half to my second brother and retired."
- **[p2] Household fragmentation increases outward confiding.** "Since the family lived in five separate households, there was more of a tendency for family issues to be confided to friends outside the family."
- **[p2] A one-week anxiety spike keyed to a threat to the functionally central member.** The second brother "developed a brief symptom slightly suggestive of a malignancy. Since the 'go power' for the family rested with him, anxiety went very high for a week until the possibility of malignancy was ruled out." Also: "each serious-appearing illness in either [parent] sent out some kind of an alarm, and precipitated some kind of family reaction."
- **[p2] Sibling conflict is not dyadic.** "conflict between siblings consists almost universally of a triangle between mother and two children in which mother has a positive relationship to each child and the conflict is fought out between the children." Pass 1 missed this entirely; it is the same displacement rule as the business-hierarchy statement, applied to siblings.
- **[p2] The negative side of a triangle is expressed indirectly, through the third corner.** Of father and uncle: "The negative side in this triangle was between my father and uncle in the business, usually expressed as discontent communicated through my mother. The stress, however, rarely reached a point of overt anger between them."
- **[p2] The cost of the outside corner is offset by a strong tie elsewhere.** "My uncle was the outside one, which caused no problems for him since he had the close relationship with his mother who was relatively uninvolved in business issues."
- **[p2] Objectivity about parents is biased in both directions.** "Few people can be objective about their parents, see and think about them as people, without either downgrading or upgrading them."
- **[p2] The family's self-account is more distorted the closer it is in time.** "With all the myths and pretense and emotionally biased reports and opinions, it is difficult to ever really know 'self' or to know family members in the present or recent past. As one reconstructs facts of a century or two ago, it is easier to get beyond myths and to be factual."
- **[p2] Deliberately disturbing a family is hard.** "There are situations that automatically disturb families, just as storms disturb a lake, but if one is trying purposely to disturb the surface of a lake, one finds how difficult it is."

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Triangle** → the smallest stable relationship system; the "molecule" of any emotional system, family or larger social system → acts on any set of three or more people. A two-person system is unstable and forms a triangle under stress; more than three people form "a series of interlocking triangles." The term *triangle* was chosen instead of *triad* because triad "has come to have fixed connotations that do not apply to this concept."
- **Triangling-in** → the mechanism by which tension in a twosome is discharged onto a third → acts on the tension level of the original pair. "As tension mounts in a two-person system, it is usual for one to be more uncomfortable than the other, and for the uncomfortable one to 'triangle in' a third person by telling the second person a story about the triangle one. This relieves the tension between the first two, and shifts the tension between the second and third."
- **Gossip as the principal triangling instrument** → conveys "We understand each other perfectly (the togetherness side of the triangle). We are in agreement about that pathological third person."
- **Position preference reverses with tension level** → in calm, a triangle is a comfortable twosome plus an outsider and "the favored position is to be a member of the twosome"; in tension, "the outside position is the preferred position, in a posture that says, 'You two fight and leave me out of it.'" Each move by one requires a compensatory move by another.
- **Triangle overflow / recruitment** → when the forces cannot be conveniently shifted inside a triangle, two members find another third person and the emotional forces run the circuits in the new triangle; the old circuits go quiet but stay "available for reuse at any time." Under very high tension a system triangles in more and more outsiders — neighbours, schools, police, clinics — so that "the family tension is being fought out by outside people."
- **Fixed triangle postures over time** → long-term positions form (commonly mother–child close twosome with father outside); minute-to-minute forces still shift but "when forces come to rest, it is always with each in the same position." A triangle "characteristically has two positive sides and one negative side."
- **Displacement down a hierarchy** → the mechanism seen in businesses and institutions: "the basic problem which exists on the highest administrative level is triangled and retriangled again and again until the conflict surfaces between two employees low in the administrative hierarchy." Bowen names his own family as an instance of the same pattern.
- **Differentiation of self** → the degree to which basic self is defined and separable from the relationship system → determines fusion intensity, symptom vulnerability, and recovery speed. Operates via the split between **basic self** (not negotiable in the relationship system; changeable only from within self on the basis of new knowledge and experience) and **pseudo-self** (acquired through the relationship system in the prevailing emotion; negotiable; borrowed and traded).
- **Fusion in marriage** → two partial selfs fuse into a common self; "One of the selfs in the common self becomes dominant and the other submissive or adaptive" — the dominant one "gains a higher level of functional self and appears 'stronger,' at the expense of the adaptive one who gave up self and who is functionally 'weaker.'"
- **Assortative mating on differentiation level** → "people choose spouses or close personal friends from those with equal levels of differentiation," and "The life style of a person at one level is so different from someone only a few points removed on the scale they do not choose each other for personal relationships."
- **Nuclear family emotional system: the three absorption areas** → a quantitative amount of undifferentiation, fixed by the spouses' level, is absorbed by (1) marital conflict, (2) dysfunction in a spouse, (3) projection to one or more children — one area primarily, or a combination, with other areas absorbing "the spill."
- **Family projection process** → parents project part of their immaturity to one or more children, most frequently through the mother, "with the mechanism which enables the mother to become less anxious by focusing on the child."
- **Multigenerational transmission process** → children emerge with higher, equal, or lower basic levels than the parents; equal-level marriage at each step compounds the drift, "generation by generation, to lower and lower levels."
- **Sibling position profiles (Toman, 1961)** → the personality profile of each sibling position; the profile of the projection-recipient is overwritten by the projection: "The more intense the projection process, the more like an infantile youngest child this one becomes, no matter which the sibling position of birth."
- **Togetherness force vs. differentiating force** → an opposed pair. Togetherness "defines the family members as alike in terms of important beliefs, philosophies, life principles, and feelings," uses "we," defines the self of another ("My wife thinks that…"), and uses the indefinite "it" ("It is wrong," "It is the thing to do"). It is bound together by valuing thinking of the other before self, living for the other, sacrifice, and feeling responsible for others' comfort; if the other is unhappy, it feels guilty ("What have I done to cause this?") and blames the other for one's own failure. The differentiating force places emphasis on "I."
- **Homeostatic restoration** → "A family system in emotional equilibrium is symptom-free at any level of differentiation." The system is disturbed when a member moves toward **regression** and moves to restore equilibrium; it is disturbed equally when a member moves toward a **slightly higher level of differentiation** and "will move as automatically to restore the family system to its former equilibrium."
- **Bilateral pull-up** → when a differentiating one holds position and the other's emotional reaction runs its course, "her emotional reaction represented a pull-up to his level… a basic increase in bilateral differentiation which can never return to the former level."
- **Detriangling by a non-taking-sides third** → "When there is finally one who can control his emotional responsiveness and not take sides with either of the other two, and stay constantly in contact with the other two, the emotional intensity within the twosome will decrease and both will move to a higher level of differentiation. Unless the triangled person can remain in emotional contact, the twosome will triangle in someone else."
- **Parental we-ness** → the poorly defined selfs of the parents fuse into a common self; clinically it "presents the child with a parental amalgam which is neither masculine nor feminine" and deprives him of an individual relationship with each parent; in triangle terms it "presents the child with a locked-in 'two against one' situation which provides no emotional flexibility unless he can somehow manage to force a rift in the other side of the triangle."
- **Person-to-person relationship** → "an ideal in which two people can communicate freely about the full range of personal issues between them." The failure mode is the mechanism: "Most people cannot tolerate more than a few minutes on a personal level. When either party becomes anxious, he begins talking about a third person (triangles in another person), or the communication becomes impersonal and they talk about things."
- **Emotional shock wave** → a death of an important family member "can 'shake' a family system for months," followed by "a series of apparently unrelated human problems throughout the family system."
- **The programmed system** → "the emotional forces within a triangle operate as predictably as an emotional reflex. The reactiveness operates in a chain reaction fashion, one reaction following another in predictable sequence." "one was 'programmed' into the system long ago and it is automatic for both parties to fall back on familiar patterns."
- **The observation/control ratchet** → "Observation is not possible until one can control one's reactions sufficiently to be able to observe. The process of observation allows for more control, which in turn, in a series of slow steps, allows for better observation."
- **[p2] Fixed triangle configuration is stress-conditional, not unconditional.** The Triangles concept section states the general form ("A triangle characteristically has two positive sides and one negative side"), but the family-history section attaches the configurations to load: "In calm periods, a triangle functions as a comfortable twosome and an outsider… In stressful periods, a triangle has two positive sides and a negative side." Pass 1 recorded the general statement as unconditional.
- **[p2] Fusion is wanted by both parties and cannot be held.** "Both partners want the emotional bliss of fusion but it is extremely difficult to maintain this equilibrium." The dominant/adaptive split is the *failure mode* of a state both parties are seeking, not an imposition by one on the other.
- **[p2] Marital conflict has two named trigger conditions.** "Marital conflict occurs when neither spouse will 'give in' to the other in the fusion, **or when the one who has been giving in or adapting refuses to continue**." The second clause is the mechanism by which a de-selfed spouse's recovery produces conflict.
- **[p2] Projection selection has three named factors.** "The lifestyle of parents, fortuitous circumstances such as traumatic events that disrupt the family during the pregnancy or about the time of birth, and special relationships with sons or daughters are among factors that help determine the 'selection' of the child for this process."
- **[p2] The anxiety wave enters from outside the family through a marital link, along a reasoned chain, and amplifies rather than creates.** "I reasoned that, sequentially, this death would 'shake' my second brother's wife, that my second brother would help her assume responsibility in her family, that he would become involved in her deep anxiety, that my family would react to his anxiety, and the anxiety could amplify minor problems into major ones at vulnerable points in my family." The channel is then drawn stochastically: "the person with the most vulnerable heart can have a heart attack, a chronic illness can flare up, a teen-aged child can wreck a car or break a bone, or any of numerous other symptoms could develop in any member of the family."

## A3. Predictive claims

Each stated as a conditional. **[E]** = Bowen reports observation/evidence in this chapter; **[A]** = asserted.

1. **If** a two-person system comes under stress, **then** it forms itself into a three-person system (triangle). **[A]**
2. **If** the triangular emotional pattern is modified in a single important triangle in the family, **and** the members of that triangle remain in emotional contact with the rest of the family, **then** other triangles will automatically change in relation to the first. **[E — the whole clinical report is offered as the test]**
3. **If** the modified triangle is a peripheral or less important one, **then** it is easier for the system to ignore the change. **[A]**
4. **If** a person moves toward a slightly higher level of differentiation, **then** a small emotional upheaval follows in the family system, in three predictable steps: (1) "You are wrong"; (2) "Change back"; (3) "If you do not, these are the consequences." **[E]**
5. **If** no emotional reaction occurs, **then** the differentiating effort was not successful. "This pattern is so predictable that absence of an emotional reaction is good evidence that the differentiating effort was not successful." **[A, stated as a diagnostic rule]**
6. **If** the differentiating one stays on course without defending self or counterattacking, **then** the emotional reaction is usually brief and the other then expresses appreciation. **[E — husband/wife example, and his own family]**
7. **If** the differentiating one yields to the demand, or attacks, **then** he slips back to the other's level. **[A]**
8. **If** he holds the position through the reaction, **then** both move to a new level "which can never return to the former level." **[A]**
9. **If** anyone else in the system knows about the differentiating plan, **then** the effort routinely fails. "a differentiating effort routinely fails if anyone else knows anything about it." **[E — he withheld it from his own wife and from trusted friends]**
10. **If** a differentiating effort is done primarily to help others, or with the expectation that others will approve and express appreciation, **then** it was for togetherness and not differentiation, and it fails. **[A]**
11. **If** it is done for self alone and succeeds, **then** "the system automatically benefits also." **[A]**
12. **If** the differentiating one requests or demands elaboration on the family's initial spontaneous "thank you," **then** the response is automatically the opposite of what was expected — devaluing or denying the importance of the event, or a critical opinion. **[A]**
13. **If** the differentiating one withdraws after the breakthrough (which is what the feeling system dictates), **then** the system "tightens up" again. **[A, from his own Monday follow-up]**
14. **If** the differentiating one becomes hostile or angry, **then** he is vulnerable to losing objectivity and to either defending or counterattacking when the issues are hurled back at him. **[E]**
15. **If** a person affirms a self around issues that can be ignored, **then** he "is quickly labeled a fool." Differentiation must be around issues important to both parties, in a relationship in which the other respects the belief and the action stand. **[A]**
16. **If** relationships with the parental family are distant, **then** the family can simply ignore detriangling moves. **[E — "All too often the family would ignore the effort."]**
17. **If** the family system is in quiet emotional equilibrium, **then** it is less amenable to discussion of emotional issues, or change, than a system in tension or stress. **[E — "My most meaningful visits have been during an illness or hospitalization of a significant family member."]**
18. **If** a nuclear family is out of meaningful emotional contact with families of origin, **then** it is more vulnerable to intense symptoms and its problems tend to be more chronic; **and** progress in therapy is several times faster in a nuclear family in contact with families of origin than in an isolated one. **[E]**
19. **If** brief frequent trips home replace infrequent long visits, **then** the work is many times more effective. **[A]**
20. **If** a person achieves progress with his parental family, **then** that progress is automatically translated into his nuclear family. **[E — trainees who worked only on families of origin made as much or more progress with spouses and children as trainees in formal family psychotherapy with their spouses for the same period]**
21. **If** the level of differentiation is lower, **then** triangle patterns are more intense; **if** the relationship is more important, **then** patterns are more intense. **[A]**
22. **If** a person is low on the scale, **then** he is more vulnerable to stress, and recovery from symptoms "can be slow or impossible," whereas higher-scale people "tend to recover rapidly." **[A]**
23. **If** the symptom's focus is removed from a system without the underlying process changing, **then** the symptom will surface elsewhere, given time. **[E — the business reorganisation]**
24. **If** the spouses' level of differentiation is lower, **then** it is more difficult to maintain reasonable emotional equilibrium and the more chronic the disability when adaptive mechanisms fail. **[A]**
25. **If** dysfunction in a spouse is the area absorbing the undifferentiation, **then** it is difficult to reverse, and it protects the other areas from symptoms. **[A]**
26. **If** a child grows up relatively outside the family projection process, **then** he can emerge with a higher basic level of differentiation than the parents. **[E — his own fifth sibling]**
27. **If** a child is the object of the projection, **then** he is the one most emotionally attached to the parents and ends up with the lower level of differentiation. **[E — his oldest sister]**
28. **If** the family projection process is intense, **then** the recipient acquires the profile of an infantile youngest child regardless of actual birth position. **[E — his oldest sister "has the personality profile of a dependent youngest child"]**
29. **If** the parents present a united front, **then** the child is locked into a two-against-one with no emotional flexibility; **if** the parental we-ness is separated early, **then** "the change in the child is usually rapid and dramatic." **[E]**
30. **If** the therapist stays "detriangled" and keeps the process between the spouses active, **then** the method works "no matter what the subject of discussion," as long as the twosome deals with issues that reveal critical triangles. **[A, explicitly a belief: "I believe this method would work…"]**
31. **If** the family psychotherapy configuration includes three or more from the natural family, **then** the emotional system runs its own built-in circuits and it takes much more time for the family to observe or modify the triangle patterns. **[A]**
32. **If** conflict exists between siblings in one generation, **then** it will be perpetuated by their descendants far into future generations. **[Predicted from his multigenerational studies; he looked for it in his own family and "it was not possible to get definite evidence to confirm my postulation nor to suggest an alternative one" at that stage — an explicitly unconfirmed prediction]**
33. **If** an important family member dies, **then** the system may show a shock wave of apparently unrelated human problems for months. **[E]**
34. **If** the trainee is not yet partially outside the emotional system, **then** a differentiating technique is "either hollow meaningless words or a hostile assault on the system, and an emotional system knows the difference and reacts accordingly." **[A]**
35. **If** a differentiating move is made from *inside* the system, **then** it fails; the move must be made while one is still "outside" the emotional system, or before one becomes fused into it. **[E]**
36. **If** a triangling "secret" message is passed to you, **then** going to the third person and reporting the message in a neutral way disengages you from it. **[E, but with a stated failure: telling his mother he was neutral did not work — see B4]**
37. **If** the differentiating one had previously used only distance and silence, **then** the system is not fooled and he is as involved as ever. **[E]**
38. **[p2] If** a differentiating step succeeds, **then** the *initial* family reaction is "negative and takes the form of surprise, anger, and a 'you must be crazy' attitude," and "people do not think while they react." **[A]** — this precedes the three-step ladder and is stated separately from it.
39. **[p2] If** the mover can contain his own emotional functioning enough to carry through, **then** the result is predictable in advance: "One can construct an amazingly accurate hypothesis from which it is possible to plan a predictable result **if** the differentiating one can contain his own emotional functioning enough to carry through." **[A]** The predictability claim is explicitly conditional on the mover, not on the system.
40. **[p2] If** direct contact with a family member falls, **then** triangling traffic about him rises. **[E — the second brother]**
41. **[p2] If** a person progresses in his family of origin, **then** the gain transfers to *any* emotional system he belongs to, including a work system he never worked on. **[E — the old clinic, 30 minutes to fusion before, no fusion after]**
42. **[p2] If** the trainee is only partially outside the system, **then** coaching can compress the work "to achieve a reasonably good beginning process of differentiation in a fraction of the time that my effort required." **[E]**
43. **[p2] If** the single-member method is compared with conventional psychotherapy, **then** "The average result with this method has been far superior to results with conventional psychotherapy." **[A — no data given]**
44. **[p2] If** a conflict between siblings is observed, **then** it is "almost universally" a triangle with the mother positive to both children, not a dyadic conflict. **[A]**

## A4. Quantities, thresholds, time courses

*Verbatim numbers as stated in the chapter.*

**The differentiation-of-self scale**
- The scale runs from "the lowest possible level of 'undifferentiation,' which is at **0** on the scale, to the highest theoretical level of 'differentiation,' which is at **100** on the scale."
- "there is some degree of fusion in close relationships, and some degree of an 'undifferentiated family ego mass' at **every scale level below 100**."
- "When the scale was first devised, the **100** level was reserved for the being who was perfect in all levels of emotional, cellular, and physiological functioning."

## A5. Terminology

- **Differentiation of self scale** — "an effort to classify all levels of human functioning, from the lowest possible levels to the highest potential level, on a single dimension." Explicitly *not* a maturity scale, *not* about health/illness/pathology, and it "eliminates the need for the concept 'normal.'" No direct correlation with intelligence or socioeconomic level.
- **Basic self** — "a definite quality illustrated by such 'I position' stances as: 'These are my beliefs and convictions. This is what I am, and who I am, and what I will do, or not do.'" Changeable from within self on the basis of new knowledge and experience; "not negotiable in the relationship system."
- **Pseudo-self** — "a mass of heterogeneous facts, beliefs, and principles acquired through the relationship system in the prevailing emotion"; "negotiable in the relationship system"; it is the pseudo-self that fuses with others.
- **Undifferentiated family ego mass** — the original term for the "emotional 'stuck togetherness' or fusion in the nuclear family." Bowen states here that it "is still accurate when applied to the nuclear family, but the term is less apt in referring to the same phenomenon in the extended families, and the term is awkward when applied to the same phenomenon in emotional systems at work, or in social systems."
- **Emotional system** — "More recently the term *emotional system* has been used to designate the same triangular emotional patterns that operate in all close relationships, with an additional term to designate the location of the system, for example, a *nuclear family* emotional system." **This is an explicit terminological succession within the chapter: `emotional system` is taking over the job `undifferentiated family ego mass` used to do.**
- **Triangle** — chosen over *triad*; "the smallest stable relationship system"; the "molecule" of any emotional system.
- **Interlocking triangles** — the series of triangles formed by any system larger than three.
- **Primary triangles / secondary triangles** — primary counted combinatorially (3-person → 1; 4 → 4; 5 → 9); secondary arise "when two or more may band together for one corner of a triangle for one emotional issue, while the configuration shifts on another issue."
- **Fixed** (of a triangle) — "refers to the most characteristic position"; even in the most fixed triangle "the positive and negative forces shift back and forth constantly."
- **Detriangled / detriangling** — the therapist or family member keeping himself "emotionally outside the emotional field" involving a twosome while staying in contact with both. Rubinstein challenges the word in discussion: "Are we destroying the triangle, are we detriangling, or are we changing the triangles to a different level of functioning?"
- **I position** — "defines principle and action in terms of, 'This is what I think, or believe' and, 'This is what I will do or will not do,' without impinging one's own values or beliefs on others."
- **The responsible I** — "assumes responsibility for one's own happiness and comfort, and it avoids thinking that tends to blame and hold others responsible."
- **The irresponsible I** — "makes demands on others with, 'I want, or I deserve, or this is my right, or my privilege.'"
- **Togetherness forces** / **differentiating force** — the opposed pair; togetherness "treat[s] differentiation as selfish and hostile."
- **Parental we-ness** — the fused common self of the parents presented to the child as a united front.
- **Person-to-person relationship** — two people communicating freely about the full range of personal issues between them, with no third person and no talk about things.
- **Family projection process** — parents projecting part of their immaturity onto one or more children.
- **Multigenerational transmission process** — the generation-by-generation drift of basic differentiation levels.
- **Nuclear family emotional system** — the developmental concept covering the emotional patterns from plans for marriage onward.
- **Sibling position profiles** — Toman's profiles, adopted into the theory.
- **Shock wave** — a death followed by a series of apparently unrelated human problems throughout the family system.
- **Anxiety wave** — used interchangeably with shock wave in the clinical section: "In such an anxiety wave period, the person with the most vulnerable heart can have a heart attack…"
- **Reversals** — "a psychotherapeutic principle I have long used of making a point by saying the opposite. This technique works predictably if the therapist is 'outside' the emotional system and can be sufficiently casual and detached."
- **Stories** — the technique of relating to a person what the system says about him, framed as "stories," refusing to separate fact from fiction, and refusing the system's rule about what he may be told. "This technique, designed to present material in terms of 'stories,' proved so effective I have since employed it routinely in my practice."
- **Tempest in a teapot** — the deliberately manufactured, low-stakes emotional stir raised from old issues, used as the working medium of the differentiating move.
- **The differentiating one** — a *role*, acquired after a successful differentiation: "I have come to have a new role in the family which I call the role of 'the differentiating one.'"
- **Concept** vs **theory** — a stipulated definitional distinction: "When a body of theoretical thinking is sufficiently accurate so that it no longer requires significant modification, is accurate in describing and predicting the human phenomenon, and can explain discrepancies as well as consistencies, it is called a 'concept.' The term *theory* has not been used loosely. After there are several consistent concepts, the term is used for the total theoretical system."
- **Family psychotherapy with a single family member** / **with one motivated family member** — the named method; the chapter is offered as "a good example" of it.
- **Coaching** — Bowen's word for what he does with a single member working on his own family: "I have been able to 'coach' motivated family therapists…" It is set against "formal psychotherapy."
- **Functional self / functional level** — the shifting level produced by borrowing and trading of pseudo-self, distinct from basic level. "A compliment can raise the functioning level of self and criticism can lower it."
- **[p2] Family psychotherapy with one spouse in preparation for family psychotherapy with both spouses** — a **third** named therapeutic configuration, given its own heading, which pass 1 omitted from B1 entirely. "This method is designed for families in which one spouse is negative and unwilling to be involved in the family psychotherapy… The goal with this method is to help the motivated spouse to understand the part that self plays in the family system, until the unmotivated spouse is willing to join the therapy as a cooperative effort."
- Terms used by discussants, not by Bowen: **enmeshed family** / **disengaged family** (Minuchin), **rubber fence** (Whitaker, citing Wynne), **ABX / POX** (Levinger).

### THE CONCEPT ENUMERATION (as at this date)

The chapter states **twice** that the theory has **six** concepts. They are presented in this order, under these names, in the section "The Theoretical Concepts":

1. **Differentiation of self scale**
2. **Nuclear family emotional system**
3. **Family projection process**
4. **Multigenerational transmission process**
5. **Sibling position profiles**
6. **Triangles**

Notes on the ordering and framing:
- Triangles is deliberately placed last: "The discussion of triangles will be listed last." But triangles is also introduced *first*, out of order, in the "Theoretical Background" section — "only one of which, the 'triangle,' will be discussed at this point" — because the clinical example is unintelligible without it.
- "Emotional cutoff" and "societal emotional process" are **not** present as named concepts in this chapter. There are six, not seven or eight.
- The material that would later be called cutoff appears here without a concept name, as descriptive observation: "There are also those who kid themselves into believing they have 'worked out' the relationship with parents and who make brief formal visits home without personal communication; they use as evidence of maturity that they do not see their parents."
- Sibling position is presented as **imported** from Toman (1961) and is described as "of peripheral interest to this presentation."
- **[p2] Verified against Ch09.** Ch09 (~1965) *excludes* the triangle from the major theoretical concepts ("more to do with therapy than the basic theory") while calling it the basic building block. Ch21 (1967/70) *includes* triangles as concept six and calls it "the 'molecule' of any emotional system." The promotion of the triangle into core theory happens between those two chapters. This closes the pass-2 flag left at L09.5.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The differentiating one moves first, and moves alone.** "Who gets out of a triangle? There is one person who is motivated to do this, if you can find this one." The move must originate inside that person; it cannot be shared. **[p2] Pass 1 truncated the quotation. The rest of it is the coach's selection policy: "In the average family, if I can get the couple to cooperate, then I do it with them, and if I can't, then I work with the motivated one." The single motivated member is the *fallback*, not the first preference.**
- **Oldest children are the preferred candidates for the single-member method.** "It works best with oldest children who usually feel more responsibility for their families and who are more motivated for such an effort."
- **The mover must be self-supporting.** "It requires that the single members be self-supporting, else they never develop the emotional courage for change that might threaten the family attitude about them."
- **Sibling profiles as inputs.** "a note about the sibling position of each parent and whether or not the profile of each parent was reasonably typical, conveys invaluable information about the way this family will adapt itself to life." Example given: "a 'fusion of selfs' mix made up of an oldest daughter and youngest son automatically conveys a wealth of information about the family, 'all things being equal.'" And such a mix "behaves differently in conflict, in the dysfunction of one spouse, and in the family projection process."
- **The symptom-bearer.** "The most common pattern is one in which one child is the recipient of a major portion of the projection, while other children are relatively less involved." "There is one such child in almost every family." In Bowen's own family this was the fourth child, the oldest daughter: "the one most emotionally triangled into the family system, the only one who did not go to college, and the one who has made the poorest life adjustment." She "has remained emotionally dependent on my parents" and "has the personality profile of a dependent youngest child, which happens with the one most involved in the family emotional process."
- **Sex-typing shaped the selection.** He asks explicitly "Why did the emotional patterns involve a daughter instead of a son, and why this child?" and answers with reality factors: mother had long wanted a daughter; father was the responsible one in the business and mother in the home; "Clear distinctions between men's work and women's work helped keep my sister in a special category."
- **Birth timing and nodal events shaped role assignment.** His second brother "was conceived within months after my grandfather's death, the reorganization of the business, and the merger into a single household. My second brother was born within the month after my uncle left. It was as if he was born to take over the family business." Bowen and his first brother were "born while my parents had their own home, and we are the only two who moved away and who have no connection with the business. There was no particular pressure on anyone to leave or stay. It just evolved."
- **The one outside the projection does better.** The fifth child "grew up more outside the family emotional system and she has the profile of a responsible oldest daughter."
- **Bowen's own position.** "My personality profile is that of an over-responsible oldest son." "As an oldest son and physician I had long been the wise expert preaching to the unenlightened, even when it was done under the guise of expressing an opinion or giving advice." He was important in the family "because of my position as 'oldest' and because I had made myself important through my various efforts."
- **Marriage of two oldests.** "My father is an action-oriented oldest son and my mother a responsible 'doing' oldest daughter. They are among that percentage of 'oldests' who make marriage into a smooth-functioning partnership." Bowen is "married to the second of three daughters, who functions more as an oldest."
- **Functional only child.** His uncle, "a functional only child by virtue of the seven years between him and my mother, was his mother's only child. He emerged with a profile of a bright youngest son."
- **Succession roles are inherited across generations.** "Emotional forces were operating for this brother to become the 'head of the clan' and for my younger sister to succeed my mother as the responsible woman in the next generation." The younger sister is addressed in the letters as "Big Mother."
- **Everyone in a fixed triangle experiences themselves as caught.** "Any member of a relatively fixed triangle perceives his self as 'caught.' My father was caught between his wife and her brother, my uncle between his sister and her husband, and my mother between her husband and her brother."

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

**With both spouses / both parents (the main configuration) — the four named functions:**
1. **Keep the emotional system between them alive but toned down.** "sufficiently alive to be meaningful and sufficiently toned down for them to deal with it objectively without undue emotional reactiveness."
2. **Keep self detriangled** from the emotional process between the two.
3. **Establish an "I position."** "The therapist takes action stands in relation to them, which then permits them to begin to do the same to each other."
4. **Teach them how emotional systems operate** and encourage each to work toward differentiation of self in relation to their families of origin.
- **Route all traffic through the therapist.** "The therapist is active with constant questions, first to one spouse, and then the other, getting the thoughts of one in reaction to what the other had communicated to the therapist. This prevents emotional exchanges between the spouses and enables each to 'hear' the other without the automatic emotional bind."
- **Constitute a new triangle with the two primaries and the therapist** — "the quickest way to modify the central triangle."
- **Target the most important triangle,** which "from experience… has been found to stem from the two parents or the two spouses."

**[p2] With one spouse, in preparation for both spouses — the third configuration, missed at pass 1:**
- Used "for families in which one spouse is negative and unwilling to be involved in the family psychotherapy."
- "The first part is similar to what will be described in the next section on family psychotherapy with one family member."
- Goal: "to help the motivated spouse to understand the part that self plays in the family system, **until the unmotivated spouse is willing to join the therapy as a cooperative effort**." The single-member work is here an *on-ramp* to the two-spouse configuration, not a substitute for it.

**With one motivated family member (the method the clinical report exemplifies):**
- **Teach first.** "The initial sessions are spent teaching the characteristics of family systems."
- **Then postulate.** "sessions are devoted to making postulations about the part this single member plays in the total system."
- **Then observe.** "time is devoted to learning to observe patterns in one's own emotional reactions in the parental system."
- **Then field-test.** "relatively frequent contact with families of origin to check postulations, to seek new observations that will confirm or refute postulations, and to develop ways to modify reactions."
- **Use work and social systems as practice grounds.** "It is also possible to use work and social relationship systems for learning the properties of emotional systems."
- **Space appointments by distance.** "Appointments are spaced farther apart when distance from family does not permit frequent visits."
- **Coach, don't treat.** "This coaching was done in the same didactic meetings in which the teaching was done."
- **Coach toward productive areas and away from pitfalls.** "This goal is achieved by helping them focus on the productive areas and avoiding the time-consuming pitfalls."

**Concrete moves the differentiating person makes (the operational repertoire of this chapter):**
- **Build individual relationships with every family member,** displacing clump-to-clump communication: personal letters to every niece and nephew, letters addressed individually, replies received at the office so his own family never saw them. "The return on this endeavor is like a long-term dividend; it has modified my image within the entire family."
- **Develop a person-to-person relationship with each parent separately.** "Time with each parent alone is essential."
- **Find a subject of interest to both that does not involve the rest of the family.** Named working subjects: the parent's own early life ("Most people are eager to talk about their own early life experiences to those interested in listening"), and family history/genealogy.
- **Use illness and hospitalisation as openings.** His two biggest person-to-person gains came at his father's hospitalisation after a moderately severe heart attack (fears of death, philosophy of life, life goals) and at his mother's major elective surgery (days with her in hospital, evenings alone with father).
- **Manufacture extended one-to-one time.** A week-long automobile trip with his mother to her ancestors' lands; later, two trips alone with his father to his father's ancestors' lands.
- **Visit during system fluidity.** "I encourage visits when the system is emotionally fluid or during family upsets such as deaths, serious illness, reunions, weddings, or other stressful or significant family events."
- **Make brief frequent trips, not infrequent long ones.**
- **Report a triangling "secret" to the person it was about, neutrally.** "One of the better ways to disengage from such a triangling 'secret' communication is to go to the third person and report the message in a neutral way." Executed as: mother writes a negative story about father → "In the next mail I wrote to my father to say that his wife had just told me this story about him, and I wondered why she told me instead of telling him."
- **Escalate from words to action when words fail.** "Action is required when words fail to detriangle in emotional systems."
- **Use "stories."** Tell the person what the system says about him; say everyone knows but him; say the family warned everyone not to tell him; ask why he has not bothered to find out; assert the right to say directly what one wants without regard to what the system thinks is good for him to hear.
- **Use "reversals"** — make the point by saying the opposite, casually and detached. (He urged his overburdened brother to be *more* responsible.)
- **Write out and rehearse the material until anger is gone.** "The letter was written and rewritten in order to eliminate hostile or derogatory comments… I played and replayed these issues so many times that I could be rather objective about each one. The more I did this, the more it was impossible for me to be angry with anyone."
- **Plan on paper.** "I had diagrammed and successfully blueprinted my way through triangle webs for many other families." "one can work out the predictable answers to its problems on a drawing board."
- **Prepare an issue for every key member**, "especially issues that would touch each relationship cleanly."
- **Neutralise allies actively.** Detriangle anyone who moves onto your side; keep the whole family in one emotional clump. When the younger sister wrote "I am back of you," he "handled [it] by telling her that I was going to tell the family she had invited me home to help her with her Big Mother role."
- **Send deliberately opposed messages to different family segments** to prevent any segment from forming a bloc with you.
- **Time the moves by the calendar.** Letter mailed exactly two weeks before the trip; phone call exactly one week later; counter-letters written within the hour of the phone call.
- **Take a reading before arriving,** under a plausible pretext (calling to ask who would meet the plane).
- **Position yourself for the meeting.** He "purposely stayed close to my parents all morning, hoping to facilitate a meeting of most of these people."
- **In the meeting: avoid defending, avoid attacking, avoid anger under provocation, have an instant casual response to any comment.**
- **Return the next day.** "To make a differentiating process work, one has to continue in relationship with the family system… it is necessary to keep talking to the system."
- **Detriangle continuously afterwards**, casually, as each new situation presents itself.
- **Take the "troublemaker" role deliberately.** He signs letters "Your Meddlesome Brother," "Your Worried Brother," "Your Anxious Brother," "Your Strategic Son." He later refers to "my assumed role as 'troublemaker.'"
- **[p2] Dial the message's intensity independently of its content.** "In all these letters, I used words such as *terrible, awful, pressing,* and *horrible* to describe the family plight. **These words were all designed to stir up the 'tempest in a teapot' for the purposes of the visit.**" Pass 1 recorded the word list as colour; it is a stated control on activation level, separate from the issues being raised.
- **[p2] Practise on a peripheral emotional system.** Work systems and the "family" of family therapists are used deliberately: "A partial success in a more peripheral emotional system would contribute something to the effort with my family of origin." The clinic is described as "a kind of 'control'" for the same phenomenon.
- **[p2] Defend the secrecy with an active togetherness-framed cover story, not with silence.** When the first brother's wife wrote repeatedly asking about the "plan and strategy," he "told her I was hurt by her implications of deviousness on my part when I had spent so much time thinking good things about people… I assured her that my only goal was to restore the basic love and togetherness in the family."

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not tell anyone in the system what you are planning.** "The plan was not discussed even with trusted friends." "From long experience, I have found that a differentiating effort routinely fails if anyone else knows anything about it. To be effective, each action and move must come from within the person who makes the effort… To discuss the plan with another person who is part of the system invites certain failure."
  - **[p2] The full passage, and the scope qualifier pass 1 flattened.** The absolute ("anyone else knows anything about it") is immediately narrowed in the same passage to "another person **who is part of the system**." The stated reason is **not** information leakage: "To be effective, each action and move must come from within the person who makes the effort. **These decisions and actions often have to be made instantaneously and, for better or worse, the individual has the responsibility.**" A shared plan cannot be executed at the required latency and cannot carry single responsibility. Pass 1's B5 #1 attributed the rule to the family being a near-perfect information channel, which is a different claim Bowen makes elsewhere ("Messages run back and forth in such a family system as if by telepathy") and does not give as the reason here.
  - **[p2] Stated three times, with descending force.** (i) "routinely fails if anyone else knows anything about it" / "invites certain failure"; (ii) "The plan was not discussed even with trusted friends" — that instance belongs to the **conference** move, not the family visit; (iii) at the close: "Only the differentiating one knows the logical, orderly thinking and planning that has to go into such an effort. **If anyone else knows about it, then it is doubtful that any differentiation will result** from the effort." The third statement is hedged where the first is absolute.
  - **[p2] Secrecy carries its own cost, paid as pre-move anxiety.** "From past experience, this anxiety was related to the **'secret' action move** with other family therapists rather than reporting 'secrets' about my own family."
  - **[p2] Secrecy did not prevent propagation to the uninformed.** "My wife had no direct knowledge of what I was doing… My wife did not ask a single question nor make positive or negative comments about my family at any time during the trip. **This had never happened before.**" The nuclear family's own habitual fusion into the extended system was interrupted without its knowing anything.
- **Do not permit allies.** "I worked out a plan that permitted no 'allies' in my effort… to keep the entire family in one big emotional clump, and to detriangle any ally who tried to come over to my side." And later, of the first brother's wife asking about the "plan and strategy": "In the past I had been 'undone' by partners and I was not about to get serious with her and risk spoiling my success."
  - **[p2] The full context and the exact scope of the claim — this matters because pass 1 over-read it.** The rule is stated as a *planning decision with a stated rationale*, not as a theoretical proposition. The rationale is peripheral-triangle alignment: "In the past I had done fairly well in detriangling myself from one triangle, only to have the tension slip into another triangle; this pattern had been my undoing. **In preparation for all the potential peripheral triangles that could align themselves with issues and prove difficult**, I worked out a plan that permitted no 'allies' in my effort. In other words, it was an effort to keep the entire family in one big emotional clump, and to detriangle any ally who tried to come over to my side for this project. **I had used this rationale before on smaller emotional systems in my practice and I knew the principle was workable.**"
  - **[p2] The active detriangling of the ally is verified, and is a counter-move, not a refusal.** "**A red flag had gone up** from her comment about 'I am back of you,' which I handled by telling her that I was going to tell the family she had invited me home to help her with her Big Mother role. **She retreated from taking sides with me** by acting as if the issues I had raised were all very serious."
  - **[p2] The conflicting-letters purpose is verified verbatim as ally prevention:** "The conflicting messages were designed to prevent any one segment of the family from getting on my 'side.'"
  - **[p2] What the chapter does NOT say.** Bowen never states that support from a family member *cancels* a differentiating move, or that it subtracts from its effect. What he states is that an ally is a red flag to be actively neutralised, that the whole family must be kept in one clump, that peripheral triangles aligning with the mover had previously been his undoing, and that partners had "undone" him before. The cancellation reading is a defensible inference from those four statements — it is not a claim in the text, and it should not enter the model as a quotation.
- **Do not become hostile, angry, or derogatory.** "If the differentiating one becomes hostile or angry he is vulnerable to losing objectivity and either defending or counterattacking when the issues are hurled back at him."
- **Do not defend and do not counterattack.** Stated as the necessary condition for a brief reaction and a subsequent expression of appreciation.
- **Do not work on the live reality issue.** He specifically refused to use the stock/control conflict: "to focus on that would make that issue into a reality issue rather than a manifestation of an emotional system. In addition, I would be more vulnerable to being triangled with that issue."
- **Do not withdraw after the reaction.** "This is the point where the feeling system dictates withdrawal and comfortable distance, which will result in the system 'tightening up' again."
- **Do not do it for others, or for appreciation.** "If it is done primarily to help others or with the expectation that others will approve and express appreciation, then the effort was for togetherness and not for differentiation."
- **Do not request or demand elaboration on the family's thank-you.** Doing so produces "automatically the opposite of what was expected."
- **Do not use distance and silence as a substitute for a position.** "Distance and silence do not fool an emotional system."
- **Do not listen to triangling communications without response and call that neutrality.** "Listening to such communications without response, pretending that one is not involved, does not fool an emotional system."
- **Do not take a stand on issues that can be ignored.** "One who affirms a 'self' around issues that can be ignored is quickly labeled a fool."
- **Do not impose your values on others while taking an I position.** "without impinging one's own values or beliefs on others."
- **Do not use the "irresponsible I"** — "I want, or I deserve, or this is my right, or my privilege."
- **Do not have the parents present a united front.** "With family research I developed the conviction that this dictum is one of the most unsound psychological principles."
- **Do not focus on the negative side of a triangle as if it were the problem.** "to focus on issues in one relationship is to misidentify the problem, to convey the impression that the problem is in this one relationship, and to make the triangle more fixed and less reversible."
- **Do not treat expression of hostility as therapeutic.** "There may be transient anxiety relief from direct expression of anger, but to focus on this dimension makes the family maladaptive."
- **Do not do open group discussion of problems as a way into a family of origin.** It made his family calmer but "made the fusions more intense."
- **Do not let the therapist get pulled inside the marital emotional field.** "It is necessary that the psychotherapy be done in a way that does not involve the therapist in the emotional system between the spouses."
- **Do not increase appointment frequency to speed things up.** "More frequent appointments do not increase the capacity to observe and to control emotional responsiveness."
- **Do not change the therapy to fit an unexpected crisis.** "The therapy was never changed to fit the situation except in emergencies. The goal was to change the hypothesis to account for the unexpected crisis, to change the therapy to fit the hypothesis."
- **Do not let intuition or clinical hunch drive the plan.** "It is commonplace for psychotherapists to make changes based more on feeling perceptions and subjectivity than on clinical fact and objectivity."
- **Do not attempt a differentiating move while still inside the emotional system.** "Until the trainee is partially outside the system, a differentiating technique is either hollow meaningless words or a hostile assault on the system, and an emotional system knows the difference and reacts accordingly."

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

**The two-step structure of differentiating from a parental family**
> "The process of 'differentiating a self' from a parental family involves two major steps. The first step is to develop the person-to-person relationships. This step helps to bring relationships more alive, it helps one to recognize old patterns that may have faded from view, and most of all, it results in livelier family response to the effort to 'detriangle' or change the old patterns. A parental family can ignore such detriangling moves if relationships are distant."

Note the stated revision: "In this report, I have put more emphasis on the person-to-person relationship in relation to triangles than I do in my current work in 'coaching' others with their families" — partly because person-to-person was developed before detriangling was well understood.

**The observation/control precondition**
- "Observation is not possible until one can control one's reactions sufficiently to be able to observe. The process of observation allows for more control, which in turn, in a series of slow steps, allows for better observation." Both are "equally difficult."
- "The process of being able to observe is the slow beginning toward moving one small step toward getting one's self 'outside' an emotional system. It is only when one can get a little outside that it is possible to begin to observe and to begin to modify an emotional system."

**Other preconditions**
- Be **outside the system, or act before fusing into it**: "the effort to define or differentiate a self is most effective if one is 'outside' the emotional system, or before one becomes fused into the system."
- Have a **meaningful relationship and a meaningful issue**: "Differentiation cannot take place in a vacuum. It has to take place in relation to others, around issues important to both people… in the context of a meaningful relationship in which the other has to respect the belief and the action stand that affirms it."
- Have a **system in motion**: "A family system in quiet emotional equilibrium is less amenable to the discussion of emotional issues, or change, than a family system in tension or stress."
- **Surprise is required**: "the element of surprise that is essential if a differentiating step is to be successful."
- **Self-support**: the single member must be self-supporting.
- **Distance**: about 200 to 300 miles is optimum — "close enough for frequent contact and far enough away to be outside the immediate emotional sphere of the family."
- **Anxiety before the move is expected and is not a stop signal.** "I was anxious and sleepless the night before the presentation… My anxiety would have sufficed to have me abandon the project had I not remembered similar anxiety before each differentiating effort in my own family. Impulses to read the formal paper continued to the very moment of presentation."
- **Conflict is easier to work with than internalised symptoms.** "It is easier to deal with overt conflict than with internalized symptoms." He explicitly waited for and then used a conflict as the working medium: "it is far easier to deal with conflict than other mechanisms in such an effort. My aim was to have a conflictual issue around which to work." He adds that "overt conflict is relatively rare in our family," so the working medium was not reliably available.
- **[p2] The issue supply differs between marriage and family of origin, and that is the stated reason the family-of-origin course is rougher.** "The course of differentiation is not as smooth and orderly when one person attempts it alone in his family of origin. One reason has to do with **the diversity of issues about which each can take an 'I position.'** … A marriage contains **an endless supply** of issues important to both spouses if they can disentangle self from the emotional system in order to define the issues… **It is more difficult to find meaningful issues in a family of origin when one has little or no contact with its members.**" Pass 1 recorded the "vacuum" quote and the contact threshold but missed the supply comparison, which is what links this chapter to Ch22's live-issue precondition.
- **[p2] Three mechanisms are given for getting outside, not one.** Pass 1 said private rehearsal was "the only method the chapter gives for raising outside-ness." That is wrong. The chapter gives (i) the **observation/control ratchet** — "Observation is not possible until one can control one's reactions sufficiently to be able to observe. The process of observation allows for more control, which in turn, in a series of slow steps, allows for better observation"; (ii) **practice on a peripheral system** — "A partial success in a more peripheral emotional system would contribute something to the effort with my family of origin"; and (iii) **private rehearsal of one's own charged material** — "I played and replayed these issues so many times that I could be rather objective about each one. The more I did this, the more it was impossible for me to be angry with anyone." Only (iii) is bounded to a single move; (i) and (ii) are slow and cumulative.
- **[p2] The abort risk is real, recurs before every attempt, and is countered by recall of prior instances.** "I was anxious and sleepless the night before the presentation… **My anxiety would have sufficed to have me abandon the project had I not remembered similar anxiety before each differentiating effort in my own family.** Impulses to read the formal paper continued to the very moment of presentation."
- **[p2] Most attempts are partial successes.** "I was so accustomed to each new effort being a partial success that I was hardly prepared for total success… It was equivalent to having reached the summit after **a hundred unsuccessful tries.**" The reported breakthrough is the tail of a long distribution, not a representative draw.

**[p2] Two success criteria, with different completion times and different tolerances.** Bowen separates them explicitly. (a) **Interrupting the anxiety wave** — "my **primary mission** for the visit" — was achieved "even by the time the family meeting was well under way," and is robust to partial failure: "Even if I had been slightly or moderately triangled, I would have more than achieved my primary mission for the visit." (b) **His own non-fusion** — achieved at about two-thirds through, and all-or-nothing: "To have completed the meeting without becoming triangled was **additional** evidence that I had attained the technical excellence to make the theoretical system work." A third confirmation is recorded at a third time: "I also knew that my postulations about interlocking triangles were accurate **by the time the family meeting started**."

**[p2] The Monday follow-up produced fresh triangling material, which he immediately spent.** The first person-to-person talk with the brother in years yielded the brother's remark about their oldest sister ("Sometimes I think she is retarded"); Bowen drove straight to the sister and used it to detriangle. The reward for a person-to-person breakthrough is new triangling content.

**The generic three-step reaction, stated as a rule and illustrated with a couple in therapy**
0. **[p2]** Stated separately, at the close of the clinical report, as what precedes the ladder: "The initial family reaction is negative and takes the form of **surprise, anger, and a 'you must be crazy' attitude.** When one person is doing a differentiating step the others react emotionally, and **people do not think while they react.**"
1. "You are wrong," or some version of that.
2. "Change back," communicable in many different ways.
3. "If you do not, these are the consequences."

**[p2] The diagnostic rule is stated twice, the second time hedged.** (i) In the theory section: "This pattern is so predictable that **absence of an emotional reaction is good evidence that the differentiating effort was not successful.**" (ii) At the close of the clinical report: "The other side is the feeling, emotional response, and **if this reaction does not occur there is strong doubt that any differentiation will take place.**" Both are verified verbatim; the first is the stronger form and is the one to model.

Worked example: a couple spent several months on togetherness issues → the husband spent a few weeks thinking about himself, his career, and where he stood on central issues → the wife's anxiety episode lasted about a week as she begged him to return to the togetherness → then a tearful, angry attack accusing him of being "selfish, self-centered, incapable of loving anyone, and an inadequate husband," sure the only answer was divorce → he maintained his calm and stayed close to her → next day the relationship was calm → at the next session: "I liked what you were doing but it made me mad. I wanted to control what I was saying but it had to come out. All the time I was watching you, hoping you would not give in. **I am so glad you did not let me change you.**" → a new, less intense level of togetherness → the wife then started on a self-determined course, with the husband now reacting emotionally to her efforts. Reported new-state statements: "We are much more separate but we are closer. The old love is gone. I miss it sometimes but the new love is calmer and better. I know it sounds crazy but that's how it is."

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Announcing the plan to anyone in the system kills it.** "invites certain failure."
- **Acquiring an ally.** "In the past I had been 'undone' by partners." Allies must be actively detriangled.
- **Hostility or anger in the differentiating one** → loss of objectivity → defending or counterattacking when the issues come back.
- **Withdrawing after the reaction** → the system tightens up again.
- **Doing it for others or for appreciation** → the effort was for togetherness, not differentiation; "an emotional system does not appreciate such stressful nefarious maneuvers in the service of togetherness."
- **Demanding elaboration on the appreciation** → the response reverses into devaluing or denying the event's importance, or into criticism if a complaint is what is sought.
- **Detriangling from one triangle while ignoring the interlocking ones** → tension slips to another triangle. "this pattern had been my undoing." He had "incorrectly assumed that I could differentiate a self from my family of origin by differentiating a self from my parents."
- **Distance and silence masquerading as neutrality** → the system is not fooled; the person is as involved as ever.
- **Passive listening to triangling communications** → "one of the key triggers for my early fusions."
- **Verbal declarations of neutrality without action** → he told his mother he was neutral; "I would leave town and the family would react as if I was on her side." Hence: "Action is required when words fail to detriangle in emotional systems."
- **Working the live reality issue** → converts an emotional-system manifestation into a reality dispute and makes the worker vulnerable to being triangled by it.
- **Taking a stand on ignorable issues** → "quickly labeled a fool."
- **Attempting the move while still inside the system** → "either hollow meaningless words or a hostile assault on the system, and an emotional system knows the difference and reacts accordingly."
- **Distant relationships** → the family can simply ignore the differentiating move; "All too often the family would ignore the effort."
- **No emotional reaction at all** → evidence the effort failed.
- **Open group discussion of family problems** → calmer system, more intense fusion, harder recovery of objectivity.
- **Angry confrontation about grievances** → short-term relief, long-term intensification of the pattern.
- **Emotional emancipation by permanent absence** → "one of the biggest delusions of psychoanalysis that people have worked things out about their families in their analysis and that they don't have to be involved with them any more."
- **The brief formal duty visit** → "an infrequent duty visit is not meaningful contact"; those who make such visits "kid themselves into believing they have 'worked out' the relationship with parents."
- **Failed field assignments Bowen reports from coaching others:** "I have sent people on special missions to parental families and have then had them report that it was impossible to get parents separated, or that there was not an 'ideal time' for talking, or that they had postponed the effort until the last few hours, when the effort turned out to be inept."
- **The united parental front** → deprives the child of individual relationships with each parent and locks him in a two-against-one with no flexibility. Bowen calls it "one of the most unsound psychological principles" and notes "There is evidence that parents automatically invoke this principle because it makes the parents more comfortable and not because it is good for the child."
- **Focusing on the negative side of a triangle** → misidentifies the problem, localises it in one relationship, and "make[s] the triangle more fixed and less reversible."
- **Removing the symptom's focus without changing the process** → the system acts as if the problem is solved; the symptom resurfaces elsewhere.
- **The trainee's own untreated family** → "the family therapist usually has the very same problems in his own family that are present in families he sees professionally, and… he has a responsibility to define himself in his own family if he is to function adequately in his professional work."
- **The system's post-success demand:** "But be sure you do not do or say anything to upset the family" — a demand for a miracle performance which by definition cannot produce differentiation, because differentiation cannot succeed with outside stimulus.
- **[p2] Deliberate perturbation of a calm system is itself hard.** "There are situations that automatically disturb families, just as storms disturb a lake, but if one is trying purposely to disturb the surface of a lake, one finds how difficult it is." This is a stated failure mode of the provocation move as such, independent of the mover's skill.
- **[p2] The telephone is an unusable channel because it has no private path and no feedback.** "I have never been effective in using the telephone for this effort. **There was always the problem of others on extensions and I could not develop effective feedback.**"
- **[p2] A parental pair may be inseparable.** "There are some situations in which the parents fuse into a common self so automatically that it is difficult to establish individual relationships."

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

*Each rule of technique, converted back into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.*

1. **"A differentiating effort routinely fails if anyone else knows anything about it."** → **[p2 — corrected.]** Pass 1 read this as an information-propagation claim ("the family is a near-perfect information channel with respect to intent"). Bowen's own stated reason is different and is about **execution, not leakage**: "each action and move must come from within the person who makes the effort. **These decisions and actions often have to be made instantaneously and, for better or worse, the individual has the responsibility.**" The assumption is therefore (a) that the move requires **sub-deliberative response latency** during the reaction, which a shared plan cannot supply, and (b) that **responsibility for the move must be undivided**, because a co-owned move is a togetherness manoeuvre by construction. The scope is also narrower than pass 1 recorded: the prohibition names "another person **who is part of the system**." Simulation: an act carries an originator-count and loses effect if >1 — the mechanism behind it is response latency and sole ownership, not gossip.
2. **"The element of surprise… is essential."** → Assumes the family has *predictive models of each member* and has pre-computed responses to expected behaviour. Only unmodelled behaviour forces the system to recompute. Surprise is therefore a resource that is spent.
3. **"Permitted no 'allies'… detriangle any ally who tried to come over to my side."** → Assumes the system is a **zero-sum coalition machine**: any two-against-one alignment restores the emotional structure the move is meant to break, *even when the coalition favours the differentiating one*. Being right, with support, is functionally identical to being wrong. Simulation: an ally acquisition should *cancel* differentiation credit, not add to it.
   - **[p2 — the strength of this reading must be marked down.** The cancellation formulation is **my inference**, not Bowen's words. What the text supplies is: a planning rule ("permitted no 'allies'"), an active counter-move ("detriangle any ally who tried to come over to my side"), a stated rationale ("**in preparation for all the potential peripheral triangles that could align themselves with issues and prove difficult**"), a prior-cost report ("In the past I had been 'undone' by partners"), and a treatment-response observation (the sister "retreated from taking sides with me"). The mechanism Bowen actually names is that an ally instantiates a **new peripheral triangle** into which the tension can slip — the exact pattern he calls "my undoing" — so the ally is a *displacement risk*, which is why the countermeasure is "to keep the entire family in one big emotional clump." Whether ally support subtracts from the move's yield, or merely opens an escape route for the tension, the chapter does not say. **Model both as the displacement route; do not implement a flat cancellation term and do not cite it as a quotation.]**
4. **Conflicting messages sent to different segments** → Assumes messages **circulate**: "Messages run back and forth in such a family system as if by telepathy." Each recipient is a broadcaster. It also assumes that segments compare notes, so contradictory content prevents coalition formation.
   - **[p2] Circulation is not automatic — it is gated by whether the recipient can use the message.** "**The only letter that was not shown to a circle of others was the 'Strategic Son' letter to my mother.**" Her reported reaction: "I got the craziest letter. I did not know what to do with it, so I burned it." The one message that would have exposed the plan was the one message that did not propagate, and it was suppressed by the recipient's own inability to place it. Simulation: forwarding probability should depend on the receiver's capacity to fit the message into the emotional field, not be a constant.
5. **"Action is required when words fail to detriangle."** / **"Distance and silence do not fool an emotional system."** → Assumes the family reads *behaviour and position*, not declarations. A stated stance with no behavioural correlate is scored at its behavioural value. Simulation: a `declare_neutral` move with no accompanying action has zero effect and may be scored as siding.
6. **Report the secret to the person it is about, in the next mail.** → Assumes the *secrecy of the triangling message is what carries its force*. Making the message public across the triangle converts a triangling act into a non-event and costs the sender something (his mother "fussed about not being able to trust me"). Simulation: `triangle_in` has a payload that is destroyed by disclosure to the third corner.
7. **"To focus on that would make that issue into a reality issue rather than a manifestation of an emotional system."** → Assumes **content is arbitrary and interchangeable**; the emotional structure is the invariant. Any issue of sufficient salience will do; but a *live, high-stakes* issue draws the worker into the system's own circuits. Simulation: issues have a `stakes` attribute, and high stakes *raise* the mover's probability of fusion.
8. **"A tempest in a teapot" from old issues** → Assumes the family's reactive machinery can be **activated by low-stakes content** and that once activated it exposes the same structure the high-stakes conflict would. Anxiety is a general-purpose activation level, largely independent of what triggered it.
9. **"Absence of an emotional reaction is good evidence that the differentiating effort was not successful."** → Assumes the system's homeostatic response is **obligatory and proportional**. The reaction is the *measurement instrument*. Simulation: a differentiating move must emit a system-reaction signal; a null reaction means the move did not land.
10. **The system reacts identically to a member moving up and a member moving down.** → Assumes homeostasis is **direction-blind**: it defends the current equilibrium, not the family's welfare. Improvement is opposed by the same machinery that opposes regression.
11. **The three-step reaction ("You are wrong" → "Change back" → "If you do not, these are the consequences").** → Assumes the restoration response is a **fixed escalation ladder**, executed in order. Simulation: implement as a state machine, not a scalar pressure.
12. **"If the differentiating one can stay on course without defending self or counterattacking, the emotional reaction is usually brief and the other then expresses appreciation."** → Assumes the reaction is **self-terminating if unreinforced**. Defending and counterattacking are the *inputs that sustain it*. Simulation: the reaction decays unless fed by a `defend` or `counterattack` event from the target.
13. **"Had he yielded… he would have slipped back to her level."** → Assumes the two parties' levels are **coupled**: the outcome of the exchange sets both. A holding position pulls the other up; a yielding position pulls the holder down. Differentiation is a *relational settlement*, not an individual attribute at the moment of change.
14. **"a basic increase in bilateral differentiation which can never return to the former level."** → Assumes basic-level change is **ratcheted and irreversible once achieved through a completed reaction cycle.**
15. **"This is the point where the feeling system dictates withdrawal… which will result in the system 'tightening up' again."** → Assumes the gain is **not consolidated at the moment of the move**; it is consolidated by *continued contact through the reaction*. Simulation: a differentiating move needs a follow-up window; failing to make contact within it reverts the gain.
16. **"Unless the triangled person can remain in emotional contact, the twosome will triangle in someone else."** → Assumes tension is **conserved**: it does not dissipate when one carrier leaves, it recruits a replacement. Withdrawal is not neutrality; it is vacating a slot.
17. **"if the differentiating one requests or demands an elaboration on the initial expression of appreciation, the response is automatically the opposite."** → Assumes appreciation is offered **only while it is unsolicited**; soliciting it reclassifies the whole move as a togetherness bid, retroactively.
18. **"A differentiating effort that is successful has to be for 'self' alone."** → Assumes the system can **discriminate the motive** behind an identical behaviour, and responds to the motive. This is the strongest assumption in the chapter: the same words produce differentiation or hollow manipulation depending on the mover's internal state.
19. **"Until the trainee is partially outside the system, a differentiating technique is either hollow meaningless words or a hostile assault on the system, and an emotional system knows the difference."** → Same assumption, stated as a precondition: the mover's **own fusion level gates whether any technique works at all.** Simulation: technique efficacy is multiplied by the actor's current "outside-ness," which is itself a state variable distinct from basic differentiation.
20. **"Differentiation cannot take place in a vacuum… around issues important to both people… in which the other has to respect the belief and the action stand."** → Assumes differentiation requires a **live, mutually invested relationship** as its medium. Cut-off relationships are inert. Simulation: `differentiate` requires a relationship with non-zero salience for *both* parties.
21. **"A parental family can ignore such detriangling moves if relationships are distant."** → Assumes a **contact threshold** below which the system does not register a move at all. Hence the ordering: person-to-person work first, to raise contact above threshold, *then* detriangling.
22. **"A family system in quiet emotional equilibrium is less amenable to… change than a family system in tension or stress."** → Assumes **plasticity scales with anxiety** — the same anxiety that produces symptoms also makes the structure modifiable. Deaths, illnesses, reunions and weddings are therefore *windows*, and the coach should time interventions to them.
23. **"Frequent short visits are many times more effective than infrequent long visits."** → Assumes the fusion clock resets on exit and that the productive work happens **before fusion sets in**. Contact time is not the resource; number of *pre-fusion intervals* is.
24. **The 200–300 mile optimum.** → Assumes physical distance modulates system pull: too close and you are inside its immediate emotional sphere; too far and you cannot make frequent contact.
25. **"Time with each parent alone is essential… but mere private talk with a single parent can accomplish little."** → Assumes the dyad is the *necessary but not sufficient* condition: the pair will default to its programmed pattern unless a subject outside the family supplies fresh content. Hence family history as the working medium.
26. **"Most people cannot tolerate more than a few minutes on a personal level."** → Assumes a **tolerance budget for dyadic intimacy**, and that the exit routes are exactly two: triangle in a third person, or depersonalise to talking about things. Simulation: person-to-person contact has a duration limit; exceeding it triggers one of two escape moves.
27. **Sickness produces the best person-to-person openings.** → Assumes the tolerance budget is **enlarged by mortality salience** — his two breakthroughs came at a heart attack and a surgery.
28. **Family history works as a subject.** → Assumes the past is **low-threat, high-content**: it is about the family without being about the present relationships, so it does not trip the triangling reflex, and "Most people are eager to talk about their own early life experiences to those interested in listening."
29. **Individual letters, answered at the office where the family never sees them.** → Assumes the family's default communication unit is the **clump**, not the person, and that changing the addressing changes the structure. Bilateral channels have to be constructed; they do not exist by default.
30. **The "stories" technique.** → Assumes every system (a) talks about the absent one and (b) has explicit rules about keeping the gossip secret from him. "This sequence is present in every family system." Breaking rule (b) is the move; the content of the stories is immaterial.
31. **"Reversals" work "predictably if the therapist is 'outside' the emotional system and can be sufficiently casual and detached."** → Assumes advice in the direction the person is already straining is **inert** (it is what he already tells himself), whereas advice pushed past that point is received as position-taking rather than instruction. Efficacy again gated on the mover's outside-ness.
32. **The four therapist functions with a couple** → Assume the spouses cannot hear each other directly ("the automatic emotional bind that develops in exchanges between them"), so the therapist must be an **obligatory relay**, and that an emotional field can be held at an intermediate temperature — hot enough to be real, cool enough to be thought about.
33. **"the quickest way to modify the central triangle is to constitute a new triangle with the two primary members of the family and the therapist."** → Assumes an outside corner can be **substituted**, and that a corner occupied by someone who will not take sides drains the twosome's intensity. A therapist is not an addition to the system; he is a *replacement occupant of a slot*.
34. **"When the family triangle includes three or more from the natural family, the emotional system runs its own built-in emotional circuits."** → Assumes a **circuit-count effect**: the more family members present, the more paths tension can take, and the longer observation and modification take. Fewer natural-family members in the room = faster change.
35. **"If the central triangle changes, and it stays in contact with others, the entire system will automatically change."** → Assumes the triangle network is **connected and propagating**, and that propagation requires maintained contact. Change is not local.
36. **"it is easier for the system to ignore a more peripheral or less important triangle."** → Assumes triangles are **weighted** and that propagation from a low-weight triangle can be absorbed without system change.
37. **Working the *interlocking* set, not one triangle.** → Assumes tension **displaces to the adjacent triangle** when one is closed; so a plan must cover the neighbours in advance. Bowen designed an issue for every key member for exactly this reason.
38. **"An issue for all key family members, especially issues that would touch each relationship cleanly."** → Assumes relationships can be addressed **individually and simultaneously** without the addresses bleeding into each other — provided each is clean.
39. **"the goal was to create a situation in which my brother would seek contact with me."** → Assumes an avoiding member can be **made to approach** by raising the cost of avoidance, and that approach initiated by the avoider is qualitatively different from contact he is pursued into.
40. **The letter closing: "it would not be necessary to see him unless he had something to say to me."** → Assumes releasing the other from obligation, while remaining reachable, is what makes approach his own move.
41. **"The letter was written and rewritten in order to eliminate hostile or derogatory comments."** → Assumes the family will **hurl the issues back**, so the mover's own text is the ammunition he will face. Objectivity must be built *into the artifact* before it is sent, because it cannot be manufactured under fire.
42. **"I played and replayed these issues so many times that… it was impossible for me to be angry with anyone."** → Assumes reactivity is **extinguishable by rehearsal**. Repeated exposure to one's own charged material, in private, discharges it. **[p2 — correction: this is not "the only method the chapter gives," as pass 1 claimed. It is the only *fast, move-scoped* one. The chapter also gives the observation/control ratchet and practice on peripheral emotional systems, both slow and cumulative — see B3 [p2].]** The rehearsal is also stated as producing a positive residue, not merely a null: "In fact, I had only heightened respect for my second brother, who had functioned so well as 'head of the clan' at home."
43. **The calendar spacing (letter T–14, call T–7, counter-letters within the hour, arrival T+0).** → Assumes the reaction has a **propagation and ripening time** across the network, and that the mover needs a reading of it *before* arriving. Two weeks was enough for the letter to be shown around and for the parents to be won over.
44. **"I had purposely stayed close to my parents all morning, hoping to facilitate a meeting."** → Assumes co-location is a controllable variable that raises the probability of the target configuration assembling.
45. **The in-meeting rules (never defend, never attack, never anger, instant casual response).** → Assumes the family's escalation depends on **getting a reactive response**, and that a casual, immediate, non-serious response is the specific thing that fails to feed it. Latency matters: a delayed answer is a reactive answer.
46. **The absurdist replies (booze prices, prosecute whoever started the story, "some really good ones about me if you had just listened better").** → Assumes an accusation only has force if its frame is accepted. Answering inside the frame but without its seriousness dissolves it.
47. **"About two-thirds through the meeting… the family system had lost its emotional punch."** → Assumes the system has a **finite charge per episode** that discharges against a non-reactive target and does not regenerate within the episode.
48. **"one has to continue in relationship with the family system… it is necessary to keep talking to the system."** → Assumes the system's re-tightening is the **default trajectory** after a disturbance, and only continued contact prevents it.
49. **The Monday compliment ("his intentions were good most of the time") after the refusal of stories.** → Assumes a refusal must be **respected explicitly** and then replaced with an offering, and that this is the transition from reaction to person-to-person contact.
50. **"Now they flowed smoothly and automatically, and I no longer had to discipline myself to do them."** → Assumes technique becomes **automatic past a threshold of differentiation** — that effortfulness is itself a marker of insufficient outside-ness.
51. **"any progress made with one's parental family is automatically translated into the nuclear family."** → Assumes the person carries **one differentiation level** across all his systems; it is not per-relationship. Simulation: `Person.differentiation` is global; relationship-level state is separate.
52. **"It is easier to make valid observations of emotional forces in the more removed, but equally important, parental family… It is also easier to take an action stand in the parental family."** → Assumes **observability and actionability decrease with dependency**. The nuclear family is harder precisely because one's needs are more intimately embedded in it.
53. **"the parental family effort requires that the trainee more quickly accept responsibility for his own life… A trainee is more on his own resources when he deals with the emotional reaction in his own family than when he sees his therapist with his spouse."** → Assumes the *absence* of a therapist in the room is itself therapeutic — that a present therapist absorbs responsibility that would otherwise be forced onto the person.
54. **"differentiation is a self-motivated, self-energized effort and it cannot succeed with outside stimulus."** → Assumes motivation cannot be supplied externally. Simulation: a coach can supply information, targeting and timing, but the *initiative* must be endogenous, and a move triggered by a coach's push is not a differentiating move.
55. **"It requires that the single members be self-supporting, else they never develop the emotional courage for change that might threaten the family attitude about them."** → Assumes **material dependency caps achievable differentiation**: a move that risks the family's regard is unavailable to someone who needs the family's support.
56. **"It works best with oldest children who usually feel more responsibility for their families."** → Assumes sibling position sets the baseline probability that a member will attempt the move at all.
57. **The united-front prohibition.** → Assumes a child's development requires **two separable parental relationships**, and that a fused parental pair is a structural deprivation, not merely a stylistic choice. Assumes further that even a very young child "is capable of managing a relationship to either parent."
58. **"Once the effort goes toward developing an individual relationship between each parent and the child, it is possible to see the intensity of the parental effort to reestablish the 'parental we-ness.'"** → Assumes the we-ness is **actively maintained**, with measurable restoration pressure — which makes it an observable in the model, not an assumption.
59. **"the negative side of the triangle is merely a symptomatic expression of a total family problem."** → Assumes the observable conflict is a **readout, not a cause**. Treating the readout as the cause fixes the triangle.
60. **The therapist must stay detriangled "no matter what the subject of discussion."** → Assumes therapeutic content is nearly irrelevant compared to the therapist's structural position. The *only* active ingredient claimed is detriangled contact.
61. **"about 100 hours spread over a period of four or five years… More frequent appointments do not increase the capacity to observe."** → Assumes the rate limit is **field experience between sessions**, not session count. Learning happens in the family, not in the office. Simulation: coaching sessions have diminishing returns per unit time; family-contact events are the state-advancing ones.
62. **[p2] "Conflict between siblings consists almost universally of a triangle between mother and two children in which mother has a positive relationship to each child and the conflict is fought out between the children."** → Assumes **no conflict edge is generated locally**. A visible dyadic conflict is a displacement readout of a triangle one level up, exactly as "the basic problem which exists on the highest administrative level is triangled and retriangled again and again until the conflict surfaces between two employees low in the administrative hierarchy." Simulation: a conflict event between two peers must instantiate, not merely reference, the parent-level triangle that sourced it — and intervening on the peer pair should therefore fail.
63. **[p2] "As the distance between my second brother and me increased, the stories about him increased."** → Assumes traffic about a node is **conserved and rerouted**, not attenuated, when the direct edge closes. Cutting contact converts direct communication into triangling communication at roughly constant volume. Simulation: closing an edge should raise indirect message volume about its endpoints, which is C3 stated at the communication layer rather than the symptom layer.
64. **[p2] "the anxiety could amplify minor problems into major ones at vulnerable points in my family."** → Assumes the shock wave is a **gain applied to pre-existing minor problems at nodes the family's absorption profile already identifies**, not a generator of new pathology. This is why "These areas provide clues about symptoms when family stress mounted" is a forecasting rule. Simulation: a wave event multiplies existing symptom load at the family's named sink, and produces nothing at a family with no loaded sink.
65. **[p2] "if one is trying purposely to disturb the surface of a lake, one finds how difficult it is."** → Assumes deliberate perturbation has a **low base success rate**, in direct tension with the ease of accidental perturbation ("situations that automatically disturb families"). Simulation: a `PROVOKE` move must be expensive and unreliable, while exogenous nodal events perturb for free. A model in which the coach can cheaply raise system anxiety on demand contradicts this.
66. **[p2] "The less differentiated family segments still tended to reply with letters to my entire family."** → Assumes converting a clump edge into a person edge **requires a minimum level in the receiving segment**. Simulation: `INDIVIDUATE_CHANNEL` succeeds probabilistically as a function of the receiver's level, not the sender's effort.
67. **[p2] The per-parent escape route (father → "Mother thinks…"; mother → talk about others), with per-parent counter-aims.** → Assumes the two escape routes from person-to-person contact are **stable dispositions of the person**, not draws from a distribution. Simulation: each Person carries a preferred escape route, and the effective counter-move differs accordingly (keep the conversation alive vs. eliminate the triangles).
68. **[p2] The uncle's outside position "caused no problems for him since he had the close relationship with his mother."** → Assumes the cost of occupying the outside corner is **offset by a strong tie outside the triangle**. Simulation: outside-position discomfort is net of the actor's other tie strengths, not a property of the corner alone.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Basic level is nearly immutable vs. differentiation moves produce basic change.** The scale section says "few [life experiences] can change the basic level of differentiation acquired while people are still with their parental families. Unless there is some unusual circumstance, the basic level from their parental family is consolidated in a marriage, following which the only shift is a functional shift." But the differentiation section says a successful holding of position produces "a basic increase in bilateral differentiation which can never return to the former level," and Bowen describes his own breakthrough as a change in kind, not a functional shift.
  - **[p2 — re-checked end to end. The chapter reconciles them nowhere.** The two statements sit in different sections (the scale concept, and "The differentiation of a self"), roughly 5,000 words apart, and neither refers to the other. Two textual hooks exist and both are hedges rather than reconciliations: **"few"** experiences can change the basic level, which is not *none*, so the differentiating move could be among the few; and **"unless there is some unusual circumstance."** Bowen never says the differentiating move is that circumstance, and never says a completed exchange is one of the "few." He also never gives a magnitude for the basic increase. The reading that deliberate, coached differentiation *is* the unusual circumstance is **my inference and must stay labelled as one.** A third, weaker hook is that the "consolidated in a marriage" claim is stated about *basic level acquired in the parental family*, whereas the bilateral increase is stated about a *marital exchange in therapy* — so the two could be read as describing different life stages, but the chapter does not draw that line either.**]
- **Terminology superseded within the chapter.** "I originally used the term *undifferentiated family ego mass*… the term is less apt in referring to the same phenomenon in the extended families, and the term is awkward when applied to… work, or… social systems. More recently the term *emotional system* has been used to designate the same triangular emotional patterns." Yet he goes on using "undifferentiated family ego mass" throughout the clinical report.
- **Method priority reversed.** He previously "considered formal psychotherapy with husband and wife together to be the most effective of all methods" and "urged members of the mental health professions to have formal family psychotherapy themselves as the best possible preparation." After the breakthrough, trainees who worked only on their families of origin "had automatically made as much, or even more progress, with their spouses and children as similar trainees who had been in formal family psychotherapy with their spouses for the same period of time," leading to: "psychotherapy as we have known it in the past may one day be considered superfluous."
- **Emphasis on person-to-person relationships downgraded.** "In this report, I have put more emphasis on the person-to-person relationship in relation to triangles than I do in my current work in 'coaching' others with their families" — one reason being that person-to-person was developed before detriangling was well understood.
- **His own earlier assumption explicitly repudiated.** "Up to this point in my family effort, I had incorrectly assumed that I could differentiate a self from my family of origin by differentiating a self from my parents… The notion about interlocking triangles had been in use almost ten years but I had not integrated this aspect of the theory into the work with my own family."
- **His own "detachment" repudiated.** "My posture to my family of origin during this period was one of kidding myself that I was 'detached' and 'objective'… Actually, I was almost as emotionally involved as ever."
- **Psychoanalytic emancipation repudiated.** "At the time I considered these confrontations to be emotional emancipation… the long-term result was an intensification of previous patterns." And in discussion: "I believe it is one of the biggest delusions of psychoanalysis that people have worked things out about their families in their analysis and that they don't have to be involved with them any more."
- **The united front repudiated.** "Before family research I believed that parents tended to become divided… and it was necessary to remind them… to present a united front… With family research I developed the conviction that this dictum is one of the most unsound psychological principles."
- **The original research hypothesis repudiated as too narrow.** "Our original hypothesis about the mother-patient relationship proved to be amazingly accurate in predicting the details of the relationship within this twosome, but it had completely omitted the observations about the way the twosome related to others."
- **The scale's own top end revised downward by experience.** Originally 100 was for a being perfect at emotional, cellular and physiological levels, with historical figures expected in the mid-90 range; "Increasing experience with the scale indicates that all people have areas of good functioning and essential areas in which life functioning is poor," and now 75 counts as very high level.
- **[p2] His own site of work oscillated before it settled.** "Most of the effort went to my own nuclear family (my wife and children)… I considered my family of origin as important in understanding my nuclear family, but less important in helping the nuclear family resolve its problems. Very early in clinical work with families, I tried to correlate each pattern in my nuclear family with similar patterns in family of origin. This effort was followed by **a short period of precise focus on my nuclear family, with the premise that focus on the family of origin avoided the most important issues in my nuclear family.** Gradually I focused more and more on my family of origin." Pass 1 recorded only the post-breakthrough reversal; the earlier swing in the opposite direction is also on the record, and it names the objection to family-of-origin work in Bowen's own voice.
- **Discussants dispute the concept itself.** Rubinstein questions whether "detriangling" is destruction, removal, or level-change of a triangle. Bell argues that triangles are trivially derivable from any figure and that "there is no triangle that you can triangulate here without taking into account a much broader context," proposing cluster or pattern analysis instead. Minuchin says Bowen "was not talking about a triangle, because he was not dealing with geometry," and calls the family enmeshed, arguing that in disengaged families "the process is not one of detriangulation, of differentiation, of getting out, but a process of reestablishing and creating units." Bowen answers none of these directly.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The theory's development rule, stated as research method.** When observations contradicted the hypothesis, "the hypothesis was modified to fit the new facts, the psychotherapy was modified to fit the hypothesis, and new predictions were made about the results of the psychotherapy." Crises were handled on interim clinical judgment "but the hypothesis was considered at fault for not 'knowing' about the situation ahead of time." Critically: "**Any failure to change in psychotherapy was as much a reason to reexamine and change the hypothesis as any other unpredicted change.**" Explicitly modelled on the space programme, where each probe's findings feed the next.
- **Stipulated definitions of "concept" and "theory"** (see A5) — an internal quality bar: a body of thinking becomes a "concept" only when it no longer requires significant modification, describes and predicts, and "can explain discrepancies as well as consistencies."
- **The whole presentation was itself a differentiating move on a second system.** Bowen states the conference format was chosen as "my continuing effort to differentiate my 'self' from the 'family' of family therapists," and that he expected "some of the same reactions from the participants of the conference as I had gotten from my own family members." The footnote records him detriangling Whitaker in real time.
- **A therapist's theoretical position is claimed to be a function of his own emotional functioning.** "I believe a major part of this problem has to do with the theoretical orientation and emotional functioning of the therapists. My theory is best understood if the therapist can listen and observe and function from a position at least partially 'outside' the emotional field of the family. Conventional theory and psychotherapy teaches and trains therapists to operate 'inside' the emotional field."
- **The inside/outside distinction is presented as the whole difficulty of transmitting the theory.** "many in my audiences hear the words that go with the theory without really grasping the concepts," and "frequently they perceive the psychotherapy as an intuitive method that goes with my personality rather than as a method determined by theory." The paper's stated purpose is to disprove the intuition reading.
- **The quoted law from the undelivered paper** (via Weakland): Bowen believes "the laws that govern man's emotional function are as orderly as those that govern other natural systems, and our difficulty in understanding the system is not so much in the complexity of the system as in **man's denial of the system**."
- **The claimed epistemic result of the experience** is not clinical but structural: "To me the most important long-term accomplishment was the proof that **an emotional system has a knowable structure and function, and that one can work out the predictable answers to its problems on a drawing board**."
- **No disabling illnesses, accidents or injuries in any of the five children, their spouses, or their children** — stated as a family-level fact.
- **The family's absorption profile is named explicitly:** "In my parental family the level of conflict is very low, the primary mechanism is projection to a child (lower life adjustment of oldest sister), and the other mechanism is physical illness, usually brief medical or surgical illnesses. **These areas provide clues about symptoms when family stress mounted.**" This is a *forecasting rule*: know the family's absorption profile and you know where the next symptom will appear.
- **Symptom prediction failed on channel, succeeded on timing.** "I had expected the 'anxiety wave' issues that autumn to be expressed more as illness" — the wave produced conflict first, then illness (the disc).
- **Factors delaying the emergence of a predicted pattern:** "the basic adaptability of the family (conflict does not occur between people if the adaptability is good), the absence of stress of sufficient degree to cause symptoms to surface, and **the number of subtriangles to absorb minor levels of disharmony**." A direct statement that buffering capacity scales with network size.
- **Mild symptoms under stress are a positive sign.** "The mild symptom expressed only under stress is evidence of a good level of emotional compensation."
- **A taxonomy of fusion styles** — none of these are named concepts here, but they are enumerated as observed types: "comfortably" fused; "uncomfortably" fused, using "hate or a covert negative attitude (either is evidence of fusion) to avoid contact with parents"; "positive fusion" who "remain so attached they never leave home"; and those who "kid themselves into believing they have 'worked out' the relationship with parents and who make brief formal visits home without personal communication; they use as evidence of maturity that they do not see their parents." **This is emotional cutoff described in full, before it was a concept.**
- **A conditional model of the projection-recipient's ceiling.** "Though the impairment in my first sister did not go beyond overall poor functioning in her life course, the emotional pattern is the same as other families in which the most involved child is severely impaired. **With less basic differentiation in my parents and more stress in the family emotional system, this daughter could have later developed severe disabling emotional or physical problems.**" Same mechanism, severity set by two inputs: parental level and stress.
- **The differentiating one becomes a system resource.** The post-differentiation role is a stable structural change in the family, and it is claimed to generalise: "I have had increasing experience with this phenomenon with others and the usual pattern is similar to that in my family."
- **Effect propagation through a changed relationship.** Bowen's claim about his father: changing *his own* relationship with his father "in turn changed his relationship to all he contacted." A single edge change propagating to all of a node's other edges.
- **Genealogy's argued value:** "In only 150 to 200 years an individual is the descendent of 64 to 128 families of origin, each of which has contributed something to one's self." Its use is de-mythologising: "More knowledge of one's distant families of origin can help one become aware that there are no angels or devils in a family; they were human beings, each with his own strengths and weaknesses, **each reacting predictably to the emotional issue of the moment**, and each doing the best he could with his life course."
- **The genealogists' surprise** that he "was as interested in family members who did poorly as those who did well."
- **Life goals differ by scale position.** Lower half: "Primary life goals are oriented around love, happiness, comfort, and security; these goals come closest to fulfillment when relationships with others are in equilibrium. So much life energy goes into seeking love and approval, or attacking the other for not providing it, that there is little energy left for self-determined, goal-directed activity." They "do not distinguish between 'truth' and 'fact,' and the inner feeling state is the most accurate possible expression of truth. A sincere person is regarded as one who freely communicates the feeling process."
- **A relationship-capacity claim at the top of the scale.** "high-scale people are free to engage in goal-directed activity, **or to lose 'self' in the intimacy of a close relationship**, in contrast to low-scale people who either have to avoid relationships lest they slip automatically into an uncomfortable fusion, or have no choice but continued pursuit of a close relationship for gratification of emotional 'needs.'" Intimacy is *available* at high levels and *compulsory or forbidden* at low ones.
- **Self-estimation accuracy is scale-dependent.** "The high-scale person is less reactive to praise or criticism and he has a more realistic evaluation of his own self in contrast to the lower-level person whose evaluation is either far above or far below reality."
- **The functional/basic gap can be large and can look permanent.** "a wife who had a functional level at marriage equal to her husband's may become de-selfed to the point of chronic alcoholism. She then functions far below her original level while the husband functions equally far above his original level. Many of these functional levels are sufficiently consolidated so that they can appear much like basic levels to the inexperienced." Note the **conservation implication**: her loss equals his gain.
- **Whitaker's counter-method,** offered in discussion: co-therapy as a continuous detriangling device — "we are constantly free to detriangulate from moment to moment" — and confusion as the signal to reach for the co-therapist.
- **Weiner's practice change,** offered in discussion: "I have also begun to send patients back to their families to relate to their families, and I feel that, in a sense, this gets me out of this triangle that exists between the patient, myself, and the patient's family… we both get detriangled."
- **Openness as maturation, at family scale:** "As families move from the compartmentalized, less mature world of secrets and foibles which they assume they are keeping under cover, and into the world of permitting their private lives to be more open and a possible example for others to follow, they grow up a little each day."
- **[p2] Bowen's own operational success metric, stated as the thing that had *not* moved.** Before the breakthrough: "I still had not significantly increased **the length of time before I would become 'fused' into the family system when I visited**, nor had I found a way **to extricate myself before the visit had ended**." Two measurable quantities — time-to-fusion on contact, and mid-episode recoverability — and both are readouts a simulation can produce directly. The work-system figures bracket them: 30 minutes before, no fusion at all afterwards.
- **[p2] The tension estimate is built from breadth of contact, not depth.** "With various versions of different issues being discussed in four separate households, and with me in reasonably good contact with them all, it was possible to keep a good reading of the family emotional tension." The observer's estimate of system tension requires simultaneous contact with several nodes; a single-node observer cannot form it.
- **[p2] The functional/basic confusion is named as a trap for the observer.** "Many of these functional levels are sufficiently consolidated so that **they can appear much like basic levels to the inexperienced.**" With "Few people can be objective about their parents… without either downgrading or upgrading them," this chapter is the source of the measurement-bias claim Ch17 states as "false readings when one attempts to estimate levels of differentiation."
- **[p2] The one-week malignancy scare is a timed instance of load keyed to structural importance, not to severity.** "Since the 'go power' for the family rested with him, anxiety went very high for a week until the possibility of malignancy was ruled out." The threat was to the functionally central member and was ruled out; the load was carried anyway.

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

**P1.1 — "50–75 is the only band with a behavioural description" is wrong.**
It is the only *numerically bounded* band with one. The chapter gives a longer behavioural profile of **the lower half**, a shorter one of **the upper half**, and a distinct one of **"high-scale people."** Pass 1's claim propagated into the ledger (L21.1) and into `_CONVERGENCES.md`'s terminology table, and it made Ch21 look poorer in scale content than it is. The corrected picture: Ch21 has *three* descriptive strata (lower half / upper half / high-scale) plus one bounded band (50–75), and the strata boundaries are described as gradual — **there is no qualitative regime change at 50 here.** Ch16's threshold at 50 is a later addition, not a restatement.

**P1.2 — The ally rule was over-read, and the over-reading is the one heading into the model.**
Pass 1's ledger says "support from a family member **cancels** the move rather than helping it," and `_CONVERGENCES.md` C9 repeats it as a finding. Bowen never says this. What he says: he "worked out a plan that permitted no 'allies'"; that the purpose was to handle "**all the potential peripheral triangles that could align themselves with issues and prove difficult**"; that the countermeasure was "to keep the entire family in one big emotional clump, and to detriangle any ally who tried to come over to my side"; that "in the past I had been 'undone' by partners"; and that the conflicting letters "were designed to prevent any one segment of the family from getting on my 'side.'" The named mechanism is **displacement**, not cancellation: an ally opens a new peripheral triangle, which is precisely the failure he calls "my undoing." Active detriangling of an ally is verified verbatim and can go into the model as stated. **The cancellation term cannot, and must not be cited as a quotation.**

**P1.3 — The stated reason for secrecy is wrong in pass 1.**
Pass 1's B5 #1 explains the secrecy rule by the family being "a near-perfect information channel with respect to intent." Bowen's own reason, in the same sentence as the rule, is about execution: "each action and move must come from within the person who makes the effort. **These decisions and actions often have to be made instantaneously and, for better or worse, the individual has the responsibility.**" Two mechanisms, not one — sub-deliberative latency during the reaction, and undivided ownership. Pass 1 also flattened the scope: the absolute "anyone else knows anything about it" is narrowed in the next sentence to "another person **who is part of the system**," and the rule is restated a third time at the close in hedged form ("**it is doubtful that** any differentiation will result").

**P1.4 — "The only method the chapter gives for raising outside-ness" is wrong.**
Private rehearsal is the only *fast, move-scoped* method. The chapter gives two more: the **observation/control ratchet** ("Observation is not possible until one can control one's reactions sufficiently to be able to observe… which in turn, in a series of slow steps, allows for better observation") and **practice on a peripheral emotional system** ("A partial success in a more peripheral emotional system would contribute something to the effort with my family of origin"). Three mechanisms on two timescales. Pass 1's model note ("the only stated mechanism") would have produced a model where outside-ness has exactly one input.

**P1.5 — A whole therapeutic configuration was missed.**
B1 recorded two: both spouses/parents, and one motivated family member. The chapter has **three**, each with its own heading — the middle one is **"Family psychotherapy with one spouse in preparation for family psychotherapy with both spouses,"** for families "in which one spouse is negative and unwilling to be involved," whose goal is to work the motivated spouse "**until the unmotivated spouse is willing to join.**" This changes the reading of the single-member method: it is an on-ramp as often as a substitute. It also completes A6's truncated quotation — "In the average family, if I can get the couple to cooperate, then I do it with them, **and if I can't, then I work with the motivated one**" — which reverses pass 1's implication that the single motivated member is the preferred target.

**P1.6 — The chapter's cleanest cross-system result was omitted.**
Pass 1 recorded that the old clinic re-fused him in thirty minutes after two or three years away. It omitted what happens next: "**Finally, after I had mastered the experience with my own family that is reported here, I returned to the old work system for a long visit and was able to relate intimately to those important to the system without a single episode of 'fusion.'**" That is a before/after on a system he never worked on, and it is the strongest evidence in the corpus that differentiation is a per-person scalar that transfers (L10.6, L22.4).

**P1.7 — Sibling conflict is not dyadic, and pass 1 has no entry for it.**
"Conflict between siblings consists **almost universally** of a triangle between mother and two children in which mother has a positive relationship to each child and the conflict is fought out between the children." This is the same displacement rule as the administrative-hierarchy statement pass 1 *did* record, applied one level down, and it is a hard constraint: a peer-conflict event in the model must instantiate the parent-level triangle, and intervening on the peer pair alone must fail.

**P1.8 — The anxiety wave's source was mis-described and its mechanism omitted.**
The deceased was **the second brother's wife's brother**, not Bowen's brother-in-law; the wave enters the family from outside its own boundary, through a marital link, from a family of origin that is not the one being modelled. More importantly, pass 1 recorded the observed sequence but not the *reasoned* one Bowen states in advance — five hops, ending in the rule that matters: "the anxiety could **amplify minor problems into major ones at vulnerable points** in my family." ## P2. What is NEW

Material in the chapter that no pass-1 entry, in this file or any other, contains.

1. **Two separable success criteria with different completion times and different failure tolerances.** Interrupting the anxiety wave was "my **primary mission**," was achieved "even by the time the family meeting was well under way," and survives partial failure — "Even if I had been slightly or moderately triangled, I would have more than achieved my primary mission." 4. **Message intensity is a control dial separate from content.** "I used words such as *terrible, awful, pressing,* and *horrible*… These words were all designed to stir up the 'tempest in a teapot.'"
