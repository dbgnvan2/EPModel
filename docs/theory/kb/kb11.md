# KB11 — Towards a Systems Concept of Supernatural Phenomena

**Cite `KB11`. ASR source — no verbatim quotation.** Most of this interview is a **proposed ninth concept**, first presented November 1980, and it is speculative throughout. It contains one correction that matters.

## ⚠⚠ K11.1 — "Eight to ten generations" was rhetorical, not a count — a calibration target must be withdrawn

> "That was the place I introduced the notion of **ten generations** for the creation of schizophrenia, which I put in **not to say that it takes ten generations**. Previously I'd said it took only three, but that was sort of to convey the notion that **it's not just three and just ten, but it is a multi-generational thing**."

*Model consequence:* `_LEDGER.md` · `L16.3` records "**eight to ten generations** to schizophrenia — revised **up** from 'at least three'", and `model_explainer.md` §8 lists it among the **durations usable as calibration targets**. **KB11 says the number was chosen to convey *multigenerational*, not to state a count.**

This is the same failure mode as the two rates withdrawn at pass 2 — a figure offered illustratively and then treated as data. **Withdraw it as a calibration target** in revision 2; what survives is the direction (many generations, not three) and nothing numeric.

## K11.2 — The multi-factor necessity structure `[M]`

The hurricane analogy: "it might take **six separate atmospheric conditions** to spawn a hurricane, and if **any one** of those is missing, the hurricane doesn't develop." Applied to cancer, with emotional tension as one of the necessary conditions.

*Model consequence:* the *structure* is useful and the model does not have it — a symptom requiring the **conjunction** of several conditions, so removing any one prevents it. That is a different shape from the model's accumulate-to-threshold symptom onset, and it is worth considering as an alternative. **The application to cancer is not usable** (see below).

## ⚠ K11.3 — The cancer claims must not be implemented `[X]`

A 1955 "striking cure of cancer with psychotherapy" — with the oncologists' response recorded honestly: "the cancer people believed that they had **not made an accurate diagnosis**." One "striking success with asymptomatic fibroids". The hypothesis that "in an ideal emotional environment, the **tumor process can regress** and go back where it came from", which he says he has never found reason to reverse.

Also recorded: a sequence in which several colleagues from one 1977 conference developed cancers, and Bateson's apparent regression, which prompted much of the thinking.

*Recorded so it is not adopted.* Single anecdotal cases, a contested diagnosis, and a hypothesis the author acknowledges he has only ever confirmed. **No emotional-state-to-physical-disease mechanism may be implemented on this basis.** The model's endogenous physical-symptom channel must rest on the routing findings, not on this.

## K11.4 — A ninth concept was proposed and never consolidated `[T]`

Presented November 1980; in March 1980 he had first said publicly that systems theory "would perhaps **50 years from now** be able to scientifically account for the biological, the psychological, and the **spiritual** phenomena", tracing it back to a 1957 statement that schizophrenia could not be understood until biological, psychological and biochemical phenomena shared one frame.

*Model consequence:* the corpus's eight concepts are what the model implements. **A ninth was proposed late and did not consolidate**, which is worth recording alongside `L21.5`'s six and `L16.1`'s eight — the concept list was never closed.
