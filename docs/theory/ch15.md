# Ch15 — Family Reaction to Death

## 0. Provenance
- Stated date / venue (from the text itself): **not stated.** The chapter carries no presentation venue, conference, or publication date. Only internal time-markers are given: "My clinical experience with death goes back some thirty years"; the Emotional Shock Wave was "first encountered in the author's family research in the late 1950s"; "Until the mid 1960s, a majority of physicians were opposed to telling patients they had a terminal illness. In the past decade the prevailing medical dictum about this has changed"; "Some twenty-five years ago, I had a clinical experience…"; "In twenty years of family practice…"; "It has now been twelve years since the death." Taken together these place the writing in the mid-1970s, but Bowen does not say so.
- Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period): Bowen's own family therapy practice "within a medical center"; "In twenty years of family practice, I have had contact with several thousand families, and I have been in the background 'coaching' families through hundreds of deaths and funerals." Also: detailed death discussions with suicidal patients going back thirty years; seriously ill people in his practice, friends, social acquaintances, and members of his own extended family; comatose patients; multigenerational family research at the Georgetown Family Section, where a check for the shock wave "is done routinely in all family histories." Two extended cases are reported: (a) a one-off demonstration interview with a terminally ill mother and her family, done as a visiting professor at another medical center before a large professional audience; (b) a neighbour family (not patients) coached through the sudden death of the young mother, followed for twelve years.
- Genre: **both**, weighted to technique. Roughly **45% theory / 55% technique**. The theory content is concentrated in the opening (open vs. closed relationship systems) and the "Family Emotional Equilibrium and The Emotional Shock Wave" section; the second half ("Therapy At The Time Of Death", "The Function Of Funerals") is almost entirely prescriptive, with the theory carried implicitly inside the prescriptions.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Terminally ill people know, and say nothing.** "From experience, every terminally ill patient has some awareness of impending death and a high percentage have an extensive amount of private knowledge they do not communicate to anyone." → observed in seriously ill people in Bowen's medical-centre practice.
- **A high percentage of people die alone**, "locked into their own thoughts which they cannot communicate to others." → dying patients generally.
- **Three simultaneous closed communication systems around one dying patient** — inside the patient, inside the family, and inside the physician/medical staff — each editing what it passes to the others. The family produces "its own carefully planned and edited medical communiqué for the patient," plus other versions "whispered within the hearing of the patient when the family thinks the patient is sleeping or unconscious." → observed in a medical centre where Bowen had contact with patient, family, and physicians.
- **Patients hear the whispers.** "Patients are often alert to whispered communications."
- **Relationships close over time inside a marriage.** Courtship is "the most open relationship that most people have in their adult lives"; after marriage each spouse becomes sensitive to what upsets the other, avoids those subjects, and the relationship "shifts toward a more 'closed' system." → general adult population.
- **Rule change without behaviour change in medicine.** After the mid-1960s the medical dictum shifted toward telling patients, but "medical practice has not kept pace with the changed attitude"; communications between physician, patient, and family "are still very much as they were before."
- **Correct medical care plus poor communication produces malpractice anger.** "Repeated situations in which the physicians thought they were communicating clearly, but the family either misperceived or distorted the messages, and the family thinking would be working itself toward malpractice anger at the physician." In all of these "the surgical and medical procedures were adequate."
- **Comatose patients can surface for a communication.** "Terminally ill people often permit themselves to slip into coma. A fair percentage can pull themselves out of the coma for important communications" — and then slip back.
- **Nobody was harmed by being talked to about death.** "I have never seen a terminally ill person who was not strengthened by such a talk. This contradicts former beliefs about the ego being too fragile for this in certain situations."
- **The Emotional Shock Wave itself:** "a series of major life events occurred in multiple, separate members of the extended family in the time interval after the serious illness and death of a significant family member." First noticed in multigenerational family research in the late 1950s; initially looked like coincidence; then found in "a sufficiently high percentage of all families" that a check for it became routine.
- **The symptoms land in people who were not close to the dead person.** After the death of an important grandparent, symptoms appear across a spectrum of children and grandchildren, and "the grandchild is often one who had little direct emotional attachment to the grandparents."
- **The family denies the connection, and denies it harder when a therapist points at it.** "The family so camouflages the connectedness of these events that family members will further camouflage the sequence of events if they become aware the therapist is seeking some connectedness. Families are extremely reactive to any effort to approach the denial directly."
- **Only one family in Bowen's experience ever made the connection on its own** before seeking therapy (the father who dated his family's troubles to his daughter's marriage two years earlier).
- **Unresolved attachment survives the death indefinitely.** A young woman opened psychoanalysis with "Let me bury my mother before we go to other things" — six years after the mother's death — and "cried for weeks."
- **Children respond to the survivor's emotion, not to the death.** In the first hours after the young mother's death, "the children had been responding to his emotionality rather than to the fact of the mother's death," and told their crying father "Please don't cry, Daddy."
- **Professional audiences split when a death is discussed openly.** After the demonstration interview, "Part of the group had been moved to tears, most were silent and serious, and a few were critical" — one young physician objecting to "having taken away her hope."

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Open vs. closed relationship system** → An open system is one in which "an individual is free to communicate a high percentage of inner thoughts, feelings, and fantasies to another who can reciprocate." No relationship is completely open; health is having *one* relationship with a reasonable degree of openness. Closing is not a decision: "The closed communication system is an automatic emotional reflex to protect self from the anxiety in the other person" — although people report it as protecting the other person. Acts on: every dyad, especially spouses and patient–family–physician.
- **Anxiety-driven topic avoidance** → each person detects anxiety in the other and reflexively removes the topic from the channel. Death is "chief among all taboo subjects." Acts on: the topic inventory of a relationship, which shrinks monotonically as anxiety accumulates.
- **Intellect vs. reflex** → "If people could follow intellectual knowledge instead of the automatic reflex, and they could gain some control over their own reactiveness to anxiety in the other, they would be able to talk about taboo subjects in spite of the anxiety, and the relationship would move toward a more healthy openness." The reflex normally wins; by the time the problem is recognised, two spouses usually cannot reverse it themselves.
- **The third person opens what two cannot** → "This is the point at which a trained professional can function as a third person to work the magic of family therapy toward opening a closed relationship." Acts on: a stuck closed dyad.
- **Anxiety degrades the physician's signal in two opposite directions** → "The more reactive the physician, the more likely he is to put in medical jargon the family does not hear or to become too simplistic"; "the more anxious the physician, the more likely he is to do too much speechmaking and too little listening." Acts on: message fidelity.
- **Anxiety in the family raises its demand for specificity** → "The more anxious the physician, the more the family asks for specific details the physician cannot answer," which the physician answers with "overgeneralizations that miss the point." A positive-feedback loop between the two closed systems.
- **The receiver responds to the affect, not the content** → "the patient is responding to the physician instead of the content of what is being said"; the patient "failed to 'hear'"; both patient and family "can pretend they have dealt clearly with each other without either being heard through the emotionality." Acts on: whether information actually transfers at all.
- **Family functional equilibrium** → "A family unit is in functional equilibrium when it is calm and each member is functioning at reasonable efficiency for that period." The equilibrium is disturbed by **either the addition of a new member or the loss of a member**. Acts on: the whole unit, not the individual.
- **Two governors of reaction intensity** → "The intensity of the emotional reaction is governed by (1) the functioning level of emotional integration in the family at the time, or (2) by the functional importance of the one who is added to the family or lost to the family." This is the chapter's core two-parameter model. **[p2] The connective is "or", not "and"** — Bowen names two alternative governors, not a product. He also never states the *sign* of the integration→intensity relation here; the only place he gives a direction is the inversion sentence below, and it runs the other way for overt reaction. Do not read this sentence as "low integration ⇒ bigger reaction."
- **Three kinds of loss** → *physical* loss (a child leaves for college, an adult child marries out), *functional* loss ("a key family member who becomes incapacitated with a long-term illness or injury which prevents his doing the work on which the family depends"), and *emotional* loss ("the absence of a light-hearted person who can lighten the mood in a family"). Acts on: equilibrium — all three disturb it, so a person's *presence* is not the same as their *function*.
- **A mood role is a structural role** → "A group that changes from light-hearted laughter to seriousness becomes a different kind of organism."
- **Integration inverts the *timing* of the response; the size is separately governed** → "A well integrated family may show more overt reactiveness at the moment of change but adapt to it rather quickly. A less integrated family may show little reaction at the time and respond later with symptoms of physical illness, emotional illness, or social misbehavior." Immediate **overt** reaction and delayed symptom production are *inversely* related. **[p2] Three qualifiers pass 1 dropped.** (a) The word is **"overt"** — Bowen contrasts *visible/expressed* reaction at the moment with *later symptoms*; he does not say total reaction is lower in the less integrated family. (b) Both halves are hedged **"may show"** — this is a possibility statement, not a law. (c) Pass 1's heading "not its size" was an inference: the preceding sentence does say integration governs intensity, so the source does not license "shape only".
- **[p2] Recovery time is the one quantity with an explicitly stated two-input dependence** → "The length of time required for the family to establish a new emotional equilibrium depends on the emotional integration in the family **and** the intensity of the disturbance." Here the connective *is* "and". So: recovery time = f(integration, disturbance intensity); reaction intensity = governed by integration **or** functional importance. Two different functional forms, stated one sentence apart.
- **The Emotional Shock Wave** → "a network of underground 'aftershocks' of serious life events that can occur anywhere in the extended family system in the months or years following serious emotional events in a family." It is explicitly **not** grief: "It is not directly related to the usual grief or mourning reactions of people close to the one who died." It runs "on an underground network of emotional dependence of family members on each other." Acts on: the whole extended family, not the household. **[p2] Correction to pass 1's gloss.** Bowen says the dependence is **denied** ("The emotional dependence is denied, the serious life events appear to be unrelated") — i.e. *invisible to the family*. He does **not** say it is a different network from emotional closeness. Pass 1 read "hidden" as "structurally distinct" and cited the unattached grandchild as proof; that case is explained by two-hop transmission through the mother, not by a second edge type. What the chapter supports is (a) the dependence graph is unobservable to the family and (b) propagation is multi-hop.
- **Denied fusion is the substrate** → "It occurs most often in families with a significant degree of denied emotional 'fusion' in which the families have been able to maintain a fair degree of asymptomatic emotional balance in the family system." So the shock wave selects for families that *looked* fine.
- **The shock wave is a trigger, not a cause of specific symptoms** → "It is as if the shock wave is the stimulus that can trigger the physical process into activity." Symptom content is whatever that member is predisposed to; the shock wave only supplies the activation.
- **Transmission through an intermediate carrier** → the daughter shows "no more than the usual grief reaction to the death but reacted in some deep way, transmitting her disturbance to a son who had never been close to the grandmother but who reacted to the mother with delinquency behavior." Mechanism: the disturbance propagates along dependency edges even when the terminal symptom-bearer has no edge to the deceased.
- **Denial that defends itself** → naming the connection provokes reaction rather than insight: "The nature of the human phenomenon is such that it reacts vigorously to any such implications of the dependence of one life on another."
- **Unresolved emotional attachment persists and redirects** → attachments "remain viable for life, … attach to significant future relationships, and … continue to direct the course of a life." Death is the strongest available opportunity to resolve them: "Few human events provide as much emotional impact as serious illness and death in resolving unresolved emotional attachments."
- **The funeral as a resolution device** → it "serves a common function of bringing survivors into intimate contact with the dead and with important friends, and it helps survivors and friends to terminate their relationship with the dead and to move forward with life." Best served "when it brings relatives and friends into the best possible functional contact with the harsh fact of death and with each other at this time of high emotionality."
- **Modern funeral custom as an anxiety-avoidance mechanism that perpetuates attachment** → customs "function to deny death and to perpetuate the unresolved emotional attachments between the dead and the living," most intensely in those most anxious about death. Named instances: refusing to view the body; disposal direct from hospital; excluding children; the private funeral.
- **Children are injured by the survivors' anxiety, not by the death** → "I have never seen a child hurt by exposure to death. They are 'hurt' only by the anxiety of survivors"; "the ability of children to deal with death depends on the adults."
- **Direct words open the system, tangential words close it** → "A direct word signals to the other that I am comfortable with the subject, and it enables others to also be comfortable. A tangential word may appear to soften the fact of death; but it invites the family to respond with tangential words." Acts on: the whole conversational field, by contagion.

## A3. Predictive claims

Each stated as a conditional. `[E]` = Bowen offers a clinical example or research observation; `[A]` = asserted without supporting evidence in this chapter.

1. If two people live in emotional interdependence over time, then their relationship moves from open toward closed. `[A]` (courtship → marriage described as the standard trajectory)
2. If a person is anxious about a subject, then the other reflexively removes that subject from the channel — regardless of the stated reason. `[A]`
3. If a relationship has closed past a certain point, then the two people **can** be unable to reopen it themselves, and a trained third person is the stated remedy. `[A]` — "by the time the average person recognizes the problem, **it can be impossible** for two spouses to reverse the process themselves." **[p2] Bowen writes "can be impossible"; pass 1 rendered it as a hard lock. There is no stated threshold and no claim that the lock is universal.**
4. If a person is terminally ill, then they already have some awareness of impending death. `[E]` — "From experience, every terminally ill patient…" (universal claim); a high percentage hold extensive private knowledge they never voice.
5. If a terminally ill person is given an unbiased listener, then they are strengthened by the talk — with no exception observed. `[E]` — "I have never seen a terminally ill person who was not strengthened by such a talk."
6. If a physician is anxious, then message fidelity falls (jargon or oversimplification, speechmaking over listening, vague distorted messages, low awareness of family misperception). `[E]`
7. If physician–family communication is poor while care is adequate, then the family drifts toward malpractice anger. `[E]` — "repeated situations."
8. If a third party interprets the physician's statements simply to the family, then the malpractice thinking is averted. `[E]` — "it is fairly easy to do simple interpretations of the physician's statements and avert the malpractice thinking."
9. If a member is added to or lost from a family, then the functional equilibrium is disturbed. `[A/E]` — stated as a general law, illustrated with birth of a child, grandparent visiting vs. moving in, child leaving for college, adult child marrying.
10. If the lost/added member has high functional importance, then the disturbance is larger. `[E]`
11. **[p2 — rewritten]** If the family's level of emotional integration is low, then **recovery to a new equilibrium takes longer**. `[A]` — "The length of time required for the family to establish a new emotional equilibrium depends on the emotional integration in the family and the intensity of the disturbance." *Pass 1 also asserted "the disturbance is larger"; that is not in this sentence and is not stated anywhere in the chapter.* Recovery time is the only quantity Bowen ties to integration with a direction.
12. If a family is well integrated, then it **may** show *more* **overt** reaction at the moment of change but adapt quickly. `[A]` **[p2] hedge and the word "overt" restored — both were dropped at pass 1.**
13. If a family is less integrated, then it **may** show *little* reaction at the time and respond **later** with physical illness, emotional illness, or social misbehaviour. `[A]` — the key delayed-response prediction. **[p2]** Hedge restored. Note this is the chapter's *only* directional statement about integration and reaction magnitude, and it points the opposite way to the simulation's assumption.
14. If a family is made to express feelings at the moment of change, then its level of emotional integration does **not** necessarily rise. `[A]` — explicit denial of catharsis as a mechanism.
15. If a significant family member dies or is threatened with death, then in the months or years following, serious life events appear in multiple separate members anywhere in the extended family. `[E]` — the shock wave, found in a high enough percentage of families to be checked routinely.
16. If the family carries significant *denied* emotional fusion while remaining asymptomatic, then a shock wave is more likely. `[A]`
17. If a shock wave is occurring, then the family will deny any connection between the events; and if a therapist probes the denial directly, then the family camouflages further. `[E]` — the six-session case that terminated after a premature interpretation.
18. If the death is threatened rather than actual, then a shock wave can still occur and can be "almost as severe." `[E]` — the grandmother's mastectomy.
19. If either parent dies while the family is young, then a serious and prolonged shock wave is among the most likely outcomes — because it removes the breadwinner or mother function "at a time when these functions are most important." `[A]`
20. If an important child dies, then the family equilibrium can be shaken "for years." `[A]`
21. If the "head of the clan" dies, then a long-term underground disturbance can follow — even if he was partially disabled, provided he "continued some kind of decision-making function in family affairs." `[A]`
22. If a grandmother "lived in the shadow of her husband," then her death is less important; but if she was "a central figure in the emotional life and stability of the family," then the reaction can be intense. `[A]`
23. If the dead member was peripheral — "neither famous nor infamous" — then only the usual grief and mourning follows, and future family functioning is not influenced. `[A]`
24. If the dead member's functioning was never critical, then a brief grief period is followed by **improved** family functioning. `[A]` — death as a positive shock. **[p2] the burden clause is optional, not a second precondition:** "the people whose functioning was never critical to the family, and who **may have been** a burden in their final illness." Pass 1 stated it conjunctively. The load-bearing criterion is non-critical functioning alone.
25. If a dysfunctional member dies, then a shock wave rarely follows — **unless** that dysfunction "played a critical role in maintaining family emotional equilibrium." `[A]` — dysfunction itself can be load-bearing.
26. If the death is a suicide, then prolonged grief and mourning commonly follows, but the shock wave is usually **minor** — unless the suicide "was an abdication of an essential functional role." `[A]` — grief magnitude and shock-wave magnitude dissociate.
27. If the therapist knows in advance that a shock wave is possible, then he can take steps toward preventing it. `[A]` — the prevention steps themselves are not specified.
28. If a therapist treats all deaths as the same, then he misses the mark; and if he assumes a well-functioning family that has already adapted needs help, then the offer is "an inept intrusion." `[A]`
29. If the helper uses direct words (death, die, bury), then the system opens; if he uses tangential words (passed on, deceased, expired), then the family answers in kind and the conversation drifts until "one wonders if we are talking about death at all." `[A]`
30. If children are excluded from a funeral, then the result can be "a lifetime of unrealistic and distorted fantasies and images that may never be corrected." `[A]`
31. If a funeral is private, then the friendship system is denied its termination of the relationship with the dead, and the family is deprived of supportive relationships from friends. `[A]`
32. If a child is exposed to death, then the child is not hurt; the child is hurt only by the anxiety of surviving adults. `[E]` — "I have never seen a child hurt by exposure to death."
33. If a surviving parent cries in front of children without reassurance, then the children stop talking and deny the death. `[E]` — "In this kind of a situation, it is common for the children to stop talking and deny the death."
34. If the surviving parent mentions the death at frequent intervals and reassures the children when he cries, then the block is broken and the channel stays open. `[E]` — within a week the children were talking about the mother in the past tense.
35. If children are given private contact with the dead body with anxious extended family excluded, then they adapt to the reality of the death. `[E]`
36. If a family involves children in a funeral, then criticism from friends and relatives should be expected. `[E]` — and in the reported case "the criticism turned to admiration after the funeral home visit."
37. If an emotional attachment is unresolved at death, then it remains viable for life, attaches to future significant relationships, and continues to direct the course of a life. `[E]` — the woman burying her mother six years late.

## A5. Terminology

- **Open relationship system** — one in which "an individual is free to communicate a high percentage of inner thoughts, feelings, and fantasies to another who can reciprocate." Never complete; the healthy state is having *one* reasonably open relationship. Note the reciprocity requirement — a listener who cannot reciprocate does not make the system open.
- **Closed relationship system** — the automatic reflex state in which taboo subjects are excluded to protect self from the other's anxiety. Applied here at three levels simultaneously (intrapsychic, family, medical staff), so "system" here means any communicating unit, not just the family.
- **Taboo subject** — a topic removed from a relationship's channel by mutual anxiety. Death is "chief among all."
- **The physician-family-patient triangle** — used once, as the unit whose "closed system emotionality" physicians must learn. This is the chapter's only use of triangle language, and it is applied to a professional-plus-family unit rather than to a nuclear-family threesome.
- **Functional equilibrium** — the state of a family unit when "it is calm and each member is functioning at reasonable efficiency for that period." Defined by calm plus per-member functioning, not by structure.
- **Level of emotional integration (in the family)** — the parameter governing both the intensity of reaction and the time to re-establish equilibrium. Used in this chapter where "differentiation" does the work elsewhere; note that it is attributed to the *family at a time* ("at the time"), i.e. a state variable, not a fixed trait.
- **Functional importance** — the weight of the added/lost member; the second governor of reaction intensity. This is the chapter's notion of structural centrality.
- **Physical loss / functional loss / emotional loss** — three distinct loss kinds (departure; incapacity that removes the work the family depends on; removal of a mood-carrying presence).
- **Emotional Shock Wave** — Bowen's own quoted term: "a network of underground 'aftershocks' of serious life events that can occur anywhere in the extended family system in the months or years following serious emotional events in a family." Explicitly distinguished from grief/mourning. Noted as "mentioned in papers and lectures, but … not adequately described in the literature."
- **Underground network of emotional dependence** — the substrate the shock wave travels along; denied by the family, and not the same as visible emotional closeness.
- **Emotional "fusion" (denied)** — the family condition that predisposes to a shock wave; Bowen puts the word in quotes and refers the reader to another chapter for the process.
- **Head of the clan** — a functional position, not a generational one: usually a grandfather retaining "some kind of decision-making function in family affairs," but it "can also be the most important sibling in the present generation."
- **The neutral ones** — members "who were neither 'famous nor infamous'" and played peripheral roles; their deaths do not influence future family functioning.
- **Grief and mourning** — used as the conventional category Bowen is contrasting against; the shock wave is what conventional grief theory misses.
- **Unresolved emotional attachment** — an attachment that "remains viable for life," attaches to future relationships, and directs the course of a life. Contrasted with "burying the dead at the time of death."
- **Coaching** — used here for background guidance of family members through a death and funeral, including with people who are not patients ("coaching friends from the background").
- **[p2] Overall level of life adaptation** — a third assessment variable, named alongside total family configuration and the dying person's functioning position as what one must know before helping. Never defined, never scaled. Missed at pass 1.
- **[p2] Vocabulary absent, listed for the evolution track.** Despite internal markers placing this in the mid-1970s, the chapter contains **no** *differentiation of self*, *scale*, *basic/functional level*, *family projection process*, *emotional cutoff*, *sibling position*, *societal regression*, *undifferentiated family ego mass*, or *over/under-functioning reciprocity*. "Triangle" appears exactly once and denotes the **physician-family-patient** unit. "Multigenerational" appears only as a description of the research method ("multigenerational family research"), not as the transmission concept. The governing parameter is **"level of emotional integration"**, attributed to the family **"at the time"** — a family-level, time-indexed state, not a per-person near-fixed trait.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **Position determines the size of the loss, not the closeness of the survivors.** The chapter's central structural claim is that a member has a *functional importance* to the system and that the shock wave scales with it.
- **[p2] What is actually Bowen's, and what was pass 1's.** Bowen states **three tiers** explicitly and in this order: deaths with "a fair chance the death will be followed by a shock wave"; deaths that "are more neutral"; and deaths that "are a relief to the family." Membership of each tier is his. The **strict ordinal ranking *within* the top tier is not** — he writes "Among the deaths most likely to be followed by a serious and prolonged shock wave are the deaths of either parent when the family is young", then adds important child and head of the clan without ranking them against each other or against the young parent. Only the young-parent case is given an extra stated reason ("it removes the function of the breadwinner or the mother at a time when these functions are most important"). Also, **the text's order of presentation puts the shadow grandmother *before* the central grandmother** — pass 1 silently reordered the pair to fit a descending scale. The severity *relation* between those two is Bowen's ("their deaths were less important" vs. "the family reaction can be intense"); the position of the pair in a single global ranking is not. Treat the list below as tiers plus two pairwise relations, not as a calibration ladder.
- **Ranked ordering of deaths by expected shock-wave severity** (**[p2]** pass 1's reconstruction, retained for reference — see the correction immediately above):
  1. Death of **either parent when the family is young** — worst, because it disturbs the equilibrium *and* removes the breadwinner or mother function "at a time when these functions are most important."
  2. Death of **an important child** — "can shake the family equilibrium for years."
  3. Death of the **"head of the clan"** — a grandfather still exercising decision-making, or the most important sibling of the present generation; produces "a long-term underground disturbance."
  4. Death of a **grandmother who was a central figure in the emotional life and stability of the family** — "The family reaction can be intense."
  5. Death of a **grandmother who lived in the shadow of her husband** — "less important."
  6. Death of a **peripheral/neutral member** — usual grief only; "not likely to influence future family functioning."
  7. Death of a **member whose functioning was never critical and who was a burden in their final illness** — brief grief, then **improved** family functioning.
  - Cross-cutting: **a dysfunctional member's death rarely produces a shock wave unless the dysfunction itself was load-bearing** for the family's emotional equilibrium.
  - Cross-cutting: **suicide** → prolonged grief, minor shock wave, unless it abdicated an essential functional role.
- **Symptom-bearer is not the bereaved.** The grandchild who breaks down is "often one who had little direct emotional attachment to the grandparents." A carrier (the daughter) with only ordinary visible grief transmits to a symptom-bearer (her son) one generation down.
- **A single person's dysfunction can distribute across the household by dependency.** In the daughter's-marriage case: the mother's functioning depended on the daughter; "Most of the subsequent dysfunction was in the mother, but the son and father were sufficiently dependent on the mother that they too developed symptoms." Dependency is directional and transitive.
- **Mood role.** A "light-hearted person who can lighten the mood in a family" holds a structural position whose loss changes the organism.
- **[p2] Vocabulary warning on the next item.** "Sibling position" is a term of art elsewhere in the corpus (Ch21 lists it as one of six concepts). **This chapter has no sibling-position concept**; the children are distinguished only by age. Pass 1's heading imported a later term. Read it as "the three children, by age".
- **Sibling position of the children in the coached case** is reported with their distinct responses: the ten-year-old daughter and eight-year-old son were calm at the funeral (the eight-year-old whispering that he would miss his mother, and earlier praying under the casket); the five-year-old clung to the father with tearfulness, asked to kiss his mother, said "If I kissed her, she could not kiss back," and — as "the one who found objects to give his mother as 'presents'" — initiated the pebble ritual that the other two then copied. **The youngest moved first and the older two followed.**
- **The person who moves first in a death crisis is the surviving spouse/parent**, whose composure sets what the children can do: "the ability of children to deal with death depends on the adults."
- **The therapist's position in the medical triangle**: outside all three closed systems, and therefore able to interpret the physician to the family.
- **The regular therapist can be the blockage.** In the demonstration interview, the family's own therapist "had known about the cancer but had been waiting for the mother to bring it up… The therapist's own emotionality had prevented the wife from talking."
- **Extended family as an active hazard at the funeral.** Bowen excluded the extended family from the children's viewing because he "considered it unwise to expose the children to the emotionality in that family"; the father's mother tried to talk him out of it ("Son, that will be too hard on you").

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **Talk to the dying person about death directly.** Offer to be "an unbiased listener who did not have to correct their way of thinking." Do this with seriously ill people, and with people who are not sick.
- **Attempt it even with comatose patients** — a fair percentage can surface for an important communication.
- **Use direct words** — "death, die, and bury" — and never the tangential ones. This is stated as a deliberate, carefully maintained practice.
- **Ask the closed-system question straight.** The operative move in the demonstration interview was to ask the patient *why she thought* the others had not told her — putting the closure itself, not the diagnosis, on the table.
- **Protect the speaker from the anxious family member.** When the father began joking to deflate his wife's account, Bowen "made a few comments to suggest he not interfere with his wife's serious thoughts."
- **Interpret the physician's statements to the family** in simple terms, to avert malpractice thinking.
- **Take a routine shock-wave history in every family.** "The incidence of the emotional shock wave is sufficiently prevalent that the Georgetown Family Section does a routine historical check for it in every family history."
- **Assess before intervening:** "Knowledge of the total family configuration, the functioning position of the dying person in the family, and the overall level of life adaptation are important for anyone who attempts to help a family before, during, or after a death."
- **Coach the funeral from the background.** Concretely, Bowen urges: visit dying family members whenever possible; include children if the situation permits; involve "the largest possible group of extended family members"; an **open casket**; "the most personal contact that is possible between the dead and the living"; **prompt obituary notices**; notification of relatives and friends; **a public funeral with the body present**; and "the most personal funeral service that is possible" — personalising even a highly ritualised service.
- **State the goal explicitly:** "to bring the entire family system into the closest possible contact with death in the presence of the total friendship system and to lend a helping hand to the anxious people who would rather run than face a funeral."
- **Outline the ideal course of action and hand back the choice.** With the widowed father: "I suggested he take as many of the ideas as were consistent with himself, and if they made sense to him, to use them as far as he could go."
- **Warn in advance of the social cost.** "I warned him of adverse emotional reactions of friends and to be prepared for criticism if he decided to involve the children."
- **Prescribe repeated mention.** Tell the surviving parent to mention the death "at frequent intervals during the coming days," and, if he cries, "to reassure the children that he was all right and not to worry about him" — keeping the channel open for any and all questions.
- **Give the children agency over small decisions** — Bowen suggested the children decide whether or not to go to school the next day.
- **Engineer the viewing.** Arrange a time before the funeral, at the funeral home, with **all other people removed from the room**, for the parent and children alone with the body.
- **Enforce the exclusion out loud, in front of the relatives.** Bowen "announced to the relatives and friends that the father would take the children to the funeral home the next afternoon, that it was to be private, and that no one else could be present."
- **Refuse to answer a question that belongs to the family member.** Asked by the father whether the five-year-old could kiss his mother, Bowen "suggested that would be between the son and his mother."
- **Stay the calmest person present.** "She and I were the calmest people in the room" — while the family's own therapist wept.
- **Keep working after the funeral** — Bowen was "in close contact with the family the following year" and is "still in periodic contact" twelve years on.
- **[p2] Give the reason with the prescription.** Bowen states his reasoning to the coachee rather than issuing bare instructions — "I suggested that the ability of children to deal with death depends on the adults"; "I reasoned that this would help the children adapt to the reality of the mother's death." The mechanism is handed over along with the move.
- **[p2] Answer the logistical questions too.** The father arrived with "dozens of questions about how to handle the present emergency, the funeral, the future of the children, and his own life" — school attendance, what to tell the teachers, whether to seek release from the overseas assignment. Bowen coached across all of it. The coach's scope here is far wider than the emotional-process-only scope of Ch20/Ch22.
- **[p2] Announce a concrete decision publicly; do not announce a programme.** Bowen announced the private viewing "to the relatives and friends" out loud. This is an announcement of *what will happen*, not of *what the mover is trying to achieve* — see P3 on the Ch10/Ch22 announcement dispute.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not make the shock-wave connection prematurely.** The explicit failure case: after six good sessions, "I made a premature connection between his mother's stroke and the affair. He cancelled the subsequent appointment and never returned."
- **Do not approach the denial directly.** "Families are extremely reactive to any effort to approach the denial directly," and will camouflage further if they detect the therapist hunting for connectedness. Also: **respect the denial** — "to respect the denial that operates in the family" is listed alongside knowing the situation and controlling one's own emotionality.
- **Do not use softening or tangential words** — passed on, deceased, expired.
- **Do not treat all deaths as the same.** "To attempt to treat all deaths as the same can miss the mark."
- **Do not assume every family needs help.** "Some well functioning families are able to adapt to approaching death before it occurs. To assume that such families need help can be an inept intrusion."
- **Do not aim the work at overt expression of grief.** Clergy who "operate with their theology, a theory about death that does not go beyond the familiar concepts of grief and mourning" and "aim their help at the overt expression of grief" provide superficial help and "miss the deeper process."
- **Do not treat crying as the goal.** "The popular notion that expression of grief through crying may be helpful to most complicates the situation for others."
- **Do not push feeling-expression at the moment of change** expecting it to help: "An attempt to get the family to express feelings at the moment of change does not necessarily increase the level of emotional integration."
- **Do not wait for the patient to raise the cancer.** The regular therapist "had been waiting for the mother to bring it up. This is a common posture for mental health professionals" — and it was her own emotionality that kept the patient silent.
- **Do not treat the sequence of events as unrelated.** "Without such knowledge, the sequence of events is treated as separate, unrelated events."
- **Do not use denial or "other extreme mechanisms" in oneself**; the therapist must have "his own emotional life under reasonable control."
- **Do not open a major issue you cannot follow up** — except on judgement. Bowen was "reluctant to take this course when follow-up interviews would not be possible" and initially "elected to avoid the critical issue"; he reversed only when the interview stalled. He does not present the reversal as a general rule.
- **Do not let an anxious family member silence the patient** — but the counter-move is a mild suggestion, not confrontation.
- **Do not expose children to the extended family's emotionality** at the viewing.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Before any death work:** know the total family configuration, the dying person's functioning position, and the overall level of life adaptation. Assessment precedes intervention.
- **Before intervening at all:** decide whether the family has already adapted — if so, do nothing.
- **Before making a shock-wave interpretation:** the family must be one of the "less reactive" ones who "can be more interested in the phenomenon than reactive to it." Six sessions was **not** enough in the failed case. No positive threshold is given.
- **The shock-wave check happens at history-taking**, in every family, as routine — before any death is in play.
- **Prevention is possible only with foreknowledge:** "If the therapist can know ahead of time about the possibility of an emotional shock wave, he can take some steps toward its prevention."
- **Within a single session,** the sequence used was: begin → interview stalls ("awkward, difficult, and sticky") → at about ten minutes, raise the closed system → protect the speaker from the interfering spouse → let her run → close, hoping the regular therapist continues.
- **Bowen's stated limit on a single session:** "It is impossible to do much toward opening an emotionally closed relationship of this intensity in a single session."
- **The funeral sequence in the coached case:** Sunday death → same evening, three hours with the surviving father, outlining the ideal course and warning of criticism → Tuesday evening, an hour with father and children together, telling them the plan → publicly announcing the private viewing to relatives → Wednesday afternoon, the private viewing (children only) → Wednesday evening, the whole family-friendship system at the funeral home → Thursday, public funeral → continued mention of the death by the father → within a week, children speaking of the mother in the past tense → a year of close contact.
- **Precondition for the children's viewing to work:** "it could work if anxious members of the extended family were excluded." The exclusion is not optional decoration; it is the enabling condition.
- **The children's private time comes before the public viewing and before the funeral.**
- **Preconditions on the therapist:** his own emotionality contained; comfortable enough with the subject to use direct words. Bowen adds a training prediction — "it would be easier to train those who cried than those who intellectualized their feelings."
- **For physicians and surgeons:** either "learn the fundamentals of closed system emotionality in the physician-family-patient triangle, or they might avail themselves of professional expertise in family therapy if they lack the time and motivation to master this for themselves."

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Premature interpretation → the family leaves.** The single most explicit failure in the chapter; the patient never returned.
- **Probing the denial → deeper camouflage.** The family becomes *harder* to read after a clumsy attempt.
- **Aiming at grief expression → superficial help, deeper process missed.** Attributed to many young chaplains and clergy.
- **Pushing feeling-expression at the moment of change → no gain in emotional integration.**
- **The therapist's own emotionality → the patient stays silent.** The waiting therapist in the demonstration case.
- **Tangential language → conversational drift.** "The conversation soon reaches the point that one wonders if we are talking about death at all."
- **Blurting → no opening.** "Closed systems do not become open when the surgeon hurriedly blurts out tense speeches about the situation." Rule change without emotional change achieves nothing: "a change in rules does not automatically change the emotional reactivity."
- **Anxious physician → distorted message, family misperception, malpractice anger** despite adequate care.
- **Assuming an adapted family needs help → "an inept intrusion."**
- **Not knowing about the shock wave → the events get treated as separate and unrelated**, and each symptom is treated in isolation.
- **Excluding children from the funeral → "a lifetime of unrealistic and distorted fantasies and images that may never be corrected."**
- **The private funeral → the friendship system cannot terminate its relationship with the dead, and the family loses friends' support.**
- **Refusing to view the body ("I want to remember them as I knew them") → the unresolved attachment is perpetuated.**
- **Listening to anxious relatives and friends instead of a professional → a less helpful funeral.**
- **Failing to bury the dead at the time of death → the attachment stays viable for life, transfers onto future relationships, and directs the course of that life** (the woman still burying her mother six years on, in psychoanalysis).
- **Trainees who intellectualise their feelings → harder to train** than those who cry.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

This section converts each prescription into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.

1. **"Use direct words; never tangential ones."** → Assumes a family's willingness to name a topic is *set by the most comfortable person in the room* and spreads by imitation. Language choice is a contagious state variable, not a stylistic one. One actor's vocabulary can shift the whole system's openness in a single conversation.
2. **"A trained third person can open what two people cannot."** → Assumes a closed dyad is a stable attractor that two insiders cannot escape, but which an *outside* node with low reactivity can perturb. Openness is therefore not a property of the pair alone but of the pair-plus-observer.
3. **"Stay the calmest person in the room."** → Assumes anxiety propagates between people and that the least-anxious participant sets a floor for everyone else. Corollary: a therapist's own anxiety is an input to the family's state, not a private matter.
4. **"Interpreting the physician to the family averts malpractice anger."** → Assumes hostility toward a caregiver is generated by *communication failure*, not by the medical facts; anger is a symptom of a closed channel and dissolves when the channel opens.
5. **"Ask why they think nobody told her."** → Assumes the patient already holds the information and is running her own model of why others are withholding it. The intervention is not disclosure; it is making the *withholding itself* discussable. Assumes information asymmetry in families is usually fake.
6. **"Don't wait for the patient to bring it up."** → Assumes silence is evidence of blockage, not of absence of need. A family member's failure to raise a topic carries no information about whether they want to.
7. **"Never make the shock-wave connection prematurely."** → Assumes the family's denial of interdependence is *load-bearing*: it is what keeps a fused family asymptomatic. Removing it faster than the family can absorb it costs the therapist the relationship, not just the point. Interdependence-denial has a rate limit.
8. **"Respect the denial."** → Assumes denial is a homeostatic component, not merely an error to be corrected. A model that simply deletes denial when a therapist names it would be wrong.
9. **"Do routine shock-wave history-taking in every family."** → Assumes the causal link between a past death and a present symptom is (a) common enough to expect by default, (b) invisible to the family, and (c) recoverable only by chronology — you find it by lining up dates, not by asking how people felt.
10. **"Know the functioning position of the dying person before helping."** → Assumes the *same event* (a death) produces radically different system responses depending on a scalar property of the person removed. Therefore the event must be parameterised by the person's role, not just by the fact of death.
11. **"Not all deaths are the same; some are a relief."** → Assumes the system's response to loss can be *positive*. Loss is a signed quantity: removing a member whose functioning was never critical and who was a burden raises family functioning.
12. **"Feeling-expression at the moment of change does not raise integration."** → Assumes emotional integration is a slow-moving structural parameter that cannot be moved by an affective episode. Catharsis and structural change are decoupled.
13. **"Don't aim at grief; the deeper process is elsewhere."** → Assumes visible grief and system disturbance are *different variables in different people*. The person who grieves visibly is often not the person who will break down.
14. **"Involve the largest possible group of extended family; open casket; public funeral; prompt obituary."** → Assumes the resolving effect of a death scales with the *number of system members brought into contact with the fact* and with the *directness of that contact*. The funeral is a system-wide synchronisation event: contact opportunity foregone is attachment left unresolved, per person.
15. **"Include the friendship system."** → Assumes the relevant system extends past kinship; friends hold attachments requiring termination too, and they supply support the family cannot generate internally.
16. **"Include children."** → Assumes a child's later functioning depends on whether they got contact with the reality, and that the absence of contact is filled by fantasy that persists for life.
17. **"Exclude anxious extended family from the children's viewing."** → Assumes anxiety transmits from adults to children with priority over the event itself — children respond to the nearest adult's state, not to the facts. Assumes further that this is *positional*: the same relatives are helpful at the public funeral and harmful in a small room with children.
18. **"Tell the surviving parent to mention the death repeatedly and to reassure when he cries."** → Assumes a family's ability to speak of the dead decays unless actively refreshed, and that a survivor's distress reads to children as a *prohibition* unless explicitly disowned. The parent must separate "I am sad" from "do not speak of this."
19. **"Warn him he will be criticised."** → Assumes the surrounding social system actively opposes the correct move, and that the coached person needs to be inoculated against that pressure in advance to hold his course.
20. **"Outline the ideal course; let him take what fits him."** → Assumes a plan the person owns is executed and a plan imposed is not — and that the person's own inner strength, not the therapist's presence, is what carries it. Bowen notes this father "had more inner strength than any other relative I have seen under stress of this intensity," which flags the result as an upper bound rather than a typical outcome.
21. **"That would be between the son and his mother."** → Assumes decisions about contact with the dead belong to the individual member, and that a therapist answering for them would substitute his own judgement into a relationship — closing rather than opening it.
22. **"Physicians must learn closed-system emotionality or bring in someone who has."** → Assumes the medical system is itself a family-like emotional unit with its own closure, and that its anxiety enters the family's process as a real input. The professional system is inside the model, not outside it.
23. **"Prevention is possible if you know a shock wave is coming."** → Assumes the shock wave is not inevitable given the triggering death — some modifiable variable stands between the loss and the aftershocks. Bowen never says which.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Ego fragility repudiated.** "I have never seen a terminally ill person who was not strengthened by such a talk. This contradicts former beliefs about the ego being too fragile for this in certain situations." An explicit reversal of prevailing psychiatric doctrine, and Bowen bases it purely on his own unbroken record of observations.
- **Bowen's own theoretical frame changed.** The woman burying her mother was seen "some twenty-five years ago" when "I was practicing within the framework of transference and intrapsychic dynamics"; her statement "was used later as a way of describing systems theory." The same datum was reinterpreted under a new theory.
- **Systems theory positioned against psychiatric theory.** "Family systems theory provides a broader perspective of death than is possible with conventional psychiatric theory, which focuses on death as a process within the individual."
- **Shock wave vs. grief/mourning.** The chapter repeatedly insists the shock wave "is not directly related to the usual grief or mourning reactions," which sets it against the whole grief-work tradition. Suicide is the sharpest case: maximum grief, minimal shock wave.
- **Catharsis rejected while emotional contact is prescribed.** Bowen simultaneously says expressing feelings at the moment of change does not increase emotional integration, and prescribes maximum personal contact with the dead body. These are not contradictory in his frame — contact with *fact* is the mechanism, not discharge of *affect* — but the distinction is easy to lose and he does not flag it.
- **"Respect the denial" vs. "use direct words."** He prescribes both. The reconciliation appears to be that direct language about the *death* is required while interpretation of the *interdependence* must wait, but he never states this reconciliation.
- **Tension in the single-session case.** He states a principle (don't open what you can't follow up), violates it, and then reports "I was pleased at having decided to take up this issue." The principle survives as a default with an unspecified override.
- **Medical rule change without behaviour change.** He notes the post-1960s dictum shift and immediately says the underlying communication is unchanged — a warning that stated policy is a poor proxy for system behaviour.
- **[p2] Bowen declares the chapter's two halves mechanistically unrelated, then leaves them entangled.** The shock-wave section opens: "This section will deal with an order of events within the family that **is not directly related to open and closed system communications**." Yet the shock wave is identified precisely by the family's **denial** of interdependence and its **camouflage** of the sequence — which is closed-system behaviour by his own definition in the first half. He never reconciles the disclaimer with the mechanism. Missed at pass 1, and it matters: if the two are separate mechanisms, opening the communication system does nothing to the shock wave; if they are the same, opening it is the unnamed prevention step he says exists.
- **[p2] The prescribed intervention produces *relief*, and relief is reported as the success criterion here.** "A thousand tons were lifted from this family today"; "The patient was relieved to be partially out of the closed system"; "improved family functioning" after a relieving death. Nowhere in this chapter does symptom or anxiety relief carry the warning it carries in Ch02/03/06/07/11/12/14/18/20/22. Recorded as a within-corpus tension in P3, not resolved here.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- Much of the chapter is framed as personal belief: "I believe it serves a common function…", "I think the best function of a funeral…", "I believe funerals were probably more effective when people died at home…", "I believe the trend toward telling patients about incurable illness is one of the healthy changes in medicine", "I believe the poor communication between the physician and the family … is the greatest problem", "I believe that professional support to a family at the time of death can help…", "I believe it provides a different dimension…"
- **The shock wave is admitted as under-documented:** "It has been mentioned in papers and lectures, but it has not been adequately described in the literature."
- **No incidence figure is ever supplied** — only "a sufficiently high percentage," "a significant percentage."
- **Prevention is asserted without method:** "he can take some steps toward its prevention" — the steps are not named.
- **Initial misreading admitted:** "At first, this appeared to be coincidence."
- **The shock-wave mechanism is stated by analogy, not mechanism:** "It is as if the shock wave is the stimulus that can trigger the physical process into activity."
- **A guess flagged as a guess:** "I guessed it would be easier to train those who cried than those who intellectualized their feelings."
- **The best case is flagged as atypical:** "this father had more inner strength than any other relative I have seen under stress of this intensity."
- **Only one self-diagnosing family ever seen:** "I have seen only one family who had made an automatic connection between such events before seeking therapy."
- **Reservation stated and then overridden without a general rule:** the reluctance to open the cancer issue without follow-up.
- **Hedged assessment of the daughter's-marriage case:** "I *would see this as* a family with tenuously balanced emotional equilibrium…" — an interpretation offered, not a finding.
- **Superficial help conceded to the grief approach:** "This may provide superficial help to a majority of people, but it misses the deeper process."
- **A "spectrum" of comatose patients, with no count and no outcome rate** — only "a fair percentage."
- **[p2] The inversion claim is double-hedged.** Both halves read "**may** show" — "A well integrated family **may** show more overt reactiveness… A less integrated family **may** show little reaction…". This is the chapter's single most simulation-critical sentence and Bowen states it as a possibility, with no case, no count, and no rate. `[A]`, not `[E]`.
- **[p2] The two-governor sentence is disjunctive** ("governed by … **or** by …") while the recovery-time sentence one line later is conjunctive ("depends on … **and** …"). Whether the disjunction is deliberate or loose prose is not determinable from the text.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **Whispering is not private.** "Patients are often alert to whispered communications" — information leaks across the supposedly closed boundary, so a model in which the patient is simply uninformed is wrong.
- **Coma may be partly volitional.** "Terminally ill people often permit themselves to slip into coma," and can come out of it for an important communication. Bowen reports patients doing so to express thanks and then immediately slipping back.
- **The patient's diagnosis of her own closed system is more precise than the theory:** "When I ask them and they say 'No,' what does it mean? It either means they are liars or I am crazy, and I know I am not crazy." A closed system forces the excluded member to choose between distrusting others and distrusting herself.
- **Reciprocal pretending consumes the whole day.** "I try to act cheerful until my husband goes to work… Then I am alone all day with my thoughts, just crying and thinking. Before my husband returns from work, I try to pull myself together for his sake." The maintenance of a closed system has an ongoing energy cost borne by the person it excludes.
- **The wish to die to stop pretending:** "I wish I could die soon and not have to pretend any longer." Closure itself, not the illness, is what she names as unbearable.
- **The dying person's concern is a role handoff, not her own death.** She was not afraid to die for herself; she wanted to live to see the daughter have a life, and "hated the responsibility of leaving the daughter the responsibility of the father." What she is tracking is who will carry which function after she is gone.
- **Joking as a silencing mechanism.** The father's "joking and kidding about the mother's vivid imagination" was an active suppression move requiring counter-action.
- **Professional observers split three ways** on watching an open death conversation: moved to tears / silent and serious / critical. Reactivity to death is distributed even among trained people.
- **The five-year-old generated the ritual.** He brought his mother a pebble because he was "the one who found objects to give his mother as 'presents'" — an existing relationship pattern re-enacted; the older two copied him; then "We can go now, Daddy." The children terminated the visit themselves once the pattern completed.
- **"A thousand tons were lifted from this family today"** — the father's own report of a discrete step-change in family state following one hour of contact.
- **The father told his own mother to shut up** and did it anyway. The successful outcome required overriding the previous generation.
- **Criticism converted to admiration** after the funeral-home visit — the surrounding social system's opposition was not stable and flipped on evidence.
- **The father later wrote the experience up** as "My God, My Wife is Dead," describing "his initial shock, his efforts to get beyond self-pity, his resolution to make his own decisions when anxiety was high, and the emotional courage that went into his plan." Note the named ingredient: *making one's own decisions when anxiety is high*.
- **"Man is an instinctual animal"** — the chapter's framing premise is that "a high percentage of human relationship behavior is directed more by automatic instinctual emotional forces than by intellect," and that "much intellectual activity goes to explain away and justify behavior being directed by the instinctual-emotional-feeling complex." Reported reasons are therefore unreliable evidence about causes.
- **Society constrains the available funeral forms.** "Society no longer permits" home death and family-made coffins, so the therapist works within a degraded set of contact opportunities.
- **[p2] Money constrains them too, and the chapter says so once, in the patient's own words.** The dying woman wanted burial above ground so people would not walk on her grave: "But… we are poor people. We can't afford a mausoleum. When I die, I will be buried in a grave just like everyone else." The only place in the chapter where a **material resource** determines what a family can do with a death. Missed at pass 1 — see Q-MATERIAL in P5.
- **[p2] Economic failure appears three times as a shock-wave *output*:** "a daughter's husband failed in business"; "his business was failing"; "failures in school or business" in the symptom list. Plus the medical-cost consequence in the self-diagnosing family: "the doctor bills have become exorbitant." Money moves in this chapter as a symptom channel, not as a state variable.
- **[p2] Occupational obligation as a constraint on presence.** The father wondered "if he should seek release from his overseas work" and afterwards "took an assignment closer to home, so he could return if he was needed." A work commitment gates physical availability to the family — the only such mechanism in the chapter.
- **The threatened-death case had no death at all** and still produced five years of symptoms across four family branches — the shock wave is triggered by the *threat signal*, not by the removal of a member.

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

1. **The "second hidden edge type" was invented.** Pass 1's headline shock-wave finding — and the corresponding ledger entry L15.2 — says the wave "travels a hidden DEPENDENCE network, through asymptomatic carriers", glossed as "a hidden topology **distinct from visible closeness**", and the model implication reads "needs a **second, hidden edge weight** (dependence) alongside any closeness/attachment weight." Bowen says the network is **denied**, not that it is a different graph: "The emotional dependence is denied, the serious life events appear to be unrelated, the family attempts to camouflage any connectedness between the events." The evidence pass 1 cited for distinctness — the delinquent grandson with no attachment to the grandmother — is fully explained by the **two-hop path** he himself gives (grandmother → daughter → son), where the terminal symptom-bearer needs no direct tie because he is two edges away. What the chapter licenses is (a) the dependence graph is **unobservable to the family** (a belief-layer claim, not a topology claim) and (b) **multi-hop propagation through a carrier who shows no symptom**. It does not license a second edge weight. A model built on the pass-1 reading adds a whole state layer this chapter never asked for.
2. **The severity ladder is not Bowen's ladder.** Pass 1 presented a seven-rung ordinal list as "Bowen's own ordering, from most to least severe" and the model section called it "a concrete ordinal calibration ladder." Bowen gives **three tiers** (shock-wave-likely / neutral / relief) and, inside the top tier, an unranked list. He never ranks important child against head of the clan, or either against the young parent. Worse, pass 1 **silently reordered** the grandmother pair — the text presents the shadow grandmother first ("their deaths were less important") and the central grandmother second ("the family reaction can be intense"); pass 1 swapped them so the list would descend. Using this as an ordinal calibration source would be calibrating against my own arrangement.
3. **"Low integration ⇒ larger disturbance" is not in the chapter.** A3 #11 asserted "the disturbance is larger and its recovery slower", quoting a sentence that says only "The length of time required for the family to establish a new emotional equilibrium depends on the emotional integration in the family and the intensity of the disturbance." That sentence is about **recovery time**. The two-governor sentence before it names integration as a governor of intensity but **gives no direction**, and its connective is **"or"**, not "and" — pass 1's M section turned it into a product ("a function of a family-level integration state **and** a per-person `functional_importance` scalar"). The only directional statement about integration and reaction magnitude in the whole chapter is the inversion sentence, and it runs the other way.
4. **Hedges stripped throughout.** The inversion is "**may** show" twice; the closed-dyad lock is "it **can** be impossible"; the threatened-death severity is "**can** be almost as severe"; the burdensome-member case is "who **may have been** a burden". Pass 1 rendered all four as flat conditionals in A3 and as mechanisms in M. The inversion in particular is a hedged, uninstanced assertion — `[A]`, no case, no count — and pass 1's ledger entry L15.2/L15.1 presents it as a finding strong enough to contradict the model. It may well be right, but the evidential weight is one hedged sentence.
5. **The burden clause was made a precondition.** Pass 1 A3 #24 required *both* non-critical functioning *and* burden in the final illness for the improved-functioning outcome. The source conjoins them with "may have been", making burden an optional attribute. The operative criterion is non-critical functioning alone.
6. **"Sibling position" imported from later theory.** A6 headed the three children's differing responses "Sibling position of the children in the coached case." This chapter has no sibling-position concept; it has three ages. The heading reads a Ch21 concept back into a chapter that does not contain it — exactly the failure mode this pass exists to catch.
7. **Missed: Bowen explicitly disclaims a link between the chapter's two halves.** "This section will deal with an order of events within the family that is **not directly related** to open and closed system communications." Pass 1 recorded neither the disclaimer nor the fact that it is undermined two paragraphs later, when the shock wave is diagnosed by the family's *denial and camouflage* — closed-system behaviour by the chapter's own definition. This is the chapter's sharpest internal tension and it bears on the one lever Bowen claims and does not specify (prevention).
8. **Missed: the material constraint.** Pass 1's M section recorded "Society constrains available funeral forms" but not the patient's own statement that **poverty** does: "we are poor people. We can't afford a mausoleum." A per-family material variable gating the funeral parameters is exactly the kind of thing an agent model would want, and it was in the text.
9. **Over-read: "explicitly not grief."** A2 says the shock wave is "explicitly **not** grief"; Bowen writes "It is **not directly related to** the usual grief or mourning reactions of people close to the one who died." Dissociation, not exclusion — and the suicide case ("prolonged grief… shock wave usually minor") shows the two vary independently, which is a weaker and more useful claim than mutual exclusion.
10. **Minor omissions in A4.** "A fair percentage of children have a reasonable version of this with a parent" and the three separate "high percentage" claims were not recorded.

## P2. What is NEW here relative to the preceding chapters

- **The Emotional Shock Wave, defined.** Ch21 (1967/70) gives a timed anxiety wave through an extended family; this chapter supplies the **named concept, the definition, the trigger class, the predisposing family type, the symptom taxonomy, and a routine screening procedure** — plus the admission that it "has not been adequately described in the literature." This is the corpus's primary source for the mechanism.
- **The event class itself.** Death, threatened death, and funerals appear nowhere else as a subject. With them come three genuinely new constructs: **functional equilibrium** as a named family state; **functional importance** of a member as the scalar that sizes an event; and the **three loss kinds** — physical, functional, emotional. The third is the most novel: a "light-hearted person who can lighten the mood" holds a structural role, and "a group that changes from light-hearted laughter to seriousness becomes a different kind of organism."
- **Loss as a signed quantity.** Deaths that "are a relief to the family and are usually followed by a period of better functioning." No earlier chapter has a system event whose expected effect on family functioning is positive.
- **Grief and system disturbance as two independent output channels.** Suicide is the clean dissociation: maximum grief, minimal shock wave. Nothing earlier separates the visible affective response from the structural one.
- **Addition as a perturbation with the same machinery as loss.** "The equilibrium of the unit is disturbed by either the addition of a new member or the loss of a member" — birth, a grandparent visiting versus moving in. Earlier chapters treat membership as given.
- **The physician as an emotional actor inside the family's process.** Ch19 has the family recruiting the doctor; here the **doctor's own anxiety degrades message fidelity** in two specified directions (jargon vs. over-simplification, speechmaking vs. listening), the family's demand for specificity rises with his anxiety, and the therapist's move is to **translate** — averting malpractice anger while the medical care was adequate throughout. Communication fidelity as a modelled quantity is new.
- **A one-shot, event-triggered coaching engagement.** Everything else in the corpus is a course of work measured in months to years (C8). Here the whole intervention is Sunday-to-Thursday, and the outcome claim is at twelve years. Also new: **coaching people who are not patients** — neighbours, friends, members of his own extended family.
- **Anniversary-free delayed causation.** Delays of months to years with no periodic structure — see P5.

## P3. What CHANGED from earlier chapters

- **vs. C3 / Ch09 / Ch16 / Ch18 — conservation is absent, and the mechanism here is amplification.** Ch09 and Ch16 state a fixed quantity of immaturity allocated across three or four sinks; Ch18 gives three inputs and four channels. This chapter has **no budget, no sinks, no allocation, and no conservation statement of any kind**. One grandmother's mastectomy produces symptoms in four separate branches simultaneously, and the symptom list is explicitly open-ended: "The symptoms in a shock wave can be **any human problem**", drawn from the affected member's own predisposition ("It is as if the shock wave is the stimulus that can trigger the physical process into activity"). A single event that raises symptom load in many people at once is not a redistribution. Either the shock wave sits outside the conserved-anxiety mechanism, or the corpus is inconsistent; this chapter gives no way to tell.
- **vs. Ch16 / Ch17 — the governing parameter is family-level and time-indexed.** Ch17 admits only two major variables, with basic level **frozen** after young adulthood and reactivity *derived* from it. Ch15's parameter is "the functioning level of emotional integration in the family **at the time**" — a property of the unit, at a moment. And it is the only governing parameter offered: no per-person differentiation appears anywhere in this chapter.
- **vs. C1 (relief opposes progress, 11 chapters) — this chapter runs the other way.** Every reported success here is a relief: "A thousand tons were lifted from this family today"; "The patient was relieved to be partially out of the closed system"; a relieving death "followed by improved family functioning." No warning is attached anywhere. Two partial reconciliations exist but Bowen does not make either: Ch22's rule that openness lowers anxiety which *permits* differentiation, and the fact that the outcome measured here is twelve-year adaptation rather than immediate symptom relief. He does, however, keep the adjacent guard rails — **catharsis is denied** ("An attempt to get the family to express feelings at the moment of change does not necessarily increase the level of emotional integration") and **intervening in an adapted family is harmful** ("an inept intrusion"). So it is the *anxiety-relief-as-objective* half of C1 that this chapter contradicts, not the *symptom-relief-is-not-structural-change* half.
- **vs. C6 (the outside position is the intervention) — the sharpest positional demonstration in the corpus.** Two therapists, one patient, one hour: the family's **regular** therapist "had known about the cancer but had been waiting for the mother to bring it up… The therapist's own emotionality had prevented the wife from talking", and she "wiped away tears" during the interview. The **visiting** professor, with no history in the system, asked one question and the patient spoke for an hour. Bowen's own statement of the difference is positional, not skill-based: "She and I were the calmest people in the room." Corroborates C6 and adds that the *incumbent* professional is captured by the system exactly as Ch19 says the doctor is.
- **vs. C9 / L21.2 / L22.2 — the coached father is a differentiating move, and it beats the ally-cancellation and announcement rules.** The father held a course against explicit family opposition, and the reaction ladder is visible in miniature: his mother's "Son, that will be too hard on you" is a "change back" move, answered with "Mother, shut up. I can do it." The opposition then converted — "the criticism turned to admiration after the funeral home visit." Two deltas. (a) **Announcement:** Bowen announced the plan out loud to the assembled relatives and it worked, where Ch22 says "It is impossible to tell a family what one is trying to do and still make it work." The objects differ — Ch15 announces a **concrete decision** ("the father would take the children to the funeral home… no one else could be present"), Ch22 forbids announcing a **programme**. This is a third data point supporting L21.3's proposed resolution of L22.3/L10.7, from an independent chapter. (b) **Ally:** the father had an actively supporting ally throughout, and the move did not cancel — but the ally was a professional outside the family, consistent with L21.2's requirement that the *family* ally be detriangled.
- **vs. C7 (coupling does not decay with distance) — extended in the right direction, sharpened.** Ch06/08/10 establish that separation does not attenuate coupling. Ch15 goes further: symptoms land in a member who was **never close** to the deceased, via a carrier. Note however (P1.1) that this is a **path-length** finding, not a second-network finding.
- **vs. C4 (the marital tie gates the child's symptom) — a different route to child symptoms.** Here the children's state is set by the **surviving adult's affect**, directly and immediately: "the children had been responding to his emotionality rather than to the fact of the mother's death"; "the ability of children to deal with death depends on the adults"; "I have never seen a child hurt by exposure to death. They are 'hurt' only by the anxiety of survivors." No investment seesaw, no marital tie, and — because one parent is dead — no dyad to gate it.
- **vs. L19.2 / L19.3 — the professional's problem is inverted.** Ch19 studies how the family captures the doctor. Ch15 studies how the doctor's own anxiety corrupts what reaches the family, and puts a **third party between them** as the fix. Same triangle, opposite direction of causation, and here the therapist is the one who intervenes on the medical relationship rather than being recruited into it.

## P4. What was DROPPED

- **The over/under-functioning reciprocity (C2, seven chapters)** is entirely absent — no overadequate/inadequate pole, no signed transfer, no borrowed self. Notable because the material is right there: "removes the function of the breadwinner or the mother", "a key family member who becomes incapacitated… which prevents his doing the work on which the family depends", and the dying woman's stated fear of "leaving the daughter the responsibility of the father" — a role handoff described without the reciprocity vocabulary.
- **The differentiation scale, in every form.** No 0–100, no quartiles, no numbers, no basic-versus-functional split, and not even the term *differentiation*. "Level of emotional integration" and "overall level of life adaptation" do the work with no scale attached.
- **The triangle as a family mechanism.** One occurrence, denoting the physician-family-patient unit. No triangulation, no inside/outside positions, no interlocking triangles — despite a chapter full of three-party configurations that Ch09/Ch17 would describe as triangles.
- **Named concepts of the mid-70s theory:** family projection process (deferred — "The basic family process has been described in another chapter"), multigenerational transmission process, emotional cutoff, sibling position, societal regression. Only "Society no longer permits" gestures at the last.
- **The conserved-quantity apparatus** (F4 / C3 / L16.2) — see P3.
- **Calendar-time dosage (C8).** No session counts, no frequency, no duration of a course. The engagement is event-shaped: a single demonstration hour, or five days plus a year of contact.
- **The nuclear-family-versus-family-of-origin question (L14.3 / L22.4).** The unit of analysis here is the extended family throughout, but Bowen never frames it as a choice of working site.
- **Symptom-relief-as-a-trap warnings** — see P3.

## P5. Assigned open questions

**Q-INVERSION — verified, with three material qualifications. The passage in full:**

> "The length of time required for the family to establish a new emotional equilibrium depends on the emotional integration in the family and the intensity of the disturbance. A well integrated family may show more overt reactiveness at the moment of change but adapt to it rather quickly. A less integrated family may show little reaction at the time and respond later with symptoms of physical illness, emotional illness, or social misbehavior. An attempt to get the family to express feelings at the moment of change does not necessarily increase the level of emotional integration."

The inversion is real and pass 1 read it correctly in substance. The qualifications: (1) the inverted quantity is **overt** reactiveness — visible, expressed reaction at the moment of change — set against **later symptoms**; Bowen does not claim the less integrated family's *total* reaction is smaller, only that little shows at the time. (2) Both halves are hedged "**may** show". (3) It is `[A]` — no case, no count, no rate; the entire evidential basis is this sentence. Pass 1 additionally claimed integration determines "the shape of the response curve, **not its size**", which the preceding sentence ("The intensity of the emotional reaction is governed by the functioning level of emotional integration…") does not support. The correct reading for the simulation: **reaction amplitude and recovery time are separate functions of integration**, the immediate *overt* component runs opposite to the delayed *symptom* component, and the claim is hedged. It does contradict a model in which low differentiation means uniformly higher reactivity at every horizon — but the contradiction rests on one hedged, uninstanced sentence, and that should travel with it. Note also the fourth sentence, which is the reason the inversion matters clinically: you cannot manufacture the well-integrated family's response, because forcing expression "does not necessarily increase the level of emotional integration."

**Q-SHOCKWAVE — the propagation claims verify; the "not visible closeness" claim does not; there are no fine-grained intervals.**
- *Dependence network:* verified as stated — "It operates on an underground network of emotional dependence of family members on each other. The emotional dependence is denied, the serious life events appear to be unrelated, the family attempts to camouflage any connectedness between the events, and there is a vigorous emotional denial reaction, when anyone attempts to relate the events to each other." **But** Bowen says the network is *denied*, i.e. invisible to the family; he never says it is a different network from emotional closeness. See P1.1.
- *The grandchild:* verbatim — "The grandchild is often one who had little direct emotional attachment to the grandparents."
- *The two-hop case:* verbatim — "after the death of a grandmother, a daughter appeared to have no more than the usual grief reaction to the death but reacted in some deep way, transmitting her disturbance to a son who had never been close to the grandmother but who reacted to the mother with delinquency behavior." Confirmed: an asymptomatic carrier with ordinary grief re-emits to a symptom-bearer with no tie to the deceased.
- *Intervals:* **this chapter gives none at Ch21's resolution.** The general statement is "in the months or years following." Case-level figures are "Within the following two years" (mastectomy, four branches), "Some symptoms were continuing five years later", "During the subsequent two years" (aphasic-mother case), and "two years ago" with a loose year-by-year sequence in the self-diagnosing family. There is **no week- or month-scale interval and no stated ordering among affected members** — the mastectomy's four branches are listed, not sequenced. Ch21's death → ~2 weeks → ~2 weeks → ~3 weeks chain has no counterpart here. The one fast onset is in a **directly involved** member, not a downstream one: the son visiting his aphasic mother began the affair "enroute home on the plane." Conclusion: Ch21 supplies the intra-wave timing; Ch15 supplies only the envelope (months to years, tail at five years) and the multi-branch fan-out.

**Q-LADDER — the tiers and two pairwise relations verify; the seven-rung ordinal ladder does not.**
- Verbatim tier statements: "Among the deaths most likely to be followed by a serious and prolonged shock wave are the deaths of either parent when the family is young. This not only disturbs the emotional equilibrium, but it removes the function of the breadwinner or the mother at a time when these functions are most important." / "The death of an important child can shake the family equilibrium for years." / "The death of the 'head of the clan' is another that can be followed by a long-term underground disturbance." / "The grandmothers in these families usually lived in the shadow of their husbands, and their deaths were less important. The family reaction can be intense following the death of a grandmother who was a central figure in the emotional life and stability of the family."
- **Bowen does not rank young parent > important child > head of the clan.** They are an unranked list inside one tier, introduced by "Among the deaths most likely…". Only the young-parent case carries an extra stated reason. And **the text presents the shadow grandmother before the central grandmother** — pass 1 reversed the pair to produce a descending scale.
- *Burdensome member:* verified — "there are the family members whose deaths are a relief to the family. This includes the people whose functioning was never critical to the family, and who may have been a burden in their final illness. Their deaths may be followed by a brief period of grief and mourning, which is then followed by improved family functioning." The burden is a *may*, not a precondition.
- *Suicide:* verified verbatim — "Suicides are commonly followed by prolonged grief and mourning reactions, but the shock wave is usually minor unless the suicide was an abdication of an essential functional role."
- Also verified, and worth keeping with the ladder: "A shock wave rarely follows the death of a dysfunctional family member unless that dysfunction played a critical role in maintaining family emotional equilibrium."

**Q-ANNIVERSARY — the negative finding is re-verified and stands.** There is no anniversary effect in this chapter. The word does not appear; no date-anchored recurrence, no periodicity, no "on the anniversary of", no seasonal or calendar-keyed reaction is described anywhere. Every time figure is either an **elapsed interval** ("within the following two years", "during the subsequent two years", "months or years following"), a **persistence** measure ("some symptoms were continuing five years later"), a **latency to a delayed onset** ("respond later with symptoms"), a **delayed mourning** ("her mother had been dead six years… She cried for weeks"), or a **follow-up horizon** ("It has now been twelve years since the death"). The twelve-year figure is when Bowen is writing, not a recurrence. Pass 1 was right to record this defensively; a second full read confirms it. Do not model an anniversary spike from this chapter.

**Q-VALIDATION — no. Nothing in this chapter validates or measures anything.** There is no differentiation scale, no rating, no instrument, no inter-rater procedure, and no external criterion. The two assessment variables that are named — "level of emotional integration in the family" and "overall level of life adaptation" — are used without definition, scale, or procedure. The one procedure that exists is a **screening** step, not a measurement: "the Georgetown Family Section does a routine historical check for it in every family history", justified by an unquantified base rate ("a sufficiently high percentage of all families", "present to some degree in a significant percentage of families"). No number is ever attached to it. The chapter's outcome claims are likewise unmeasured clinical judgements — "the family adjustment has been perfect", "an optimum result", "I have never seen a terminally ill person who was not strengthened by such a talk" — all single-observer, none blinded, none counted. Bowen's own strongest supporting claim is a denominator with no numerator: "In twenty years of family practice, I have had contact with several thousand families, and I have been in the background 'coaching' families through hundreds of deaths and funerals."

**Q-MATERIAL — yes, but only in three unmodellable forms; there is no per-person resource variable.**
1. **A constraint on what a family can do with a death**, stated once and by the patient herself: she wanted a mausoleum so people would not walk on her grave, "But… we are poor people. We can't afford a mausoleum. When I die, I will be buried in a grave just like everyone else." This is the only place in the chapter where money determines a family's available action, and it acts on exactly the parameter Bowen says matters most — the directness of contact between the living and the dead. It pairs with the societal constraint ("Society no longer permits" home death and family-made coffins) as a second gate on the funeral-coaching move.
2. **Economic failure as a shock-wave output**, three times: "a daughter's husband failed in business", "his business was failing", and "failures in school or business" in the symptom taxonomy — plus the cost consequence "the doctor bills have become exorbitant." Money here is a symptom channel, an effect and not a cause.
3. **An occupational commitment gating physical presence:** the father's overseas assignment, his question about seeking release from it, and his later decision to take "an assignment closer to home, so he could return if he was needed."
Against these: the **breadwinner** function is named as a *function*, not as income — "it removes the function of the breadwinner or the mother at a time when these functions are most important" — which is functional importance, not an economic variable. No per-person material, economic, or financial state is defined, tracked, or given a mechanism anywhere in the chapter.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Two governors of reaction intensity (integration level, functional importance of the member added/lost) → `Family` + `Person` → an event's impact should be computed from a family-level integration state and a per-person `functional_importance` scalar, not as a fixed constant per event type. **[p2] Do not implement this as a product.** Bowen's connective is **"or"**, and he gives **no direction** for the integration term. Only the *recovery-time* sentence is conjunctive ("depends on the emotional integration in the family **and** the intensity of the disturbance"). Safe implementation: `functional_importance` sizes the impact; integration sets the **recovery rate**; the integration→amplitude relation is left unspecified except through the inversion below.
- `functional_importance` is distinct from emotional closeness and from generational position → `Person` → needs its own attribute; the "head of the clan" can be a disabled grandfather or a mid-generation sibling.
- **[p2 — rewritten]** Death severity comes in **three tiers**, not a seven-rung ladder → `event-kind` → tier 1 "a fair chance the death will be followed by a shock wave" (young parent — the only one with a stated extra mechanism, loss of the breadwinner/mother function; important child; head of the clan; a grandmother central to the family's emotional life); tier 2 neutral, "no more than the usual grief and mourning" (peripheral, "neither famous nor infamous"); tier 3 relief, followed by **improved** functioning (functioning never critical). Two pairwise relations are Bowen's and usable: central grandmother > shadow grandmother, and a load-bearing dysfunctional member > an ordinary dysfunctional member. **The strict ordinal ranking within tier 1 was pass 1's construction — do not calibrate against it.**
- Death of a non-critical member improves family functioning → `event-kind` → the loss impact must be **signed**; a model that only ever adds stress on death is wrong. **[p2] the criterion is non-critical functioning; "a burden in their final illness" is optional in the source ("may have been"), so do not gate the positive outcome on it.**
- Death of a *dysfunctional* member produces no shock wave *unless* the dysfunction was maintaining equilibrium → `Person` + `Family` → symptom-bearing itself can carry positive functional importance; `functional_importance` cannot be derived from functioning level.
- Suicide → high grief, low shock wave (unless it abdicated an essential role) → `event-kind` → grief magnitude and system-disturbance magnitude are **separate output channels** and must not be collapsed.
- Emotional Shock Wave: symptoms appear "anywhere in the extended family" over "months or years" → `Family` + `event-kind` → requires a delayed, distributed propagation mechanism with a long tail, operating over the *extended* family, not the household.
- **[p2 — rewritten, this was pass 1's largest error]** Shock wave travels an "underground network of emotional dependence" that the family **denies** → `Family` + `readout` → the requirement is a **belief layer** (the family cannot see the dependence graph and camouflages it harder when probed), **not** a second edge type. Pass 1 called for "a second, hidden edge weight (dependence) alongside any closeness/attachment weight"; the chapter does not license that. The unattached grandson is explained by path length, not by a distinct graph — see the next item. Corroborates L09.4 (the family's account is a separate, actively distorted layer) rather than adding new topology.
- Two-hop transmission (deceased → daughter with only ordinary grief → son who symptomatises) → `Relationship` → propagation must be transitive through carriers who show no symptom themselves; the carrier absorbs and re-emits without registering.
- Dependence is directional and fans out (mother dependent on daughter; son and father dependent on mother → all three symptomatic) → `Relationship` → dependence edges are directed and stress flows along them.
- Well-integrated family: high immediate **overt** reaction, fast recovery. Poorly integrated: low immediate overt reaction, delayed symptoms → `Family` → a single "stress" number cannot capture this; needs *overt reaction amplitude* and *recovery time* as separate, inversely coupled functions of integration. **This inversion is the single most simulation-critical claim in the chapter.** **[p2] Three caveats that must ship with it:** the inverted quantity is **overt** reaction, not total reaction — Bowen never says the less integrated family reacts less, only that less shows; both halves are hedged "**may** show"; and the claim is `[A]` with no case, count or rate behind it. It still contradicts a uniformly-higher-reactivity model, but a `test` built on it should assert the **delayed symptom channel**, which is the well-evidenced half, rather than a low immediate amplitude.
- Threatened death produces a shock wave "almost as severe" as death (mastectomy: 4 branches affected within 2 years, some symptoms at 5 years) → `event-kind` → a `THREATENED_LOSS` event kind distinct from `DEATH`, with comparable magnitude and no member removal.
- Symptom content is arbitrary — physical (colds, respiratory infections, onset of diabetes/allergies, acute medical/surgical illness), emotional (mild depression → phobias → psychotic episodes), social (drinking, school/business failure, abortions, illegitimate births, accidents, behaviour disorders) → `Person` + `test` → the shock wave should set an activation level; the *type* of symptom is drawn from the person's own predisposition.
- Three loss kinds — physical, functional, emotional → `event-kind` → departure, incapacity, and loss of a mood-carrier are three distinct events that all perturb equilibrium; a member can be functionally lost while still present.
- Addition disturbs equilibrium too (birth of a child; a grandparent moving in vs. visiting) → `event-kind` → `ADD_MEMBER` needs the same machinery as loss, and *duration of presence* matters (visit ≈ brief shift; moving in ≈ long-period change).
- Open/closed relationship system with anxiety-driven topic avoidance → `Relationship` → an `openness` scalar per dyad that decays with accumulated anxiety; courtship high, post-marriage declining.
- Closing is an automatic reflex to *the other's* anxiety, misreported as protecting the other → `Relationship` → the update rule reads the partner's anxiety, and the agent's stated reason is not the mechanism.
- Below some openness threshold a dyad cannot self-reopen; a third person is required → `Relationship` + `Triangle` + `coach` → a hysteresis/lock state on the openness variable that only an external low-anxiety node can release.
- The physician-family-patient triangle; professional anxiety as a system input → `Triangle` + `unmodelled` → the model likely has no place for a *professional* actor as a stress source inside the family system.
- Anxiety degrades message fidelity in both directions (jargon / oversimplification) and raises the receiver's demand for detail → `Relationship` → information transfer should have a fidelity term driven by sender and receiver anxiety, with a positive feedback loop.
- The receiver "responds to the affect, not the content"; both sides can believe they communicated → `Relationship` + `test` → a message can register as *delivered* while transferring nothing; a mutual-illusion-of-communication state.
- Anxiety propagates adult → child with priority over the event itself; children are hurt by survivors' anxiety, not by death → `Person` + `Relationship` → child stress should be sourced from the nearest adult's state, not from the event directly.
- Calmest person sets a floor; therapist's own reactivity is an input → `coach` → the coach agent needs its own anxiety state that affects outcomes, and low coach reactivity is a precondition for every move.
- Premature interpretation of interdependence → family terminates (6 sessions was not enough) → `move:INTERPRET_SHOCK_WAVE` + `coach` → a high-risk move with a readiness precondition and a hard failure outcome (disengagement), not merely a no-op.
- Probing denial directly → the family camouflages *further* → `Family` → denial should **increase** in response to a mistimed probe; the state gets worse, not unchanged.
- Denial is homeostatic and must be respected → `Family` → denial is a stabiliser with a cost, not an error flag.
- Routine shock-wave history check in every family → `move:TAKE_SHOCK_WAVE_HISTORY` → a low-risk assessment move available at intake that reveals hidden dependence edges to the coach.
- Direct vs. tangential words → `move:USE_DIRECT_LANGUAGE` → a cheap, contagious openness modifier applied to the whole conversational field.
