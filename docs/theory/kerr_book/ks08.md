## ⭐⭐ KS08.1 — The staircase: a differentiating change with **no move emitted** `[T]` `[M]`

Kerr, aged thirty, seven or eight months into studying the theory. His mother appears on the landing:

> "I watched her **place the back of her hand on her forehead** and saw that her **posture slumped a bit.** In retrospect, I had seen this change in her **many hundreds of times before. My stomach immediately tightened**, and I experienced an associated attitude that **I needed to do something to relieve Mother's distress.** I also felt the **urge to move toward her**… **I even started to move slightly, but I caught myself and stopped.**"

> "The impact of **seeing the process as between us rather than as a mother problem** resulted **immediately** in my feeling **less threatened and amazingly relaxed.** I smiled at her and asked if she was coming downstairs, which she did. **Smiling at her and feeling compassionate at such a moment had never occurred before.**"

And the crucial line for the model:

> "**I did not take any obvious I-position with Mother; I just did not anxiously hover over her.**"

*Model consequence:* **a differentiating change whose entire content is the withholding of a move.** `M5.A.1`'s repertoire has nine moves and `M4.D.1` requires each agent to select exactly one per tick — there is no representation for *the automatic move being generated, detected, and not emitted.* Yet this is the canonical instance in the book, and it is where the change happens.

Two requirements follow:

1. `M4.D` needs a **non-move** outcome that is distinct from `M4.D.5`'s fused default. Not an absence of action — an **interception**, with the automatic move computed and suppressed, which is exactly `M4.D.1a`'s self-directed channel overriding the automatic one on a single tick.
2. The **effect on the other is real and immediate** — the mother came downstairs, the moment passed, "she was fine". So a suppressed move **MUST** still change the tie state. An implementation in which not acting is a no-op cannot represent this.

Note the mechanism is *perception-side and pre-emptive*: the reappraisal happened at the cue, before selection, which places `systems_perspective` at appraisal exactly as `KS06.15` requires.

## ⭐⭐ KS08.2 — Guilt is **a product of the interaction**, not a property of the person `[T]`

> "**The guilt I had felt in dealing with Mother disappeared.** The pattern was that, **if Mother appeared to be slipping into an underfunctioning position, I would move into an overfunctioning position.** This happened enough over the years that it was **a source of chronic worry** for me… Now I saw a two-person emotional process at work for which neither person was to blame. **Guilt was the product of a relationship interaction, not a property of me.**"

> "This was my first experience of **intrapsychic process (guilt and 'should' feelings) being significantly modified by functioning with more solid self** in a relationship."

> "**dividing problems into relationship ones and intrapsychic ones, as many therapists do, is a false dichotomy. The relationship process and intrapsychic process are intertwined.**"

*Model consequence:* a strong licence for the model's architecture. Internal states the model does **not** represent — guilt, "should" feelings, self-worth — are **derivable from relational position** rather than requiring their own machinery. That is `M10.A.2`'s parsimony rule vindicated on the most tempting candidates for extra state.

It also means the model may legitimately **report** such states as readouts computed from position, which is worth having for interpretability without adding variables.

## ⭐⭐ KS08.3 — Anticipatory anxiety, and what dissolves it `[T]` `[M]`

> "**I recognized my increase in anxiety prior to contacts with Mother.** I loved Mother, wanted her to come, and was very glad to see her, but **there was anxiety afoot that I did not recognize as such.**"

> "**the anxiety I experienced prior to seeing Mother physically was related to my lack of confidence in dealing with just this type of experience on the stairs.** Having recognized the process… **I now had the confidence I could manage myself better with her. My anticipatory anxiety evaporated.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS02.8`'s airport vignette noted that all three parties were primed *before* contact; this gives the **mechanism**, and it is not simple expectation of a bad event. Anticipatory anxiety is a function of **predicted inability to manage oneself in the coming encounter** — a confidence term, not a threat term. It falls when the *capacity* rises, without the other party changing at all.

`M4.A`'s standing load should therefore carry a term derived from the agent's own recent performance on that tie, not only from the tie's history of events. That is implementable and it explains why the anticipatory component collapses discontinuously after a single successful encounter.

## ⭐⭐ KS08.4 — Erasing an emotional cue: the phone message `[T]` `[M]`

The pattern, given as a chain of **functional facts**: mother does not hear from daughter → begins catastrophising → leaves a message, "**I haven't heard from you for a while. Are you all right?**" → "the **anxiety in her voice and her message amounted to an expectation that the daughter call her** — and soon — to reassure her" → "**The daughter never responded to such messages**" → "**This predictably further increased the mother's anxiety and frustration.**"

The changed move:

> "**Hi. It's Mom. Haven't spoken with you for a while, and I just wanted you to know that I'm all right.**"

> "The mother's phone message, unlike previous messages too numerous to count, was **devoid of any expectation that the daughter respond.**"

> "The content of her message and lighthearted delivery had **erased a decades-old emotional cue from the system. It disrupted the emotional logic of the system. People immediately take notice when that happens because the expected sequence is not playing out.**"

> "**The daughter was able to hold onto her 'self' better when less burdened by her mother's anxiety-driven expectations. She began reaching out to her mother more.**"

*Model consequence:* **the clearest available statement of what `M5.F.1`'s hidden actor state actually modulates.** The two messages are the *same move type* to the *same target* with near-identical content. What differs is the **expectation embedded in it** — which is precisely `M1.A.9a`'s outward impingement axis. Same move, opposite effect, from the actor's state alone: `M5.F.2`'s requirement, demonstrated.

And the result is the model's causal claim run in reverse: **removing the impingement raised the other's functional level** ("hold onto her 'self' better") and **increased her voluntary contact**. That is a two-arm criterion — an `M11.C` candidate — and it is the mechanism behind `M11.C.5`'s success state from `KS00.6`.

Two guards Kerr states explicitly:

> "**Some react to Bowen theory and the above sort of exchange as being manipulative.** Some might say the mother was trying to guilt-trip her daughter… **Her tone of voice in the phone message was lighthearted, not a guilt-inflicting tone.**"

> "**If the mother had not developed emotional neutrality**… the daughter could have easily **heard her comment as sarcastic. People sense the difference between neutrality and same-old same-old.**"

> "The impact… by virtue of being based on **a new way of thinking and not on a technique** was powerful."

**A technique-versus-state discriminator, stated as such.** The identical utterance delivered from an unchanged internal state is read as sarcasm or manipulation and produces the opposite effect. This is `M5.F.2` again and it is why the model **MUST NOT** let a differentiating move be selected on move-type alone — `M5.F.4`'s degradation is not an edge case, it is the default outcome for an unprepared agent.

## ⭐ KS08.5 — The objectivity sequence, in two named parts `[T]`

> "**The first part in the sequence is recognizing one's own anxiety as just that: one's own anxiety. The second part is recognizing the verbal and nonverbal cues in others that trigger the anxiety and the impact that an anxious response has on others.**"

The client's own account of part one:

> "Before I studied anything about Bowen theory, I would have said that **I am… a perfectionist**… And now I have identified that **I am anxious.** And that was a big thing for me to recognize… before I would always **attach my anxiety to something someone did** in relation with me or something I did. And so it was a lot of time spent either **blaming someone else or blaming myself.**"

*Model consequence:* an **ordering** on `M1.A.18`'s development, and it maps onto quantities the model has. Part one is re-attributing an internal state from a trait ("I am a perfectionist") to a state ("I am anxious") — trait-to-state re-attribution. Part two is the reciprocity model.

The client's phrasing also shows the two blame directions arriving **together** ("blaming someone else or blaming myself"), which is the fourth statement that `M1.A.18a` needs both.

Dose recorded: "**about ten coaching sessions spaced over nearly a year.**" `[#]`

## ⭐ KS08.6 — `functional facts` and `functioning position`, defined `[T]`

> "Two key terms… are central to understand for developing more emotional objectivity: **functional facts** and **functioning position**."

> "**The term functioning position refers to how a person's position in a relationship system regulates that person's functioning and contributes to a system process.**"

A functional fact, exemplified: "**a predictable change in her emotional functioning in response to a predictable social cue.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS01.2`'s functional facts get a formal definition that is **exactly an event-response rule** — predictable change in functioning given a predictable cue — and `functioning position` is exactly the model's per-agent relational state. The theory's two working units and the model's two core objects coincide. Worth stating in `M0` as the architectural justification, since it is now stated in three chapters.

## ⭐ KS08.7 — The theory was validated on **what people did**, not what they said `[T]`

> "**Observing repeated interactions between mother and offspring revealed that both people perpetuated the intense attachment.** The conclusion was based on the **functional facts of their interactions, on what they did, not on what they said they did.**"

*Model consequence:* **fifth independent source** for the claim that replaced the withdrawn "content is noise" (`KB02` · K02.2, `KB08`, `KS00.12`, `KS01.2`). At five sources across three media this is as well-established as anything in the project, and it directly warrants the event-log-without-utterances architecture.

## KS08.8 — "Coach" defined, and why the term `[T]`

> "the **coach** (an alternative term to therapist to highlight that **the learning takes place in real world relationships more than in the relationship with the therapist**)"

> "as neutral and nonjudgmental as any psychoanalyst can be, **that person is not your mother. The analyst does not place the back of his hand on his forehead and slump a bit.** Working things out in the real world of important relationships has distinct advantages."

> Bowen's opinion, recorded: "**more intrapsychic change occurred by developing more of a 'self' with one's parents and important others than could occur with psychoanalysis**" (personal communication, 1970).

*Model consequence:* the project's own term is justified from the source, and the reason is architecturally load-bearing: **the change happens on the real ties, not on the tie to the external agent.** `M1.E`'s external agent is therefore correctly modelled as a *supplier of mindware* (`M1.E.7`) rather than as a relationship in which the work occurs. An implementation in which the coach tie carries the differentiating exchanges is a failing implementation.

## KS08.9 — "Emotional logic" — the system has an expected sequence `[T]`

> "when Bowen was developing his ideas about differentiation of self in one's family of origin, he hit on the idea of the **logic of an emotional system. It is not a rational logic; it is an emotional logic.**"

> "It **disrupted the emotional logic of the system. People immediately take notice when that happens because the expected sequence is not playing out.**"

*Model consequence:* a name for what the model computes. The family has a **predicted next move** at every point, and the differentiating act is defined by *violating that prediction* rather than by its own content. That is directly implementable from the learned repertoire (`M4.D.6`) — the automatic channel's own distribution **is** the expectation, so surprise is computable as the emitted move's improbability under it.

That gives `M5.D`'s landing condition (`M5.E.3` — absence of reaction means the move did not land) a **quantitative form**: a move lands to the degree it was unexpected under the family's learned distribution. Worth carrying into pass 2 as a candidate mechanism for `M5.F.3`'s continuous gradability.

## KS08.10 — Another's observation was required to see it `[T]`

> "**My wife had to point that out to me** from listening to me talk with my mother on the phone. **I denied it initially**, but her critique soon helped me observe what she had been observing."

*Model consequence:* a small but sharp point for `M1.E.7`: the input that starts the process came from **inside the system**, from a spouse, not from a coach — and it was **rejected on first delivery**. So the mindware channel is not coach-exclusive (compare `KS05.14`'s POW case, a third route), and delivery does not equal receipt. `M1.E.7`'s *landed* qualifier is carrying real weight, and the model should allow a contact to fail and later succeed on the same content.
