## Reading

Written by hand 2026-11-02 (T-176) and restated the same day after the review passes (learning-qa found that the rows
on the run before a cutoff carried the bias this page identifies; they now read cut ties with a contact only). It
quotes the tables above and computes nothing of its own.

**The trace reproduces the criterion.** The first two rows of each table are the record's `negative_run`
(`docs/phase_d2_ensemble_record.md`): −0.319 ± 0.104 on the Phase B family and −0.113 ± 0.062 on the twelve-person
family.

**What does not explain the reversal.**

- *Which uncut ties are compared.* On the Phase B family every member tie not declared cut off is cut off in the run on
  every seed (0 never cut off), so the row against ties cut off later equals the criterion's. On the twelve-person
  family the row against ties cut off later is −0.107 ± 0.062; the row against ties never cut off has 5 seeds and says
  nothing.
- *The mix of relations.* Against uncut ties of the same declared relation the difference is −0.340 ± 0.099 and
  −0.101 ± 0.069. (The table by relation pools every matched cutoff, those with no contact among them, so it carries
  the bias described next.)

**What does explain it: the two arms are read under different conditions.** The readout counts an uncut tie only if
it had a contact by the cutoff's week (`first_cutoff_runs`: "A tie with no contact in the window is not an uncut tie
in it"), but it counts a cut tie whether or not it had one, and a cut tie with no contact enters with a run of 0. 329
of the 1137 matched cutoffs on the Phase B family, and 1419 of the 4791 on the twelve-person family, are on a tie with
no contact at all by the cutoff's week. Put the same condition on the cut tie and the difference is +0.029 ± 0.136 on
the Phase B family (the interval contains zero) and +0.344 ± 0.078 on the twelve-person family (the direction the
criterion states). So the reversal comes from the comparison, as the review asked: from the contact condition the
baseline has and the treatment lacks, not from the choice of uncut ties. The version 2 plan's design for the
criterion (`docs/version_2_plan.md`, G1) said ties with no contact in the window are "reported, not dropped"; the
readout drops the uncut ones and reports no count of them.

**What the run before a cutoff does, on cut ties with a contact.** Each lag row reads the cutoffs in week k or later,
so the rows read different cutoffs; the row "the 4-week row on the cutoffs the 13-week row reads" puts two of them on
one set. A run grows on every tie between positive contacts, so the difference against the uncut ties over the same
weeks is the row that bears on the criterion.

- *The twelve-person family.* The cut tie's run lengthens before its cutoff (+0.378 ± 0.036, +0.966 ± 0.068 and
  +1.765 ± 0.113 over 4, 13 and 52 weeks) and by more than the uncut ties' over the same weeks (+0.054 ± 0.039,
  +0.218 ± 0.078 and +0.419 ± 0.141). In the 52 weeks up to the cutoff's week a
  cut tie had more contacts than the uncut ties (+0.353 ± 0.126) and more negative contacts (+0.540 ± 0.080).
- *The Phase B family.* The cut tie's run lengthens (+0.631 ± 0.089 over 4 weeks, +1.754 ± 0.397 over 13), but not
  by more than the uncut ties' (−0.021 ± 0.109 and −0.223 ± 0.504), and its contacts and negative contacts in the 52
  weeks do not differ from theirs (+0.136 ± 0.153, +0.060 ± 0.123). The 52-week rows have 2 seeds and say nothing.

**When the cutoffs come.** On the Phase B family the median matched first cutoff falls in week 6, 936 of the 1137
fall in weeks 0–12, and 2 seeds have one after week 52; on the twelve-person family the median is week 27 and the
criterion's difference over the cutoffs after week 52 is −0.022 ± 0.133 (that row keeps the cut ties with no contact).
The Phase B family's first cutoffs come before most ties have had time to build a run.

**Not computed.** The other way to read the arms alike, counting uncut ties with no contact in the baseline at a run
of 0, is not here: on the twelve-person family it needs the week from which each tie to a person born in the run
existed, and the trace does not read it. The trace changes no rule and no readout; whether the criterion's readout
should change is the owner's decision (T-179).
