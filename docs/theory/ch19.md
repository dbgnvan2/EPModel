# Ch19 — Problems of Medical Practice Presented by Families With a Schizophrenic Member

## 0. Provenance
- Stated date / venue (from the text itself): **Not stated.** No presentation date, conference, or journal is named in the chapter body. The only internal time anchors are the citations "(Bowen 1957, 1959, 1960)" for the parent project and the statement that the medical clinic's "experience over a three-year period" is the data base. Authorship is stated as **Robert H. Dysinger, M.D., and Murray Bowen, M.D.** — Dysinger is named first, and the chapter reads as written from the physician's chair.
- Clinical setting and data source:
  - **Seven family groups**, each consisting of both parents and a schizophrenic son or daughter, participating for periods "ranging from four to thirty-three months."
  - Families **lived on a special psychiatric ward operated by the project staff**; the clinical work centered on a psychotherapy designed for the family unit.
  - The medical data comes from a **ward clinic operated by a psychiatrist on the project staff**, deliberately "structured to operate as much as possible like an outpatient service," supplemented by consultation and other services available in a large medical research center.
  - Observation is explicitly **continuous and multi-context**: the staff saw each individual in relation to family and others in psychotherapy and on the ward, so medical encounters could be read "in its current context." Normal siblings are present in the observational field as a contrast group.
  - Retrospective data: "the histories indicated that the families' use of medical services in the past had been similarly involved in acted out emotional issues."
  - Three anonymised case vignettes (a mother with pelvic mass/fibroid uterus; a mother in her fifties with neck pain; a father with loss of function in "an important sense organ").
- **[p2] Not an independent data source.** The seven in-residence families, the special psychiatric
  ward "operated by the project staff", and the citations "(Bowen 1957, 1959, 1960)" identify this as
  the **same NIMH residential project** reported in Ch01, Ch05 (which names "seven in-residence
  families") and Ch08. Ch19 is a different *window* onto one dataset, not a replication. Any
  convergence count that treats Ch01 + Ch19 as two independent chapters overstates the evidence —
  this bears directly on C6, which lists both.
- **[p2] The medical service was free at point of use** — a project-run ward clinic inside a large
  medical research centre. No fee, insurance, or cost of any kind is mentioned anywhere. The family's
  ability to buy a second opinion cost only "visits to the city". Recorded because it is a structural
  precondition of the splitting move that the chapter never names.
- Genre: **both, weighted to theory/observation — roughly 80% descriptive-theoretical, 20% technique.** The chapter is primarily a phenomenology of how a family behaves toward a physician, generalised into two "modes of functioning." Technique is present but almost entirely *implicit* — it must be read out of what the doctor is described as doing well or being pulled into doing badly. There is no explicit list of instructions to the physician.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Medical practice on the ward met "consistent difficulties"** in accomplishing ordinary medical evaluation and treatment — difficulties consistent enough to look like "manifestations of patterned emotional processes" rather than accidents of personality. → Ward clinic, seven families with a schizophrenic son or daughter, over three years.
- **The difficulty was not proportional to the medical facts.** Settling "even trivial medical matters in a satisfactory sensible way was often laborious and difficult," and the difficulty occurred "whether or not significant medical problems were present." → Same population.
- **Real medical problems coexisted with the emotional use of the clinic.** "Significant, poorly defined medical problems were not uncommon especially in the parents, and effective medical service was often clearly indicated." The emotional process therefore obstructed care that was genuinely needed. → Parents especially.
- **Actual emergency was the one condition under which the difficulty subsided**: the emotional use of the medical setting "was minimal on the few occasions of actual medical emergency." → Same population.
- **The difficulty was distributed by family position, not spread evenly.** It was "characteristic in the medical work with the parents and the schizophrenic sons or daughters, but were rarely experienced with normal siblings." → Within the same families, the normal siblings are the negative control.
- **Turning to medicine was itself an emotional act.** "The turning to and using of medical services was often to a striking extent an enacted emotional process which took the form of an interest in medical diagnosis and treatment." → Observed across the seven families.
- **Family members split the professional field.** The mother in the first vignette, already examined by a project gynecology consultant who found a pelvic mass, "with no mention of it on the ward, ... consulted practicing gynecologists during visits to the city," withholding her history from the outside doctor and asking for an opinion "on the basis of examination only." The outside doctor's phone call back to the ward is the only reason the staff learned of it.
- **Poor discrimination between feeling and physical fact.** "The parents and the schizophrenic child discriminated poorly between intense feelings of helplessness and anxiety on the one hand and evidences of medical problems on the other."
- **Two reproducible presentation styles** were identifiable across members and episodes (acted-out helplessness; acted-out denial of helplessness), plus a third variant in which the helplessness is located in another person.
- **The pattern is historical, not situational.** Past histories show the same involvement of medical services in acted-out emotional issues, so the ward clinic is reproducing a pre-existing family pattern rather than creating one.
- **Documented medical harm.** A father's delay in getting attention to a sense-organ problem meant "the serious loss of function that resulted was largely a consequence of delay in arranging appropriate medical attention."
- **[p2] A claim's accuracy is independent of its emotional function.** "A person could notice something physical and estimate it to be of no consequence. The estimate **might be accurate or not** and yet be largely in the service of a denial of feelings of helplessness." Pass 1 missed this sentence entirely. It is the chapter's cleanest statement that a health communication carries *content*, *truth value*, and *emotional function* as three separable attributes — a true statement can be a full-strength emotional act.
- **[p2] The acting-out is continuously graded and can be behaviourally undetectable.** Mode 1 "could be gross or so subtle as to masquerade for sensible action." There is no behavioural signature at low amplitude; at the low end the move is indistinguishable from ordinary sensible medical help-seeking.
- **[p2] Concurrence is partial, not binary.** "The finding of any physical condition is one of the things that the doctor can do which appears to be taken as at least a **token concurrence**." Pass 1 missed this. A doctor can partially satisfy the demand without deciding to, and without knowing he has.
- **[p2] The splitting vignette ended in successful medical care.** "Later a laparotomy was performed and a rather large fibroid uterus successfully removed." Pass 1 wrote this episode up purely as system defeat; the medical outcome was good. Splitting cost the *system* visibility, not the *patient* her treatment.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Helplessness–illness equivalence (the core mechanism).** Family members function "from an assumption that feeling helpless was equivalent to being ill, and not feeling helpless was equivalent to being healthy." → What it does: converts an internal affective state into a claim about the body. → What it acts on: the person's own reading of physical sensation, and hence everything they say to a doctor. Note this is a two-sided equivalence: it also makes *not* feeling helpless into evidence of health, which is what powers the denial mode.
- **Acting out of feelings of helplessness (mode 1).** → What it does: the person "related himself at each step of his dealings with the doctor in a way that accented helplessness," elaborating physical experience and incapacity "beyond the actual difficulty," distorting past histories, and citing past diagnoses "to document a picture of major illness." → What it acts on: the doctor's estimate of severity, and ultimately the doctor's willingness to certify illness. Characteristic of mothers and of the schizophrenic sons or daughters; "also common for the fathers."
- **Acting out of a denial of feelings of helplessness (mode 2).** → What it does: symptoms and incapacity "minimized out of proportion to actual difficulty," negative findings from previous check-ups emphasized, value of previous medical efforts minimized, "the possibility that a problem was psychological could be given prominence." → What it acts on: the doctor's threshold for investigating at all. Characteristic of fathers, occurs also in mothers, "but was not seen in the son or daughter."
- **Emotional tone as the transmission channel.** In mode 1 the tone is "grim, urgent, and serious," possibly "imperiously demanding, plaintive, or simply insistent"; the chapter says explicitly "this feeling was infectious and could tend to hurry the doctor into a view that the problem was actually an emergency." In mode 2 the tone is "casual, friendly, jocular, and appealing," and "tended to lull the doctor into a premature view that no problem existed." → What it does: transfers the family's emotional position into the doctor's clinical judgment without passing through evidence. → What it acts on: the doctor's affect first, his diagnosis second.
- **Induction of the doctor into responsibility.** The stated end of the process: the acted-out helplessness "appeared to maintain an emotional pressure to induce the doctor to assume a very great responsibility for the intense feelings by virtue of a diagnosis of illness." The denial mode does the same "by virtue of a diagnosis of health." → What it does: relocates the ownership of an unbearable feeling into a professional's formal opinion. → What it acts on: the doctor's role, which becomes a functional position inside the family's emotional system.
- **The action messages** (Bowen/Dysinger state each mode as an unspoken sentence the behaviour enacts):
  - Mode 1: *"I feel intensely helpless, therefore I am ill. The doctor must recognize this, and when he has agreed, I then have an answer to my problem."*
  - Mode 2: *"I am almost certain that I am not helpless, therefore I am in good health; a doctor must recognize this and when he has agreed I will then be certain of my answer to the problem."*
  - Mode 1-externalised: *"I feel helpless, it is because he is ill; a doctor must agree with me, and when he does, I then have an answer to my problem."*
- **Externalisation onto another family member (mode 1 variant).** "On many occasions two or more family members were active about a medical matter," differing from mode 1 "in that the problem was seen to exist in another rather than in the self." Most common form: a parent acting from concern for the schizophrenic son or daughter, mother more commonly than father; the mother's acted-out concerns about the father "were also significant." → What it does: discharges the concerned member's helplessness through a medical claim about someone else. → What it acts on: the other member's body as the site, and the doctor as the required adjudicator.
- **Displacement from the doctor-contact position.** Where medical work has already been initiated by one member, a second member "might even move to displace the other from his position of dealing with the doctor about his own problem" — or in milder form "simply invite himself to be also present at the other's appointment." → What it does: contests ownership of the relationship with the professional. → What it acts on: who holds the channel to the doctor.
- **The ambiguity mechanism (professional vs. social framing).** Contact is sought outside the clinic — "vague allusions to health matters in the context of a social greeting, acting sick or speaking of symptoms to others in the doctor's presence, and messages about vague ailings that the doctor heard as a rumor from others." The doctor "could later discover that his response or lack of it had been given the weight of a professional opinion." → What it does: extracts a binding professional verdict from a non-professional exchange, with no examination and no record. → What it acts on: the boundary of the professional role itself. The same ambiguity appeared inside booked appointments "when the person related himself as though he were in a social situation."
- **Resistance to confirmation.** Medical inquiry in mode 1 "often met with obtuseness, vagueness, irrelevant elaboration, helpless unresponsiveness, or pressure for treatment"; in mode 2 with "vagueness about simple facts, plausible explanations, reassurances that the doctor need not worry, and when anxiety mounted, actual retreating." → What it does: prevents the emotional claim from being tested against fact.
- **[p2] Token concurrence as a graded concession.** "The finding of any physical condition is one of the things that the doctor can do which appears to be taken as at least a token concurrence." → What it does: partially discharges the mode-1 demand *without* the doctor conceding a diagnosis. → What it acts on: the pressure level, which falls in proportion to how much of the claim the professional's output can be read as endorsing. Consequence: concession is a scalar, not a branch. Note the asymmetry with the critical point — a *finding* is partial credit; a *statement of no evidence of a medical problem* is a critical point.
- **[p2] Accuracy-independence of the mode.** "The estimate might be accurate or not and yet be largely in the service of a denial of feelings of helplessness." → What it does: decouples the truth of a health claim from the emotional work it performs. → What it acts on: any professional (or model) that tries to detect the mode from the correctness of the claim. It cannot be done.
- **The critical point.** When the doctor's finding contradicts the emotional position — a statement of no medical problem (mode 1), completion of treatment (mode 1), or an opinion that a problem exists (mode 2) — the relationship reaches "a critical point"/"an anxious point." At that point the person "could create an unmistakable impression that he found the doctor unsatisfactory," which "operated as an intense pressure to alter medical opinion to accommodate to emotional goals." → What it does: presents the doctor with a forced choice, "losing working contact or compromising his best judgment." → What it acts on: the persistence of the professional's own position.

## A3. Predictive claims

Stated as conditionals. Evidence status marked. The chapter's evidence throughout is direct clinical observation of seven families over three years plus three illustrative vignettes; it offers no statistics or controls, so "observational" below means "reported as regularly observed, not measured."

1. **If a family contains a schizophrenic son or daughter, then its use of medical services will be extensively involved in emotional process** — for the parents and the schizophrenic member, but *not* for the normal siblings. → Observational; the sibling contrast is asserted from the same clinical field ("rarely experienced with normal siblings").
2. **If a person cannot discriminate helplessness from illness, then feeling helpless will be presented as being ill, and not feeling helpless as being healthy.** → Asserted as the interpretation that gives the observations "some order"; inferred from many observations rather than independently tested.
3. **If a family member acts out helplessness, then the doctor is more likely to overdiagnose and overtreat.** Stated in the summary: "the emotional pressures tended to lead to inaccurate medical overdiagnosis and overtreatment in response to the acted out feelings of helplessness." → Observational.
4. **If a family member acts out denial of helplessness, then the doctor is more likely to make an inaccurate estimate of good health** — "inaccurate medical estimates of good health in response to the acted out denial pattern." → Observational.
5. **If the presentation is delivered with urgent, grim emotional tone, then the doctor is more likely to be hurried into treating the problem as an emergency** ("this feeling was infectious"). → Observational.
6. **If the presentation is casual, friendly, and jocular, then the doctor is more likely to be lulled into a premature conclusion that no problem exists.** → Observational.
7. **If medical findings differ from the emotional view, then the clinical relationship reaches a difficult/critical point.** Stated twice, once in the body and once in the summary. → Observational; presented as regular. **[p2] CORRECTED — three triggers, not four.** The chapter names exactly three: a statement that diagnostic study revealed no evidence of a medical problem (mode 1); completion of treatment (mode 1); an opinion that a medical problem existed (mode 2, called "an anxious point"). **A definite diagnosis is NOT a critical point** — pass 1 listed it as one. The text says the opposite: a definite diagnosis "tended to be seen as the source of all problems", and any physical finding is "taken as at least a token concurrence". Diagnosis is the *relief* branch, not the *rupture* branch.
8. **If a critical point is reached, then the doctor faces pressure to alter his opinion, and appears to face a choice between losing working contact and compromising his judgment.** → Observational. **[p2]** The hedge is verbatim: "The doctor **seemed** to be faced with the choice of losing working contact or compromising his best judgment."
9. **[p2] WITHDRAWN AS A CONDITIONAL — the chapter states no antecedent.** Pass 1 wrote: "If the doctor holds his judgment through the critical point, then the encounter could resolve toward a more adequate recognition." The chapter supplies **only the consequent**: "This anxious encounter between the emotional process and medical judgment could then resolve toward a more adequate recognition of the actual problems." The grammatical subject is *the encounter*, not the doctor, and no action by the doctor is named as its condition. The chapter never uses the words hold, maintain, persist, or stand at this point. What the sentence does carry is a weak structural implication — an "encounter **between** the emotional process **and medical judgment**" presupposes medical judgment is still present, i.e. not yet surrendered. That is an inference from a noun phrase, not a stated rule. → Status: **consequent observational and hedged ("could"); the conditional is the reader's.**
9b. **[p2] The one demonstrated instance of a doctor holding a differing position, and what the family did about it.** In the neck-pain vignette the mother "repeat[ed] her own diagnosis as though to ask the doctor's assent. When he replied that his impression was somewhat different, **the mother introduced another problem**." → Single case, but this is the chapter's only *behavioural* evidence for the third branch: the position was held, contact was not lost, and the family's change-back move was **topic substitution** — opening a new front — rather than attack or withdrawal.
10. **If the doctor states an initial clinical impression, then the patient (mode 1) often becomes more thoughtful and may postpone medical work.** → Observational; notable because it says a *provisional, early* statement of position damps the process.
11. **If a definite diagnosis is made (mode 1), then it tends to be seen as the source of all problems.** → Observational.
12. **If treatment is undertaken (mode 1), then it will be complicated by vagueness about symptoms and pressure to prolong medication; and completion of treatment will itself produce a critical point.** → Observational.
13. **If treatment is undertaken (mode 2), then the denial continues into treatment as "casualness about treatment measures and unreliable reports of progress."** → Observational.
14. **If denial of helplessness governs, then real pathology is delayed and functional loss may follow** — the father's sense-organ loss was "largely a consequence of delay." → Single case, causal attribution asserted.
15. **If contact is sought socially rather than through an appointment, then the doctor's casual response (or non-response) may later be treated as a professional opinion.** → Observational.
16. **If two or more members become active about one medical matter, then the doctor will be looked to for a resolution of the intense differences between them.** → Observational.
17. **If one parent acts out helplessness externalised into the son or daughter, then that is the most intense of all multi-member situations** — "the most prominent and intense of all the situations involving more than one member." → Observational, and stated as a ranking. **[p2] Note the scope of the ranking, which pass 1 broke.** It is a maximum *within* multi-member situations only. The chapter nowhere compares multi-member to single-member intensity, and nowhere states that intensity is a function of the number of people a claim spans. Also note the subject is "**one or both** parents", so it is not restricted to a single parent.
17b. **[p2] If a definite diagnosis is made, then any physical finding is taken as at least a token concurrence** — so the demand is partially satisfied and the pressure partially discharged, short of full agreement. → Observational and doubly hedged ("appears to be taken as **at least** a token").
17c. **[p2] If a person's own estimate of a physical sign is emotionally driven, then its accuracy carries no information about whether it is emotionally driven.** "The estimate might be accurate or not and yet be largely in the service of a denial." → Stated as an illustrative example, not a finding, but stated unambiguously.
18. **If there is an actual medical emergency, then the emotional use of the medical situation is minimal.** → Observational; a real, dischargeable threat appears to displace the enacted one.

## A5. Terminology

- **Acting out of feelings of helplessness** → mode 1. A way of relating to the doctor in which every step "accented helplessness": illness emphasised, incapacity elaborated beyond the actual difficulty, histories distorted toward major illness, tone urgent. In this chapter "acting out" means *enacting a feeling as behaviour toward another person* rather than the psychoanalytic sense of discharging impulse; the emphasis is on the relational effect (what it induces in the doctor).
- **Acting out of a denial of feelings of helplessness** → mode 2. The mirror image: health emphasised, symptoms minimised, past negative findings emphasised, tone casual and reassuring.
- **Feelings of helplessness** → the primary affect the whole chapter is organised around. Never separately defined; treated as the intense, unbearable state that the family's medical behaviour is a management strategy for. Paired once with anxiety ("intense feelings of helplessness and anxiety"). *This term appears to be doing the job that "anxiety" does in Bowen's later vocabulary* — it is the quantity that seeks discharge, that is externalised onto another member, and that a professional is recruited to absorb.
- **Enacted emotional process** → the family's turning to medical services described as an emotional act taking "the form of an interest in medical diagnosis and treatment." The medical content is the vehicle, not the substance.
- **Action message** → an unspoken sentence the behaviour is functionally saying; the chapter gives three, each quoted in full above. A useful device: it converts a behavioural pattern into an explicit demand on the other party.
- **Critical point / anxious point in the clinical relationship** → the moment at which medical finding and emotional position openly diverge, and the emotional use of the medical situation "was unequivocally apparent." Named as a specific, recurring, high-information event rather than a breakdown.
- **Concern about another** → the externalised variant of mode 1: "the problem was seen to exist in another rather than in the self." *This is functionally a two-into-three move* — the concerned member, the designated ill member, and the doctor as adjudicator — but the chapter does not use the word triangle, projection, or undifferentiation anywhere.
- **Ambiguity about ... professional capacity** → the state produced by health-talk in social settings, in which neither party can say whether a professional opinion has been given.
- **[p2] Token concurrence** → the chapter's own term for a *partial* satisfaction of the demand: "the finding of any physical condition… appears to be taken as at least a token concurrence." Important because everything else in the chapter is written as an all-or-nothing agreement. This is the one place where the professional's output is graded.
- **[p2] Masquerade for sensible action** → the chapter's phrase for the low-amplitude end of mode 1, at which the acting-out is behaviourally indistinguishable from reasonable help-seeking. Establishes mode 1 as a continuum with a detectability floor, not a discrete state.
- **[p2] "Feelings of helplessness" — cross-chapter check.** Pass 1 guessed that this term is doing the job "anxiety" does later. The corpus supports it lexically: **Ch05** describes the same seven in-residence families as "never able to deal with their **helplessness**". Same project, same word, same quantity. But note the chapter pairs the two terms rather than substituting one for the other — "intense feelings of helplessness **and** anxiety" — so they are named side by side, not identified. Treat as **the same quantity under an earlier name**, with the caveat recorded.
- **Normal siblings** → the non-symptomatic children, used here purely as a contrast: the medical difficulties "were rarely experienced" with them. **[p2] "Rarely", not "never" and not "exempt".** The chapter makes a frequency claim, offers no explanation for it, and never says the siblings are outside the emotional process.
- Not present in this chapter: differentiation, triangle, projection process, fusion, undifferentiated family ego mass, emotional system, self. The vocabulary is deliberately medical-clinical.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **Composition of the studied unit:** both parents plus one schizophrenic son or daughter, living together on the ward. Normal siblings exist and are observed but are not the unit of treatment.
- **[p2] CORRECTION — mode is role-BIASED, with exactly one hard exclusion.** Pass 1's framing ("mothers → mode 1, fathers → mode 2") reads a bias as a rule and the model implication section then hardened it further into a role→mode mapping. The chapter's actual distribution, verbatim:
  - Mode 1 "was characteristic of the mothers and the schizophrenic sons or daughters and **was also common for the fathers**."
  - Mode 2 "was characteristic of the fathers, **occurred also in mothers**, but **was not seen in the son or daughter**."
  So **both parents show both modes**; only the symptomatic child is constrained. The single stated hard fact of role structure in this chapter is *the son or daughter never denies*. Everything else is a tendency.
- **Mothers** → mode 1 characteristic, mode 2 also occurs. Also the most frequent actor in the externalised variant: "a parent's action from concern for the schizophrenic son or daughter, the mother more commonly than the father." Also generate significant "acted out concerns about the father."
- **Fathers** → mode 2 characteristic, mode 1 "also common for the fathers." The one vignette of medical harm through delay is a father.
- **Schizophrenic son or daughter** → mode 1 only. The chapter is explicit that mode 2 "was not seen in the son or daughter." This is the sharpest role asymmetry in the chapter: the symptomatic member has access to only one of the two available modes.
- **Normal siblings** → difficulties "were rarely experienced" with them. **[p2] CORRECTION — pass 1 wrote "outside the pattern" / "exempt". The chapter says "rarely".** And there is a **confound pass 1 did not see**: the treatment unit was "both parents and a schizophrenic son or daughter" living on the ward; normal siblings were not resident members of it. Their exposure to the ward clinic was therefore structurally lower than the parents' and the patient's. The chapter offers no explanation for the difference and does not consider exposure. So the sibling contrast is **suggestive, not clean**, and pass 1's inference from it (below) has to carry that qualification.
- **[p2] The inference pass 1 drew from the siblings is NOT the chapter's.** Pass 1 concluded that professional-recruitment behaviour gates on position inside the emotional process rather than on family membership, and the ledger (L19.4) records it as a chapter finding. The chapter never says this. It states the distribution and stops — no mention of position, involvement, projection, or any variable that would distinguish an involved from an uninvolved member. The inference is *available and consistent* with the chapter (and is strongly supported elsewhere in the corpus — Ch16's generational update rule grades siblings by involvement, Ch10's L10.6 splits per-person self from per-relationship intensity), but it must be sourced to the corpus, not to Ch19.
- **The symptom-bearer as a site rather than an actor:** in the most intense multi-member situations, the parents' helplessness is "externalised as a medical problem seen in the son or daughter." The child's body becomes the location of the parents' feeling.
- **The doctor** → an occupied position, not an outside observer. He is pressured to take on "a very great responsibility for the intense feelings," and is looked to "for a resolution of the intense differences" between members. The professional service is described from the start as "one part of the clinical services" — i.e. structurally inside the same field as the psychotherapy and the ward.
- **Who moves first:** contact is often initiated obliquely and by the person acting out, outside the appointment structure; in multi-member episodes a second member may arrive after the first and displace him from the doctor-contact position.
- **The member whose health is in question** has four enumerated response options to another's concern: "might accept the concern of the second member as his own; might go along with it without acceptance; defensively oppose it, or on occasion maintain his own view." That is a clean four-state role-response taxonomy.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

Note: this chapter's "therapist" is a **physician**, not the family therapist. Almost everything here is about how a non-psychiatric professional keeps his function while an emotional system works on him. That makes it unusually directly usable as a specification for an external-agent role.

## B1. What the therapist (physician) does

**[p2] Read this section as a reconstruction, not a transcript.** The chapter contains no instructions to the physician at all. Every bullet below is either (a) a description of how the clinic was set up, or (b) a single vignette instance of something a doctor did. Pass 1 said this in the provenance note and then wrote the section in the imperative anyway. The items marked *[single instance]* below occur exactly once, in one vignette, and are not stated as practice.

- **Define the boundary of the role in advance.** "The responsibilities of the clinic were carefully defined to distinguish them from psychiatric functions." **[p2]** Pass 1 wrote "in advance **and in writing**" — "in writing" is nowhere in the chapter. Withdrawn. The doctor's job ends when the *nature* of the problem has been recognised: "the responsibility of the clinic was considered discharged when the nature of the problem had been recognized."
- **Route emotional problems to the channel that owns them.** "The treatment of emotional problems was a psychiatric responsibility, and many things commonly handled symptomatically in medical practice were, in this situation, matters for attention in psychotherapy."
- **Deal only with the emotional material that impairs the medical function.** "The doctor necessarily dealt with those emotional problems that operated to impair the medical functioning of the clinic." — a narrow, explicitly bounded warrant for engaging emotional process.
- **Structure the clinic to look like an ordinary outpatient service.** "The clinic was structured to operate as much as possible like an outpatient service." Structure is itself the intervention.
- **Persist through vagueness to get a usable history.** *[single instance]* In the neck-pain vignette: "Persistence by the doctor developed a reasonable trustworthy history out of much diffuse talk."
- **State an initial clinical impression early.** Doing so is reported as changing the patient's behaviour: the patient "often became more thoughtful and at times decided to postpone medical work." **[p2]** This one *is* generalised ("often"), and it is stated for **mode 1 only** — the chapter reports no equivalent effect in the denial mode.
- **State a differing impression plainly when asked to assent.** *[single instance]* When the mother repeated her own diagnosis "as though to ask the doctor's assent," the doctor "replied that his impression was somewhat different." **[p2] And record what happened next, which pass 1 omitted: "the mother introduced another problem."** Holding the position cost no contact and produced no attack; it produced a *new front*. This is the chapter's only observed instance of a professional not moving, and the only observed change-back reaction to one.
- **Continue diagnostic study rather than settle at the first impression.** *[single instance]* In the same vignette, weeks of further study established both a minor chronic problem *and* that it did not account for the complaints — a two-part finding.
- **Say when findings support an emotional basis.** *[single instance]* The study "supported the view that these were on an emotional basis" — stated, not implied.
- **[p2] WITHDRAWN as a stated technique: "hold the medical position through the critical point."** Pass 1 listed this as the chapter's technique and its "only stated good outcome". The chapter states the outcome — "This anxious encounter between the emotional process and medical judgment could then resolve toward a more adequate recognition of the actual problems" — but names **no action by the doctor as its condition**, and uses no verb of holding anywhere in the passage. What the corpus does supply, from outside this chapter, is the same operation under other names: Ch10's neutral third position ("the therapist's neutrality and a family member's self-control are the same operation from different positions"), Ch20's "failed to state position" as one of three named lapses, and Ch13/Ch21's hold-course-through-the-attack state machine. **Keep the design commitment; move its source.**
- **Read the medical encounter in the family's current context.** The staff's continuous observation of each person "in relation to his family and others both in psychotherapy and on the ward" is what made the pattern visible at all — the doctor is not working from the consulting room alone.
- **Use consultants and accept information from outside professionals.** The gynecology consultant, the outside doctor who telephoned; the outside doctor's call is what surfaced the split.

## B2. What the therapist (physician) must NOT do

Mostly stated as pressures to be resisted rather than as commandments; each is written here as the prohibition it implies.

- **Do not let the emotional tone set the urgency.** The urgent tone "could tend to hurry the doctor into a view that the problem was actually an emergency" — so do not take urgency as data.
- **Do not let a casual, friendly tone set the all-clear.** It "tended to lull the doctor into a premature view that no problem existed."
- **Do not compromise medical judgment to preserve the working relationship.** At the critical point "the doctor seemed to be faced with the choice of losing working contact or compromising his best judgment." **[p2] CORRECTION — pass 1 over-read the hedge.** Pass 1 wrote that "the word *seemed* marks this as a false dilemma the doctor is not meant to accept on its terms," and the ledger escalated it to "*the hedge is load-bearing*". It is not load-bearing. Pass 1's own X2 section lists ten instances of "appeared to / seemed to / the indications were that" in a ~2,600-word chapter: this is the authors' uniform house hedge, applied identically to claims they plainly do believe ("the acted out helplessness **appeared to** maintain an emotional pressure"). "Seemed" here carries no more authorial signal than any of the others. The hedge **is** verbatim present, as pass 1 said — but it does not by itself establish that the dilemma is false. The false-binary reading is the model's, and it should be argued from the "encounter **between** the emotional process **and medical judgment**" phrasing and from the neck-pain vignette, not from one modal verb.
- **Do not give opinions in social contexts.** Health talk in a social greeting, symptoms spoken to others in the doctor's presence, ailments reaching him as rumour — because "the doctor could later discover that his response or lack of it had been given the weight of a professional opinion." Note that *silence* is also captured and used ("his response or lack of it"); avoidance is not a defence.
  **[p2] But the chapter contains a counter-instance, and pass 1 flattened it.** In the father vignette, the father "casually mentioned" his sense-organ difficulties "to a physician friend" — a social-frame contact. "His friend **apparently sensing that the matter might be of some moment** examined him and quickly recognized a serious problem." The professional **promoted** the social mention into a professional act, on his own initiative, and that is what saved what function remained. So frame promotion runs both ways: the family retroactively promotes a professional's non-response into an opinion; a professional can promote a family member's social mention into an examination. A rule of "never engage outside the professional frame", applied literally, produces the father's outcome. The chapter never reconciles these.
- **Do not let an appointment be conducted socially.** The ambiguity "was frequently present in clinic appointments also when the person related himself as though he were in a social situation."
- **Do not accept authoritative-sounding medical language as a definition of the case.** "Medical terms were used authoritatively as though they adequately defined the situation."
- **Do not accept reported past histories and prior diagnoses at face value.** Histories were distorted in *both* directions — toward illness in mode 1, toward health in mode 2.
- **Do not accept reassurance ("the doctor need not worry") as evidence.**
- **Do not treat symptomatically to relieve the pressure.** On this ward, things "commonly handled symptomatically in medical practice" were deliberately not so handled.
- **Do not extend medication under pressure.** Mode 1 brought "pressures to prolong the use of medications."
- **Do not accept the patient's reports of progress uncritically in the denial mode** — "unreliable reports of progress."
- **Do not become the arbiter between two family members' views of a third's health.** "The doctor was often looked to for a resolution of the intense differences" — named as a pressure, and the chapter offers no instance where accepting it helped.
- **Do not accept a diagnosis being made into the explanation of everything.** "When a definite diagnosis was made it tended to be seen as the source of all problems."

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition, structural:** the clinic's responsibilities must be defined and distinguished from psychiatric ones *before* the work starts. Every later move by the doctor depends on this boundary already existing.
- **Precondition, informational:** the doctor needs the family context. The chapter attributes its whole reading to continuous observation across psychotherapy and ward; without that, the medical encounter is uninterpretable.
- **Precondition, structural:** a psychiatric channel must actually exist to receive what the doctor declines to treat. The clinic can discharge responsibility at recognition only because psychotherapy is there to take it.
- **Typical episode sequence (mode 1):** oblique/social approach → (possibly) appointment avoided → appointment with urgent presentation → doctor persists for history → doctor states initial impression → patient becomes more thoughtful / may postpone → diagnostic study → **branch**: either a *finding* (→ "token concurrence"; and if a **definite** diagnosis, it "tended to be seen as the source of all problems") **or** a statement of *no evidence of a medical problem* (→ **critical point**) → if treated, vagueness about symptoms and pressure to prolong medication → **critical point at completion of treatment**.
  **[p2] CORRECTION — pass 1 put a critical point at "a definite diagnosis". The chapter puts the opposite there.** The two mode-1 critical points are at **no-finding** and at **completion of treatment**. A definite diagnosis is the branch on which the family gets what it came for. This error propagated into pass 1's A3, X3 and the model implications section, and into ledger entry L19.2 ("at a negative finding, **a definite diagnosis**, completion of treatment, or an assertion that a problem exists"). Three triggers, not four.
- **Typical episode sequence (mode 2):** problem noticed and not reported → minimising/casual mention → doctor's inquiry meets plausible explanation and reassurance → as anxiety rises, "actual retreating" → **critical point when the doctor states a problem exists** → if treated, casualness and unreliable progress reports.
- **Multi-member sequence:** either the concern of the second member initiates the consultation, *or* work already initiated by one member "could become the focus of the acting out process of a second," who may then displace him or attend his appointment.
- **Timing observation:** the mother in the second vignette commented on ailments during the early weeks on the ward and arranged an appointment "several weeks after the family arrived" — the oblique phase preceded the formal one by weeks.
- **Time cost:** establishing a trustworthy history took persistence within a session; establishing that a found problem did not explain the complaints took "a number of weeks."
- **The critical point is the moment of maximum information**, not the moment of failure: "when such a point was reached the extent of the emotional use of the medical situation was unequivocally apparent." It is therefore something to be reached, not avoided.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Overdiagnosis and overtreatment** in response to acted-out helplessness — named in the summary as an outcome that actually occurred, not a hypothetical.
- **Inaccurate estimates of good health** in response to acted-out denial — the counterpart error.
- **Premature emergency framing**, from the infectious urgent tone.
- **Premature all-clear**, from the lulling casual tone.
- **Compromised judgment at the critical point**, to keep the relationship.
- **Loss of working contact**, the other horn of the same choice.
- **Opinion given without a professional frame** — a response, or a non-response, in a social exchange later carrying the weight of professional opinion.
- **Real medical harm through delay** — the father's serious, permanent loss of function attributed largely to delay.
- **Loss of visibility when the family goes outside the system** — the mother consulting city gynecologists "with no mention of it on the ward," and withholding her history from the outside doctor so that even he could not function properly. The system only learned of it because the outside doctor telephoned. This is the chapter's clearest case of the family *defeating* the professional structure: fragment the professionals, give each an incomplete picture, and no one holds the case.
  **[p2] Two corrections.** (i) **The episode did not end in medical harm** — "Later a laparotomy was performed and a rather large fibroid uterus successfully removed." What was lost was the *system's* visibility, not the patient's treatment. Pass 1 and the ledger both present it as unqualified defeat. (ii) **The telephone call was not unprompted altruism, and the chapter states the motive**: the outside doctor "said he had done the best he could for her under difficult circumstances and **asked to be informed of the findings if surgery was done in order to check his clinical impression**." It was *unauthorised by the family* — which is the load-bearing point — but it was self-interested in an ordinary professional sense. Ledger L19.3(a) says "telephoned unprompted"; correct to "unauthorised by the family, motivated by checking his own clinical impression". The countermeasure the model should take from this is cheaper than altruism: professionals who **audit their own work** generate cross-professional traffic as a by-product.
- **Diagnosis becoming the total explanation** — when a definite diagnosis was made "it tended to be seen as the source of all problems," which forecloses the emotional question the diagnosis was supposed to help clarify.
- **Prolonged medication** under pressure.
- **The doctor adjudicating between family members** about a third member's health.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

*(highest-value section — each rule of practice converted back into the claim about family functioning that makes it necessary)*

**[p2] Audit of this section.** Re-read against the chapter, three of the eighteen "implied mechanisms" are not implied by anything in the chapter — they are model hypotheses pass 1 wrote in the chapter's voice. They are marked **⚠ NOT SOURCED** at their entries below (#3, #10, #14) and must not be cited to Ch19. Two of the three are defensible from elsewhere in the corpus; one (#10) is defensible from nowhere and is the more serious error, because the ledger and the model-implications section both propagated it into a proposed feedback loop and a test.

1. **Because the clinic's responsibilities had to be "carefully defined to distinguish them from psychiatric functions" —** the family is assumed to *exploit undefined role boundaries*. Where a professional's remit is ambiguous, the emotional process expands into the ambiguity. A defined role is not administrative tidiness; it is the load-bearing defence. **Implied mechanism: emotional process flows into whatever functional space is left undefined.**
2. **Because responsibility is discharged "when the nature of the problem had been recognized" —** the family is assumed to seek not *care* but *concurrence*. What is being extracted is an opinion, not a treatment. Therefore the professional's exposure is greatest at the moment of opinion, and a doctor who defines his output as a recognition rather than a fix has less surface to be leveraged.
3. **Because many things "commonly handled symptomatically" were routed to psychotherapy —** the family is assumed to convert emotional pressure into somatic requests at a high rate. **⚠ NOT SOURCED [p2]:** pass 1 continued "and symptomatic medical treatment is assumed to *reward and reinforce* the conversion. Implied mechanism: symptomatic relief of an emotionally driven complaint increases the rate of emotionally driven complaints." The chapter states the **policy** and gives **no effect at all** — it never reports what happened when symptomatic treatment was or was not given. Retain the claim as a **model hypothesis sourced to C1** (relief opposes progress, 11 chapters) and to Ch08's L08.3 (staff became more helpful; the incident *rate stayed flat* and relocated), not to Ch19.
4. **Because the doctor "dealt with those emotional problems that operated to impair the medical functioning of the clinic" and no others —** the family is assumed to draw in any professional who engages the emotional material beyond his function. A narrow warrant is the only stable one; an unlimited one guarantees absorption.
5. **Because the emotional tone was described as "infectious" —** the family is assumed to transmit affect *directly* into professionals, independent of the information exchanged. The doctor's judgment is not merely informed by the encounter; it is *pushed* by it. **Implied mechanism: affect propagates from family member to professional and then re-emerges as the professional's own clinical conviction, felt as reasoning.**
6. **Because the doctor must not accept urgency or reassurance as data —** the family is assumed to encode its position in *style* rather than content, and to encode it *bidirectionally* (urgency to force action; calm to prevent it). Any system modelling this needs a channel for affect that is separate from, and can contradict, the propositional content of a communication.
7. **Because histories were distorted in both directions —** the family is assumed to *edit its own record* in the service of the current emotional position. Past facts are not stable inputs; they are outputs of the present state. Any professional relying on family-supplied history is receiving a variable, not a constant.
8. **Because the doctor must not give opinions socially, and because non-response was also captured —** the family is assumed to be able to *manufacture a professional act out of a non-professional one*, and to treat silence as assent. **Implied mechanism: a professional's relational position is assigned by the family, not chosen by the professional. Merely declining to speak does not exit the position.**
9. **Because a critical point regularly arrives when finding and feeling diverge, and the doctor's apparent choice is only "seemed" —** the family is assumed to present professionals with **false binaries** at exactly the moment their position becomes valuable. The dilemma is the emotional system's product, not a feature of the situation. A third option — hold the position, keep the contact, tolerate the anxiety — exists and is what produces "a more adequate recognition of the actual problems."
10. **⚠ NOT SOURCED [p2] — the worst single error in pass 1's reading of this chapter.** Pass 1 wrote: "Because holding judgment at the critical point can resolve toward better recognition — the family is assumed to be responsive to a professional who does not move… **Implied mechanism: the intensity of the pressure is a function of the likelihood of it succeeding; an unmovable professional lowers the pressure rather than raising it.**" Every clause of this is invented. The chapter (a) never says the doctor holds judgment (see A3 #9 [p2]); (b) never says the family is responsive to an unmoving professional; (c) never says anything whatever about how pressure evolves over time, in either direction, under any professional behaviour. The one observed response to a doctor stating a differing impression is that "the mother introduced another problem" — the pressure **redirected**, it did not fall. Pass 1's formulation then propagated into the model-implications section as a proposed feedback loop and a test ("an unmovable external agent should *reduce* subsequent pressure over time"). **Both are withdrawn as Ch19-sourced.** If the model wants a pressure-decay term it must be argued from Ch09's L09.3 (an "I" position held for even a few days produces a *permanent* decrease in that triangle's intensity), which is a real source and says something different — a permanent decrement per completed episode, not a decline proportional to expected success.
11. **Because stating an initial impression makes the mode-1 patient "more thoughtful" —** the family is assumed to be *destabilised by the professional taking a definite position early*. The process depends on the professional remaining indefinite long enough to be recruited.
12. **Because the doctor should not become the arbiter between two members about a third —** the family is assumed to try to *convert the professional into a decision-maker inside its own internal disagreement*. Once the doctor supplies a verdict, he has taken a side inside the family, and the family's own conflict is temporarily settled by an outsider rather than by its members. **Implied mechanism: an external authority's opinion can be imported as a substitute for internal resolution — which relieves the family and freezes the conflict.**
13. **Because the outside-consultation vignette exists at all —** the family is assumed capable of **splitting the professional field on purpose**: seeking a second opinion outside the system, withholding history from the outside professional so his opinion is uncheckable, and reporting nothing back. **Implied mechanism: a family under pressure from one professional will recruit another, and will manage the information each holds so that no professional has a complete picture.** The corollary assumption is that professionals do not routinely talk to each other — the vignette resolves only because one of them chose to telephone.
14. **Because the emotional use was minimal during actual emergency —** the family is assumed to have a *real-threat mode* that displaces the enacted one. **⚠ NOT SOURCED [p2] (partially):** the observation is verbatim ("was minimal on the few occasions of actual medical emergency"), but the *mechanism* pass 1 attached — "enacted helplessness and real crisis compete for the same capacity" — is an interpretation, and the chapter offers no account at all. Note the alternative reading pass 1 did not consider: in a real emergency the medical facts are unambiguous, so there is nothing left for the emotional process to be ambiguous *about*. That reading fits the chapter's own framing (the process operates on "poorly defined" problems, on "vagueness", on "trivial medical matters") at least as well as a competing-capacity model, and predicts something different — suppression by *evidential clarity*, not by *load*. Flag as an open modelling choice; the chapter does not decide it.
15. **Because normal siblings were exempt —** the involvement in the professional relationship is assumed to be a property of *position within the family's emotional process*, not of the family's culture, class, or beliefs about doctors. The same family behaves one way through its involved members and another through its uninvolved ones.
16. **Because the same medical behaviour appears in the families' past histories —** the pattern is assumed to be a *stable trait of the family across time and across professionals*, not a reaction to this ward or these doctors. Any professional entering this family will meet the same process.
17. **Because the whole chapter reads the medical encounter as a derivative of the family's emotional life — "in much the same way that difficulties in psychotherapy are studied as derivatives of emotional processes" —** the professional system is assumed to be a **legitimate observation instrument for family process**. The trouble the professionals have *is* data. **Implied mechanism: the professional system, once engaged, becomes an extension of the family's emotional system and therefore displays that system's structure in its own dysfunction.** This is the chapter's central and most transferable assumption.
18. **Because the parents' helplessness externalised into the son or daughter is the most intense configuration —** the family is assumed to reach maximum pressure on a professional when a member is asking about *someone else*. **[p2] Scope correction:** the chapter's ranking is explicitly *within* multi-member situations ("the most prominent and intense of all the situations **involving more than one member**"), so "maximum pressure" here means maximum among multi-member configurations, not maximum overall. The comparison with single-member presentations is not made.
19. **[p2] Because a physical finding is "taken as at least a token concurrence" —** the family is assumed to score the professional's output on a **continuum of agreement**, not to await a verdict. A professional therefore concedes *by degrees*, through outputs he does not experience as concessions at all (a finding, a test ordered, a prescription written). **Implied mechanism: pressure is discharged in proportion to how much of the emotional claim the professional's most recent output can be read as endorsing.** This is the chapter's own sentence and it is the one place it grades the interaction; it is a better foundation for a pressure model than anything pass 1 built.
20. **[p2] Because an estimate "might be accurate or not and yet be largely in the service of a denial" —** the family is assumed to produce claims whose **truth value is uncorrelated with their emotional function**. A professional cannot infer the mode from whether the patient turns out to be right. This forbids the obvious modelling shortcut of treating an emotionally driven claim as a *false* claim: correctness and function are orthogonal axes on the same message.
21. **[p2] Because mode 1 "could be gross or so subtle as to masquerade for sensible action" —** the move is assumed to be **continuously graded with a detectability floor**, and the family is assumed to have instances that no observer can distinguish from reasonable behaviour. Any detector in the model must have a false-negative region by construction, and the population of moves is not fully observable even to a perfectly attentive agent.
22. **[p2] Because the father's rescue came from a physician friend "apparently sensing that the matter might be of some moment" —** professionals are assumed to be capable of **unilaterally promoting a social contact into a professional act**, on a hunch, against the presenting member's framing. Together with the gynecologist's telephone call, this is the shape of *both* recoveries in the chapter: each came from a professional acting outside the frame the family had set, without the family's authorisation. **Implied mechanism: the professional field's only observed defence against a family that manages it is professional-initiated action the family did not sanction.**

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **No explicit contradiction or revision is stated.** The chapter references the parent project (Bowen 1957, 1959, 1960) as background and does not correct it.
- **Implicit tension with the chapter's own framing:** the clinic is described as "structured to operate as much as possible like an outpatient service," yet it is run by a psychiatrist on the project staff, inside the ward where the families live, with continuous cross-context observation. It is emphatically *not* an outpatient service, and the chapter's own best findings depend on it not being one. The claim of ordinariness sits awkwardly with the claim that the context is what made the pattern visible.
- **Terminological divergence from Bowen's better-known vocabulary:** "feelings of helplessness" occupies the role that anxiety occupies elsewhere; "concern about another" describes what would elsewhere be a projection/triangling move; the doctor's induction into responsibility is a triangle described without the word. Whether this is early vocabulary or a deliberate accommodation to a medical audience is not stated in the chapter.
- **A quiet asymmetry that is stated but not explained:** mode 2 "was not seen in the son or daughter." The chapter offers no account of why the symptomatic member has no access to the denial mode, and does not flag the omission.
- **[p2] Consistent with the corpus rather than in tension with it — with one exception.** Nothing in Ch19 contradicts a later chapter. The doctor's induction into responsibility "by virtue of a diagnosis" is Ch08's L08.6 (the professional system runs the family's projection process; responsibility is a conserved transferable quantity) seen from inside the professional's chair, and Ch10's L10.9 (institutional acts write to a family-level who-is-sick belief with hysteresis) with the writing mechanism supplied. The exception is directional: Ch19 makes the *doctor's* judgment the thing that drifts, whereas Ch10/Ch17/Ch20 make the *coach's own reactive state* the readout. These are the same finding about different outputs of the same agent, and the model needs both — a drifting judgment **and** a drifting reactivity.
- **[p2] The chapter is silent on every one of Bowen's named concepts, and by 1957–60 that is expected rather than notable.** Pass 1 recorded the absence of differentiation, triangle, projection, fusion and ego mass and left it ambiguous whether this was early vocabulary or accommodation to a medical audience. The terminology timeline settles the first half: Ch01 (1957, same project) has no triangle, no differentiation and no multigenerational transmission either, and Ch03 (~1960) has only the fixed-membership "interdependent triad". So the vocabulary here is **date-appropriate**, not translated for the audience. What remains genuinely odd is "feelings of helplessness" as the driving quantity, since Ch01 already uses "transfer of anxiety" — this chapter picks a different word for the same job in the same years, and the reason is not stated. Dysinger is first author; that is the most likely explanation and the chapter does not confirm it.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The professional's own difficulty is treated as a measuring instrument.** The stated method — study difficulties in the medical situation "in much the same way that difficulties in psychotherapy are studied as derivatives of emotional processes" — makes the friction experienced by any engaged professional into a readout of the family's state. For a simulation, this means an external agent's *own* dysfunction is a legitimate observable.
- **Silence is an act.** "His response **or lack of it** had been given the weight of a professional opinion." A professional cannot abstain out of a position the family has assigned him. Non-response has content.
- **The four-state response taxonomy** for a member whose health another member is concerned about — accept as own / go along without accepting / defensively oppose / maintain own view — is unusually clean and directly implementable as an enumerated state.
- **The critical point is a scheduled event, not an accident.** It arrives at predictable junctures: at a negative finding, at the completion of treatment, at the assertion that a problem exists in the denial mode. A simulation can place these deterministically. **[p2] "At a definite diagnosis" struck** — the chapter puts the opposite there; see B3 [p2].
- **Real emergency quenches the process.** The observation that enacted helplessness was minimal during actual emergencies suggests a competitive relationship between real and enacted threat that is unusual and testable.
- ~~**The externalised form ranks highest in intensity**, above any single-member form. Intensity is a function of *how many people the claim spans*, not of the severity of the medical fact.~~ **[p2] REVISED — pass 1 over-extended a scoped ranking into a general law.** What the chapter states is one ranking inside one class: parents-externalising-into-the-son-or-daughter is "the most prominent and intense of all the situations **involving more than one member**." No comparison to single-member presentations is made, and the phrase "intensity is a function of how many people the claim spans" is pass 1's, not the chapter's. Severity-independence *is* supported but is a **separate claim about a different quantity**: the difficulty of medical work "was the case whether or not significant medical problems were present" — that is about the *difficulty*, not about the *intensity ranking*. Two findings, and pass 1 fused them into one.
- **[p2] Concurrence is a scalar and it is the chapter's own idea.** "Taken as at least a token concurrence" is the single most useful sentence in the chapter for the model and pass 1 did not record it. It means the professional's exposure is continuous: he can be partially recruited by ordinary competent acts, and the family reads every output as a partial score against its claim. Nothing else in the corpus grades the professional's capitulation — Ch08's L08.6, Ch10's L10.9 and Ch20's L20.2 all treat responsibility-absorption as taken or not taken.
- **[p2] Truth and function are orthogonal on the same message.** "The estimate might be accurate or not and yet be largely in the service of a denial." This is the sharpest version in the corpus of the content/process split — sharper than Ch20's L20.3 ("content is noise"), because Ch20 says content carries no signal about anxiety while Ch19 says a *correct* statement can be a full-strength emotional act. A model that flags emotionally driven claims by their falsity is wrong by this sentence.
- **[p2] The move has a detectability floor.** "Gross or so subtle as to masquerade for sensible action." Not amplitude-plus-noise: the low-amplitude instances are *definitionally* indistinguishable from sensible action. Any observation readout over this population is censored from below.
- **[p2] Both recoveries in the chapter come from a professional acting outside the family's frame without authorisation.** The gynecologist telephoning the ward to check his own clinical impression; the physician friend who "apparently sensing that the matter might be of some moment" examined a man who had mentioned it casually. Pass 1 recorded the first and missed that the second has the same shape. Neither is a rule, a protocol, or a structure — both are one professional's unrequested initiative. The chapter's whole apparatus of defined roles and bounded warrants prevented nothing in either episode; what worked was outside it.
- **Displacement from the doctor-contact position** — one member moving to take over another's medical relationship — is a discrete, observable relational move with a mild form (attending the appointment) and a strong form (taking over).
- **Splitting the professional field is described concretely enough to implement**: seek an outside professional, withhold history from him, request an opinion on examination only, report nothing back. The countermeasure that actually worked was not a rule but a *professional-to-professional communication* that nobody in the family authorised.
- **The chapter never mentions the schizophrenic member's psychosis as a factor in the medical encounters.** The son or daughter behaves in the medical situation the way the mother does. Whatever produces the pattern is not the illness.
- **The doctor is asked for a diagnosis as a *possession*, not a service** — something the person will "then have an answer to my problem" with. The medical opinion is being sought as an object to carry back into the family.
- **Two failure directions are symmetrical.** The clinic can err toward illness or toward health, and which way it errs is set by which family member is presenting. This means an external agent's error is *diagnostic* of the presenting member's mode.
