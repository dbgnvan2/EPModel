## ⭐⭐ KS04.1 — **`S2.15` / D9 resolved.** Distance **binds** anxiety; it neither destroys it nor is costless `[T]` `[M]`

Bowen's original four, and the status of the fourth:

> "'three major mechanisms to control the intensity of the ego fusion': (a) marital conflict, (b) dysfunction in one spouse, and (c) transmission of the problem to one or more children. **He described a fourth mechanism, emotional distance, as inevitably accompanying each of the other patterns.**"

The mechanism, from Figure 4.1:

> "Both people experience **less internal anxiety** in this process, but **the anxiety is now evident or bound in distancing behaviors**… **If people are unable to distance from each other for whatever reason, internalized anxiety reappears.**"

> "Another way of describing the binding of anxiety is that **the anxiety is integrated into the structure of a relationship.**"

*Model consequence:* **this settles the question the user raised on D9 and the older 3-vs-4-sinks question at the same time, and it settles them differently from `M1.D.2a` as written.**

- `M1.D.2a` says distance **absorbs without symptomising**. Half right. Distance **binds** — the anxiety leaves the person and is **stored in the structure of the tie**. It is neither discharged nor destroyed.
- The user's note — *"the Distance IS the symptom, and… that intensity can lead to other symptoms"* — is **exactly right and now sourced.** Bound anxiety is visible *as* the distance, and it is a **stored quantity that reappears** the moment distancing is blocked.
- **Distance therefore accompanies all three other patterns rather than competing with them.** Not a fourth sink beside three; a substrate under all of them.

**Requirement:** `M1.D.2a` **MUST** be rewritten. Distance moves anxiety from `Person.acute_anxiety` into `Relationship` state, where it (a) persists, (b) is readable as an observable, and (c) **returns to the person if the distance is prevented.** A model in which `DISTANCE` reduces total system anxiety is a failing implementation.

## ⭐⭐ KS04.2 — Kerr **changed his mind** about who becomes symptomatic, and the corrected rule is computable `[T]` `[K-ext]`

The original view, and its refutation:

> "In my early years… it appeared that it was the **underfunctioning spouse** who was most vulnerable to symptom development… However, clinical experience indicates that the overfunctioning-underfunctioning reciprocity **typically predates the development of symptoms.**"

> "In 1976, I began a study of families in which one member had developed cancer. **The research changed my thinking**… In many cancer cases **it was the overfunctioning person** in the family who developed the cancer… The overfunctioners **looked dominant, but these were cases of the overfunctioners being pinned down in the one-up position.**"

The corrected rule:

> "**the person most vulnerable to dysfunction… is the one making the most internal adjustments in functioning to preserve harmony. This can happen on either side of the reciprocity.** Assessing which spouse is most compromised… requires careful examination — **appearances can be deceiving.**"

*Model consequence:* **a correction to `M11.C.2` and to how the model picks its symptom-bearer.** The predictor is not *position* (under- versus over-functioning, insider versus outsider) but **the magnitude of internal adjustment made to preserve harmony** — which is a quantity the model can compute directly from the event log, as cumulative deviation from the agent's own position under relational pressure.

This also breaks a tempting shortcut: an implementation that assigns symptoms to the underfunctioner, or to the projection target, by *role* is exactly the "appearances can be deceiving" error. **Candidate acceptance criterion:** an overfunctioning-arm and an underfunctioning-arm with equal internal adjustment **MUST** show equal symptom risk.

Graded `[K-ext]` — Kerr states it as his own revision from his own research, not as Bowen's.

## ⭐ KS04.3 — A damage ordering over the patterns: child focus is worse than either parental pattern `[T]` `[D]`

> "If the dominant-adaptive (deferential) or emotional conflict patterns **adequately bind anxiety in the marital relationship, it protects the children** to some degree from family anxiety. Clinical observations show that **a major chronic dysfunction in one parent or extreme conflict between the parents does not preclude their raising reasonably functional kids.** This is not to say that a major dysfunction in a parent has no effect on the kids but that **it creates less impairment than child focus does.**"

*Model consequence:* **an ordering the spec does not have.** For impact on the next generation's `basic_level`: `TRIANGLE`/child-focus **>** marital conflict ≈ spouse dysfunction. This gives `M7.E` a directional constraint and gives `M11.C.6` a sharper form — the projection line declines *more* than a line whose parents fought or where one parent was chronically ill.

Bowen's stated bias, quoted here, points the same way: "**if there is any bias in his theory, it was to keep the family problem off the back of the child.**"

## ⭐ KS04.4 — Substitution stated as an inverse law `[T]`

> "marital conflict, dysfunction in one spouse, and transmission of the problem to one or more children are **all expressions of the same thing**: undifferentiation and associated chronic anxiety in the nuclear family system. **The more it manifests in one area, the less likely it is to manifest in other areas.**"

*Model consequence:* `M7.D.2`'s substitutability confirmed and strengthened to an **inverse relationship**, not merely "can present as any of the three". `M11.C.12` (curing a symptom raises conflict) is the direct corollary and is well grounded.

## ⭐ KS04.5 — Adolescent rebellion is **reactive distancing, not differentiation** — and its quiet twin is no better `[T]`

> "**Adolescent rebellion is reactive distancing, not differentiation.** It stems from both parent and adolescent having trouble maintaining 'self' in their relationship. **It does not indicate that the teenager is developing 'self.'**"

And the other pole:

> "**Absence of adolescent rebellion does not always indicate that the relationships in the parental triangle are more mature.** Some adolescents will manage the lack of differentiation… by **trying to please, always do the 'right thing,' and excel in school.** These kids commonly **view their parents as normal and think that they themselves are the problem** if anything goes wrong… They do little if any fighting with their parents but **internalize** any relationship-generated anxiety."

*Model consequence:* **`M5.F`'s counterfeit family gains its two clearest cases, and they are the two axes of `M1.A.9a` again** — the rebel fails outward, the pleaser fails inward, and **neither is differentiating.** Note the pleaser's readout: *self-blame* and *idealisation of the parents*, which is `KS00.2`/`KS01.3`'s self-blame axis appearing as a diagnostic.

Also note the paradox recorded: in an intense fusion, "**the teenager often reports not feeling close to the parents. This results not from a lack of parental involvement but from the reactivity that keeps them at a distance.**" — a readout warning: *reported closeness is not fusion*, and may invert.

## ⭐ KS04.6 — "Not taking the bait" escalates, with a stated end state `[T]`

> "A common but **conspicuously unsuccessful strategy**… is one person **trying not to take the bait**… **It only makes the one who wants some type of response angrier.** The spouse feeling ignored… **typically escalates attacks on the other spouse until that spouse finally loses it and explodes.** Some report **more sense of connection when they succeed at provoking the other to explode.** '**Finally, I know what you feel!**'"

*Model consequence:* second statement of this in two chapters (`KS00.5`), and here with the **full escalation trajectory and its terminus**. This is `M5.F.4`'s assertion-form failure on the inward axis, run to completion: withholding response **raises** the other's move intensity monotonically until a discharge occurs, and **the discharge is experienced as connection** — which is why the pattern is self-reinforcing. `M4.D.6`'s reinforcement on the automatic channel will learn it.

## ⭐ KS04.7 — The family projection process, as a complete implementable cycle `[T]` `[M]`

> "It starts with the mother's **worry about some aspect of the child**, such as whether the child is happy. She **focuses on the child, looking for and fearing that she will find** indications that the child is, in fact, unhappy. If she spots something, **or imagines she has**, she begins to **relate to the child as if he is unhappy.** She may talk to her husband about it. **He may tell her it is all in her mind but still tends to support her agenda** on how to help the child. **The child goes along with the mother's projection… because this rewards the child with a calmer mother.** The mother is calmer because she **sees the anxiety as coming from outside herself**… Furthermore, the child **increasingly looks to the mother to solve any problems**, which **pressures her to maintain an overfunctioning posture.**"

*Model consequence:* **an eight-step closed loop with a named reinforcement on each side**, which is more than `M7.E` currently specifies. Two features matter for implementation:

1. **"or imagines she has"** — the projection does not require a real signal from the child. `M4.C.5`'s perception-side readout is the trigger, not an emitted event.
2. **The child's compliance is rewarded** (a calmer mother) and **the mother's overfunctioning is rewarded** (externalised anxiety). Both sides reinforce on the automatic channel, so the loop is stable without either party intending it. This is a **worked example of `M4.D.6` producing a pathological equilibrium**, and a strong candidate fixture.

Also the structural precondition, stated plainly: "**The parents may agree that their marriage is just fine. On the surface it is, but it is one side of a triangle. The closeness between the parents is supported by the focus on the child.**" — an apparently good marriage is *evidence for* child focus, not against it. A readout warning for `M11.C.2`.

## ⭐ KS04.8 — Differentiation named as **free will**, and one member is sufficient — if important enough `[T]`

> "This capacity to self-regulate is embodied in the concept of differentiation of self. **Differentiation of self is one way to talk about the concept of free will.**"

> "**One person with sufficient importance to the system is enough to halt the spread of anxiety.**"

*Model consequence:* the first sentence is the strongest available statement of §G7 — agency **is** differentiation, not a separate faculty. It supports `M10.A.1a` (agency derived, not parameterised).

The second gives `M11.C.7`/`KB12` · K12.1 a **gate the spec does not have**: sufficiency is conditioned on the mover's **`structural_importance`**. A peripheral member's held position does **not** halt the spread. That is a testable and currently-missing coupling between `M1.A.13` and `M5.D`.

## KS04.9 — Anxiety is necessary, never sufficient, for symptom `[T]`

> "**Other factors, such as genes, toxins, and pathogens, have to be at play as well for symptoms to emerge.**"

> "**Systems thinking does not assign cause to any one variable**; it is the interaction of all the variables that is important to understand."

*Model consequence:* `M7.D.1`'s threshold **MUST NOT** be reachable by relational load alone. A per-agent susceptibility term is required, and the model **MUST NOT** report a symptom as caused by family process. Framing requirement for `M11.F`, alongside `KS01.5`'s no-culpability and `KS03.3`'s no-victim rules — **three now, and they belong together as one output constraint.**

## KS04.10 — The "open relationship" and its four natural habitats `[T]`

> "In an open relationship **both parties are able to communicate their innermost thoughts and feelings without fear of hurting the other person.** Such a relationship **enhances the functioning of both people** and is health promoting."

> four contexts in which it most often exists: "(1) **the early mother-infant relationship**; (2) **courtship**; (3) **a psychoanalytic relationship**; and (4) **a fantasied relationship** with someone."

> "**There are two ways to deal with anxiety: one is with drugs, the other is with 'self'; 'self' is better.**" — Bowen

*Model consequence:* `M1.B.10`'s `taboo_set` gets its opposite pole defined: an open relationship is one with an **empty** taboo set, and it is **health promoting for both**, not merely neutral. Note that three of the four habitats are transient or artificial, which is the pessimistic half — `M1.B.10`'s default growth is the norm and openness is the exception requiring maintenance.

## KS04.11 — Distance is **not pathological** — Bowen's own rebuke `[T]`

> An attendee implied something was wrong with emotional distance. "Bowen responded by straightening up, holding the microphone close to his mouth, and firmly asking, '**Do you think emotional distance is pathological?**'"

> "**Both the inability to have a reasonably comfortable emotional connection with another person and the inability to distance emotionally** if the relationship becomes overwhelming **could impair functioning to the point of symptom development.** **The most differentiated person on the planet will from time to time emotionally distance** to some degree."

*Model consequence:* the two-sided pattern again, now on distance itself, and it **confirms `M5.B.3a`'s floor on `REDUCE_CUTOFF`** from a fifth source. `DISTANCE` **MUST NOT** carry a monotone penalty; an implementation in which less distance is always better is failing. Sits alongside `KS04.1` without contradiction: distance binds anxiety at a cost, *and* the capacity to distance is necessary.

## KS04.12 — Overfunctioning/underfunctioning is **not** role differentiation `[T]`

> "An overfunctioning-underfunctioning reciprocity **does not reflect role differentiation**… Role differentiation results from people taking on specialized tasks that render a family or social system **more functional and adaptive. It is not anxiety driven but reality driven.**"

> "**The overfunctioner is not necessarily more capable than the underfunctioner. Real differences between people do not account for the functioning positions they occupy.**"

> "The **overfunctioner may appear to be the dominant partner, but that is not always the case.** The overfunctioner may be **adapting disproportionately in the relationship under pressure from the underfunctioner.**"

*Model consequence:* confirms `KS00.10` (driver, not type) and adds two constraints. Functioning position **MUST NOT** be derived from any capability attribute — it is purely relational. And **dominance and overfunctioning are independent axes**; an implementation that conflates them cannot represent the pinned-down overfunctioner of `KS04.2`, who is the one that matters.

## KS04.13 — Emotional conflict externalises; the improvement it produces is not progress `[T]`

> "People **blame each other for the problem, which is calming for both**… But the anxiety is now **bound in both people dogmatically adhering to their own viewpoints.** The process is fueled, in part, by **each spouse's hypersensitivity to being controlled by the other.**"

> "**one spouse's depression may improve if the couple starts fighting more. This does not necessarily represent progress because the anxiety and reactivity are the same, just expressed differently.**"

> "Emotional conflict is a fascinating pattern in that **people maintain contact and enforce distance at the same time. Poking at the other gets a reaction and pushes the other away at the same time.**"

*Model consequence:* **a readout trap the model must be able to reproduce.** Symptom remission accompanied by rising conflict is a *lateral move*, not improvement — the exact inverse of `M11.C.12`, and it should be asserted in both directions. And the last quote makes `CONFLICT` a move that changes **both** impingement axes at once, which a two-axis `outside_ness` (`M1.A.9a`) can express and a scalar cannot.

> "Conflict arises from **neither person having enough 'self' not to feel that going along with the other's wants and ideas is 'giving in.'**"

The conflict cycle is also given a shape — "**punctuated disequilibrium**": long arguing, then distance for "days or weeks" with obsessive focus on the other's faults, then a brief closeness, then resumption. And the durability note: "**conflictual marriages are often enduring.**"

## KS04.14 — Each pattern solves the same dilemma differently `[T]`

> "**Each of the three patterns solves the dilemma posed by the intense need for closeness and equally intense aversion to too much of it. Each pattern solves it in a different way.** Up to a point, the patterns **stabilize** relationships but lead to dysfunction occurring somewhere in the system if sufficiently high levels of chronic anxiety develop."

*Model consequence:* the patterns are **alternative solutions to one optimisation**, which is why they substitute (`KS04.4`). Gives `M4.D.2`'s propensity vector its organising principle: all reactive moves are attempts on the same closeness-distance optimum from `KS03.2`, differing in method.

## KS04.15 — The symbiosis is the substrate, not the consequence `[T]`

> "Some might argue that the unresolved attachment between the mother and the adult dysfunctional child is so great **because** the young adult child is schizophrenic… **Bowen saw it differently: the unresolved attachment is the substrate on which schizophrenia and other types of major dysfunctions develop.**"

> "When a successful emotional separation… has developed by the time the child leaves home, this renders the young adult **largely invulnerable to developing a major clinical symptom during the transition into adult life, and throughout life.**"

> "**Less fusion does not mean lack of closeness**; it means that enough maturity exists in both people to permit a **stable and open connection throughout life.**"

*Model consequence:* the causal direction is fixed — fusion precedes symptom, never the reverse. And the last sentence is a **hard constraint on any closeness readout**: low fusion and high closeness are compatible, so the model **MUST NOT** derive fusion from contact frequency or from expressed warmth. Same family as `M1.B.8`'s valence-blind investment.

## KS04.17 — Anxiety transfer between people, and its physical channel `[T]`

> "**one person's anxiety can express itself in another person's symptoms. The anxiety transfer is not mystical.** Distancing gets communicated in subtle and not so subtle ways. Anxiety-driven distancing can manifest, for example, in **talking less to a partner, having less eye contact, and being more distracted.**"

*Model consequence:* named low-level channels for the `DISTANCE` move's delivery, and an explicit disclaimer of the telepathy-like reading flagged at `KB02` · K02.9. Supports `M1.F`'s event fields carrying `route` and `fidelity`: distance is transmitted by **reduction in ordinary contact signals**, which is representable as attenuated ordinary events rather than as a special channel.

## KS04.18 — Patterns shift over time and coexist `[T]`

> "**The most active pattern… can change over time.** A dominant-adaptive (deferential) pattern may characterize the early years of a marriage, but this can **shift into one of emotional conflict or emotional distance later on.** Additionally, **more than one pattern can be active in a family at the same time.**"

*Model consequence:* forbids implementing "family style" (`M4.D.6`) as a single latched mode. The learned repertoire must be a **distribution that drifts**, with more than one pattern carrying weight at once.
