# KS12 — Sibling Position

**Citation rule:** cite `KS12`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

## ⭐⭐ KS12.1 — **`M11.C.9` is repairable.** Toman's profiles are valid only at mid-scale `[T]`

The project's most unfalsifiable acceptance criterion is `M11.C.9` — "sibling position shapes functioning at constant `basic_level`" — flagged in `F2` because the effect size is invented. This chapter supplies the missing scoping.

> "**Bowen qualified his thoughts about Toman's profiles by saying that they accurately describe people at the midrange on the continuum of differentiation of self.**"

> "**Sibling profiles are always modified, sometimes to an extreme, because all other things are never equal; differences in basic level of differentiation are the most important modifying influence.** A moderately differentiated oldest brother of brother(s) can be a leader but is **vulnerable to being self-righteous when anxious. A poorly differentiated oldest brother may exhibit very few characteristics of an oldest profile.**"

> "**The aspects of the profile that remain in a poorly differentiated oldest may not be evident except under certain circumstances.**"

*Model consequence:* **the sibling-position effect is not constant across the scale — it is largest at mid-range and attenuates at both ends.** `M1.A.14` currently makes `sibling_position` static profile data affecting the propensity vector; `M11.C.9` then tests it "at constant `basic_level`", which is why the test is close to unfalsifiable — it holds fixed the very thing that modulates the effect.

**Rewrite `M11.C.9` as a three-arm test:** low, mid, and high `basic_level`, same sibling position, same stressors. The **mid-range arm MUST show the largest position-typical behaviour**, and the low arm the least. That is a direction of difference between arms — the project's own required form — and it needs no invented effect size. **This resolves an `F2` item.**

The mechanism for the attenuation at the low end is given in `KS12.2`; at the high end it is `KS12.5`.

## ⭐⭐ KS12.2 — Position is **functional**, not ordinal — and it can be reassigned `[T]` `[M]`

> "**the family projection process can so impair the functioning of a firstborn son that his younger brother may function more like an older brother than the firstborn does. In such instances, the younger son becomes a 'functional oldest.'**"

> "Alternatively, **the younger son may be so babied and protected that he becomes highly vulnerable to helplessness and indecisiveness, which are not characteristic of all youngest children.**"

Kerr's own case: "**That dynamic also fostered some functional oldest traits in me, despite my position as last-born.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.14` is wrong as stated.** It requires `sibling_position` to be "static profile data affecting the propensity vector and nothing else." This says the *functional* position can diverge from the birth-order position, and that the divergence is **produced by the projection process** — which is exactly the same functional-versus-structural distinction as `M1.A.13a`'s `structural_importance` (derived from the functional-doer position, not from role labels).

**Requirement:** `sibling_position` stays static as **birth-order data**, and a derived `functional_sibling_position` is computed from the family's actual functioning. The propensity vector reads the **derived** one. This is the same correction Tape 6 forced on `structural_importance`, now for a second attribute — worth stating once, generically: *no role label may be read directly; every positional attribute is derived from function.*

## ⭐ KS12.3 — Complementarity: rank **and** sex, and it predicts ease not maturity `[T]`

> "if an **older brother of a sister** marries the **younger sister of a brother**, they have **rank and sex complementarity**… If an **older brother of a brother** marries an **older sister of a sister**, they have **neither**."

> "**If spouses have rank and sex complementarity, it is easier for them to get along than if they lack the complementarity. This does not mean that one marriage is more mature than the other.**"

*Model consequence:* a **second, orthogonal pairing dimension** beside `M2.A.0c`'s basic-level matching, and it affects **conflict propensity, not differentiation**. That separation matters — the model would be wrong to let complementarity raise `basic_level` or to let a complementary pair read as healthier.

This also gives `KS07.9`'s courtship "comfortable fit" its formal statement, and it makes the two-dimensional pairing rule explicit: **match on level (`M2.A.0c`), then complementarity modulates the tie's baseline conflict**. Both are computable at construction.

## ⭐ KS12.4 — Position persists under extreme regression — the ward-fire observation `[T]`

> "when I was a Navy psychiatrist and taking care of a **ward filled with young men diagnosed with acute schizophrenic reactions, a small fire broke out**… as I was moving rapidly down the corridor… I alerted patients as to what was happening and asked for help. By the time I got to the fire, a corpsman was already extinguishing it. **I also saw that five or six patients had responded and were standing around me ready to help. I realized later that all of them were the oldest children in their families!**"

> "**the most fundamental characteristics of a person's sibling position always seem to be there.**"

*Model consequence:* the counterweight to `KS12.1` — at the **lowest** functional levels the profile is *latent but not absent*, and it surfaces under a **specific circumstance** (an unambiguous, external, non-relational demand). That is a testable refinement: position effects are suppressed by anxiety in the *relational* domain and re-emerge on a **task** demand.

An anecdote of `n≈6` with no control, so grade `[D]` at best — but it is a precise and unusual observation, and it constrains the shape of the attenuation in `KS12.1` (suppression, not erasure).

## ⭐ KS12.5 — Each position has assets **and** liabilities; maturity selects which `[T]`

> "**Toman and Bowen theory both hold that there is not a best or worst sibling position. Each position has its assets and liabilities.**"

> "**An immature older brother of brothers is likely to be overly controlling and dogmatic**, provided his spouse functions in a reciprocal opposite way, such as being passive and indecisive. **A mature older brother of brothers can be a very effective and responsible leader and respect the emotional boundaries and integrity of others.**"

*Model consequence:* the two-sided pattern applied to `M1.A.14`: **the same position yields opposite behaviour depending on level.** So `sibling_position` **MUST NOT** be a fixed additive offset on the propensity vector — it selects *which* behaviours are available, and `basic_level` selects *which end of each* is expressed. That is a multiplicative or gating relationship, not additive, and it is why the mid-range shows the effect most clearly.

Note the reciprocity clause — the immature elder's controlling behaviour is conditional on the spouse functioning oppositely. Position expression is **relational**, not intrinsic, which is `KS12.2` again.

## ⭐ KS12.6 — The eleven profiles, with best/worst matches — recorded for construction `[T]`

Toman (1961), from "**the study of over three thousand families**". Kerr gives condensed profiles; the model-relevant content is the **complementarity graph** and a few functional attributes.

| Position | Key functional attributes | Best marital match | Worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oldest brother of brother(s) | takes charge, leads, good worker, builds property, long-range plans | youngest sister of brother(s) | **oldest sister of sister(s)** — "battles over being in charge" |
| Youngest brother of brother(s) | not a natural leader; **reactive, oppositional, rebellious**; poor with money/property | oldest sister of brothers | youngest sister of sisters |
| Oldest brother of sister(s) | communicates best with females; less driven to accumulate; more casual in authority | youngest sister of brother(s) | — |
| Youngest brother of sister(s) | doted on; **less reactive and competitive** than youngest-of-brothers; squanders property; grand plans over specifics | oldest sister of brother(s) | — |
| Oldest sister of sister(s) | caretaker, in charge, detail-oriented; "**may appear more strong and independent than she really is**" | youngest brother of sister(s) | — |
| Youngest sister of sister(s) | adventurous, capricious; hard to pin down; "**difficulty deciding on a direction**"; prone to intensify the gossip system | oldest brother of sister(s) | — |
| Oldest sister of brother(s) | takes care of men yet independent; "**thrives on their acceptance… It is almost all she needs**"; strongly child-oriented | youngest brother of sister(s) | — |
| Youngest sister of brother(s) | submissive without subservient; self-starter; "**lacks a strong desire to excel**" | oldest brother of sister(s) | — |
| Intermediary | mixture, weighted by **age gaps**; parents' positions bias it | — | — |
| Only child | expects acclaim; not ready for peer relationships; **learns position from the same-sex parent** | — | — |
| Twins | oldest and youngest profiles **blend**; identical twins harder to separate; "**glued more tightly… more likely to remain attached for life**" | — | — |

Two structural rules worth extracting:

> "**if siblings are six or more years apart, they tend to grow up like only children.**" `[#]`

> "**the sibling positions of the parents of an only child have more influence on the only child's eventual makeup than they do on children with siblings.**"

*Model consequence:* the complementarity graph is directly encodable as `M2.A` construction data, and the **six-year rule** is a concrete, quantitative, checkable constraint — one of very few in the corpus. Note it is `[#]` from Toman, not Bowen, and no method is given for the threshold.

The twin entry is also the mechanism behind `KS11.4`'s entrepreneur: twins "glued more tightly… attached for life" is why the surviving twin's release on the other's death was so marked.

## ⭐ KS12.7 — Position can be **moved** by differentiating effort `[T]`

At a 1971 conference, Toman responded to Kerr's paper by saying "**a youngest child is more likely to do a public presentation about his own family.**"

> "My response to him was, '**I am not as much of a youngest child as I used to be.**' What I was trying to convey is that my beginning efforts to be more of a 'self'… **had somewhat changed my particular youngest brother of brother(s) profile. I had become a slightly more mature youngest.**"

*Model consequence:* consistent with `KS12.5` — differentiation does not change the *position* but changes **which end of the position's range is expressed**. "A slightly more mature youngest", not "less of a youngest". That is precisely the gating relationship, stated by the person it happened to, and it is the cleanest available warrant for implementing `sibling_position` as a **range selector** rather than an offset.

## ⭐ KS12.8 — A worked case: reciprocity plus position, and the therapist's neutrality `[T]`

An oldest brother of a brother and two younger sisters, married to an only child; she "**significantly depressed and indecisive, and her self-esteem was in shambles**"; living amid **his** extended family, away from hers.

> "It was **tempting to feel sorry for the wife and blame the overly controlling husband**… **Seeing the reciprocity between the husband liking to take the lead and make decisions and the wife stating that she felt relieved not to have to lead or make decisions helped me maintain my neutrality.**"

The wife's history: an only child who "**fit the profile of a younger sister of a brother. She had sided with her mother, who was a dysfunctional younger sister of a brother, against her overly domineering, oldest sibling father and, consequently, developed less of a 'self' related to her functioning position in the system.**"

The husband's supply: "a physician and a kind of superstar in his family, strongly supported emotionally by his parents. **They gave him additional pseudo-self-based strength in dealing with his wife.**"

The trajectory: the wife re-contacts her own family, "**saw the replicating emotional processes, and, as a consequence, began to be more decisive and less helpless.**"

> "**This turned an overfunctioning-underfunctioning marital reciprocity into a more conflicted pattern for a time**, but the couple got through it, and the wife's functioning improved significantly."

> The husband "**reacted to the change in his wife by wanting to get a different family therapist. The wife said, 'Go ahead! I'm going to stick with this one.' The husband backed off.**"

*Model consequence:* three things, all assertable.

1. **Pattern conversion under improvement.** The dominant-adaptive pattern became **conflict** *on the way to* resolution. `M11.C.12` asserts that relieving a symptom without changing the deficit raises conflict; this is the harder and more interesting case — **conflict rising as a sign of genuine progress**. The model must be able to produce both, and a readout that treats rising conflict as deterioration is wrong. **Seventh readout trap.**
2. **Extended-family supply is a real term.** His parents "gave him additional pseudo-self-based strength" — an inflow from outside the nuclear family that sustained his functional position. `M6.I.4`'s transfer must not be nuclear-family-scoped. Same mechanism as `KS10.4`'s castle.
3. **The change-back attempt targeted the coach.** "Wanting to get a different family therapist" is a `M5.E` ladder move aimed at the *external agent*, not at the mover. `M5.E` currently runs the ladder on the acting tie and permits symptom in a third person (`M5.E.5`); this adds **attacking the source of the mindware** as a distinct countermeasure. Worth a requirement — it is the obvious systemic defence and the model should exhibit it.

## KS12.9 — Position, politics, and the establishment axis `[T]` `[D]`

> "**oldest children tend to support establishment views. Youngest siblings, in contrast, are more prone to antiestablishment views, even to being radically rebellious.** Again, **level of maturity in each case plays a key role in whether someone dogmatically adheres to one political view versus another.**"

> "**It is compelling to see how much the emotional system can affect which lever a person pulls in the voting box.**"

*Model consequence:* consistent with `M9`'s beliefs being downstream (`KS03.6`, `KS06.13`) and with `KS06.10`'s content-versus-holding distinction — position biases *which* content is adopted, level determines *how rigidly*. Both dimensions are already in the model; this connects them. No new machinery, but a usable readout.

## KS12.10 — The concept's status: contested, and Bowen's defence `[T]`

> "**Many clinicians and researchers debate the degree of influence sibling position has**… Some consider it not particularly important, and others, like Toman, estimate that it accounts for **10–25 percent of a given personality**. **Bowen theory does not estimate its degree of influence but holds that sibling position is always influencing aspects of how a person functions.**"

> A Georgetown faculty member "**once objected to Bowen including Toman's concept in his theory, stating that it was an individual theory concept.**" Bowen's plaque to Toman is the reply, and Kerr's own argument is: "**Variations in the functioning of family members are determined as much by the reciprocal functioning of others as by forces within the individual.** For example, **the functioning of those interacting with a firstborn during that child's development instill and continually reinforce the traits** common to most firstborns."

*Model consequence:* two things worth recording honestly.

- **Toman's 10–25% is not adopted by Bowen theory.** It should **not** enter the model as an effect size. Grade `[#]` **rejected** — the source explicitly declines it, which is unusual and worth preserving.
- The **defence against the individual-concept objection is the model's own architecture**: position has effects *because the system continually reinforces them*, not because it is a trait. That is the argument for `KS12.2`'s derived functional position, and it means an implementation storing position as an individual attribute with a fixed effect has committed exactly the error Bowen was accused of.

## KS12.11 — Predictability as a blow to free will `[T]`

> "**The predictability is humbling in the blow it deals to how much free will human beings possess. An important factor in people rejecting Toman's sibling profiles is their wanting to see people as more autonomous in their emotional functioning than they really are.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS02.1` and `KS04.8` again — agency is real, graded, and much smaller than assumed. Three statements now across four chapters, converging. `M4.D.1a`'s mixing weight should be **low by default**, with the self-directed channel a minority contributor at every level and a small one at most levels, given `KS05.4`'s left-shifted distribution.
