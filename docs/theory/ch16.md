# Ch16 — Theory in the Practice of Psychotherapy

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** not stated. No footnote, venue, or publication line appears in the chapter text. The chapter is internally datable from its own statements: the most recent events it reports are the 1975 addition of two concepts and the 1975 renaming to "the Bowen theory"; it cites Minuchin (1974) and Paul (1975) as current; it says "After some twenty years of experience with this theory" and "I have spent almost three decades on clinical research in psychotherapy" (research formally begun 1954). It says of the 1966 differentiation profiles that they "are still amazingly accurate ten years later." Bowen also notes "I have devoted almost half of this presentation to some broad background issues," and refers to it as a "presentation," implying a written-for-publication chapter or address.
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):** No new case series is reported. The chapter is retrospective and cites the author's own prior work as its data base: formal research begun 1954 after several years of preliminary work; the NIMH research ward on which entire families lived together (mothers with schizophrenic patients, later extended so that fathers and normal siblings also lived on the ward); a later period in which much of the family therapy was with both parents and a behavior-problem adolescent, in which the average motivated family continued for thirty to forty weekly appointments; and multigenerational family histories. Explicitly stated that early observations from schizophrenia were later checked against neurotic-level and normal families, and stimulated research studies of normal families in the late 1950s and early 1960s. No counts of families are given anywhere in this chapter.
- **Genre: theory / technique / both — and roughly what proportion.** Both, heavily weighted to theory. Roughly **80% theory / 20% technique**. The first half is a critique of the theory/practice split in psychotherapy generally and in family therapy, plus a history of the family movement; the second half is a formal, concept-by-concept statement of the eight concepts. Technique is present but subordinate and mostly embedded: the "significant other" analysis of the therapeutic relationship, the shift to two-parents-plus-therapist, detriangling, coaching into the family of origin, and the paragraph on therapy based on differentiation. This is the chapter's own self-description as a systematizing statement rather than a clinical report.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

Observations Bowen reports the theory had to account for. Facts as stated, not interpretation.

- **Maternal deprivation does not sort the sick from the well.** Large numbers of people judged normal had been exposed to more maternal deprivation than people who were sick. → General clinical/psychiatric population, pre-research decade.
- **The "schizophrenogenic mother" produces non-schizophrenic siblings.** The same parents described in detail as schizophrenogenic had other children who were not only normal but appeared supernormal. → Families of schizophrenic patients.
- **Single traumatic events do not sort either.** Large numbers of people suffered the same trauma without developing symptoms. → General clinical population.
- **Psychiatric nomenclature is symptom description, not etiology.** Except for the small percentage of cases where symptoms could be connected to actual pathology, diagnoses were not consistent with etiology. → The psychiatric field itself.
- **Severe problems arise too fast for a one-generation explanation.** Psychoanalytic theory located emotional illness in a process between parents and child in a single generation, and there was little to explain how severe problems could be created so rapidly.
- **The same basic emotional illness is present in all cultures**, contradicting explanations resting on forces of socialization.
- **A similar process is present in lower forms of life**, contradicting the assumption that emotional illness is specific to humans.
- **The mother-child symbiosis turned out to be a fragment of a larger unit.** Bowen's initial research hypothesis (illness in the child as product of a less severe problem in the mother, with balancing forces maintaining equilibrium) was very quickly shown by observation to be a dependent fragment of the larger family unit; the design was modified so fathers and normal siblings lived on the ward, producing a completely new order of observations.
- **Whole families living together on a research ward produced a new order of clinical data never before recorded in the literature** (1955–1956). Other researchers were observing the same things but conceptualizing them with discrepant models (psychoanalysis, psychology, mythology, physics, chemistry, mathematics).
- **Common denominators across those independent research groups**: "stuck togetherness," bonds, binds, and interlocking of family members with each other; and, for the balancing forces, complementarity, reciprocity, magnetic fields, hydraulic and electrical forces.
- **Parents of schizophrenic people, who appear on the surface to function well, have difficulty distinguishing between the subjective feeling process and the more objective thinking process — most marked in a close personal relationship.** This was the seed observation for differentiation of self, subsequently traced through all levels of families, from the most impaired to normal to the highest-functioning people that could be found.
- **The relationship patterns first found in schizophrenia are present in neurotic-level families and in normal families.** The intensity of the observed patterns is related more to the anxiety of the moment than to the severity of the emotional illness being studied. Summary of the normal-family studies: "the patterns originally thought to be typical of schizophrenia are present in all families some of the time and in some families most of the time."
- **Patterns repeat.** Relationship patterns were seen to repeat over and over, and it was possible to establish some notion of the conditions under which they repeated. In the parent-parent-adolescent work, it became possible to observe a family and know the next move in the family before it occurred.
- **Introducing an outsider changes the family.** Family relationships are remarkably different when an outsider is introduced into the system. A disturbed family is always looking for a vulnerable outsider.
- **An outside relationship can drain family tension.** A therapist maintaining a viable, moderately intense relationship with a patient who stays in viable contact with the family can calm the family; a sufficiently intense outside relationship (including a sexual affair) can keep a family calm and blind to evidence, until it becomes overinvested, at which point the family's tension rises and the other spouse becomes "a suspicious detective, alert to all the evidence previously ignored."
- **Ostensibly "objective" observers fuse with families.** Bowen states that observers entering homes to make neutral observations were fused with the family as soon as they entered, the family automatically became different, and their belief in their objectivity was erroneous. A publicized movie-television study of a family: the movie crew automatically became a significant other which helped propel the parents toward divorce.
- **In the parent-parent-adolescent therapy period, families did not change their original patterns** even while the passive father became less passive, the aggressive mother less aggressive, and the symptomatic child asymptomatic — and the family terminated with great praise for the "good result."
- **Autistic / severely impaired children co-occur with calm, healthy marriages.** The most striking cases were families with little marital conflict, both spouses healthy, and the full weight of the undifferentiation directed to a single maximally impaired child.
- **No family has ever been seen with no projection to a child**, and no family has ever been seen in which children were equally involved in the family emotional process.
- **Families cut off from the extended family get into cyclical, long-term family therapy without improvement.**
- **Some disabled spouses miraculously regain functioning after the overfunctioning spouse dies unexpectedly.** If there is a subsequent marriage, it follows the pattern of the previous one.
- **The mid-1950s family movement arose independently in several places** (Lidz at Johns Hopkins and Yale; Jackson at Palo Alto; Bowen at Bethesda; Ackerman; Bell) before the workers heard of each other — itself offered as an observation.
- **[p2] Overt feeling intensity peaks in the MIDDLE of the scale, not at the bottom.** "People in the moderate range of differentiation have the most intense versions of overt feeling." The 0–25 group is described as feeling-dominated but its energy goes to keeping relationships in balance; the open, direct, impulsive expression of feeling is a 25–50 characteristic. Pass 1 missed this entirely and it makes observable emotionality a **non-monotonic** function of differentiation.
- **[p2] The usual product of the projection process is undiagnosed.** "The basic pattern of the family projection is the same, except for minor variations in form and intensity, whether the eventual impairment in the child be one that leads to serious lifelong dysfunction, or one that never develops serious symptoms and is never diagnosed." And: "The greatest number of people impaired by the projection process are those who do less well with life and who have lower levels of differentiation than their siblings, and who may go for a few generations before producing a child who becomes seriously impaired symptomatically."
- **[p2] Low differentiation can be selected FOR by a relationship system.** "A no-self person who is adept at pleasing his boss may make a better employee than one who has a self." An observation that the environment can reward the de-selfed position.
- **[p2] Toman worked from an individual frame of reference and only with normal families**, yet his ordering of the data was directly incorporable into differentiation of self and the family projection process.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

How Bowen says it works. This chapter is the formal statement of the machinery, so it is given here in the chapter's own order and naming.

**The two master variables (stated as such):**
- **Degree of anxiety** → sets the current intensity of everything else → acts on all patterns in all concepts. Sub-variables named: intensity, duration, and different kinds of anxiety. Acute anxiety is absorbable — all organisms are reasonably adaptable to it and have built-in mechanisms for short bursts. **Sustained or chronic anxiety** is the operative one and is what is useful in determining differentiation of self.
- **Degree of integration of self (differentiation)** → sets how much anxiety it takes to produce symptoms and how reversible they are → acts on the person and, as a family average, on the family.

**The general anxiety→symptom mechanism:** if anxiety increases and remains chronic for a certain period, the organism develops tension — either within itself or in the relationship system — and the tension results in symptoms, dysfunction, or sickness. The symptom may be physiological/physical illness, emotional dysfunction, or social illness characterized by impulsiveness, withdrawal, or social misbehavior. Anxiety is also **infectious**: it can spread rapidly through a family or through society.

**Substrate: three systems.**
- **Emotional system** → governs the life process; includes states of contentment/agitation/fear/weeping/laughing, plus all automatic functions governing the autonomic nervous system; considered synonymous with instinct that governs the life process in all living things; part of man's phylogenetic past shared with all lower forms of life; governed by the same laws that govern all living things. Operates with predictable, knowable stimuli.
- **Intellectual system** → a function of the cerebral cortex, which appeared last in evolution and is the main difference between man and lower forms; confers ability to think, reason, and reflect.
- **Feeling system** → postulated as a **link** between the emotional and intellectual systems, through which certain emotional states are represented in conscious awareness.
- **Brain-center model (offered as a reasonably accurate comparison, not as anatomy):** one center controlling emotions, another controlling intellectual functions, connected by nerve tracts; fusion = the centers being grown together. The more the separateness, the more the intellectual center can **block or screen out** a spectrum of stimuli from the emotional center. **The screening process — "which might be biochemical" — operates best when anxiety is low.** The emotional center handles myriad sensory stimuli from digestive, circulatory, respiratory and other organ systems, plus stimuli from all sensing organs perceiving the environment and relationships. When the emotional center is flooded by stimuli, there is little intellectual functioning not governed by it.
- **Channels:** the emotional system operates through all five senses, most often visual and auditory; plus a sixth sense that can include extrasensory perception. All living things learn to process this data very early and use it in relation to others. Human verbal language is as often used to **deny** the automatic emotional process as to confirm it. The automatic emotional process is held to be far more important in establishing and maintaining relationships than verbal language.

**Concept 1 — Differentiation of self.** Defines people by the degree of **fusion** or **differentiation** between emotional and intellectual functioning. Single continuum, all people. At the fusion end, whatever intellect exists is dominated by the emotional system; the total life course is determined by what "feels right" rather than by beliefs or opinions; the intellect exists as an appendage of the feeling system and may function well in mathematics, physics, or impersonal areas while being emotionally controlled on personal subjects. At the differentiated end, intellect retains relative autonomy under stress. Only *relative* separation is ever possible.
- **Solid self / pseudo-self.** Solid self is made up of clearly defined beliefs, opinions, convictions, and life principles, incorporated into self from one's own life experiences by intellectual reasoning and careful consideration of alternatives; each belief is consistent with all the others; self will act on them under high anxiety and duress. Pseudo-self is **created by emotional pressure and modifiable by emotional pressure**: a vast assortment of principles, beliefs, philosophies and knowledge acquired because it is required or considered right by the group; because acquired under pressure, the items are random and inconsistent with one another without the person's awareness. Pseudo-self is *appended onto* self; solid self is *incorporated into* self. Pseudo-self is "a 'pretend' self," an actor that can be many different selfs.
- **The exchange mechanism.** Every emotional unit — family or the total of society — exerts pressure on members to conform to the ideals and principles of the group. **Only pseudo-self participates in fusion.** In any exchange one gives up a little self to the other, who gains an equal amount. This can be brief (criticism that makes one feel bad for a few days) or long-term (the adaptive spouse becomes so de-selfed as to be unable to make decisions and collapses in "selfless dysfunction — psychosis or chronic physical illness"). The same borrowing and trading occurs automatically in a work group, ending with one employee in the one-down or de-selfed position while others gain self.
- **Functional level of differentiation** shifts from moment to moment or stays fairly constant for most of a life; the basic level is distinct from it. Because shifts are so great and the network so complex, functional level can only be estimated by following a life pattern over long periods.
- **[p2] Solid self is BUILT in calm and SPENT under anxiety.** "In periods of calm, they have employed logical reasoning to develop beliefs, principles, and convictions that they use to overrule the emotional system in situations of anxiety and panic." A two-phase mechanism pass 1 did not record: calm is not merely the period in which the system is diagnostically silent, it is the period in which the durable stock is manufactured.
- **[p2] Differentiation has an instinctual driving force, not only opposing ones.** "The instinctual force toward differentiation is built into the organism, just as are the emotional forces that oppose it." Two built-in opposed forces, both instinctual — pass 1 recorded only the togetherness/opposition side.
- **[p2] No one is exempt under chronic stress.** "Even in people who exhibit some degree of separation between emotion and intellect, and in whom the intellect can hold its own with the emotional system in certain areas most of the time, there are periods of chronic stress in which the emotional system is dominant."
- **[p2] A discretionary override in the 50–75 band.** "There may be periods of laxness in which they permit the automatic pilot of the emotional system to have full control, but when trouble develops they can take over, calm the anxiety, and avoid a life crisis."
- Anchoring: the concept was chosen because *differentiation* has specific meanings in the biological sciences — differentiation of self is meant as a process similar to the differentiation of cells from each other. Same for *fusion*, *symbiosis*, and *instinctual* (used exactly as in biology, not in the restricted psychoanalytic sense).

**Concept 2 — Triangles.** The triangle, a three-person emotional configuration, is **the molecule or basic building block of any emotional system**, family or other group, and **the smallest stable relationship system**. Mechanism:
- A two-person system may be stable as long as it is calm; when anxiety increases it immediately involves **the most vulnerable other person** to become a triangle.
- When tension in the triangle is too great for the threesome, it involves others, producing **interlocking triangles**.
- *In calm*: a comfortably close twosome plus a less comfortable outsider. The twosome works to preserve the togetherness lest one become uncomfortable and form a better togetherness elsewhere. The outsider seeks to form a togetherness with one of the twosome, by numerous well-known moves. Forces are in constant motion even in calm. In moderate tension the tension is characteristically felt by one member of the twosome while the other is oblivious, and **it is the uncomfortable one who initiates a new equilibrium**.
- *In stress*: the **outside position is the most comfortable and most desired**; each works to get it. When forces cannot shift within the triangle, one of the involved twosome triangles in a fourth person, leaving the former third person aside for reinvolvement later; the emotional forces duplicate the exact patterns in the new triangle. Over time forces move from one active triangle to another and finally rest mostly in one triangle as long as the system is fairly calm.
- *At high tension with available family triangles exhausted*: the family triangles in people from outside the family, such as police and social agencies. **A successful externalization of the tension occurs when outside workers are in conflict about the family while the family is calmer.**
- *Structure*: a triangle in moderate tension characteristically has **two comfortable sides and one side in conflict**. Because patterns repeat, people come to have **fixed roles** in relation to each other.
- *Organizational form of the same mechanism*: tension between the two highest administrators can be triangled and retriangled until conflict is acted out between two who are low in the hierarchy; firing or removing one of the conflictual twosome is followed by the conflict erupting in another twosome.
- Each structured pattern in triangles is available for **predictable moves and predictable outcomes** in families and social systems.

**Concept 3 — Nuclear family emotional system.** Patterns of emotional functioning in a single generation. Mechanism chain:
1. Two spouses begin marriage with the lifestyle patterns and levels of differentiation developed in their families of origin. Mating, marriage, and reproduction are governed to a significant degree by emotional-instinctual forces.
2. **Fusion of the two pseudo-selfs into a common self occurs at the point of permanent commitment** — engagement, wedding, or establishing the first home — not before. Living-together relationships are commonly harmonious, with fusion symptoms developing when they finally marry: "It is as if the fusion does not develop as long as they still have an option to terminate the relationship."
3. The fusion produces anxiety in one or both spouses.
4. **[p2 — corrected] "The most universal mechanism is emotional distance from each other."** Pass 1 wrote "the most universal *absorbing* mechanism"; the word *absorbing* is not in the text and the insertion is exactly the error. Distance is named as the most universal of "a spectrum of ways spouses deal with fusion symptoms" — present in all marriages to some degree and in a high percentage to a major degree.
5. **[p2 — corrected] The quantitative budget covers THREE areas and distance is explicitly outside it.** The text is "Other than the emotional distance, there are three major areas in which the amount of undifferentiation in the marriage comes to be manifested in symptoms": marital conflict; sickness or dysfunction in one spouse; projection of the problems to children. The conservation statement is then made over those three and only those three, twice: **"It is as if there is a quantitative amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed in the nuclear family, which may be focused largely in one area or distributed in varying amounts to all three areas"** and, in the projection section, "It is as if there is a definite amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed by marital conflict, sickness in a spouse, and projection to the children." Amount absorbed in one area reduces the amount available for the others. Which mix is used comes from patterns in the families of origin and "the variables involved in the mix in the common self."
6. **Dominant/adaptive differentiation of role.** One spouse becomes the dominant decision maker for the common self while the other adapts. One may assume dominance and force the other to adapt, or assume adaptiveness and force the other to dominate; both trying for dominance produces **conflict**; both trying for adaptiveness produces **decision paralysis**. The dominant one gains self at the expense of the adaptive one.
- **Marital conflict mechanism:** neither gives in, or neither is capable of an adaptive role. High emotional energy invested in the other, thinking or action energy, positive or negative, but self focused mostly on the other. **Cycle**: intense closeness → conflict, which provides a period of emotional distance → making up, which starts another cycle of intense closeness. Anger and negative feeling as intense as the positive. They think of each other even when distant.
- **Dysfunction-in-one-spouse mechanism:** the pseudo-self of the adaptive one merges into that of the dominant one, who assumes more and more responsibility for the twosome. The long-adaptive one **gradually loses the ability to function and make decisions for self**; from there it takes no more than a moderate increase in stress to trigger dysfunction — physical illness, emotional illness, or social illness such as drinking, acting out, irresponsible behavior. Adaptiveness is determined from long-term functioning posture, not verbal report; each does some adapting and each usually believes he or she gives in more.
- **Two variables governing intensity in the nuclear family** (stated explicitly): (a) the degree of emotional isolation, or cutoff, from the extended family or from others important in the relationship system; (b) the level of anxiety.

**Concept 4 — Family projection process.** Parental undifferentiation impairs one or more children, operating **within the father-mother-child triangle** and revolving around the mother, as the key figure in reproduction and usual principal caretaker. Mechanism sequence:
1. **Begins with anxiety in the mother.**
2. The child responds anxiously to the mother.
3. **The mother misperceives the child's anxious response as a problem in the child.**
4. Anxious parental effort goes into sympathetic, solicitous, overprotective energy — directed more by the mother's anxiety than by the reality needs of the child.
5. This establishes a pattern of **infantilizing** the child, who gradually becomes more impaired and more demanding.
6. Once started, the process can be driven either by anxiety in the mother or by anxiety in the child.
- **Father's role:** a support role. Sensitive to the mother's anxiety, he tends to support her view and help her implement her anxious efforts at mothering.
- **Triangle form of the same process:** basic tension between the parents; the father gains the outside position (called passive, weak, distant), leaving the conflict between mother and child; the mother (called aggressive, dominating, castrating) wins over the child, who moves another step toward chronic functional impairment. Families replay the same triangular game over and over for years as though the winner were in doubt, but the final result is always the same. **Over the years the child accepts the always-lose outcome more easily, even to volunteering for the position.**
- **Variation:** the father finally attacks the mother, leaving the child in the outside position; this child learns the techniques of gaining the outside position by playing the parents off against each other.
- **Distribution rule:** the undifferentiation **focuses first on one child**; if the amount is too great for that child, the process selects others for lesser degrees of involvement. Where the amount is very great it can seriously impair most of the children and leave one or two relatively out of the process.
- Can result in primary emotional impairment of the child, or superimpose itself on some defect or on chronic physical illness or disability.
- **Selection driver, simplistic level:** the degree of emotional **turn on or turn off** the mother feels for the child — stated as equal in emotional-systems terms. It is an automatic emotional process **not changed by acting the opposite**.
- **Selection driver, specific level:** the level of undifferentiation in the parents; the amount of anxiety at the time of conception and birth; the orientation of the parents toward marriage and children.
- **Orientation mechanism:** early thoughts about marriage and children are more prominent in the female than the male and begin to take orderly form before adolescence. A female whose early thoughts run to the husband she will marry tends toward marriages where she focuses emotional energy on the husband and he on her, with symptoms in marital conflict and sickness in a spouse. A female whose early thoughts and fantasies run more to the children she will have tends to become the mother of impaired children — so intensely in some women that **the husband is incidental to the process**. Spouses from lower levels of differentiation are less specific about marriage and children.

**Concept 5 — Emotional cutoff.** Deals with how people separate themselves from the past in order to start their lives in the present generation. **[p2] The chapter gives the reason for its promotion to concept status**, which pass 1 omitted: "It was accorded the status of a separate concept to include details not stated elsewhere, and to have a separate concept for emotional process between the generations." Prior status: "a poorly defined extension of other concepts for several years." Mechanism:
- **All people have some degree of unresolved emotional attachment to their parents.** The lower the level of differentiation, the more intense the unresolved attachment.
- **The degree of unresolved emotional attachment to the parents is equivalent to the degree of undifferentiation that must somehow be handled in the person's own life and in future generations.**
- Handled by: (a) the intrapsychic process of denial and isolation of self while living close to the parents; (b) physically running away; or (c) a combination of emotional isolation and physical distance.
- Runners and stayers are equally emotionally dependent: "They both need emotional closeness, but they are allergic to it."
- **Stayer profile:** some degree of supportive contact with parents; less intense overall process; **internalized symptoms** under stress — physical illness and depression. Exaggerated version: collapse into psychosis, isolating intrapsychically while living with the parents.
- **Runner profile:** more inclined to impulsive behavior; sees the problem as being in the parents and running away as the method of gaining independence. The more intense the cutoff, the more vulnerable to **duplicating the parental pattern with the first available other person** — an impulsive marriage; when problems develop there, he tends to run from that too, through multiple marriages, then more temporary living-together relationships. Exaggerated versions: relationship nomads, vagabonds, hermits with superficial relationships or who give up and live alone.
- **Substitute families** (living-together and communal arrangements made up of people who ran away) are very unstable: when tension builds, members cut off from that too and move on to another. Under the best conditions substitute families and outside relationships are poor substitutes for original families.
- Named societal expression: **the generation gap** — the age-old cutoff process became more pronounced as a result of societal anxiety.

**Concept 6 — Multigenerational transmission process.** The family projection process continues through multiple generations. Mechanism:
- In any nuclear family there is **one child who is the primary object** of the projection process; that child emerges with a **lower** level of differentiation than the parents and does less well in life.
- Other children, **minimally involved** with the parents, emerge with **about the same** level as the parents.
- Those who grow up **relatively outside** the family emotional process develop **better** levels than the parents.
- Following the most impaired child through successive generations yields **one line of descent producing individuals with lower and lower levels of differentiation**. The process may go rapidly for a few generations, remain static for a generation or so, then speed up again.
- Following the same-level line shows remarkable consistency of family functioning through the generations (family traditions, family ideals).
- Following the higher-level line shows a line of highly functioning and very successful people.
- **A family at the highest level can have one child who starts down the scale; a family at the lowest level can have a child who starts up the scale.**
- This is the process that produces the poorly functioning people who make up **most of the lower social classes**.
- Restated evolutionary phenomenology (attributed to Bowen's own earlier description): schizophrenia as a natural process that helps keep the race strong — the weakness from the family is fixed in one person who is **less likely to marry and reproduce and more likely to die young**.

**Concept 7 — Sibling position.** An adaptation of Toman's work on personality profiles of each sibling position. Mechanism / use:
- Important personality characteristics fit with the sibling position in which a person grew up. Toman's ten basic sibling profiles let one know the profile of any sibling position and, all things being equal, hold a whole body of presumptive knowledge about anyone.
- **[p2] The first stated use is SELECTION, not measurement.** "His ideas provided a new dimension toward understanding how a particular child is chosen as the object of the family projection process." Pass 1 recorded only the measuring-instrument use. Sibling position therefore enters the theory at two distinct points: as an input to which child gets projected onto, and as the instrument for reading the result.
- **The degree to which a personality profile fits with normal provides a way to read the level of differentiation and the direction of the projection process from generation to generation** — i.e. deviation from the expected profile is the measuring instrument.
- Together with differentiation and projection, permits assembling reliable presumptive personality profiles on people in past generations for whom verifiable facts are missing.
- Provides predictive data on how spouses will handle the mix in a marriage and how they will handle their effort in family therapy.

**Concept 8 — Societal regression.** The family concept extended to society. Mechanism:
- **Family form:** when a family is subjected to chronic, sustained anxiety, the family begins to lose contact with its intellectually determined principles and to resort more and more to emotionally determined decisions to allay the anxiety of the moment; the results are symptoms and eventually regression to a lower level of functioning.
- **Societal form (the postulate):** the same process is evolving in society; we are in a period of increasing chronic societal anxiety; society responds with emotionally determined decisions to allay the anxiety of the moment; this results in symptoms of dysfunction; efforts to relieve the symptoms result in more **emotional band-aid legislation**, which increases the problem; and the cycle keeps repeating, just as the family goes through similar cycles to the states we call emotional illness.
- **The link Bowen says he identified**: first, the delinquent teenage youngster, who is a responsibility for both the parents and society; second, changes in the way parents and agents of society deal with the same problem.
- **Postulated source of the chronic anxiety:** the population explosion; decreasing supplies of food and raw materials necessary to maintain man's way of life on earth; pollution of the environment slowly threatening the balance of life necessary for human survival. Explicitly chosen as concepts consistent with **man as an instinctual being rather than man as a social being**.

**Additional mechanism, stated outside the eight concepts — the "significant other" law of emotional systems.** Two opposed characteristics:
1. **The successful introduction of a significant other person into an anxious or disturbed relationship system has the capacity to modify relationships within the system.**
2. **The higher the level of tension or anxiety within an emotional system, the more the members tend to withdraw from outside relationships and to compartmentalize themselves with each other.**
Variables named as governing (1): who the significant other is; what counts as *successful* introduction; how the significant other is introduced; how long he remains a member of the system; the importance of the involved family member to the rest of the family; and above all **the assumed, assigned, or actual importance of the significant other person**, with assuming and assigning usually a bilateral operation. Modality of change follows from the emotionality: high emotionality / messianic qualities / exaggerated promises / evangelism → change can be sudden and magical and is less likely to be long term; low emotionality / dealing in reality → change comes slowly and is solid and long lasting.
**[p2] The "successful" variable is an inverted U, stated explicitly:** "The successful involvement depends on whether or not the relationship works. This involves the family member devoting a reasonable amount of thinking-feeling energy to the relationship **without becoming too emotionally preoccupied**." Too little engagement and the relationship does not work; too much and it alienates the person from the family and raises family tension. Pass 1 listed the variable but not the optimum.

**Fusion of any entering outsider.** "As soon as a vulnerable outside person comes into viable emotional contact with the family, he becomes part of it, no matter how much he protests the opposite." Complete objectivity is impossible; the best version of objectivity is possible with significant others who know triangles.

## A3. Predictive claims

Stated as conditionals. Evidence status flagged as **[E]** where Bowen points to observations, **[A]** where asserted.

**Differentiation → outcome**
1. If fusion between feeling and thinking is high, then the person functions the poorest and inherits a high percentage of life's problems. **[E — asserted from the graded family series]**
2. If differentiation is high, then the person has the most flexibility and adaptability in coping with life stresses and the most freedom from problems of all kinds. **[E]**
3. If a person is at the fusion end, then he is more easily stressed into dysfunction **and** it is difficult for him to recover from dysfunction. **[A]**
4. If a person is well differentiated and is stressed into dysfunction, then he recovers rapidly. **[A]**
5. If a person has intense fusion **but** keeps his relationships in balance and is never subjected to severe stress, then he may never develop symptoms and appear normal — **but** his life adjustment is tenuous, and if stressed into dysfunction the impairment can be chronic or permanent. **[A]** — explicitly decoupling differentiation from presence/absence of symptoms.
6. If a person is below 50, then in critical situations the emotional system tells the intellect what to think, say, and decide. If above 50, then the intellectual system begins making a few decisions of its own. **[A]**
7. If a person is in the lower part of the 50-to-75 range, then he *knows* there is a better way but, intellect being poorly formed, follows a life course similar to those below 50. **[A]**
8. If anxiety is sufficiently low, then almost any organism can appear normal in the sense of being symptom free. **[A]**
9. If anxiety increases and remains chronic for a certain period, then the organism develops tension within itself or in the relationship system, and the tension results in symptoms, dysfunction, or sickness. **[A]**
10. If chronic severe stress recurs, then that is the real test of the stability of differentiation. **[A]**
11. If people are at moderate levels (25–50), then when anxiety is low their functioning can resemble good differentiation and when anxiety is high it can resemble low differentiation. **[A]**
11a. **[p2]** If a person is in periods of calm, then he can employ logical reasoning to develop the beliefs, principles and convictions later used to overrule the emotional system under anxiety and panic — stated of the 50–75 group. **[A]** *Solid self accrues only in calm.*
11b. **[p2]** If chronic stress persists, then even a person whose intellect normally holds its own in certain areas has periods in which the emotional system is dominant. **[A]** *No exemption at any level.*
11c. **[p2]** If a person is in the moderate (25–50) range, then he has "the most intense versions of overt feeling" of any level. **[A]** *Observable emotionality is non-monotonic in differentiation.*
11d. **[p2]** If the significant other relationship is to work, then the family member must devote "a reasonable amount of thinking-feeling energy to the relationship without becoming too emotionally preoccupied." **[A]** *An inverted U on engagement with the outside relationship.*

**Mate selection and marriage**
12. **People pick spouses who have the same levels of differentiation.** Stated twice, once as "marry spouses with equal levels of differentiation." **[A]** With the mechanism: the lifestyle of a spouse at another level would be sufficiently different to be considered emotionally incompatible.
13. If the level of differentiation is lower, then the emotional fusion of marriage is more intense and the potential problems for the future are greater. **[A]**
14. If the pair are still able to terminate the relationship (living together, pre-commitment), then fusion does not develop; if they commit permanently, then the two pseudo-selfs fuse into a common self. **[E — from the observation that living-together couples are commonly harmonious and develop fusion symptoms on marrying]**
15. If both spouses try for the dominant role, then conflict results. If both try for the adaptive role, then decision paralysis results. **[A]**
16. If the spouses can alternate the dominant and adaptive roles, then the marriage is healthier. **[A]**
17. **The dominant/adaptive assignment is not predicted by sex** — there are as many dominant females as males and as many adaptive males as females; it is determined by the position each had in the family of origin, and these characteristics played a major role in the original choice of each other. **[E — "From my experience"]**

**Absorption/allocation of undifferentiation**
18. If a quantity of undifferentiation is absorbed in marital conflict or in sickness in a spouse, then the amount directed to the children is reduced. **[A]** (Stated three times; the nearest thing in the chapter to a conservation law.)
19. If most of the undifferentiation goes into marital conflict and the spouses are so invested in each other that the children are largely outside the emotional process, then **marital conflict does not in itself harm children**. If marital conflict *and* projection to children are both present, then it is the projection process that is hurtful to children. **[A]**
20. If the problem shifts from one area to another more often, then the less chance the process will be crippling in any single area. **[A]**
21. If a spouse functions in the adaptive position for long periods, then he or she gradually loses the ability to function and make decisions for self; thereafter no more than a moderate increase in stress is required to trigger dysfunction. **[A]**
22. If dysfunction-in-one-spouse is used as the principal means of controlling undifferentiation, then the illnesses can be chronic and most difficult to reverse. **[A]**
23. If one parent is dysfunctional but someone else functions instead, then the children can be almost unaffected — the main problem in the children being an **inherited life pattern as caretaker of the sick parent, which will project into the future**. **[A]**
24. If the pattern is overfunctioning/underfunctioning without marital conflict, then **divorce is almost impossible** and the marriages are enduring. **[A]**
25. If the overfunctioning spouse dies unexpectedly, then the disabled one may miraculously regain functioning; if there is a subsequent marriage, it follows the pattern of the previous one. **[E — "There have been families…"]**
26. If anxiety is low, then any nuclear-family symptom (marital conflict, spouse dysfunction, child symptom) is less intense; if anxiety is high, more intense. **[A]**

**Projection process**
27. If the mother's early orientation was toward the husband she would marry, then symptoms tend to focus in marital conflict and sickness in a spouse; if toward the children she would have, then she tends to become the mother of impaired children. **[A]**
28. Children selected for the projection process are predicted to be: those conceived and born during stress in the mother's life; the first child; the oldest son or oldest daughter; an only child of either sex; one who is emotionally special to the mother; or one the mother believes to be special to the father. **[A]**
29. If a child was fretful, colicky, rigid, and nonresponsive to the mother from the beginning, then the initial special emotional investment in that child is great. **[A]**
30. **It is impossible for mothers to have equal emotional investment in any two children, no matter how much they try to protest equality for all.** **[A]** — with the corroborating claim that no family has ever been seen in which children were equally involved.
31. If the mother-child fusion is intense and remains in positive, symptom-free equilibrium until adolescence, then when the child attempts to function on his own the relationship can turn negative and the child develop severe symptoms. If the fusion is more intense still, then the child may remain relatively asymptomatic until young adulthood and **collapse in psychosis when he attempts to function away from the parents**. **[A]**
32. If the projection process is acted against by "acting the opposite," then nothing changes — the emotional turn-on/turn-off is an automatic process not changed by acting the opposite. **[A]**
33. **Almost every family has one child who was more triangled than the others, and whose life adjustment is less good than the others.** Given multigenerational history data on sibling life adjustments, that child can be identified. **[A]**

**Multigenerational**
34. If a child is the primary object of the projection process, then he emerges **lower** than the parents; if minimally involved, **about the same**; if relatively outside the family emotional process, **better**. **[A]** — the transmission rule.
35. If a family encounters severe stress in perhaps the fifth or sixth generation of a ten-generation process, then it may produce a **social failure who is less impaired than the schizophrenic person**. **[A]**
36. If a lineage keeps the relationship system in relatively symptom-free equilibrium for several more generations at a poorly differentiated level, then the degree of impairment reaches schizophrenia. **[A]**
37. If the person is the one in whom the family weakness is fixed, then he is less likely to marry and reproduce and more likely to die young. **[A]**

**Cutoff**
38. **The more intense the cutoff with the past, the more likely the individual is to have an exaggerated version of his parental family problem in his own marriage, and the more likely his own children are to do a more intense cutoff with him in the next generation.** **[A]** — the chapter's most compact multigenerational prediction.
39. If a nuclear family maintains some kind of viable emotional contact with past generations, then the life process in **both** generations is more orderly and asymptomatic. **[A]**
40. **Controlled comparison as stated by Bowen:** take two families with identical levels of differentiation. If one remains in contact with the parental family, then it remains relatively free of symptoms for life and the level of differentiation does not change much in the next generation. If the other cuts off with the past, then it develops symptoms and dysfunction, and a **lower** level of differentiation in the succeeding generation. **[A — presented as a thought comparison, not a measured pair]**
41. If a symptomatic nuclear family is emotionally cut off from the family of origin, then it can get into cyclical, long-term family therapy without improvement. **[E]**
42. If one or both parents can reestablish emotional contact with their families of origin, then the anxiety level subsides, the symptoms become softer and more manageable, and family therapy can become productive. **[E]**
43. If the level of anxiety is higher, then the degree of generation gap in poorly differentiated people is greater. **[A]**
44. If people run away and form substitute families, then those families are very unstable and are cut off in turn when tension builds. **[A]**

**Triangles**
45. If a two-person system's anxiety increases, then it immediately involves the most vulnerable other person. **[A]**
46. If tension in a triangle exceeds what the threesome can hold, then it involves others into interlocking triangles. **[A]**
47. In stress, if forces cannot be shifted within the triangle, then one of the twosome triangles in a fourth person, and the emotional forces **duplicate the exact patterns** in the new triangle. **[A]**
48. If family triangles are exhausted at very high tension, then the family triangles in outsiders such as police and social agencies; **successful externalization is identifiable by outside workers being in conflict about the family while the family is calmer**. **[A]** — an operational test.
49. In an organization, if conflict between two low-hierarchy people is "settled" by firing or removing one, then the conflict erupts in another twosome. **[A]**
50. If one knows triangles, then one can observe a family and know the next move before it occurs. **[E — reported from the parent-parent-adolescent period]**

**Therapeutic-relationship predictions** (also technique; listed here because stated as system laws)
51. If a significant other is successfully introduced into an anxious system, then relationships within the system are modified. **[A]**
52. If tension/anxiety in a system rises, then members withdraw from outside relationships and compartmentalize with each other. **[A]**
53. If the therapist-patient relationship becomes more intense, then the patient withdraws from emotional contact with the family and **the family becomes more disturbed**. **[A]**
54. If an outside affair is kept at the right emotional level, then the family system can be calm and blind to the evidence; the moment it becomes emotionally overinvested, it alienates the involved person from the family and increases tension within it. **[A]**
55. If the involved family member is important to the rest of the family and relating actively, then the family responds quickly to his outside involvement; if withdrawn and inactive, the family responds slowly unless the outside relationship is fairly intense. **[A]**
56. If the relationship is endowed with high emotionality, messianic qualities, exaggerated promises, and evangelism, then change can be sudden and magical and is less likely to be long term. If emotionality is low and the relationship deals in reality, then change comes slowly and is solid and long lasting. **[A]**
57. If a vulnerable outsider comes into viable emotional contact with the family, then he becomes part of it regardless of his protests. **[A]**
58. If the therapist can stay in viable emotional contact with the two most significant family members and remain relatively outside the emotional activity in the central triangle, then the age-old fusion between the family members will slowly begin to resolve, and **all other family members will automatically change** in relation to the two parents in the home setting. **[E — "the results were far superior to anything before that time"]**
59. If one family member can master a step toward differentiation, then **other family members automatically take similar steps**. **[A]**
60. If any small step toward differentiation is taken, then it is met with **vigorous disapproval of the group** — the togetherness forces maintaining the status quo. **[A]**
61. If the differentiating one gets no help, then he falls back into the togetherness to get emotional harmony for the moment. **[A]**
62. If the individual attempts differentiation without some conviction of his own, then he is blindly following the therapist's advice and is caught in a self-defeating togetherness with the therapist. **[A]**

**Societal**
63. If a family is subjected to chronic, sustained anxiety, then it loses contact with its intellectually determined principles, resorts increasingly to emotionally determined decisions to allay the anxiety of the moment, and the result is symptoms and eventually regression to a lower level of functioning. **[A]**
64. If society does the same, then symptomatic dysfunction follows, efforts to relieve the symptoms produce emotional band-aid legislation, which increases the problem, and the cycle repeats. **[A]**

## A5. Terminology

Terms as this chapter defines or uses them.

- **Emotional system** — the phylogenetically old system governing the life process; includes contentment/agitation/fear/weeping/laughing plus all automatic functions governing the autonomic nervous system; **synonymous with instinct** that governs the life process in all living things.
- **Intellectual system** — function of the cerebral cortex; ability to think, reason, reflect; evolutionarily last; the main difference between man and lower forms.
- **Feeling system** — postulated **link** between the emotional and intellectual systems, through which certain emotional states are represented in conscious awareness. *(This is a three-system model, not a two-system model; the feeling system is given a specific structural job here.)*
- **Emotional illness** — used to replace "mental illness" and "psychological illness"; a **disorder of the emotional system**; a deep process involving the basic life process of the organism.
- **Differentiation of self** — the degree of fusion or differentiation between emotional and intellectual functioning; chosen because *differentiation* has specific meanings in the biological sciences, meant as a process similar to the differentiation of cells from each other.
- **Fusion** — used in its biological sense; the grown-togetherness of emotional and intellectual functioning within a person, and the merging of pseudo-selfs between people.
- **Solid self** — clearly defined beliefs, opinions, convictions and life principles, internally consistent, incorporated by intellectual reasoning and careful weighing of alternatives; acts on principle even under high anxiety and duress; **does not participate in the fusion phenomenon**; stable.
- **Pseudo-self** — principles/beliefs/philosophies/knowledge acquired under emotional pressure because required or considered right by the group; random and inconsistent with one another without the person's awareness; **appended onto** self; "a 'pretend' self," an actor that can be many different selfs; unstable, **negotiable in the relationship system**.
- **Undifferentiated family ego mass** — Bowen's own earlier term for the emotional "stuck togetherness" in families. He states explicitly that it was **an assemblage of words from conventional theory** that did **not** conform to his plan to use concepts consistent with biology, and that he used it for a few years only because more people could hear the concept in words they understood. Still used once here in an extended form, **undifferentiated societal ego mass**, for the lowest range.
- **Functional level of differentiation** — the level that can shift from moment to moment or remain fairly constant for most of a life; distinguished from basic level; estimable only by following a life pattern over long periods.
- **Triangle** — a three-person emotional configuration; the molecule or basic building block of any emotional system; the smallest stable relationship system. **The word was chosen over *triad*** because the concept came to include much more than the conventional term *triad*, and there was a communication problem with people who assumed they knew what *triad* meant. The 1956 research group had been calling it "**triads**"; the 1957 formulation was called the "**interdependent triad**."
- **Interlocking triangles** — the series produced when tension exceeds what one triangle can hold.
- **Detriangling** — control of one's own automatic emotional participation in the emotional process, obtained by reading the automatic emotional responsiveness via the concept of triangles. "No one ever stays outside, but a knowledge of triangles makes it possible to get outside on one's own initiative while staying emotionally in contact with the family."
- **Triangled child** — clinical term for the one who was the main focus of the family projection process.
- **Nuclear family emotional system** — the patterns of emotional functioning in a family in a single generation.
- **Family projection process** — the process through which parental undifferentiation impairs one or more children, operating within the father-mother-child triangle and revolving around the mother.
- **Emotional cutoff** — the way people separate themselves from the past in order to start their lives in the present generation: separation, isolation, withdrawal, running away, or denying the importance of the parental family. Bowen notes he searched for a better term: "However much *cutoff* may sound like informal slang, I could find no other term as accurate for describing the process."
- **Generation gap** — the popular name for the cutoff process as it became more pronounced under societal anxiety.
- **Multigenerational transmission process** — the continuation of the family projection process through multiple generations, producing descending, static, and ascending lines.
- **Sibling position** — adapted from Toman; personality characteristics fitting the position in which a person grew up; used as an instrument for reading differentiation and projection.
- **Societal regression** — the eighth concept; society losing contact with intellectually determined principles under chronic sustained anxiety and resorting to emotionally determined decisions.
- **Family systems theory / the Bowen theory** — the same theory; formally renamed in 1975 "in order to denote the specificity that is built into this family systems theory," despite Bowen's stated long-standing opposition to proper names in terminology. Explicitly **not** synonymous with general systems theory: "it is grossly inaccurate to consider family systems theory as synonymous with general systems, although it is accurate to think of family systems theory as somehow fitting into the broad framework of general systems theory."
- **System** — "Any relationship with balancing forces and counterforces in constant operation is a system." Used from 1963 as a shorthand for the complex balancing of family relationships. **Dynamics** is explicitly rejected as inadequate to describe the idea of a system.
- **Modify** — deliberately chosen in place of **change**, "which has come to have so many different meanings in psychotherapy." *(A terminological decision with an operational consequence: the significant-other law is stated as modifying, not changing, relationships.)*
- **Growth** — explicitly declared meaningless: "The word *growth* has been so misused during the past decade, that it has become meaningless."
- **Normal** — the concept of differentiation is said to **eliminate** the concept of normal, "which psychiatry has never successfully defined… It is not possible to define normal when the thing to be measured is constantly changing."
- **Supervising / coaching** — Bowen's own substitute terms for the therapy process: "supervising" the effort the family makes on its own behalf, and "coaching" a family member in working with his own family.
- **Unstructured state of chaos** / "healthy, unstructured state of chaos" — Bowen's name for the state of family therapy after 1957.
- **Revolution** — defined here idiosyncratically as "a convulsion that prevents change"; relationship-oriented energy going back and forth on the same points, each side's issue determined by the other's position.
- **Instinctual** — used exactly as in biology, not in the restricted psychoanalytic sense.
- **Emotional ingroup** — used of the psychoanalytic profession: a group, like a family or religion, that devotes energy to defining differences with each other and defending dogma, cannot generate new knowledge from within, and cannot admit knowledge from without.
- **Distant drumbeat / foreground drumbeat** — the theory listens to a distant drumbeat people have always heard, obscured by the noisy insistence of the foreground drumbeat, which stands for the concepts excluded from individual theory.

- **[p2] We-ness** — the common self formed when two pseudo-selfs fuse at permanent commitment ("two pseudo-selfs fuse into a we-ness in which one becomes the dominant decision maker"), and the unit that projects ("parents operate as a we-ness to project the undifferentiation to one or more children"). Pass 1 recorded "common self" but not this term, which is the one Bowen actually uses at both points.
- **[p2] Pretend intellect** — the intellect of a person below 50; permitted by the emotional system "to go off into a corner and think about distant things as long as it does not interfere in joint decisions that affect the total life course."
- **[p2] No-self person** — the 0–25 term for the fully de-selfed; also the endpoint of the long-term adaptive position ("collapses in selfless dysfunction").
- **[p2] Profile band labels** — the four ranges are named in the chapter only by their profile headings: **low** (0–25), **moderate** (25–50), **moderate to good** (50–75); 75–100 is unnamed and unprofiled. Bowen never uses "quartile" or "band."

*Terms doing a job done elsewhere by another term:* **undifferentiated family ego mass** and **fusion / emotional stuck-togetherness** are explicitly the same phenomenon under two vocabularies, one abandoned as off-plan. **Triangled child** is the clinical name for the object of the **family projection process**. **Modify** and **change** are treated as distinct where much of the field treats them as one.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The central triangle is father–mother–child**, described as "the best example" of fixed roles in a triangle. Most common configuration: basic tension between the parents; **father gains the outside position** and is called passive, weak, distant; conflict is left between **mother and child**; **the mother wins over the child**, who moves another step toward chronic functional impairment. The outcome is never actually in doubt, and the child in time accepts and even volunteers for the always-lose position.
- **Variation:** the father finally attacks the mother, leaving the **child in the outside position**; that child learns to gain the outside position by playing the parents off against each other.
- **The two most significant family members** are, in the therapeutic frame, "usually the two parents or two spouses" — the central triangle for treatment purposes.
- **Dominant / adaptive positions in marriage** are **not determined by sex**. As many dominant females as males; as many adaptive males as females. They are determined by **the position each had in their families of origin**, and played a major role in the original choice of each other as partners.
- **The mother is the key figure** in the family projection process — as the key figure in reproduction and the usual principal caretaker for the infant. **The father plays a support role**, sensitive to the mother's anxiety, supporting her view and helping her implement her anxious efforts at mothering.
- **Symptom-bearer selection among children.** The undifferentiation focuses **first on one child**; if too great for that child, others are selected for lesser degrees. Named candidates: those conceived and born during stress in the mother's life; the first child; the oldest son or oldest daughter; an only child of either sex; one who is emotionally special to the mother; one the mother believes to be special to the father. "Common special children": only children, an oldest child, **a single child of one sex among several of the opposite sex**, a child with some defect. Also: children who were fretful, colicky, rigid, and nonresponsive to the mother from the beginning.
- **Maternal sex preference:** "A good percentage of mothers have a basic preference for boys or girls, depending upon their orientation in the family of origin."
- **Sibling position as diagnostic of role.** Deviation from Toman's expected profile reads the projection process: "if an oldest turns out to be more like a youngest, that is strong evidence that he was the most triangled child. If an oldest is an autocrat, that is strong evidence of a moderate level of impaired functioning. An oldest who functions calmly and responsibly is good evidence of a better level of differentiation." Bowen: "no single piece of data is more important than knowing the sibling position of people in the present and past generations."
- **[p2] Sibling position enters the theory twice, at opposite ends of the causal chain.** As an *input* to selection — "His ideas provided a new dimension toward understanding how a particular child is chosen as the object of the family projection process" — and as the *readout* of the result (deviation from the expected profile). Pass 1's A6 carried only the readout. Structurally these are different couplings and a model needs both or neither.
- **Sibling differentiation outcomes** (the transmission rule as a structural statement): the primary projection object emerges lower than the parents; minimally involved siblings emerge at about the parents' level; those growing up relatively outside the family emotional process emerge better than the parents.
- **The triangled child** is identified, in multigenerational history-taking, from historical data about the life adjustments of each sibling: "Almost every family has one child who was more triangled than the others, and whose life adjustment is less good than the others."
- **Caretaker role as inherited structure:** where one parent is chronically dysfunctional, the main problem in the children is inheriting a life pattern as caretaker of the sick parent, which projects into the future.
- **Who moves first.** Two explicit statements: within a triangle in moderate tension, tension is felt by one while the other is oblivious, and **"it is the uncomfortable one who initiates a new equilibrium"** toward more comfortable togetherness for self. Within therapy, the mover is the **motivated family member** — one member's step is what the others then follow automatically.
- **Position in the differentiated marriage:** spouses can be autonomous selfs together or alone; "the wife is able to function more fully as a female and the husband more fully as a male without either having to debate the advantages or disadvantages of biological and social roles."
- **Outside-the-family positions used structurally:** at very high tension, police and social agencies are triangled in and take up the conflict; in organizations, the conflict of the two highest administrators is retriangled down to two low in the hierarchy.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **Work with the two spouses and the therapist only; exclude the child.** Bowen states this hypothesis and its result explicitly: "I hypothesized the situation would be different by excluding the child and limiting the therapy to the two parents and the therapist… Therapeutically, the results were far superior to anything before that time. This has remained the one basic therapeutic method since the early 1960s."
- **Stay in viable emotional contact with the two most significant family members** — usually the two parents or two spouses.
- **Stay relatively outside the emotional activity in the central triangle** — keep self detriangled from the emotionality between the spouses.
- **Read the automatic emotional responsiveness through the concept of triangles** in order to control one's own participation in it. That control is **detriangling**.
- **Make the family members responsible for each other.**
- **Promise no benefits except from the family's own effort to learn about itself and change itself.**
- **Actively reduce the assigned and assumed overimportance.** Named concrete measure: **charging average fees**, "which helps avoid the emotional pitfalls inherent in charging high fees."
- **Rename the operation.** Bowen uses "supervising" the effort the family makes on its own behalf, and "coaching" a family member in working with his own family — the terms themselves are technique.
- **Attain and maintain emotional neutrality with individual family members** — described as "a long-term effort" with "many subtleties."
- **Keep the approach calm and low-keyed.** "A calm, low-keyed, detriangling approach is more effective with a single appointment or with many."
- **[p2] Hold the stated over-all goal**, which pass 1 left out: "The over-all goal is to help individual family members to rise up out of the emotional togetherness that binds us all." Paired with the claim that the drive to do so is already present — "The instinctual force toward differentiation is built into the organism" — so the therapist is releasing an existing force, not supplying one.
- **Aim at helping the motivated family member take a microscopic step toward a better level of differentiation**, in spite of the togetherness forces that oppose it.
- **Be present at the point where the group disapproves.** "The togetherness forces are so strong in maintaining the status quo that any small step toward differentiation is met with vigorous disapproval of the group. This is the point at which a therapist or guide can be most helpful."
- **Direct effort at decreasing anxiety and at opening the relationship cutoff.** "Some of the most important family therapy efforts are directed at decreasing anxiety and opening the relationship cutoff."
- **Coach a spouse to reestablish contact with, and differentiate a self in, his own family of origin.** Techniques for this are stated to be sufficiently developed to be "a family therapy method in its own right."
- **Convert the cutoff into an orderly differentiation of a self from the extended family** — the stated therapeutic objective of the cutoff concept.
- **Take a careful history and reconstruct past generations**; use sibling position plus differentiation plus projection to assemble presumptive profiles where verifiable facts are missing; identify the triangled child from historical data on each sibling's life adjustment.
- **Think in terms of what, how, when, and where — not why.** "Systems thinking focused on what happened, and how, when, and where it happened, insofar as these observations could be based on observable facts. The method carefully avoids why explanations and the discrepant reasoning that follows."
- **Use the fact/content separation formulas.** "That man dreams is a scientific fact, but what he dreams is not necessarily a fact"; the same formula applied to talking, thinking, and feeling. Stay on the facts of loving and hating and avoid the content of the intense emotions.
- **Assess adaptiveness from long-term functioning posture, not from verbal reports.**
- **Estimate differentiation from the average functional level of self as it operates through periods of stress and calm** — not from calm periods alone.
- **Keep the whole spectrum of variables in mind at once.** Bowen's closing statement of the therapist's task: "the therapist must keep the whole spectrum of variables in his head at once; but, after some experience, knowing the variables well enough to know when one is out of balance becomes automatic."

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not cultivate or work in the transference.** "I began to work toward avoiding the transference." Bowen states that a therapist with knowledge of systems theory and especially triangles "can deal largely in reality and facts and eliminate much of the emotional process that usually goes into a transference." He rejects the reframing that he merely "handles it well."
- **Do not permit or accept the assignment of importance to yourself.** Explicitly listed among the initial measures: "avoiding the family tendency to assign importance to me."
- **Do not accept responsibility for the outcome.** The failure mode named is the family installing the therapist in an all-important position and holding him responsible for success or failure.
- **Do not plead, exhort, advertise, evangelize, or promise great things.** These are the far end of the "how the significant other is introduced" variable, and are tied to sudden, magical, short-lived change.
- **Do not enter uninvited.** The opposite pole, endorsed: the significant other "enters the system only on unsolicited invitation and with a contract either verbal or written that comes closer to defining the reality of the situation."
- **Do not become emotionally overinvolved with the patient / wrap into the family emotionally.** Named as the unhealthy aspect of countertransference and as the family "wrapping itself around the therapist."
- **Do not intensify the therapist-patient relationship** — that drives the patient's withdrawal from the family and makes the family more disturbed.
- **Do not resolve or talk out conflict as a goal.** "Conventional therapy is designed to resolve, or talk out, conflict. This does accomplish the goal of reducing the conflict of that moment, but it can also **rob the individual of his budding effort to achieve a bit more differentiation** from the family togetherness."
- **Do not let the family passively wait for you to change them.**
- **Do not simply tell a family to go back to the family of origin.** "Merely telling a family to go back to the family of origin is of little help."
- **Do not send an anxious person back to the family without coaching.** "Some people are very anxious about returning to their families. Without systems coaching, they can make the problem worse."
- **Do not ask or answer "why."**
- **Do not treat the free expression of feelings as the goal or as evidence of high functioning.** Bowen criticises exactly this as the implicit theory of team/group family therapy ("emotional illness is the product of suppressed feeling and poor communication, that treatment is the free expression of feelings and open communication").
- **Do not accept the disappearance of symptoms as evidence of change.** Named as a common professional error; contrasted with "basic, documentable, structural alteration in the underlying situation that gave rise to the symptoms."
- **Do not attempt to use the scale without knowing the concept.** "Knowledge of the concept permits the easy development of all kinds of research instruments, but to attempt to use the scale without knowledge of the concept can result in chaos" — and "To try to apply it without knowing it is an exercise in futility."
- **Do not let the family member act on the therapist's conviction instead of his own.**
- **Do not assume you have stayed out.** "One never reaches the point of not being vulnerable to automatically falling back into the emotionality of transference." "No one ever stays outside." And, of the systems method generally: "no one ever achieves more than partial success at it."

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition on the therapist:** enough knowledge of triangles to read the emotional responsiveness and control his own participation. Bowen explicitly places the breakthrough here: "it was knowledge of triangles that provided the important breakthrough in the effort to stay outside the emotional complex." He also states this expertise can be **routinely reproduced in a good percentage of professional trainees** — in contrast with conventional training, whose result depends on intuitive and intangible qualities in the trainee.
- **Precondition on the therapist's stance under anxiety:** "When anxiety is high, even the most disciplined systems thinker will automatically revert to cause and effect thinking and why explanations." So the discipline must be maintained, not assumed.
- **Entry:** on unsolicited invitation, with a contract, verbal or written, that comes closer to defining the reality of the situation.
- **Order of the basic method:** contact with the two most significant members → therapist relatively outside the central triangle → the fusion between them slowly begins to resolve → all other family members automatically change in relation to the two parents in the home setting.
- **Cutoff first, when present.** If the symptomatic nuclear family is cut off from the family of origin, family therapy can cycle for a long time without improvement. Reestablishing contact comes first: contact → anxiety subsides → symptoms become softer and more manageable → **then** family therapy can become productive.
- **Coaching precedes return.** Systems coaching must precede the return to the family of origin; without it an anxious person can make the problem worse, and a person who returns and continues the same emotional isolation accomplishes nothing.
- **The differentiating step must come from the member's own conviction** before it is attempted — otherwise it is a self-defeating togetherness with the therapist.
- **After a step is taken, expect the group's vigorous disapproval**; the therapist's help is most needed at that point, because without it the member falls back into togetherness to get emotional harmony for the moment.
- **Order of propagation:** one motivated member masters the step → other family members automatically take similar steps.
- **Scale of change and its time course.** "I believe that the level of differentiation of a person is largely determined by the time he leaves the parental family and he attempts a life of his own. Thereafter, he tends to replicate the lifestyle from the parental family in all future relationships." "It is not possible ever to make more than minor changes in one's basic level of self; but from clinical experience I can say it is possible to make slow changes, and each small change results in the new world of a different lifestyle."
- **The critical stage, stated as a threshold:** "the critical stage is passed when the individual can begin to know the difference between emotional functioning and intellectual functioning, and when he has developed ways for using the knowledge for solving future problems in a **lifelong effort of his own**."
- **Substitution rule:** the process can proceed with any third person who can keep self detriangled — but such an outside relationship would be difficult to find.
- **Content-independence:** "The process can proceed regardless of content or subject matter discussed. The critical issue is the emotional reactiveness between the spouses, and the ability of the therapist to keep self relatively detriangled."
- **Dose-independence:** the method "is as successful as other methods in short-term crisis situations," and the calm, low-keyed, detriangling approach "is more effective with a single appointment or with many."
- **Superiority claim about the family of origin route:** "a spouse who can do a reasonable job at differentiating self in his parental family will have accomplished more than if he was involved in regular family therapy with self and his spouse."
- **Assessment timing:** differentiation is difficult to assess during calm periods; estimates come from the average functional level across stress and calm; the real test comes when the person is again subjected to chronic severe stress.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **The intense therapeutic relationship drains the family and disturbs it.** As therapist and patient become more intensely involved, the patient withdraws from emotional contact with the family and **the family becomes more disturbed**.
- **The therapist becomes another triangle.** "Without some special effort, it is easy for the family to wrap itself around the therapist emotionally, install the therapist in an all-important position, hold the therapist responsible for success or failure, and passively wait for the therapist to change the family."
- **The observer fuses and believes he did not.** Neutral home observers are fused as soon as they enter, the family automatically becomes different, and their belief in their objectivity is erroneous.
- **The outsider can become the triangle that breaks the marriage.** The movie crew case: it "automatically became a significant other which helped propel the parents toward divorce" — with the qualifying note that "This situation might have found another triangle that would have served the same triangle force."
- **Symptom relief mistaken for change.** In the parent-parent-adolescent period: the passive father became less passive, the aggressive mother less aggressive, the symptomatic child asymptomatic, and the family terminated after thirty to forty appointments praising the good result — **"In my opinion, the family had not changed."**
- **Conflict resolution robs a differentiation step.** Talking out the conflict reduces this moment's conflict and can cost the member his budding differentiation.
- **Falling back into togetherness.** Without help at the point of group disapproval, the differentiating one falls back to get emotional harmony for the moment.
- **Borrowed differentiation.** Attempting it without one's own conviction = blindly following the therapist and being caught in a self-defeating togetherness with him.
- **Cyclical long-term therapy without improvement** in families cut off from the extended family.
- **Naive return to the family of origin** — either worsening the problem (anxious return without coaching) or accomplishing nothing (returning and continuing the same emotional isolation).
- **High-emotionality change does not last.** Messianic qualities, exaggerated promises, and evangelism → sudden and magical change that is less likely to be long term.
- **Reversion under anxiety.** Even the most disciplined systems thinker will automatically revert to cause-and-effect thinking and why explanations when anxiety is high.
- **The scale used without the concept "can result in chaos."**
- **The training failure mode.** Training that emphasises the therapeutic relationship, learning about one's own emotional problems, and management of self in relation to the patient — with a few didactic lectures on theory appended — produces professionals oriented around the therapeutic relationship, assuming they know the nature and origin of emotional illness, unable to question the theoretical base, and assuming the therapeutic relationship is the basic treatment.
- **The eclectic failure mode.** "the eclectic shifting may be more for the needs of therapist than the patient."
- **The ingroup failure mode.** A profession that becomes an emotional ingroup cannot generate new knowledge from within nor admit knowledge from without that might threaten the dogma; the result is splintering and resplintering.
- **The general failure mode of the field:** "The separation between theory and therapy in most family therapy is far greater than with individual therapy" — the "unstructured state of chaos."

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

Each rule of technique converted into the claim about family functioning it presupposes. **This is the section to test.**

1. **Rule:** treat only the two spouses, exclude the child. → **Assumes:** the child's symptom is a downstream product of emotional reactiveness between the two most significant adults; the symptomatic member is not the locus of the problem and does not have to be present for it to change. Corollary assumption: **change at the central dyad propagates outward automatically** — "all other family members will automatically change in relation to the two parents in the home setting." This is a strong claim about direction of causal flow *and* about automatic propagation without direct intervention on the other nodes.
2. **Rule:** the therapist must stay outside the central triangle while staying in viable emotional contact. → **Assumes:** the pathological pattern is *stabilized by the participation of a third party*, and any available outsider will be automatically recruited into that role. Remove the third party's participation while keeping the connection, and the twosome's fusion has nowhere to discharge and must resolve. Two separate assumptions here: (a) triangling is automatic and non-optional for a connected outsider; (b) an outsider's *non-participation* is itself an active force on the twosome.
3. **Rule:** contact must be *maintained* while remaining outside; withdrawal is not a solution. → **Assumes:** distance and cutoff are not neutral — they are themselves a mechanism of the pathology (the cutoff concept), so a therapist who withdraws to stay objective would be replicating the family's own failed solution. Only contact-without-fusion is a new input.
4. **Rule:** coach one motivated member. → **Assumes:** the family is a *coupled* system in which one node's change forces re-equilibration in every other node; and that motivation is unevenly distributed, so the system is addressable through whoever is available and willing. It also assumes the family cannot be moved by addressing everybody at once.
5. **Rule:** refuse assigned importance; charge average fees; promise no benefit but the family's own effort. → **Assumes:** the *emotional loading* of the helping relationship, not its content, determines the durability of the result; and that assigning overimportance to an outsider is itself a de-selfing exchange in which the family gives up self to the therapist. High-emotionality help therefore produces change that reverses; low-emotionality reality-based help produces slow, structural change.
6. **Rule:** do not resolve or talk out conflict. → **Assumes:** conflict at a given moment can be the *visible surface of a differentiation step in progress* — the group's disapproval reaction. Removing the conflict removes the pressure that the step was generating and returns the system to its prior equilibrium. Implies conflict is not a defect to be minimized but a signal whose meaning depends on what produced it.
7. **Rule:** expect vigorous group disapproval after any step toward differentiation, and be present for it. → **Assumes:** the family maintains an **active homeostatic force** ("togetherness forces") proportional to the deviation, that acts on the deviator specifically, and that is strong enough to reverse the step unless externally counterweighted. Also assumes the reaction is *predictable in timing* — it follows the step.
8. **Rule:** open the cutoff before, or as a precondition of, productive family therapy. → **Assumes:** the intensity of nuclear-family symptoms is gated by the degree of emotional isolation from the extended family — that is, an *unmodelled external variable* controls the nuclear family's anxiety level. It follows that a nuclear family treated in isolation from its extended family is being treated with a controlling variable held at a bad value.
9. **Rule:** coaching must precede return to the family of origin, and mere physical return accomplishes nothing. → **Assumes:** contact is not measured by presence but by *functional position*; a person can be physically present and emotionally cut off. And it assumes the family of origin will re-exert its old pull on an unprepared returner, i.e. the old pattern is stored in the relationship system, not only in the person.
10. **Rule:** differentiating self in the parental family accomplishes more than regular couple therapy. → **Assumes:** the *original* attachment is the load-bearing one; the marriage merely replicates it. Implies that a person's current relationship problems are a re-expression of the unresolved parental attachment, so working the original is more efficient than working the copy.
11. **Rule:** never ask why; stay on what/how/when/where. → **Assumes:** the family's own causal explanations are products of the emotional system rather than reports about it — the intellect "attempts to explain or justify [emotionally directed action] after the action," producing "an instant excuse that appears most acceptable to self and others." Therefore explanation-content carries no information about mechanism and pursuing it entangles the therapist in the emotional process.
12. **Rule:** separate the fact of the communication from its content ("that man talks is a fact, what he says is not necessarily factual"). → **Assumes:** the observable, countable, positional facts of a relationship system (who talks to whom, who adapts, who is outside) carry the causal signal, and the semantic content does not. This is the strongest methodological assumption in the chapter and the one most directly usable as a modelling constraint.
13. **Rule:** the process proceeds regardless of content or subject matter discussed. → **Assumes:** the state variable being moved is *emotional reactiveness between the spouses*, and it is orthogonal to the topic. Content is a carrier, not a cause.
14. **Rule:** assess adaptiveness from long-term functioning posture, not verbal reports; each spouse believes he gives in more. → **Assumes:** self-report about one's own position in the exchange is systematically biased, in a specific direction (toward claiming the adaptive side). The functional position is a fact; the account of it is not.
15. **Rule:** estimate differentiation from the average functional level across stress and calm, never from calm alone; and only from following a life pattern over long periods. → **Assumes:** a single observation is uninformative because functional level is a fast variable riding on a slow one. The family's *state* is not observable at a point in time.
16. **Rule:** the real test is chronic severe stress. → **Assumes:** the differentiation variable is only identifiable through the system's response to load — the system is diagnostically silent at low anxiety, because at low enough anxiety anyone can appear normal.
17. **Rule:** take a sibling-by-sibling life-adjustment history to identify the triangled child. → **Assumes:** the projection process leaves a **permanent, observable, differential trace** across siblings — one child measurably worse off — and that this trace is stable enough to read decades later, including in generations for which only hearsay is available.
18. **Rule:** use deviation from Toman's expected sibling profile as an instrument. → **Assumes:** sibling position produces a *predictable baseline personality*, so any departure from it is attributable to the projection process rather than to individual variation. This makes structural position a measuring rod for a non-structural quantity.
19. **Rule:** the method is as effective in one appointment as in many, and a calm low-keyed approach beats an active engaged one in crisis. → **Assumes:** the therapeutic effect is not cumulative dose of relationship but a *change in the field* the family is operating in. Contradicts any dose-response model of therapy.
20. **Rule:** aim only at a microscopic step; more than minor change in basic level is not possible. → **Assumes:** the basic level of differentiation is largely fixed by the time the person leaves the parental family, and thereafter the person replicates the parental-family lifestyle in all future relationships. Everything achievable in therapy is either functional-level improvement or a slow, small, real shift in the basic level.
21. **Rule:** the member must act from his own conviction. → **Assumes:** a position taken to please the therapist is *pseudo-self* by construction (acquired under relationship pressure), so it is negotiable and will be traded back. Only positions arrived at by the member's own reasoning are solid self and survive anxiety.
22. **Rule:** direct effort at decreasing anxiety as a legitimate independent target. → **Assumes:** anxiety and differentiation are separable variables, and symptom intensity is a function of both — so the same family can be made materially less symptomatic without any change in basic level.
23. **Rule:** the family will triangle in police and social agencies at high tension, and successful externalization looks like outside workers in conflict while the family is calm. → **Assumes:** the tension is conserved and displaced rather than dissipated; an observer can therefore locate the current active triangle by looking for **where the conflict is being carried**, including outside the family boundary.
24. **Rule:** the therapist may be substituted by any third person who can keep self detriangled. → **Assumes:** nothing about the therapist's professional role is load-bearing; only the structural position (connected, outside, non-reactive) matters. The therapist is a *position*, not a person.
25. **[p2] Rule:** reduce assigned importance, but stay in viable contact and expect the family member to invest "a reasonable amount of thinking-feeling energy… without becoming too emotionally preoccupied." → **Assumes:** the outside relationship's effect on the family is a **non-monotonic function of its intensity** — too weak and it does not modify the system, too strong and it drains the member out of the family and raises family tension. The therapist is therefore tuning an intensity, not minimising one. This is the same inverted-U Ch22 states for issue intensity, arrived at through a different variable.
26. **[p2] Rule:** never estimate differentiation in calm; but the differentiating work of building principles happens in calm. → **Assumes:** calm periods are **productive but unobservable** and anxious periods are **observable but unproductive**. The variable the coach is trying to move and the variable it can measure are legible at opposite phases of the cycle — which makes any within-episode feedback loop for the coach structurally unavailable.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Generations to schizophrenia, revised upward by roughly threefold.** "Once I said it required at least three generations to produce a child so impaired he would collapse into schizophrenia… However, since I now know the process can slow down or stay static a generation or two, I would now say that it would require perhaps eight to ten generations." Bowen names the reason for the revision: the earlier figure assumed a starting point of fairly good surface functioning and a process proceeding at maximum speed.
- **The 75–100 range is quietly withdrawn.** Having presented it in previous papers, Bowen now says it is "more hypothetical than real, and… conveys an erroneous impression of the human phenomenon to concretistic thinkers who are searching for another instrument to measure human functioning," and declines to pursue it. The four-band profile structure is thereby effectively reduced to three usable bands.
- **The scale itself is de-emphasized as an instrument while retained as a concept.** "The theoretical concept is most important." Bowen reports that presenting a schematic and calling it a *scale* produced hundreds of letters requesting copies of "the scale" from people who had grasped neither the concept nor the variables governing functional levels — and that **"The letters slowed down my effort to develop a more definite scale that could be used clinically."** A stated retreat caused by misuse. **[p2] The verb is "slowed down," not "abandoned."** This chapter reports a delay; Ch17 is the chapter that reports research on scale levels being **stopped**. Pass 1's `M.` and the ledger's L16.1 both wrote "abandoned" and attributed it here — see P1.
- **[p2] Two internal date conflicts, unflagged at pass 1.** Triangles: "I began work on this basic concept in 1955" (concept section) versus "started in 1957 when it was called the 'interdependent triad'" (history section). Societal regression: "had been rather well defined by 1972" versus "was first defined in 1972."
- **[p2] A garbled antecedent in the projection section.** "There are a few families in which most of the undifferentiation goes into marital conflict, essentially none to sickness in a spouse, and relatively small amounts to the children. **The most striking examples of this** have been in families with autistic, or severely impaired, children in which there is little marital conflict, both spouses are healthy, and the full weight on the undifferentiation is directed to a single, maximally impaired child." The second sentence describes the *opposite* allocation from the first. The only coherent reading is that "this" refers to **concentration in a single area**, not to the conflict-dominant case. Pass 1 read the autism sentence as a standalone observation, which is the defensible reading, but the ambiguity should travel with it.
- **[p2] A stated claim of predictive accuracy with no procedure attached.** "Based on experience in family research, the predictions of ten to twenty years ago have been rather accurate" — offered immediately after conceding "No one person lives long enough to check the accuracy of predictions into the future." No cases, no counts, no criterion. See P5/Q-VALIDATION.
- **"Undifferentiated family ego mass" is disowned as off-plan.** Bowen states it was an assemblage of words from conventional theory that did not conform to his plan of using biology-consistent concepts, and that he used it for a few years only because more people could hear the concept in those words. It nonetheless reappears here, extended, as "undifferentiated societal ego mass."
- **The renaming to "the Bowen theory" reverses a stated principle.** "I have long opposed the use of proper names in terminology, but in order to denote the specificity that is built into this family systems theory, I am now calling it the Bowen theory."
- **The prediction about family therapy's trajectory is recorded as wrong.** Bowen called the post-1957 chaos "healthy" in the belief that new therapists would discover the discrepancies in conventional theory and that the conceptual dilemma would lead to new concepts and ultimately a new theory. "This did not occur. I did not realize the degree of therapeutic zeal that makes psychiatrists oblivious to theory."
- **"Family therapy" was not the plan.** "I had built a method of individual therapy into my research design for studying the families. Within six months there was evidence that some method of therapy for family members together was indicated. I had never heard of family therapy. Against the strong theoretical and clinical admonitions of the time, I followed the dictates of the research evidence…"
- **The initial mother-child symbiosis hypothesis is superseded within the research itself.** "Very quickly it became apparent that the mother-child relationship was a dependent fragment of the larger family unit."
- **The schizophrenia-specific reading of the patterns is superseded.** Patterns first thought typical of schizophrenia turned out to be present in neurotic-level and normal families; the intensity tracks anxiety of the moment, not diagnosis.
- **"Theory" was withheld in 1957 and granted in 1963.** Jackson urged Bowen to use *theory* in the 1957 paper; Bowen refused "on the basis that it was no more than a concept in a much larger field," and only used *theory* when the six interlocking concepts were assembled by 1963.
- **Emotional cutoff is described as not really new** — "merely a refinement and a new emphasis of former theoretical principles" — yet is given full concept status. A revision of taxonomy rather than of content, stated as such.
- **Family systems theory vs. general systems theory.** Bowen states flatly he knew nothing of general systems theory when his theory was developed, having attended one Bertalanffy lecture in the 1940s that he did not understand and one Wiener lecture "perhaps a little more understandable"; and that "There are those who believe family systems theory was developed from general systems theory, in spite of my explanations to the contrary."
- **A minor internal tension worth flagging:** Bowen says he "carefully excluded all concepts that dealt with inanimate things" — yet in the same chapter he uses the brain-center model, the screening process "which might be biochemical," and quantitative absorption language for undifferentiation. He also acknowledges "There are a few minor exceptions to this over-all plan, which will be mentioned later," without listing them.
- **Contradiction between the significant-other law and the therapeutic prescription.** The law says an introduced significant other *modifies* the system; the prescription says the therapist must avoid being a significant other in the assigned-importance sense. Bowen resolves this by making the *kind* of significance the variable (reality/knowledge vs. assigned importance) rather than treating significance as binary — but the two statements sit in tension as written.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- **On the scale bands:** "In previous papers I have described a level of 75 to 100, which is more hypothetical than real."
- **On 100:** "a hypothetical notion of perfection to which man might evolve **if** his evolutionary change goes in that direction."
- **On the projection process's selection rule:** "There are **suggestions** about the way children become the objects of the projection process. On a **simplistic** level…"
- **On exceptions:** "There **may be some exceptions** to the process described here, but the over-all patterns are clear, and the theory accounts for the exceptions."
- **On the emotional system:** "The emotional system is **hypothesized** to be part of the instinctual forces that govern automatic functions."
- **On the brain model:** "It is **reasonably accurate** to compare… I **conceive of** one brain center… The screening process, **which might be biochemical**…"
- **On the sixth sense:** "In addition, there is a sixth sense that **can include** extrasensory perception." — asserted without qualification but without evidence.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The commitment threshold for fusion is a discrete event, not a gradient.** "It is as if the fusion does not develop as long as they still have an option to terminate the relationship." Fusion is triggered by the removal of the exit option — engagement, wedding, or first shared home — not by duration or intimacy. This is a state transition with a named trigger.
- **Turn-on and turn-off are the same thing.** "the degree of emotional turn on or turn off (both equal in emotional systems terms) the mother feels for the child." Emotional intensity is unsigned. Same point made for marital conflict: "The intensity of the anger and negative feeling in the conflict is as intense as the positive feeling."
- **Acting the opposite does not work.** "This is an automatic emotional process that is not changed by acting the opposite." A direct constraint on any intervention modelled as behaviour-flipping.
- **The exchange is conserved and equal.** "In any exchange, one gives up a little self to the other, who gains an equal amount." A zero-sum conservation statement about self within a dyad, distinct from the quantitative-undifferentiation statement about the nuclear family.
- **Two distinct conserved-quantity statements coexist**: (a) self traded between two people in equal and opposite amounts; (b) "a quantitative amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed in the nuclear family" distributed across marital conflict / spouse dysfunction / child projection, where more in one means less in the others. These are different currencies with different conservation domains.
- **The de-selfed collapse has a specific endpoint.** The adaptive spouse can become so de-selfed as to be "no longer able to make decisions and collapse in selfless dysfunction — psychosis or chronic physical illness." Physical and psychiatric outcomes are treated as interchangeable expressions of one variable.
- **Symptom class is chosen by cutoff style.** The stayer produces *internalized* symptoms (physical illness, depression); the runner produces *impulsive behaviour* (impulsive marriage, serial marriage, nomadism). A rule mapping a relational strategy to a symptom class.
- **The dysfunctional-spouse marriage is highly stable and hard to break.** Enduring marriages; underfunctioner grateful, overfunctioner not complaining; "Divorce is almost impossible in these marriages unless the dysfunction is also mixed with marital conflict." An attractor, not a transient.
- **Recovery on the death of the overfunctioner, and pattern replication on remarriage.** Both reported as observed, and both are direct tests of a relational rather than individual model of chronic disability.
- **Mixing mechanisms is protective.** "The more the problem shifts from one area to another the less chance the process will be crippling in any single area." Concentration, not quantity, determines severity of a given outcome.
- **The always-lose child eventually volunteers for the position.** A role becomes self-maintaining over time — the system's fixed roles become preferences.
- **Successful externalization is observable from outside.** "A successful externalization of the tension occurs when outside workers are in conflict about the family while the family is calmer." A directly operational, falsifiable observation.
- **Removing one member of a conflictual pair does not remove the conflict.** The administrative example: fire one of the pair and the conflict erupts in another twosome. Direct evidence that conflict is a property of the field, not the pair.
- **The uncomfortable one moves.** "Moderate tension states in the twosome are characteristically felt by one, while the other is oblivious. It is the uncomfortable one who initiates a new equilibrium." A concrete rule for who acts, and a claim that the discomfort is asymmetric within a dyad.
- **The outside position is preferred under stress and avoided under calm.** The valence of the outside position **flips** with the anxiety level. This is a sign change, not a magnitude change.
- **Revolution defined as a convulsion that prevents change** — polarized opposites, each side's position determined by the other's, neither capable of an independent position. Structurally the same as the conflictual marriage.
- **"Rugged individualism" is a pretend posture,** the exaggerated struggle against emotional fusion — explicitly *not* differentiation. Differentiation is not distance; "The differentiated person is always aware of others and the relationship system around him."
- **Anxiety is infectious** and can spread rapidly through a family or through society. A transmission process independent of the exchange and projection processes.
- **The family average has individual spread.** "There is a kind of average level of differentiation for the family which has certain minor levels of difference in individuals within the family."
- **Runners and stayers are equally dependent:** "They both need emotional closeness, but they are allergic to it."
- **The closeness–distance cycle in the 25–50 range.** Pursuit of the ideal close relationship → closeness achieved → increased fusion → reaction with distance and alienation → stimulates another closeness cycle. Failing to achieve closeness → withdrawal and depression, or pursuit of closeness in another relationship. A named oscillator with a named failure branch.
- **Intellect can be intact in impersonal domains while personal life is chaotic** — brilliant academically, emotionally-directed personal life. Domain-specific decoupling of the intellectual system.
- **Substitute families (communes, living-together arrangements) are very unstable** because they are composed entirely of people who ran away, so cutoff is their only tension-handling repertoire. A composition effect: a group of runners cannot hold tension.
- **Schizophrenia framed phenomenologically as a species-level process** — the weakness fixed in one person, less likely to marry and reproduce, more likely to die young.
- **The lowest range extends beyond the family boundary.** "The emotional fusion is so intense that the variables extend beyond the undifferentiated family ego mass into the undifferentiated societal ego mass." The family is not a closed system at low differentiation.
- **[p2] Overt feeling is loudest in the middle of the scale.** The 25–50 group "have the most intense versions of overt feeling," is "in a lifelong quest for the ideal relationship with emotional closeness to others and direct, open communication of feelings," and responds "openly with direct expression of feeling or impulsive action." The 0–25 group is feeling-*dominated* but its energy goes into keeping relationships in balance, and the 50–75 group states convictions "calmly." So the observable signal a naive readout would use — visible emotionality — is a **hump function** of differentiation, not a decreasing one.
- **[p2] The de-selfed position can be rewarded by the environment.** "A no-self person who is adept at pleasing his boss may make a better employee than one who has a self." Low differentiation is not uniformly penalised; a system can select for it.
- **[p2] Solid self is manufactured in calm and expended under anxiety.** Beliefs and convictions are "employed logical reasoning to develop… in periods of calm" and then "use[d] to overrule the emotional system in situations of anxiety and panic." The stock and the drawdown occur at opposite phases of the anxiety cycle.
- **[p2] Differentiation has its own instinctual drive.** "The instinctual force toward differentiation is built into the organism, just as are the emotional forces that oppose it." Two opposed built-in forces, not one force and one absence.
- **[p2] Authority-citing as a diagnostic marker of the 25–50 band.** They "avoid 'I think,' or 'I believe,' positions by using another person or body of knowledge as their authority," producing statements like "The rule says…" or "Science has proved…", "taking information out of context to make their points." A concrete observable tied to a specific band.
- **[p2] Rebel and disciple are the same posture.** "The pseudo-self may be a conforming disciple… or, when frustrated, he can assume the opposite posture as a rebel or revolutionary person. The rebel is lacking a self of his own. His pseudo-self posture is merely the exact opposite of the majority viewpoint." Conformity and opposition are one variable with a sign, not two states.
- **The observational-blindness claim.** "man's failure to see what is in front of him unless it fits his theoretical frame of reference," with the Darwin/prehistoric-bones analogy, and the deliberate research-staff exercise of banning conventional psychiatric terminology in favour of simple descriptive words. The discovery in that exercise: "we had discovered the degree to which well-trained professional people use the same terms differently, while assuming that everyone understands them the same way."

  cooperative team"; the intellect "can hold its own and function autonomously without being dominated
  by the emotional system when anxiety increases"; solid self "on most of the essential issues in life,"
  built in calm and used "to overrule the emotional system in situations of anxiety and panic"; the
  lower part "know there is a better way; but intellect is poorly formed, and they end up following life
  courses similar to those below 50"; the upper part are "no longer a prisoner of the emotional-feeling
  world," can "participate fully in emotional events knowing that they can extricate themselves with
  logical reasoning," may have "periods of laxness" on emotional autopilot but "when trouble develops
  they can take over"; less relationship directed; able to state convictions "calmly without attacking
  the beliefs of others or without having to defend their own"; "They marry spouses with equal levels of
  differentiation"; the marriage "a functioning partnership" with "the full range of emotional intimacy
  without either being de-selfed"; children permitted "to grow and develop their own autonomous selfs";
  "remarkably free from the full range of human problems."
- *The threshold at 50, in full:* "In people below 50, the emotional system tells the intellectual system
  what to think and say, and which decisions to make in critical situations. The intellect is a pretend
  intellect. The emotional system permits the intellect to go off into a corner and think about distant
  things as long as it does not interfere in joint decisions that affect the total life course. Above 50,
  the intellectual system is sufficiently developed to begin making a few decisions of its own." Plus:
  "People above 50 have developed a reasonable level of solid self on most of the essential issues in
  life."
- *75–100:* **confirmed withdrawn.** "In previous papers I have described a level of 75 to 100, which is
  more hypothetical than real, and which conveys an erroneous impression of the human phenomenon to
  concretistic thinkers who are searching for another instrument to measure human functioning. Rather
  than pursue the hypothesis about the upper extremes of differentiation, I shall instead make some
  general comments about differentiation." No profile is written for it in this chapter.
- *Ch21 comparison:* **confirmed** — Ch16 has the four-range scheme, Ch21 does not. But Ch16 does not
  place its introduction between 1970 and 1976; it places it "ten years" back, in the paper that first
  presented the scale. That conflict is recorded in P3 and is not resolvable from this chapter.
- *The clinical-scale claim:* **NOT confirmed as stated.** Bowen did not say he abandoned it. Verbatim:
  "The schematic framework and the use of the term *scale* resulted in hundreds of letters requesting

  functional levels of differentiations. **The letters slowed down my effort to develop a more definite
  scale that could be used clinically.**" What he does say flatly: "The theoretical concept is most
  important," "to attempt to use the scale without knowledge of the concept can result in chaos," and
  "To try to apply it without knowing it is an exercise in futility."

**Q-SINK — how many, named how, and is distance a fourth?** **Three**, and **no**. Named: "**marital
conflict**; **sickness or dysfunction in one spouse**; and **projection of the problems to children**"
(the third also headed "Impairment of one or more children"). Emotional distance is named separately and
placed outside the set: "The most universal mechanism is emotional distance from each other. It is present
in all marriages to some degree, and in a high percentage of marriages to a major degree. **Other than the
emotional distance**, there are three major areas…" The conservation statement is made twice, both times
over the three: "a quantitative amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed in the nuclear family, which
may be focused largely in one area or distributed in varying amounts to all three areas," and "a definite
amount of undifferentiation to be absorbed by marital conflict, sickness in a spouse, and projection to
the children." So distance is **always-on but not a sink** — it is the first-line handling of fusion
symptoms, prior to and outside the absorbed quantity. Ch16 (1976) therefore does not carry forward Ch18's
(1973) four-channel scheme.

**Q-CONCEPTS — enumeration, order, names, and the 1975 additions.** Confirmed. Order of presentation:
**1. Differentiation of self · 2. Triangles · 3. Nuclear family emotional system** (with three subsections:
marital conflict; dysfunction in one spouse; impairment of one or more children) **· 4. Family projection
process · 5. Emotional cutoff · 6. Multigenerational transmission process · 7. Sibling position ·
8. Societal regression.** Count before: **six** — "By 1963, these six interlocking concepts were
sufficiently defined that I was willing to put all six together into family systems theory… The six
concepts were finally published as a coherent, theoretical system in 1966." Count after: **eight** —
"Finally, in 1975, two new concepts were added. The first, the emotional cutoff, was merely a refinement
and a new emphasis of former theoretical principles. The last and eighth concept, societal regression, had
been rather well defined by 1972, and was finally added as a separate concept in 1975." Cutoff's stated
reason for promotion: "to include details not stated elsewhere, and to have a separate concept for
emotional process between the generations." Bowen also partitions the eight — "Three of the theory's eight
concepts apply to over-all characteristics of the family. The other five focus on details within certain
areas of the family" — **without saying which three**. Matches Ch21's six exactly.

**Q-MOVEMENT — does Ch16 resolve Ch21's contradiction?** **Partially, and by changing both halves.**
Verbatim: "I believe that the level of differentiation of a person is largely determined by the time he
leaves the parental family and he attempts a life of his own. Thereafter, he tends to replicate the
lifestyle from the parental family in all future relationships. **It is not possible ever to make more than
minor changes in one's basic level of self; but from clinical experience I can say it is possible to make
slow changes, and each small change results in the new world of a different lifestyle.**"
Three deltas against Ch21: (a) the determination point moves **earlier** — leaving the parental family,
not consolidation in a marriage — which agrees with Ch17; (b) the "only shift is a functional shift" half
is **withdrawn**: basic-level change is possible, merely bounded and slow; (c) Ch21's irreversibility
("can never return to the former level") is **not restated**, so the ratchet is unsupported by this
chapter. How far in a lifetime: **no more than "minor" changes**, unquantified — there is no number and no
rate anywhere in this chapter. The stated endpoint is qualitative: "the critical stage is passed when the
individual can begin to know the difference between emotional functioning and intellectual functioning,
and when he has developed ways for using the knowledge for solving future problems in a lifelong effort of
his own."

**Q-SIBLING — where does it enter, and what does the chapter do with it?** It enters as **concept 7**, an
adaptation of Toman's *Family Constellation* (1961); historically it "had been poorly defined since the
late 1950s, but it had to wait until Toman… provided structure." Bowen notes Toman "had worked from an
individual frame of reference and only with normal families, but he had ordered his data in a way no one
else had done." Four uses, and the chapter's own order puts selection first: (1) **selection** — "a new
dimension toward understanding how a particular child is chosen as the object of the family projection
process"; (2) **measurement by deviation** — "The degree to which a personality profile fits with normal
provides a way to understand the level of differentiation and the direction of the projection process from
generation to generation," with three worked readings ("if an oldest turns out to be more like a youngest,
that is strong evidence that he was the most triangled child. If an oldest is an autocrat, that is strong
evidence of a moderate level of impaired functioning. An oldest who functions calmly and responsibly is
good evidence of a better level of differentiation"); (3) **historical reconstruction** — "assemble
reliable presumptive personality profiles on people in past generations on whom verifiable facts are
missing"; (4) **prediction** — "how spouses will handle the mix in a marriage, and how they will handle
their effort in family therapy." Weight assigned: "**no single piece of data is more important than
knowing the sibling position of people in the present and past generations.**" Ten basic profiles.

**Q-GENERATIONS — transmission rule, rate, and the eight-to-ten figure.** All three confirmed verbatim.
Rule: "In any nuclear family, there is one child who is the primary object of the family projection
process. This child emerges with a lower level of differentiation than the parents and does less well in
life. Other children, who are minimally involved with the parents, emerge with about the same levels of
differentiation as the parents. Those who grow up relatively outside the family emotional process develop
better levels of differentiation than the parents." Rate: "The process may go rapidly a few generations,
remain static for a generation or so, and then speed up again" — plus reversal at both extremes ("A family
at a highest level of differentiation can have one child who starts down the scale. A family at the lowest
level can have a child who starts up the scale"). Revision: "Once I said it required at least three
generations to produce a child so impaired he would collapse into schizophrenia. That was based on the
notion of a starting point with fairly good surface functioning and a process that proceeded at maximum
speed through the generations. However, since I now know the process can slow down or stay static a
generation or two, I would now say that it would require perhaps **eight to ten generations**." With the
intermediate checkpoint: "If a family encounters severe stress in perhaps the fifth or sixth generation of
a ten-generation process, it may produce a social failure who is less impaired than the schizophrenic
person." **[p2] One qualifier pass 1 missed:** the modal projection outcome is sub-clinical — the pattern
is the same "whether the eventual impairment in the child be one that leads to serious lifelong
dysfunction, or one that never develops serious symptoms and is never diagnosed."

**Q-VALIDATION — any external validation or measurement procedure?** **None, and the chapter argues
against the idea.** The nearest positive statements are (a) "There are several rather clear variables that
determine the way the family functions in the present generation, which can be measured and validated by
direct observation" — no variables named, no procedure, no instrument; (b) "Knowledge of the concept
permits the easy development of all kinds of research instruments" — none offered; (c) "Based on
experience in family research, the predictions of ten to twenty years ago have been rather accurate" — no
cases, counts or criterion, offered immediately after conceding that no one lives long enough to check a
prediction. Against these stand explicit obstacles Bowen states himself: "There is no way to chi square a
feeling and make it qualify as a scientific fact"; "research should be directed at making theoretical
contact with other fields, rather than applying the scientific method to subjective human data"; "it is
impossible to estimate functional levels of differentiation except from following a life pattern over long
periods"; and the admission that the solid/pseudo-self boundary is hard to locate, which "also applies to
therapists, mental health professionals, and researchers who may attempt to estimate the level of
differentiation in themselves or in others." The only controlled procedure named anywhere in the chapter
is the placebo, and it is cited about medicine in general, not about anything of Bowen's: "the placebo
effect is routinely built into responsible research to check the emotional factor." **The scale has no
external validation in this chapter, which is the corpus's primary source for it.**

**Q-MATERIAL — any per-person material, economic or financial resource variable?** **No.** Money and
material circumstance appear only in four places, none of them a per-person state: as a *downstream
correlate* of low differentiation ("This group inherits a major portion of the world's serious health,
financial, and social problems"; "the poorly functioning people who make up most of the lower social
classes"); as something the concept explicitly **transcends** ("it also transcends categories such as
genius, social class, and cultural-ethnic differences"); as a therapist-side control (charging average
fees; insurance companies becoming "more lenient about providing payment"); and as a **societal**
exogenous driver ("decreasing supplies of food and raw materials"). There is no wealth, income, or
resource attribute on a person, and no mechanism by which material resources affect functioning.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Basic vs functional level of differentiation; functional shifts moment to moment, basic barely at all → `Person` → two fields, with functional as a fast variable riding on a slow one; only functional is observable at a point in time.
