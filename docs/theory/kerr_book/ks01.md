# KS01 — Systems Thinking

**Citation rule:** cite `KS01`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

---

## ⭐ KS01.1 — Systems thinking defined by the question it asks — `how, what, when, where`, never `why` `[T]`

> "**Cause-and-effect thinking asks, Why?**… Cause-and-effect thinking seeks the explanation for another's words and actions **within that individual**. In contrast, **systems thinking does not ask why; it asks how, what, when, and where.**"

*Model consequence:* `systems_perspective` (`M1.A.18`) gets an **operational definition** rather than only a readout. It is the disposition to attribute to **process** rather than to **person**. This is also the second source for `KB10` · K10.9's method statement — the same four question-words, from Bowen in 1980 and Kerr in 2019.

## ⭐ KS01.2 — "Functional facts" — the theory's unit of observation, named `[T]`

> "Systems thinking describes **functional facts** about relationship interactions… it is a functional fact that **if the husband says things that blame the wife, she retreats in reaction**; it is a functional fact that **if she retreats, he makes more blaming comments**. Functional facts are **easy to observe and document** once the observer is thinking systems. **Speculations about why the husband makes blaming remarks are subjective and unnecessary.**"

*Model consequence:* **the strongest available warrant for the event-log architecture.** A functional fact is exactly the model's `Event`: sender, target, move type, and the response it drew. Kerr states that these are the data and that motive is not needed. Fourth independent source for what replaced the withdrawn "content is noise" (`KB02` · K02.2, `KB08`, `KS00.12`).

## ⭐ KS01.3 — Blame is reacting to oneself; and self-blame is "an equal problem" `[T]`

> "It is **nonsense for them to blame each other** because they both help create changes in each other, to which they both then react — **blaming the other is like reacting to oneself. People see their own behavior in others' reactions.**"

> "It is easier, more automatic, to react to a situation and blame someone for it (**or blame oneself, which is an equal problem**)."

*Model consequence:* second statement in two chapters that self-blame counts equally (`KS00.2`). `M1.A.18a` **MUST** carry both axes: blame/praise *and* other/self. A detector reading only outward blame misses half.

## KS01.4 — One member shifting is sufficient, and leadership rotates `[T]` `[D]`

> "**Even if just one of the two people sees the 'dance,' it makes a major difference.** The change is both **small and profound.**"

> "**Invariably, one family member gets it first** and changes his or her response, and the others follow. **Ideally, the family leader will shift over time among family members.**"

*Model consequence:* corroborates `KB12` · K12.1 (one person at a time) from the latest source, and **adds a claim the model does not have: the leading position rotates.** `M1.D.4`'s leadership office is currently static per run. Candidate requirement — leadership as a *position that can transfer*, not an attribute.

## KS01.5 — Systems view does not absolve responsibility — stated against the hardest case `[T]`

> "Many people recoil at applying systems thinking to family situations in which **physical abuse** is occurring… **Viewing the family as a system does not absolve people of responsibility for their actions. Physical abuse is clearly an irresponsible behavior.** The anxiety-driven interactions that can culminate in physical abuse are well understood and **each member of the system plays a part in the process.**"

The worked case names both sides in the model's own terms: the wife "**losing functional differentiation** by trying to please, placate, forgive, and eventually distance"; the husband's "**equal lack of 'self'** manifested in overly controlling possessiveness."

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.9a`'s two axes for the fourth time**, and here explicitly at *equal* magnitude — "equal lack of 'self'" — with pleasing/placating/distancing on the inward axis and controlling possessiveness on the outward. `M11.C.19`'s equal-magnitude arm is not an artificial construction; it is the standard clinical picture.

**Also a framing requirement.** `M11.F` **MUST** carry: reciprocity **MUST NOT** be reported as shared culpability. The model computes participation in a process, which is not a moral finding, and no readout may present it as one.

## KS01.6 — Reactivity is not proportional to overt expression `[T]`

> "The process may be **overt or covert**. **People who place a premium on getting along can be just as reactive to getting approval as people who argue and yell a lot.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.0`'s consequence and `C5` (overt emotionality peaks mid-scale) confirmed from 2019. Third source. The get-along person and the argue-and-yell person are `M1.A.9a`'s two axes again.

## KS01.7 — The four social cues that mediate all interaction `[T]`

> "the primary social cues that mediate interactions between people are **sensitivities to approval, attention, expectations, and distress**. Such sensitivities are part of all people, but **the less 'self' people have, the more they are governed by reactions to such real or imagined cues** in the relationship system."

*Model consequence:* **a named, closed set of four appraisal channels**, with an explicit gain term on differentiation. The spec's `M4.C.1` raises anxiety by `intensity × conductance / functional_level` with no channel structure at all. This is a candidate decomposition — and it is a *closed* list, which the model likes. Note "**real or imagined**": the cue need not have been sent, which is `M4.C.5`'s perception-side reading generalised to all four.

## KS01.8 — Anxiety degrades systems thinking — the mechanism `[T]`

> "**If anxiety escalates, it becomes harder to do the type of thinking and reflection necessary to maintain a systems understanding** of a situation. Decades of clinical experiences have shown that, **with persistence, people can get better at it.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M4.C.4` confirmed** — `systems_perspective` falls with acute anxiety — plus the counterweight: persistence raises it. Both directions from one paragraph.

## KS01.9 — Anxiety-management techniques do **not** change the mode of thinking `[T]`

> "**Many techniques exist for better managing anxiety, but they do not change our way of thinking** — that type of change depends on our ability to **make more factual observations of the relationship system.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.E.7` confirmed and sharpened.** `systems_perspective` cannot be raised by lowering anxiety alone; it requires factual observation of the system. This closes a loophole the spec left open — an implementation that let calm alone raise the variable would be wrong. It also constrains what a *landed* coach contact must deliver: observations, not relief (`M1.E.2a`).
