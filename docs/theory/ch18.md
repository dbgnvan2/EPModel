# Ch18 — Society, Crisis, and Systems Theory

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):**
  - Invited **in 1972 by the Environmental Protection Agency** to write a paper titled *"Cultural Myths and Realities of Problem Solving"* for **a symposium on the environmental crisis**, alongside scientists working on population explosion, the energy crisis, air/water pollution, and food supply. Bowen's assigned brief was **"specifying predictable human reactions to crisis situations."**
  - The chapter is *"a slightly modified version of my first paper about emotional process in society"*; the final draft was **"written early in 1973"** and *"was never published in more than an abstract form, it was never made available to the mental health professions."*
  - Bowen's **first presentation on "Societal Regression" to mental health professionals** was at the **1973 Annual Georgetown Family Symposium**.
  - **1975**: attempted a fuller rewrite retitled **"Emotional Process in Society"** (he says it was "inaccurate to think of societal regression without the opposite process which is societal progression"); the manuscript grew "as detailed as a book" and missed the deadline. **1976**: another shortening attempt, another missed deadline. **1977**: too occupied with papers on schizophrenia and differentiation of self in one's family of origin. He publishes the 1973 version to unblock the delayed symposium volume.
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):**
  - The original **NIMH-style live-in family research**: *"maximally impaired, institutionalized, young schizophrenic patients in which the patient and his entire family lived on a research ward together for indefinite periods of time."* Began *"almost twenty years ago"* relative to writing.
  - The **"missing link"** that bridged family and society came from **clinical notes on families with delinquent teenaged children, covering sixteen years**, in which he observed *"a marked change in the way the family, and the total of society, had conceptualized and dealt with transgressions against society"* — i.e. *"factual evidence of a changing process in the family and complementary change in society."*
  - Broader base: *"This systems theory and systems therapy has been used with hundreds of families over a period of twenty years."*
  - Personal file-keeping: *"For many years I had been sufficiently interested in societal issues to keep files of professional and popular articles on the subject."*
  - Two composite/illustrative clinical profiles (a mother with two children of divergent differentiation; a hospitalized mental patient given a town pass) plus a cited outside study on keeping patients out of hospitals.
- **Genre: theory / technique — and roughly what proportion.**
  - Overwhelmingly **theory**, with an epistemological/methodological preamble. Approximately **80% theory : 20% technique**. Technique appears in four concentrated places: (a) the "Clinical patterns during therapy" section on differentiating change; (b) the rule about never seeing the symptomatic child; (c) the mental-patient town-pass vignette contrasting "behavior" framing with "sickness" framing; (d) the closing analogy applying family differentiation method to society.
  - Bowen himself flags the genre mismatch: *"There was a misfit between the title and the content… For the audience there was too much detail about emotional problems in the family and too little about societal issues."*

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Family research on a live-in ward produced observations absent from the literature.** Living together with the whole family of an institutionalized young schizophrenic *"revealed a fascinating new world of clinical observations never previously reported in the literature."* Population: maximally impaired, institutionalized young schizophrenic patients and their entire families, on a research ward, indefinite duration.
- **Observers saw only what their theory permitted.** *"The observers had all been trained in conventional psychiatric theory and they tended to see only what theory had taught them to see."* Setting: the research staff itself.
- **Emotionally impaired people did not distinguish feeling from thinking.** They routinely said *"I feel that…"* where *"I think"* or *"It is my opinion"* would be accurate; they *"considered it truthful and honest to speak of feelings, and insincere and dishonest to speak of thoughts, beliefs, and convictions"*; they avoided **"I position"** statements. Population: the research families with severe impairment.
- **The parents of impaired patients were "no-selfs" yet socially and occupationally successful.** *"'no-selfs' in terms of their own beliefs and convictions but were so adept at getting along with others that they were successful in their businesses, in their professions, and in social lives."* This is presented as *especially striking* — competence coexisting with zero solid self.
- **Action and words diverge.** *"There is a wide discrepancy between what man does and what he says he does."* A research-team paper in year two, *"The Action Dialogue in an Intense Relationship,"* told a story from action alone that *"appeared to have more validity than verbal dialogue."*
- **The same mother produces children at very different levels.** In the clinical profile, one daughter grows up relationship-oriented and severely impaired; the son, born eighteen months later to the same mother, grows up goal-directed and unaffected. Observation: *"The same mother can have two children that are quite different."*
- **Anxiety and marital disharmony *decreased* during the pregnancy with the child who later became impaired.** Bowen reads this as *"good evidence a family projection process is already in progress."*
- **The impaired child could "read" the mother's non-verbal signals.** *"able to 'read' mother's facial expressions, tone of voice, body posture, and footsteps."* The mother could read the daughter's equally well.
- **School performance of the impaired child tracked the relationship, not the subject.** With a teacher she "liked" she was top of the class; with teachers giving less relationship attention, performance fell, she was frequently absent through illness, and she was more dependent on the mother.
- **Junior high school (seventh grade, about age thirteen) is a common breakdown point.** *"The breakdown in the successful school adjustment period came at the beginning of junior high school, which is a common breakdown point for impaired children."*
- **Teenage peer groups stratify by level of self.** Adolescents *"form themselves into groups determined by the lifestyle of 'giving and receiving' into which they were programmed by their mothers."* *"Those with the lowest level of functional self do the greatest rejection of the parents, and therefore have the most emotional 'needs' to be met in the group."*
- **Families exhaust their money on a patient who does not improve, and become disgruntled if the patient does improve.** *"It has been a common experience in expensive private mental hospitals to have families exhaust their financial reserves in futile efforts for improvement in the patient… The same families can become disgruntled and remove the patient from the hospital if the patient does improve."*
- **A well-designed study keeping patients out of hospitals was ignored by the profession.** About 80% of patients already approved for public mental hospital admission could be kept at home, at a fraction of personnel/time/expense, with superior five-year follow-up; the profession's reviews described it as *"interesting and worthy of further study."*
- **Differentiation, once started by one member, provokes a stereotyped three-step counterattack from the rest of the family**, then a single statement of appreciation, then the whole group rises to the new level, then another member begins.
- **Distribution on the differentiation scale is not what Bowen expected.** He expected people to *"group themselves more evenly on the scale."* They do not.
- **Societal indicators moved in parallel with family patterns over sixteen years** (delinquency handling), and over twenty-five years (major crimes, riots, "rights" thinking, overlenient officials).
- **Professional audiences cannot hear systems thinking.** *"no more than one-third can really 'hear.' Most of the others react emotionally with doubts or antagonism."* Also: *"I was surprised to find that scientists could hear systems thinking better than mental health professionals."*
- **Even a molecular scientist reverts.** *"A molecular scientist who knows systems in his work, and who is beyond cause-and-effect thinking there, will lose all objectivity and revert to cause-and-effect thinking in emotional systems."*
- **[p2] Cutoff intensity does not track physical distance.** Among the ways attachment to parents is handled there are *"those who deny the attachment while living near the parents and who do more definite 'cutoffs' from parents than others who live at a distance."* Proximity and cutoff are independent — a phenomenon requiring explanation, and a fresh corroboration of C7 from a chapter not previously counted toward it.
- **[p2] Families pay without limit while the patient stays ill and stop paying when he improves.** Pass 1 recorded the observation; what it did not record is that the observation is **about money**: *"exhaust their financial reserves in futile efforts."* A material stock is depleted by the emotional process, and the spending rate is governed by the patient's non-improvement.
- **[p2] Adolescents and parents run an asymmetric two-currency exchange.** *"They are still financially dependent on parents and the parents are emotionally dependent on the youngsters."* Money flows down, emotional dependence flows up. *"It is easy for parents to yield to meeting excessive demands for money and privileges, in the hope the youngster has finally changed."*
- **[p2] There are not enough differentiated people to staff the roles that require them.** Of the police: *"There would be enough well differentiated people for this only by depriving other professions."* A stated global scarcity constraint on high-differentiation individuals across all roles at once.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Emotional system** → the function of *"the life forces inherited from his phylogenetic past, that he shares with the lower forms, and that governs the subhuman part of man"*; *"would be synonymous with instinct, if instinct is considered to include forces that operate automatically."* Acts on: all behavior, mostly outside awareness.
- **Intellectual system** → *"the function of his highly developed cerebral cortex."* Acts on: reasoning, fact, principle-based decision.
- **Feeling system** → *"a bridge between the emotional and intellectual systems through which subjective states from the upper levels of the emotional system are registered in the cerebral cortex."* Acts on: the coupling between the other two.
- **Two opposing basic life forces** → *"a built-in life growth force toward individuality and the differentiation of a separate 'self,' and the other an equally intense emotional closeness."* Acts on: every relationship; the tension between them generates all the patterns below.
- **Emotional reactiveness / "emotional reflex"** → an automatic, out-of-awareness, reflex-like transmission that *"goes from one family member to another in a chain reaction pattern."* Operates *"with antennae like extensions of all sensory modalities, but a high percentage operates from visual and auditory stimuli."* Can be *"brought within limited observation and under limited conscious control just as one can control a knee jerk with specific energy."* Acts on: dyads first, then the whole network.
- **The electronic/circuit model of a family** → each person is *"a nodal point or an electronic center through which impulses pass in rapid succession, or even multiple impulses at the same time"*; every person is *"'wired' or connected by radio, to all the other people with whom he has relationships"*, with **two-way circuitry**. Acts on: the whole family, and outward into other families and *"the total system of all society."* Per-node variables named: **native ability** for handling impulses, **style** (personality characteristics), *"a narrow range of choice in rejecting or transmitting impulses,"* and **intelligence** (awareness of how the system operates). Per-impulse variables named: **kind**, **intensity spectrum**, **degree of importance**.
- **Programming from birth** → *"Each person is programmed from birth to serve a certain set of functions and each 'senses' what is required or expected, more from the way the system functions around him than from verbal messages that he is free to function as he pleases."* Acts on: role assignment inside the family.
- **Panic amplification loop at the head of the unit** → *"If the head of the family unit is calm, the entire family unit can be calm… When the head goes into panic and transmits panic impulse, the others send back panic messages which further panics the head, in a mounting cycle of panic, with poor handling of messages, disorderly and conflicting messages, and increasing paralysis of functioning."* Acts on: the whole unit; if panic becomes **chronic** *"one or more of the individual units can collapse (become sick)."*
- **Blame as the operating mode of cause-and-effect thinking** → *"Under tension, every person tends either to place the 'blame' outside of self (blamer), or within self (self-blamer), or alternate between the two."* Acts on: attribution, and thereby on where the system directs its corrective energy.
- **Differentiation of self** → *"defines all people, from the lowest to the highest possible level of human functioning, according to a single common denominator… the way the human handles the intermix between emotional and intellectual functioning."* At the high end: intellect and emotion are distinguishable, so decisions can be made *"based on intellect and reasoning when confronted with reality issues"*; at the low end they are *"fused"* and *"their lives are dictated by emotionality."*
- **Solid self vs pseudo-self** → *"relatively fixed levels of differentiation called 'solid self' determined by forces from within self, and tremendous areas of 'pseudo-self' or functional self which is determined by relationship forces."* Acts on: the difference between a person's basic level and their moment-to-moment functional level.
- **Functional-level shifts by appending self** → *"Function shifts include appending one self onto another, or permitting other selfs to append themselves to one's own self."* Acts on: apparent (functional) competence, which can run far above or below basic level for long periods.
- **Triangles** → *"the predictable pattern of emotional forces between any three people… the smallest stable emotional unit… the molecule of emotional systems."* *"A two person relationship is unstable in that it automatically becomes a three person system under stress."* Movement mechanism: *"the most uncomfortable one attempts to establish a more comfortable state of emotional closeness-distance. When the uncomfortable one achieves equilibrium, it disturbs the balance between the other two and the subtle activity shifts to the other most uncomfortable one."* Overflow rule: *"When stress increases, and it involves additional people, the emotional forces continue the action between three poles in the system."*
- **Nuclear family emotional system** → the pattern of emotional forces over the years in the nuclear family. **Intensity is governed by three named inputs:** *"the degree of undifferentiation, by the degree of emotional cutoff with families of origin, and the degree of stress in the system."* **Output takes four forms:** (a) emotional distance between spouses; (b) dysfunction in one spouse — physical, emotional, or social illness; (c) marital conflict; (d) projection of the problem to one or more children.
- **Family projection process** → *"a triangular emotional process through which two powerful people in the triangle reduce their own anxiety and insecurity by picking a defect in the third person, diagnosing and confirming the defect as pitiful and in need of benevolent attention, and then ministering to the pitiful helpless one, which results in the weak becoming weaker and the strong becoming stronger."* Powered by *"benevolent overhelpfulness that benefits the stronger one more than the recipient, and is justified in the name of goodness and self-sacrificing righteousness."* Necessary ingredient: *"the mother's perception of the child as pitiful."* Father's role: *"a passive role in the mother-child relationships, adding his approval of her actions."*
- **The anxious-mothering feedback loop inside the projection process** → mother is anxious → child becomes anxious in response → *"Instead of controlling her own anxiety, she anxiously tries to relieve the child's anxiety by more anxious mothering, which makes the child more anxious, which further drives her anxious mothering, etc."* *"She can never slow down to see her own part."*
- **Absorption / conservation of undifferentiation** → *"The mother's degree of undifferentiation that fuses with the child is determined by her total amount of undifferentiation and by the amount absorbed elsewhere. If the mother's undifferentiation is absorbed in this child, her relationships with her other children will be more normal."*
- **Multigeneration transmission process** → *"In each generation the most emotionally involved child moves toward a lower level of differentiation of self and the least involved child toward a better level of differentiation."*
- **Sibling position** → modifications of *Toman's* work: *"Unless there are variables to prevent the process, children develop certain fixed personality characteristics determined by the sibling position in which they grew up."*
- **Emotional cutoff** (added to the theory in 1975) → *"the most prominent mechanism involved in emotional process between the generations."* Previously split between the nuclear family emotional process and the family projection process.
- **Emotional process in society** (added 1975) → *"the extension of family emotional process into larger social systems and to the totality of society."*
- **Three mechanisms for handling marital fusion**, all preceded by **emotional distance** (*"one way that almost all use to one degree or another… which is hard to maintain over time"*): (1) **marital conflict** — *"permits them to keep reasonable emotional distance most of the time and intense closeness during 'makeups'"*; (2) **continuation of the fusion** — one spouse takes the *"dependent or 'number two position,' leaving the other as the functional decision maker for the 'we-ness'"*; (3) **transmission of the problem to the child**.
- **Togetherness counterforce against differentiation** → a three-step, stereotyped opposition: *"(1) 'You are wrong,' with volumes of reason to support this; (2) 'Change back and we will accept you again'; and (3) 'If you don't these are the consequences,'"* with accusations of *"indifference, meanness, lack of love, selfishness, coldness, the sadistic disregard for others."*
- **Reciprocal cruelty in the relationship-oriented person** → *"the relationship-oriented person, if he continues his course long enough, will eventually meet a crisis in which he operates with a level of punitive cruelty which is the reciprocal opposite of leniency."* *"The degree of nonviolent overleniency on one side can shift to the same undifferentiated degree of violent, cruel, meanness on the other side."*
- **Societal projection process** → the identical mechanism at scale: *"Two people get together and enhance their functioning at the expense of a third, the 'scapegoated' one."* Bowen explicitly prefers *"projection process"* to *"scapegoat"* *"to indicate a reciprocal process in which the twosome can force the third into submission, or the process is more mutual, or the third can force the other two to treat him as inferior."*
- **Physical distance as an anxiety-relief mechanism, now unavailable** → *"In the past, man has used physical distance to relieve the tension of emotional fusion. Physical distance is harder to arrange with an exploding population."*
- **[p2] One currency behind all of them: "living space for self."** After listing distance and the three fusion patterns Bowen closes: *"All of these patterns are ways man has to deal with overcloseness. All have to do with the way the family impinges other family members in an effort to maintain living space for self."* Immediately after the cutoff sentences he adds *"This has implications for man and his living space in society."* Emotional distance, the three fusion patterns, cutoff and physical distance are presented as **draws on one quantity**, which is the chapter's actual family→society bridge.
- **[p2] Two-stage triangling with an explicit transition condition.** *"When another takes sides emotionally, he is potentially triangled. When he talks (to influence others) or he takes action based on feelings, he is actively triangled."* A latent state entered by taking sides, and an active state entered only by **speech intended to influence, or action taken on feeling**.
- **[p2] Selective recruitment, and a full triangle lifecycle in one sentence.** *"it begins with emotional tension in a bipolar situation, it spreads by involving emotionally vulnerable others, it is fed by emotional reactiveness and response to denial and accusation and it becomes quiescent when emotional energy is exhausted."* Recruitment is not uniform — it targets *"emotionally vulnerable others."*
- **[p2] The functional level of self is defined at three levels, with a different determinant at each.** *"It is possible to assign a functional level of self for an individual, for an entire family which is determined by the level of self in the head of the family, or in the total of society which is determined by prevailing environmental forces."* The family's level is **the head's level, not an average of members'**.
- **[p2] An operational criterion for the level, used identically at family and societal scale.** *"These observations are based on the same criteria used to estimate family functioning, which is the amount of principle determined 'self' in comparison to the 'feeling orientation' which strives for an immediate short-term feeling solution to the anxiety of the moment."* The quantity is a **ratio**, and the low pole is defined by its **time horizon**.
- **[p2] Intellectual fact and emotional fact are separated by a lag, and the lag has a named channel.** *"Man can 'know' something intellectually a long time before he 'knows' it as part of his total being. The main sensory modalities that operate in the emotional reactiveness of relationships are vision and hearing. It might be that television, which uses both modalities, was the most important factor."*

## A3. Predictive claims

*(Flagged: [clinical] = claims repeated clinical experience; [asserted] = no evidence offered; [cited] = external study named.)*

**Family level**

1. **If the head of a family unit is calm, then the whole unit can be calm; if the head panics, then panic amplifies reciprocally and functioning becomes paralysed.** [clinical]
2. **If panic in a unit becomes chronic (rather than periodic), then one or more members collapse (become sick).** *"Any unit can recover from periodic panic or overloads, but when the panic becomes chronic one or more of the individual units can collapse."* [asserted]
3. **If anxiety rises, then every pattern in the system becomes more pronounced** — triangles, symptom outbreaks, and the projection process alike. **Conversely, if anxiety falls, pattern intensity falls**; *"the functioning of the triangle patterns is not observable in a completely calm system."* [clinical]
4. **If a twosome is under stress, then it becomes a three-person system** — automatically. [asserted, as definitional]
5. **If undifferentiation is absorbed by one child, then the other children's relationships with the mother are more normal and they are free to grow toward goal-directed lives.** [clinical]
6. **If a mother projects her anxiety onto a child and treats it as a problem in the child, then the problem in the child is perpetuated.** [clinical]
7. **If parents are asked to assume the working premise that the problem is in the parents and the child is never seen, then results are better and faster than if the child is involved.** *"it has been the repeating experience with systems therapy that results are better and faster if the child is never involved in any process designed to modify the parents."* [clinical]
8. **If the child is seen at all, then there is automatic partial confirmation that the child is sick.** *"In the act of seeing the child, there is some degree of automatic confirmation that the child is 'sick.'"* [asserted]
9. **If the projection process reaches an advanced stage, then it is irreversible** — *"the patient is so functionally impaired and so programmed to act the part of the pitiful one that the process is irreversible."* [clinical]
10. **If the emotional attachment to the parental family is denied, then one has less choice in the pattern established with one's own spouse and children.** *"The more one denies the attachment to the past, the less choice one has in determining the pattern with his own wife and children."* [asserted]
11. **If there is a divorce or threatened divorce, or an emotional problem in a child, then that is implicit evidence of an unresolved emotional attachment to the parental families.** [asserted — stated as inference rule]
12. **If contact with parental and extended families is denied/cut off, then symptom intensity in the nuclear family increases. If meaningful relationships with them are opened up, then tension and symptoms in the entire nuclear family automatically reduce.** [clinical] — Bowen adds: *"This has implications for man and his living space in society."*
13. **If a spouse remains long in the dependent "number two" position, then that spouse becomes dysfunctional — physical illness, emotional illness, or social dysfunction such as drinking or irresponsible behavior.** [clinical]
14. **If two people's levels of differentiation are known before marriage, then the degree of emotional fusion early in the marriage is predictable, and symptoms from fusion appear at marriage or shortly after.** [asserted]
15. **If a child is "programmed" to a certain level of "giving and receiving" with mother, then that level remains relatively fixed throughout life**, and the child can have an "open and loving" relationship *only* when the conditions for that level of investment are met. [asserted]
16. **If an impaired, relationship-oriented child meets a teacher with a similarly low level of differentiation, then the child stays relatively symptom-free; a less favorable "fit" produces more symptoms earlier.** [clinical, from the profile]
17. **If the school structure changes to many teachers (junior high), then the impaired child's chance of finding an efficient "giving and receiving" relationship drops and breakdown follows.** [clinical]
18. **If a key member of an emotional system can (a) control his own emotional reactiveness, (b) accurately observe the system and his part in it, (c) avoid counterattacking when provoked, and (d) maintain active relationship without withdrawing or falling silent — then the entire system changes in a series of predictable steps.** [clinical] Bowen calls this *"the essence of change in emotional systems."*
19. **If the differentiating one defends himself, counterattacks, or falls silent, then he slips back into the old emotional equilibrium. If he stays on his own calm course, then the accusations peak and quickly subside, the opposition expresses appreciation, and the entire group pulls up to the new level.** [clinical]
20. **Differentiation never occurs without opposition:** *"The togetherness opposition to individuation, or differentiation, is so predictable that differentiation does not occur without opposition from the togetherness forces."* [asserted, strong]
21. **If an unsure, changeable parent becomes more sure of his own operating principles and can calmly state his position without trying to force others, then the calming trend is often dramatic.** [clinical]
22. **If communication is opened up in an anxious system (where members are isolated and communication is underground gossip), then tension reduces.** [clinical]
23. **If a relationship-oriented ("overlenient") person continues his course long enough, then he eventually meets a crisis in which he operates with punitive cruelty — the reciprocal opposite of his leniency.** [clinical, generalised to war crime atrocities as speculation]
24. **If a mental patient is told passes are suspended because he is "sick" and must get "better," then he faces two conditions outside his control and may go into chronic illness; if told they are suspended until he "learns to behave himself in public," then he has something he can control, and does.** [clinical vignette] Generalised: *"Hospitalization has been markedly shorter for those put there for 'unacceptable behavior' than similar patients hospitalized for 'sickness.'"*
25. **If the child is the vehicle of the projection process, then the family will pay unlimited costs for treatment directed at the patient, and will withdraw the patient if he improves.** [clinical]
26. **[cited]** If patients approved for public mental hospital admission are treated at home instead, then ~80% can be kept out, at a fraction of the resource, with superior five-year outcomes.

**Society level**

27. **If society's functional differentiation is lower, then the number and intensity of emotional disruptions — major crimes, riots — rises.** [asserted, offered as the *evidence* for the lower level]
28. **If a society's level of differentiation is low, then it will elect or pressure officials who reflect that average level.** *"Society tends to elect public officials, from local level to congressional level, who reflect the average functioning of society."* Bowen picks between two postulations and comes down on one: *"Most of the evidence supports the thesis that the emotional orientation is set by society and such public officials are slowly forced into that mold."* [asserted]
29. **If a critical issue is exposed to public debate and congressional action, then the resulting corrective action will automatically reflect the average (emotionally determined) level of society.** [asserted]
30. **If a corrective program is directed at symptoms as they surface, then it produces (a) the delusion the problem is solved, (b) complacency, (c) a rude awakening with new and more serious crises, and (d) a basic problem that grows worse.** [asserted]
31. **If one group in society is made an object of benevolent, oversympathetic help, then that group is chronically impaired by the very attention designed to help.** *"No matter how good the principle behind such programs, it is essentially impossible to implement them without the built-in complications of the projection process."* [asserted]
32. **If one projection-process target is removed, then the process finds a new one.** *"For over a century, the Negro has been the main object of the projection process to minority groups. Now that this has been greatly modified, the process will find new objects."* Predicted next targets: **welfare recipients and the poor**. [asserted — an explicit forward prediction]
33. **If a functional dysfunction is labelled with a diagnosis (e.g. alcoholism as a disease), then the problem is fixed in the patient and the family and society are absolved of their contribution.** [asserted]
34. **If a specific charged societal issue is debated directly, then the result is non-productive polarization and further fixation of current policy.** [asserted] But: **if society pulls up to a higher level of functioning, then such issues are automatically modified to fit the new level.**
35. **If the most influential segment of society worked toward differentiation of self, then it would automatically spread through the less influential segments, benefit the less fortunate, and raise the functional level of all society.** [asserted]
36. **If any key person in society differentiates, then it automatically "rubs off" on others** — *"Anyone who moves in this direction benefits society."* [asserted]
37. **The lower the level of differentiation, the harder it is to start a differentiating effort.** [asserted — an explicit monotone relation]
38. **If a more differentiated society existed, then it would not have as serious an environmental problem**, because *"we would have a higher percentage of people oriented to responsibility for self and others, and for the environment, and a lower percentage focused on rights and force and on legal mechanisms to guarantee rights."* [asserted]
39. **If man is a cause-and-effect thinker — "which is most of the time in calm periods and all of the time in tense periods" — then his assignment of causality is as inaccurate as his ancestors' witch-hunting.** [asserted]
40. **If people achieve better differentiation, then they function far better than the societal level and can live calmly and productively inside society's emotional system.** [clinical] — verbatim: *"Those who are able to achieve better differentiation come to function far better than the societal level, and to find ways to live calmly and productively in the emotional system of society."* **[p2] Pass 1's wording put "than the societal level" in the antecedent as well, making the claim circular; the source does not.** The antecedent is *having achieved* better differentiation, and the sentence directly follows the motivation gate (*"Only a fraction of disturbed families are motivated"*), so the decoupling is conditional on that gate having been passed.
41. **[p2] If a person is well differentiated, then population density does not impair him; if he is poorly differentiated, it does.** *"A relatively differentiated self can live a more orderly life whether alone, or in the middle of the human pile. A poorly differentiated person is not productive alone."* An explicit **interaction** between a societal condition and individual level — the mechanism form of claim 40.
42. **[p2] If a person takes sides emotionally he is *potentially* triangled; if he then speaks to influence others or acts on feeling, he is *actively* triangled.** [asserted]
43. **[p2] If a role requires a high level of differentiation to perform, then society cannot staff it, because the supply is fixed.** *"There would be enough well differentiated people for this only by depriving other professions."* [asserted]
44. **[p2] If parents are pressed by an adolescent, then they yield money and privileges as an anxiety-relieving move.** *"It is easy for parents to yield to meeting excessive demands for money and privileges, in the hope the youngster has finally changed."* [clinical]

## A5. Terminology

- **Systems thinking** — in this chapter defined operationally by what it *excludes*: *"It focuses on what happened, and on how and when and where it happened, insofar as observations are based on fact. It carefully avoids man's automatic preoccupation with why it happened."* And: *"Systems theory focuses on what man does and not on his verbal explanations about why he does it."* Historically, the term *"was spontaneously used to refer to the automatic predictable behavior between family members."*
- **Facts of functioning** — the unit of observation systems theory admits. The electronic model *"appears to have the potential and the flexibility to accurately account for almost every item of human relationships that can be structured as facts of functioning."*
- **Functional fact / the functional-concept formula** — *"That man dreams is a scientific fact, but what he dreams is not necessarily a fact,"* generalised to feeling, thinking, talking, love, hate. Bowen's **functional definition of love**: *"I am not able to accurately define love, but it is a fact that statements to another important person about the presence or absence of love in self, or in the other, predictably results in an emotional reaction in the relationship."*
- **Emotional reflex** — Bowen's preferred synonym for emotional reactiveness, chosen because it *"makes it a little more synonymous with biology."* Observability rule: *"In low tension they are not observable, and in high tension, the reflexes are too chaotic to see much order"* — best seen at **moderate** tension.
- **Nodal point** — a person, considered as a junction in the family's circuit. **Note:** this chapter uses *nodal point* in a **second, unrelated sense** as a *point in time* — *"The first nodal point came when the children started school"*; *"The end of World War II was an important nodal point"*; *"When the differentiating one is through his first nodal point, then another."* The two senses are never reconciled in the text.
- **Emotional system / feeling system / intellectual system** — the three-layer architecture (see A2).
- **Solid self / pseudo-self (functional self)** — fixed-from-within vs determined by relationship forces.
- **No-self** — a person with no independently held beliefs or convictions. Used of the parents of impaired patients and of the overlenient parent and, by extension, of society's mode of operation.
- **"I position"** — a statement establishing oneself as separate from another; avoided by the low-differentiation person working for togetherness.
- **Differentiation of self scale** — *"scales people according to the degree their selfs fuse into each other in close relationships, or the degrees that can function separately from the emotional system even in intense feeling states."*
- **Triangle** — *"the smallest stable emotional unit… the molecule of emotional systems."*
- **Undifferentiated family ego mass** — used here mainly as the *societal* comparator: society is *"much more similar to a family with an intense 'undifferentiated family ego mass.'"*
- **Family projection process** — used interchangeably with **societal projection process** at scale; Bowen explicitly rejects **"scapegoat"** (the social scientists' word) in favour of **projection process** because scapegoat implies a one-directional act and misses the reciprocity.
- **Goal-directed child vs relationship-oriented child** — the pair labelling the two children in the profile; *"relationship-oriented"* later becomes the term for society's preferred qualities (humanitarian, responsive, sensitive) and for the overlenient official.
- **"Giving and receiving"** — the currency of the relationship-oriented attachment; a *level* of it is programmed by the mother and stays fixed for life.
- **Emotional cocoon** — the term for the daughter-and-boyfriend dyad living in total mutual investment.
- **Societal regression / societal progression** — Bowen notes the 1973 title used only *regression* and that this *"was inaccurate… without the opposite process which is societal progression"*; the concept was later retitled *"Emotional Process in Society."*
- **Overspecialization hypothesis** — *"it is the most highly developed forms that become extinct the fastest."* Applied to man's brain.
- **Biological man vs intellectual man** — the closing framing: the environmental problem was created by *"biological man (in contrast to intellectual man)"*, and solutions depend on *"the highest functioning of intellectual man in directing total man toward solutions."*
- **Medical model** — cause-and-effect: *"careful examination, the establishment of etiology (cause), making an accurate diagnosis, and specific treatment directed at the etiology."*
- **[p2] "Living space for self"** — the quantity all the distance and fusion mechanisms are said to be competing for, at both family and societal scale.
- **[p2] "Potentially triangled" / "actively triangled"** — two named states of a bystander, with the transition condition stated (see A2).
- **[p2] "Multigeneration transmission process"** — spelled *multigeneration*, not *multigenerational*, in the numbered concept list.
- **[p2] "Partial cutoff"** — named as the modal pattern: *"The most common pattern is the partial cutoff in which the nuclear family lives away and maintains a token contact with parents."*

## A6. Structure and roles

- **Head of the family unit** is a load-bearing position: it sets the calm/panic level for the whole circuit, and *"the functional level of self… for an entire family… is determined by the level of self in the head of the family."* The same is claimed for *"heads of small social and work groups."*
- **The one who moves first** in differentiation is *"one responsible family member in a key position"* — in therapy, *"a motivated head of the system, usually the one parent who is most motivated."* Sequence: one pulls up → *"then another, and then others automatically do the same."*
- **Mother** is the primary projector: *"an overanxious mother devoted to being the best possible mother and having the most wonderful child."* Also the primary object of unresolved attachment: *"most frequently to the mother for both men and women."*
- **Father** is peripheral and approving in the projection triangle: *"The father plays a passive role in the mother-child relationships, adding his approval of her actions."* In the societal analogue he *"originally had a viewpoint different from mother, but his pseudo-selfs and ideas about responsibility were not sufficiently firm to stand against the strong emotional forces when he entered the emotional arena on the mother's side. He lost his pseudo-self to the mother."*
- **The symptom-bearer is the most emotionally involved child**, and this position is generationally directional: *"In each generation the most emotionally involved child moves toward a lower level of differentiation of self and the least involved child toward a better level."*
- **Uninvolved siblings escape**: *"There is a spectrum of families in which other children 'grow up' outside the parental fusion with the involved child and they are free to grow toward goal-directed lives."* The son in the profile *"was not functionally affected by his sister's erratic course. He maintained close contact with his parents without being affected by their emotional involvement with his sister."*
- **Sibling position** confers fixed personality characteristics (after Toman) and is used to determine *"the part a child will play in the family emotional process"* and to *"predict family patterns in the next generation."* In the profile: first-born girl = impaired, second-born boy (18 months later) = goal-directed. Bowen attributes the difference to the projection process, not to sibling position, in this instance.
- **Non-family role occupants who enter the emotional system:**
  - **Teacher** — becomes a substitute relationship figure; her *own* level of differentiation determines the child's symptom level. The teacher in the profile: *"a shy woman with a poor level of differentiation who was relationship-oriented in her own life."* She actively places the child with other "understanding" teachers.
  - **Psychiatrist** — becomes the third strong party in the commitment triangle: *"it is the two parents acting as one agent, soliciting and getting approval of another, the psychiatrist and legal commitment procedure, to take the action about the patient."*
  - **Juvenile court** — *"A parent can declare a child 'out of parental control' and the juvenile court relieves the parents of responsibility and continues the focus on the child."*
  - **Peer group** — takes over the mother's function at adolescence: *"They put as much energy into the relationships in the group as they formerly devoted to their mothers, with primary energy into the current 'best friend.'"* Groups are organized around *"'leaders' and 'best friend' principles"* and *"quickly become an active network of triangles."* The group *"prescribes language, dress, and behavior."*
  - **Police** — a structurally impossible role: *"It would require a person with a high level of differentiation to meet requirements of total firm fairness. There would be enough well differentiated people for this only by depriving other professions. A less differentiated person would automatically go either toward leniency or cruelty."*
  - **Public officials / college presidents / judges** — occupy the "father-who-lost-his-pseudo-self" position at societal scale.
- **Positional asymmetry in the circuit:** *"The larger unit can punish a single member, and a single member in a key position can hurt the whole unit."*
- **[p2] The head's level *is* the family's level.** *"a functional level of self… for an entire family which is determined by the level of self in the head of the family."* This is stronger than "the head is weighted more heavily": the family-level variable is not a function of the membership at all, only of one occupant. **Cross-chapter conflict, unresolved:** Ch12 states that *"any one significant family member"* who cools the anxious response de-escalates the system, and Ch20 states that the differentiating person *"does not have to be the boss."* Ch18 privileges the head; those two chapters explicitly deny that the head is required.
- **[p2] Attachment-handling taxonomy, with a stated mode.** Live within the parental emotional field / deny the attachment while living near the parents / cut off, leave and never return or communicate / cut off from own parents and append to the spouse's family / **partial cutoff — "the most common pattern"** — nuclear family lives away with token contact. Note that the second type is explicitly *more* cut off than people who live at a distance.
- **[p2] Society forces its role occupants into a mold, and the loop closes.** *"the emotional orientation is set by society and such public officials are slowly forced into that mold"*; of the police, *"Society is quick to charge 'brutality' and force police into the mold."*
- **Adolescent dependency is bidirectional:** *"They are still financially dependent on parents and the parents are emotionally dependent on the youngsters, who still have the ability to dissolve the selfs of the parents."*
- **Activist roles in a societal triangle:** those siding with the "victim" *"are more likely to demonstrate and take activist postures"*; those who *"feel more responsible"* for the total group *"side with the parental side… more likely to stay silent or to take action in letters to the editor."* A distinct sub-role: *"members of professional and scientific organizations who attempt to use knowledge and social status to further entangle the triangular emotional system."*

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

1. **Work with one motivated person rather than the whole group.** *"This is a method of teaching one person the predictable functioning of systems and supervising his effort to modify his functioning in ongoing relationships. It is not 'therapy' in the usual sense."* Bowen states it is *"proving even more effective than working with multiple family members at the same time."*
2. **Teach the person to control his own emotional reactiveness** — the first of the four operative conditions for system change.
3. **Teach the person to accurately observe the functioning of the system and his own part in it.**
4. **Coach him to avoid counterattacking when provoked.**
5. **Coach him to maintain an active relationship with the other key members without withdrawing or becoming silent.**
6. **Help spouses discover their emotional reflexes.** *"Systems therapy directed at helping spouses discover the reflexes can give each a bit of control over the automatic emotional reactiveness."*
7. **In the early, high-symptom phase, use anxiety-relieving measures first** — *"meetings with the entire family unit or with the parents, the heads of the unit, to reestablish communication and settle disharmony."*
8. **Calm the system through the most motivated head.** *"One of the most effective ways of calming a tense system is through a motivated head of the system, usually the one parent who is most motivated."* Help that person *"become more sure of his own operating principles"* so he *"can calmly state his position without trying to force others."*
9. **Open up communication in an anxious system** — where members are isolated and communication is *"underground gossip"*, *"Anything that improves open communication will reduce the tension, as an initial step to more definitive efforts to modify the system."*
10. **Primary effort goes to separating feeling from thinking.** *"In therapy, primary effort goes to helping people distinguish feeling states from intellectual functioning and helping them dare to develop firmer opinions, beliefs and convictions, in spite of pressure from the relationship system to retain the former level of amorphous 'no-self.'"*
11. **Ask the parents to assume the working premise that the problem is in the parents, not the child** — and never see the child.
12. **Open up meaningful relationships with parental and extended families** — this *"automatically reduces the tension and symptoms in the entire nuclear family."*
13. **Frame a patient's problem as controllable behavior, not uncontrollable sickness.** The town-pass instruction: passes suspended *"until he learned to behave himself in public"*, not until he was "better."
14. **Use knowledge of triangles to introduce emotional cues.** *"The therapist can use his knowledge to introduce emotional cues, which result in predictable shifts in emotional flow."* Bowen claims a *"method of therapy as predictable as triangles are predictable."*
15. **De-intensify a triangle by staying in low-key contact.** *"It can be deintensified or stopped by a calm person who stays in 'low key' contact without defending self or counterattacking."*
16. **Estimate differentiation over years, not months** — and use that estimate to predict. *"It is impossible to do scale values for any month or year but it is possible to do estimates of the general levels of differentiation over a period of years, and from this one can do fairly accurate predictions of things to come."*
17. **Collect the three items that make a family's future predictable at marriage,** in stated priority order: (1) *"a reasonably accurate estimate of the levels of differentiation of self in each spouse"*; (2) *"an accurate estimate of emotional functioning in the families of origin of each spouse, the function that each had in that family, and efficiency with which he or she functioned"*; (3) *"the over-all functioning patterns within each spouse."*
18. **Direct the differentiating person to take work on indecision OUTSIDE the family.** *"discussion should be with those emotionally removed from the family, or he could go to the literature, or go into isolation to work it through for himself."*
19. **Instruct him to devote primary attention to self** — *"to be sure of self on all life principles that involve himself and his family, to have the courage to take action on his convictions, and to devote primary attention to becoming the most responsible possible person."*
20. **[p2] Assess the level as a ratio, not a trait score** — *"the amount of principle determined 'self' in comparison to the 'feeling orientation' which strives for an immediate short-term feeling solution to the anxiety of the moment."* Stated as *"the same criteria"* used for family and for society, which makes the two scales commensurable by construction.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

1. **Do not ask "why."** *"It carefully avoids man's automatic preoccupation with why it happened. The conclusion of why thinking into systems theory automatically results in a reversion to conventional theory, and the loss of the unique advantage in systems concepts."*
2. **Do not diagnose.** *"A systems approach that avoids diagnosis has been superior in a wide range of problems."*
3. **Do not see the symptomatic child.** *"results are better and faster if the child is never involved in any process designed to modify the parents,"* because *"In the act of seeing the child, there is some degree of automatic confirmation that the child is 'sick.'"*
4. **Do not blame.** Blame — of the other or of self — is the operating mode of cause-and-effect thinking; the medical model *"'blames' the parents for the illness, even though the psychiatrist may deny that he blames the parents."*
5. **Do not treat the patient as the pathology.** The psychiatrist who *"examines the patient, diagnoses his illness, and agrees to treat the illness as a pathology in the patient"* is described as re-enacting *"another step in the family projection process."*
6. **Do not exclude other family members from the treatment process** — a named defect of the medical model.
7. **Do not tell a patient he is "too sick" and must wait to get "better"** — that presents *"two conditions out of his direct control"* and invites chronic illness and passive waiting.
8. **Do not counterattack, defend self, or fall silent** (as the differentiating person) — *"he slips back into the old emotional equilibrium."*
9. **Do not try to force others into the position you have taken.** The calming parent *"can calmly state his position without trying to force others."*
10. **Do not try to change another person.** *"It is never possible to really change another person, but it is possible to change the part that self plays."*
11. **Do not discuss unresolved principles with the spouse or close family during the differentiating effort.** *"it is common for people to discuss issues with spouses or other close family members who use this opportunity to sell their own values, which, if accepted, modifies the self of the differentiating one toward a 'family self.'"*
12. **Do not oversell the systems viewpoint.** *"The medical centers in which a family orientation has been most successful are those in which conventional psychiatry has not been too strict in enforcing the medical model and family therapists have not tried to oversell their viewpoint."*
13. **(Societal analogue) Do not debate the specific charged issue.** *"To debate such a specific issue in society, with the amount of intense emotion in the issue, would result in non-productive polarization and further fixation of current policy and procedures."*
14. **(Societal analogue) Do not attack symptoms as they surface.** *"any program directed at symptoms as they surface can well lead to missing more basic issues and a long-term 'making the problem worse.'"*
15. **Do not confuse benevolent helpfulness with help.** *"more hurtfulness to others is done in the service of pious helpfulness than in the name of malevolent intent."*

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Phase 1 — anxiety relief.** *"In the beginning, when symptoms are high, it might be indicated to use anxiety relieving measures, such as meetings with the entire family unit or with the parents, the heads of the unit, to reestablish communication and settle disharmony."*
- **Phase 2 — narrow to one person.** *"If the goal is toward long term stability and the differentiation of self, this eventually becomes the effort of one person who can give primary attention to self."*
- **Precondition for observing anything:** anxiety must be at a moderate level. *"In low tension they are not observable, and in high tension, the reflexes are too chaotic to see much order."* And *"the functioning of the triangle patterns is not observable in a completely calm system."* Also: *"The ability to observe and 'see' emotional reflexes is dependent on the level of emotional tension."*
- **Precondition for words to land:** *"The words used in triangular emotional exchange, based on rational thinking, are usually not heard by the other except to defend or prepare a rebuttal. The words can be heard only after the emotion is reduced."*
- **Duration of the differentiating person's preparation:** *"It may require months or longer for this one to become reasonably sure within himself"* before he can openly state and act on his own principles.
- **The differentiation sequence, in order:** one member defines and states his own inner life principles → takes responsible action on them → togetherness counterforce steps (1) "You are wrong," (2) "Change back and we will accept you again," (3) "If you don't these are the consequences" → **if** he holds his calm course, accusations peak and quickly subside → a single statement of appreciation → *"the entire group pulls up to the new level attained by the first"* → *"Later, another member of the group will start his or her own effort."* Then: *"When the differentiating one is through his first nodal point, then another, and another, and other family members begin the same kind of effort."*
- **Prerequisite state for system change (all four required):** control of own reactiveness; accurate observation of the system and one's own part; no counterattack when provoked; active relationship maintained without withdrawal or silence.
- **Ordering of assessment information** before predicting a family's course: differentiation level of each spouse first, then families of origin, then within-spouse functioning patterns.
- **Timescale constraint on assessment:** a differentiation estimate is impossible *"except over a period of years or an entire segment of a life"*, because functional levels shift so much and for so long that *"it is hard to find an average baseline."*
- **Motivation gate:** *"Only a fraction of disturbed families are motivated for the emotional stress of moving to higher differentiation."*
- **Irreversibility gate:** in the severest projection cases, *"By the time this severe process has reached an advanced stage… the process is irreversible."*
- **[p2] Collapse gate is chronicity, but chronicity is not stated as sufficient.** The full sentence is *"Any unit can recover from periodic panic or overloads, but when the panic becomes chronic one or more of the individual units can collapse (become sick), **and there are several variables for handling that**."* Pass 1 truncated the clause. Bowen names *"can collapse"*, not *will*, and leaves the moderating variables unnamed here.
- **Societal sequencing proposal:** keep the critical issues away from the emotional average. *"any proposed solutions should come from the best brains and the highest levels of technical and emotional functioning in society who can lead and set an example… Perhaps an agency similar to the Space Agency could accomplish the mission."*

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Reversion to cause-and-effect under tension.** *"A family systems therapist who knows emotional systems fairly well may be able to remain objective, without 'blaming' as long as emotional tension is within ordinary limits, but will immediately revert to his former cause-and-effect thinking in higher levels of tension."* Consequence: loss of the entire advantage of the method.
- **Letting "why" back in.** *"The conclusion of why thinking into systems theory automatically results in a reversion to conventional theory."*
- **Seeing the child.** Consequence: automatic confirmation that the child is sick; slower and worse results.
- **Diagnosing / labelling.** Consequence: *"labeling with a diagnosis invokes the ills of the societal projection process, it helps fix the problem in the patient, and it absolves the family and society of their contribution."*
- **The therapist joining the projection triangle.** The psychiatrist accepting the case as pathology in the patient *"reenacts another step in the family projection process."* At commitment, the parents *"acting as one agent"* recruit *"the psychiatrist and legal commitment procedure"* as the approving third.
- **The differentiating person defending, counterattacking, or falling silent.** Consequence: he *"slips back into the old emotional equilibrium."*
- **Consulting the family about one's own unresolved principles.** Consequence: they *"sell their own values,"* which *"modifies the self of the differentiating one toward a 'family self.'"*
- **The overlenient person's swing to cruelty.** *"The degree of nonviolent overleniency on one side can shift to the same undifferentiated degree of violent, cruel, meanness on the other side."* Bowen names this as *"important on a societal level"* and speculates it may have been *"part of war crime atrocities."*
- **The mother's anxiety loop.** She cannot slow down to see her own part; instead she *"seeks for causes for the problem in the child and goes to physicians seeking a positive diagnosis and a new avenue through which to structure and focus the mothering."* In periods of calm *"she might neglect reality needs of the child."*
- **Treating a projection-produced symptom as the target.** *"Society probably spends more time and energy in futile attempts to remove the products of 'sinning' than in trying to stop the 'sinning.'"* The family analogy: they demand *"that the product of the 'sinning' be removed without doing anything to disturb family patterns."*
- **The family withdrawing the patient when he improves.** Named as evidence of the force *"that functions to keep the patient sick."*
- **Programme design that bypasses parents.** The Child Advocacy Act *"would attempt to diagnose and treat innumerable problems in children that are the product of the family projection process, it would bypass the parents through whom the problems are most reversible, and it had a high potential for replicating the family projection process in society."*
- **Symptom-directed crash solutions at any scale.** Consequence chain: delusion the problem is solved → complacency → *"the rude awakening with new and more serious crises, and a basic problem that grows worse."*
- **Helping programmes with oversympathetic staff.** *"Such programs attract workers who are oversympathetic with less fortunate people. They automatically put the recipients in a 'one down' inferior position, and they either keep them there, or get angry at them."*
- **Overselling the viewpoint.** Named as a cause of failure of family orientation in medical centres.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

*(Each rule of technique converted into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.)*

1. **"Work with one motivated person, not the group"** ⟹ **A family is a connected system in which a change of state at any sufficiently central node propagates to every other node.** Bowen states this directly: *"When any key member of an emotional system can control his own emotional reactiveness… the entire system will change in a series of predictable steps."* Implied testable claim: the system's equilibrium is *not* independently maintained by each member; it is maintained by a coupling that one node can unilaterally alter.
2. **"Work through the head of the system"** ⟹ **Nodes are not equivalent. There is a distinguished position whose anxiety level sets the unit's anxiety level.** *"If the head of the family unit is calm, the entire family unit can be calm."* Implies a weighted, non-uniform influence graph, and that a family's functional level of self *"is determined by the level of self in the head of the family."*
3. **"Never see the child; work only with the parents"** ⟹ **The child's symptom is not located in the child; it is a state of the parental relationship expressed through the child, and the symptom is maintained by attention paid to it.** The corollary claim is stronger than "the child isn't the cause": *observing* the child is itself an input that reinforces the symptom. Implies symptom level is a function of the observation regime, not only of internal state.
4. **"Do not diagnose"** ⟹ **A label is a causal input to the system, not a description of it.** Diagnosis *"helps fix the problem in the patient, and it absolves the family and society of their contribution"* — i.e. it simultaneously raises the symptom-bearer's fixity and lowers everyone else's felt responsibility. The family's allocation of responsibility is a live, mutable variable that external actors can move.
5. **"Frame it as behavior he can control, not sickness he must wait out"** ⟹ **A person's functional level responds to whether the demand placed on him is inside or outside his locus of control.** Same patient, same voices, two framings, two trajectories. Implies functional level is partly set by the *expectation* the system holds, not solely by the person's basic level.
6. **"Do not ask why; observe what, how, when, where"** ⟹ **Verbal accounts of motive are unreliable data about the system, but actions are reliable data.** *"There is a wide discrepancy between what man does and what he says he does."* Implies a family model should be driven by observable transitions, not by stated intentions.
7. **"Do not blame; do not counterattack"** ⟹ **Blame is not commentary — it is an *input* that raises system anxiety and recruits others into the triangle.** *"It can be started by one person who, intentionally or unintentionally, touches an emotional trigger in the second. The triggered person characteristically defends or counterattacks which adds emotional fuel."* Implies an explicit accusation → defence → escalation channel between nodes.
8. **"Stay in low-key contact without defending or counterattacking"** ⟹ **Triangle intensity is a function of the *sum of reactive responses*, and a non-reactive node is an energy sink.** *"it becomes quiescent when emotional energy is exhausted"* — so the system has a finite reactive-energy budget that reactivity replenishes and non-reactivity drains.
9. **"Reduce emotion before expecting words to be heard"** ⟹ **Information transfer between family members is gated by anxiety level.** Above some threshold, content is not processed — *"not heard by the other except to defend or prepare a rebuttal."* Implies a channel whose bandwidth is inversely related to system anxiety.
10. **"Anxiety-relief measures first, differentiation later"** ⟹ **Differentiation work is only available below some anxiety threshold.** High-symptom families cannot start there; the meetings-with-the-whole-unit phase exists purely to bring anxiety into range.
11. **"Observation requires moderate tension"** ⟹ **The observable signature of the mechanisms is non-monotonic in anxiety: invisible when calm, legible at moderate anxiety, chaotic when high.** This is a claim about the *measurement* of the system, and it implies the system has qualitatively different regimes at different anxiety levels — not merely a scaling.
12. **"Open up relationships with the extended family"** ⟹ **Cutoff is an *amplifier* of nuclear-family symptom intensity, and the amplification is reversible.** *"Denying and cutting off emotional contact with parental and extended families increases the intensity of symptoms in a family. Opening up meaningful relationships with the parental and extended families automatically reduces the tension and symptoms in the entire nuclear family."* Implies a family's symptom level depends on the state of edges to nodes *outside* the household.
13. **"Estimate differentiation only over years, never over a month"** ⟹ **There are two distinct quantities: a slow-moving basic level and a fast-moving functional level, and the fast one can depart from the slow one substantially and for long periods.** *"The functional shifts of those with lower levels of self are so great, and they operate for such long periods of time, it is hard to find an average baseline."* Implies basic level must be modelled separately from functional level, with an averaging window measured in years.
14. **"Take the three assessment items in that priority order"** ⟹ **The variance in a family's future trajectory is explained mostly by spousal differentiation, secondly by family-of-origin functioning and the function each spouse held there, thirdly by within-spouse patterns.** This is an explicit, ranked feature-importance claim.
15. **"Take your indecision outside the family"** ⟹ **A family exerts continuous pressure to converge members' principles toward a shared "family self", and this pressure operates specifically at moments of uncertainty.** Implies the togetherness force acts on *undecided* state, not on already-settled state.
16. **"Expect and ride out the three-step counterattack"** ⟹ **The togetherness force is a restoring force proportional to displacement, not a constant.** It escalates through defined stages, peaks, and — if unopposed by reactivity — collapses. *"differentiation does not occur without opposition from the togetherness forces."* Implies opposition intensity is a *signal that differentiation is real*, not a sign of failure.
17. **"After the first person succeeds, the whole group rises, then another starts"** ⟹ **The family has a shared level that ratchets, and the level is set by the highest achieved by a key member rather than by an average.** Implies a group-level state variable coupled to individual states asymmetrically (upward-pulled by a leader).
18. **"You cannot change another person, only your own part"** ⟹ **Every member's behavior is jointly produced.** *"all family members play a part in everything that goes on in the family."* Implies no event in the family has a single-agent cause — a direct structural constraint on how a simulation should attribute events.
19. **"Devote primary attention to self and you automatically become more responsible toward others"** ⟹ **Responsibility-to-others and overinvolvement-with-others are inversely related, not positively related.** *"As he devotes primary energy to self, he automatically becomes more responsible toward others, and less irresponsibly overinvolved with others."* This inverts the folk model and is directly testable.
20. **"Help spouses discover their reflexes"** ⟹ **Reactivity is reflexive and out of awareness by default, but is partially bringable under conscious control by observation** — *"just as one can control a knee jerk with specific energy."* Implies each person has a small, effortful, awareness-mediated modulation on an otherwise automatic response.
21. **"Do not oversell; success needs a permissive surrounding institution"** ⟹ **Whether a family can change depends partly on the surrounding institutional emotional field**, not only on the family. The same technique performs differently in different institutional settings.
22. **"Do not attack symptoms as they surface"** ⟹ **Symptoms are outputs of an underlying process with conserved total intensity; removing one output re-routes rather than reduces.** The clearest statement is the projection-target one: *"Now that this has been greatly modified, the process will find new objects."*
23. **"Motivation gates access to the method"** ⟹ **Differentiation costs the person something real** — *"the emotional stress of moving to higher differentiation"* — so only *"a fraction of disturbed families"* will pay it. Implies a cost term, not a free upgrade.
24. **"Advanced projection is irreversible"** ⟹ **The projection process has a point of no return** — beyond it the symptom-bearer is *"so programmed to act the part of the pitiful one"* that the state is absorbing. Implies a hysteresis/absorbing-state boundary rather than a linear reversible scale.
25. **[p2] "Take your indecision to the emotionally removed, the literature, or isolation"** ⟹ **The family's convergence pressure is *sourced*, and any within-family source cancels the move.** Ch21 states this as two separate gates — secrecy, and ally cancellation. Ch18 gives the same rule from an independent chapter and names the three admissible substitutes. What it adds is the *trigger*: the pressure operates specifically at *"such moments of indecision"*, so the vulnerability is a state of the mover, not a constant.
26. **[p2] "Estimate the level as principle-determined self versus a solution to the anxiety of the moment"** ⟹ **The differentiation variable is operationally a planning horizon.** The low pole is defined by *what time-scale the decision optimises over*, not by an amount of any substance. This makes differentiation and the coach's objective function the same kind of quantity, and it is why a short-horizon greedy policy (C1) is the *low-differentiation* policy by definition rather than by coincidence.
27. **[p2] "The family's functional level is the head's level"** ⟹ **The family-level state variable is not a mean field.** Any implementation that computes family functioning or family anxiety as an average or sum over members contradicts the stated definition. Held against Ch12 and Ch20, which deny that the head is the required mover — see A6.

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **"Societal regression" was itself a mis-titling.** *"It was inaccurate to think of societal regression without the opposite process which is societal progression. The title was changed to 'Emotional Process in Society.'"* The chapter is published under the earlier framing while the note disowns it.
- **Six concepts became eight.** *"The family systems theory is made up of six separate interlocking theoretical concepts"* — followed immediately by *"Two additional concepts were officially added to the theory in 1975."* **Emotional cutoff** was *"formerly included partly in the nuclear family emotional process, and partly in the family projection process"* and was promoted to a separate concept. This chapter therefore contains both the six-concept and the eight-concept statement side by side.
- **Bowen's own explanation of the societal slump changed at the mid-1960s.** *"Until the mid 1960s, I considered society's slump to be functional, and perhaps a cyclical phenomena related to the depression of the 1930s or to World War II, and that after World War II man became lazy and greedy… I was guessing that he meet another challenge and rise to the occasion. After the mid 1960s there was more evidence of an even lower level of societal functioning."* The explanation shifted from post-war laziness/cycle to the **disappearance of land frontiers** plus population growth.
- **The scale distribution was not what he predicted.** *"When I first developed the differentiation of self scale in the late 1950s, I expected people would group themselves more evenly on the scale than has been the experience."*
- **"Nodal point" is used in two incompatible senses** (a person in the circuit; a point in time) without acknowledgment — see A5.
- **Cause-and-effect is condemned in theory but the whole societal argument is a causal chain.** Bowen builds an explicit causal sequence (loss of land frontiers → anxiety → lower functional differentiation → overleniency → criminals/riots) while insisting systems thinking *"is directed at getting beyond cause-and-effect thinking."* He does not reconcile these.
- **"Only a few areas" of difference, then a substantive one.** He says *"There are only a few areas in which societal patterns are slightly different from family patterns. Most of these have to do with the ways leaders come into, or are selected, for policy-making responsibility"* — but leader selection is precisely the mechanism his whole societal argument turns on, so the "slight" difference is load-bearing.
- **The direction of family↔society causation is stated both ways within a page.** *"Family and societal emotional forces function in reciprocal equilibrium to each other, each influencing the other and being influenced by the other"* — then the question *"whether family patterns influence society, or the obverse"* is raised and answered one way: *"Most of the evidence supports the thesis that the emotional orientation is set by society and such public officials are slowly forced into that mold."*
- **The evolution material is disclaimed as he presents it:** *"These ideas about evolution are presented, not because they have any direct relation to this paper or to systems theory, but to communicate that this systems theory has made a continuing effort to view man as an evolving integral part of life on earth."*

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The bridge between family and society was found in *change over time*, not in structural analogy.** The sixteen years of delinquency notes showed *"a changing process in the family and complementary change in society"* — the coupling was detected as *co-movement of two time series*, which is exactly what a simulation with societal dials feeding a family would be validated against.
- **The electronic/circuit model is Bowen's own explicit computational metaphor**, and he says so: *"I believe this may have been the point computer scientists reached a decade or two ago in theorizing about the construction of the human brain."* He states its scope and its one exclusion: *"appears to have the potential and the flexibility to accurately account for almost every item of human relationships that can be structured as facts of functioning, except for that which is determined by biology and reproduction and evolution."*
- **"Choice" is explicitly demoted to a system output.** *"all of the characteristics described under 'emotional reactiveness,' including all those 'choices' man appears to have, are all part of that part of man he shares with lower forms of life."* Each node has only *"a narrow range of choice in rejecting or transmitting impulses."*
- **Physical distance and emotional distance are treated as substitutable anxiety-relief resources, and the supply of physical distance is finite.** *"There is very little opportunity for new 'colonies' anywhere on earth."* **[p2] Correction to pass 1's reading:** what Bowen says depletes is not a stock consumed by use but the *knowledge that an escape exists* — *"It was important for him to know there was new land for him, **even if he never went to it**."* The relief is delivered by availability, not by consumption, so no per-move draw-down is stated. And mobile/travel-heavy jobs are presented as a substitute man **found** in the past twenty-five years, i.e. still available, not as the last rung before nothing. See P1.4.
- **Television is proposed as the specific transmission channel by which an intellectual fact became an emotional fact.** *"The main sensory modalities that operate in the emotional reactiveness of relationships are vision and hearing. It might be that television, which uses both modalities, was the most important factor."* Bowen's general rule: *"Man can 'know' something intellectually a long time before he 'knows' it as part of his total being."*
- **The "human pile" observation.** *"Society has been gravitating into the human piles in large urban centers where the individual may become more alienated from his fellowman than before. Group activity, including encounter groups, and promiscuous sexuality become panicky pretenses to overcome the alienation of too much fusion proximity to others."*
- **Density interacts with differentiation asymmetrically:** *"A relatively differentiated self can live a more orderly life whether alone, or in the middle of the human pile. A poorly differentiated person is not productive alone."*
- **Era-dependence of symptom form.** *"This girl could have been a periodically hospitalized, marginally adjusted mental patient if she had been born a generation earlier."* Same person, same level — different societal era, different symptom.
- **The impaired-child symptom can be over- or under-achievement at the same level of differentiation.** *"A better school adjustment would have been one in which the child would have been an overachiever… Another child of about the same level of differentiation and a little less free intellect… could become an underachiever to relieve the pressure of academic success, and still receive an acceptable amount of psychic energy directed to the poor school record."*
- **Adolescent group membership is a *sorted assignment* by level of self, and the group's activity profile is a function of the cutoff intensity.** *"each has its own specified type of activity determined by intensity of the negative 'cutoff' with parents."* The daughter belonged to *"one of the middle range 'groups'"*, with things her group would not do that lower groups did routinely, and things it did that better-integrated groups would not.
- **Provocation of parents is misread by observers.** The satisfaction is *"not because they enjoy hurting (as is commonly assumed), but from the satisfaction of 'being a grown-up self.'"*
- **The "emotional cocoon" dyad has a stated stability condition and a stated failure trigger.** Stable *"as long as they can keep the total investment in each other, and as long as neither works and the cocoon is not threatened by inner or outer forces."* Failure: *"neither is able to cope with serious illness, injury, or other serious reality need, and either will run away from the disabled one."*
- **Blame direction is a stable trait pair with a societal aggregate.** Overlenient parents blame society; disciplinarian parents blame themselves to each other; children of permissive parents blame parents for lack of love; children of disciplinarian parents blame parents for cruelty. *"The net result is an increasing trend to blame society for the problem and to expect society to find solutions."*
- **The words "humanitarian, responsive, sensitive" are analysed as the vocabulary of an overinvolved mother–child relationship.** *"It would be considered 'humanitarian' to invest so much of self in another person, 'responsive' to automatically know the needs of the other, and 'sensitive' to be constantly aware of feelings. All are relationship-oriented terms that ignore the field of goal-oriented activity."* Critically: *"A well differentiated person would automatically have these qualities, but a focus on them excludes a goal orientation."*
- **The differentiated middle/upper class is identified as the *engine* of the societal projection process, not a bystander.** *"the whole of the middle and upper classes of society spend a fair percentage of their time, energy, and money being concerned about and trying to be helpful to the less fortunate. The effort activates the family projection process and the well-to-do segment of society, through the projection process, further impairs the less fortunate."*
- **Bowen wants "crisis" replaced.** *"I think the word crisis should be removed from the term environmental crisis and replaced by a term implying a long-term process."* The stated reason is that crisis framing invites *"crash solutions directed at symptoms which lull people into the belief the problem is solved."*
- **The final claim is pessimistic and explicit:** *"man is not going to change the environment enough to correct the disharmony, and… the ultimate change will require an order of change in man he is not yet able to contemplate."*
- **Man functions better under adversity:** *"There is fair evidence that man functions at his best under adversity or when he is challenged."* This is a *positive* effect of stress, distinct from every other stress claim in the chapter.
- **[p2] Material resources appear inside three separate mechanisms, always as something the emotional process consumes or withholds.** Family financial reserves exhausted by the projection process and the spending stopped by the patient's *improvement*; parental money and privileges yielded to an adolescent as an anxiety-relieving move; and the emotional cocoon conditioned on *"as long as neither works"* — with the pair living *"without working except for part-time jobs in antiestablishment activity."* Employment is a **destabiliser** of fusion in this chapter, which reverses the usual sign on a material variable.
- **[p2] Job mobility is a per-family attribute; population density is not.** *"Families now have choices of work that require frequent moves for the entire family, or other jobs that require one spouse to travel most of the time."* That is a per-spouse job attribute and it is the means by which physical distance is actually obtained. Density — *"the human piles in large urban centers"* — is stated only as a societal condition, with no per-person counterpart.
- **Population growth is singled out as the one environmental variable under direct emotional-system control:** *"The population growth is one factor that is of key importance in the environmental problem and one under direct control of man's emotional system. Sex and reproduction is an instinct."*

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

### P1.1 — The sink count is not four. The chapter says three-plus-distance, twice, and pass 1 propagated the wrong one into the ledger and the convergences.

This was the assigned question and pass 1 is wrong where it matters most — not in `ch18.md`'s A2, which recorded both enumerations correctly, but in **L18.1** and in **C3**, which both read "Ch18 names four, adding emotional distance" and "this is where distance is promoted to a peer mechanism." It is not.

The chapter enumerates twice, and the two disagree.

- **Concept list:** *"the emotional problem becomes manifest as (a) emotional distance between the spouses; (b) dysfunction in one spouse which is manifested as physical illness, emotional illness, or social illness; (c) marital conflict; or (d) projection of the problem to one or more children."* Four lettered items, distance first, joined by *"or"*.
- **Clinical exposition, some forty lines later:** *"There is one way that almost all use to one degree or another. This is emotional distance from each other, which is hard to maintain over time. There are three important patterns to deal with this. One is marital conflict… Another pattern is the continuation of the fusion… The third pattern is transmission of the problem to the child."*

In the second passage distance is explicitly **not counted**. It is the mechanism *"almost all use"* — universal, always-on — and it is characterised by its **failure mode**: *"hard to maintain over time."* The three are what handles what distance cannot hold. That is the same three as Ch09 and Ch10, and it is exactly Ch16's formulation, which L16.2 already recorded correctly as *"emotional distance as an always-on fourth sink."*

So the corrected reading: **Ch18 corroborates Ch16 and does not contradict Ch09/Ch10.** Distance is neither a peer nor an aside — it is a distinct third thing: a universal, non-durable baseline whose insufficiency is the reason the other three exist. The four channels are also not symmetric on Bowen's own account: *"The projection of the family problem to children is so important it has been accorded a position as a separate theoretical concept."*

**Consequences.** `L18.1` and `C3` need editing. Any implementation that samples one of four sinks with a categorical split is wrong twice over: distance should be always-on and leaky, and the remaining three take what distance fails to hold.

### P1.2 — Cutoff is an input *and* an output. Pass 1's clean "input, not outcome" reading is half the loop, and the scope conclusion drawn from it is right for the wrong reason.

Assigned as Q-CUTOFF-INPUT, and the conclusion is *"about to change the project's scope"*, so it earns a hard look.

**Verified as stated.** *"The intensity of the process is governed by the degree of undifferentiation, by the degree of emotional cutoff with families of origin, and the degree of stress in the system."* Three inputs, verbatim, cutoff second. Corroborated by the directional claim: *"Denying and cutting off emotional contact with parental and extended families increases the intensity of symptoms in a family. Opening up meaningful relationships with the parental and extended families automatically reduces the tension and symptoms in the entire nuclear family."*

**But it is not only an input.** The same chapter makes cutoff an *output* in two places. Generationally: *"the denied emotional attachment to the past replicates itself with one's spouse and one's children"* — the cutoff a couple carries is the product of the previous generation's process. And within a generation: adolescent peer groups have *"its own specified type of activity determined by intensity of the negative 'cutoff' with parents"*, where cutoff is downstream of the child's programmed level. Ch16 already named this loop (L16.3, *"the more intense the cutoff… the more intense the next generation's cutoff"*). Ch18 supplies its within-generation half. So the correct shape is a **positive feedback loop**, not a boundary condition — which is a stronger claim than pass 1 made, not a weaker one.

**The scope conclusion holds, but the requirement is narrower than "simulate three generations."** What the chapter actually demands is:
1. a **per-person** state for unresolved attachment / cutoff to the family of origin, *"most frequently to the mother for both men and women"*, with a stated modal initialisation (*"the most common pattern is the partial cutoff"*) and a taxonomy of five handling styles;
2. that this state be **mutable in both directions by a move**, with the sign of the effect on nuclear symptoms stated;
3. that it be **independent of physical distance** — *"those who deny the attachment while living near the parents and who do more definite 'cutoffs' from parents than others who live at a distance"*;
4. that **family-of-origin functioning** be available as a predictor: assessment item two is *"an accurate estimate of emotional functioning in the families of origin of each spouse, the function that each had in that family, and efficiency with which he or she functioned."*

Items 1–3 are satisfiable inside a household model by two per-person scalars and one move. Only item 4 needs anything outside, and it needs *state*, not *simulation*. So: pass 1's "the model cannot be closed at the household" is correct as a statement about **state**, and overstated if read as requiring a simulated extended-family population. Say it that way in the ledger.

**One quantity pass 1 did not extract, and it constrains the coefficients:** the assessment items are given in explicit priority order — differentiation of each spouse first, family-of-origin functioning second, within-spouse patterns third. That orders the cutoff input **below** undifferentiation. Stress is not in the ranking.

### P1.3 — The "three anxiety regimes" is a claim about the observer, not about the dynamics. The integrator finding survives, with a truncated quote repaired.

Assigned as Q-INTEGRATOR. Both halves verify, but `L18.2` merges two different claims into one and the merged version would be implemented wrongly.

**Chronicity — verified, with a clause pass 1 dropped.** *"Any unit can recover from periodic panic or overloads, but when the panic becomes chronic one or more of the individual units can collapse (become sick), **and there are several variables for handling that**."* Duration-dependent, not level-dependent: confirmed, and an integrator is the right structure. But the modal is *"can collapse"*, and the trailing clause says explicitly that chronicity is **not sufficient** — there are unnamed moderators. Pass 1 quoted up to the parenthesis and stopped. Context also matters: this sentence sits inside the electronic-circuit passage about the panic amplification loop at the head of the unit, not in the general symptom-onset material, so *"unit"* is the family and *"individual units"* are members.

**Vanishing when calm — verified, but the chapter says it twice with different force, and pass 1 quoted the weaker one as if it were the stronger.**
- *"the functioning of the triangle patterns is **not observable** in a completely calm system"* — a measurement claim.
- *"The triangle emotional system is most intense when anxiety is high. It **disappears** when the system is calm."* — a system claim.

Only the second licenses latent triangles. Pass 1's M section used the second correctly; the ledger entry used the first.

**The correction that matters:** the non-monotone "invisible / legible / chaotic" triple is about *"the ability to observe and 'see' emotional reflexes"* — *"In low tension they are not observable, and in high tension, the reflexes are too chaotic to see much order."* That is an **observer** statement. The chapter's statement about the *dynamics* is monotone: *"all the patterns in a system are more pronounced when anxiety is high… any process that lowers the anxiety reduces the intensity of the patterns."* `L18.2` says "three regimes" as though the system behaves differently in three bands. It does not say that. The one piece of support for a genuine high-anxiety disorder regime comes from a different passage — the panic loop's *"poor handling of messages, disorderly and conflicting messages, and increasing paralysis of functioning."* Implement anxiety as a monotone gain on pattern intensity, a disorder term on message handling at the top, and the three-band structure only on the **readout**.

### P1.4 — Physical distance is not a depletable stock. It is an availability *belief*, and the mechanism pass 1 proposed is not in the text.

Assigned as Q-DEPLETABLE: is this Bowen's framing or pass 1's? Answer: the substance is Bowen's, the mechanism is pass 1's, and the mechanism is the part the model would have implemented.

Bowen states: *"In the past, man has used physical distance to relieve the tension of emotional fusion. Physical distance is harder to arrange with an exploding population"*; the regression *"may be related to the disappearance of land frontiers"*; after the war *"the colonial powers began to grant independence to their colonies, and it became more difficult for citizens to get away to a colony"*; *"There is very little opportunity for new 'colonies' anywhere on earth."*

Three corrections.

1. **Not consumption — availability.** *"It was important for him to know there was new land for him, **even if he never went to it**."* The relief is delivered by the knowledge that an exit exists. Nobody's distancing move draws the stock down. Pass 1's "a society-level reservoir the distancing move draws down and cannot replenish" describes a mechanism the chapter does not contain, and it would produce a completely different model: a scalar `perceived_escape_availability` entering the societal anxiety term for *every* agent, versus a consumable inventory decremented per move.
2. **The ordered ladder "frontiers → colonies → mobile jobs → nothing" is pass 1's synthesis, and the last two rungs are wrong.** Mobile and travel-heavy jobs are presented as what man **found** *"in the past twenty-five years"* — a currently-available substitute, not the rung before exhaustion. And "nothing" is not stated; *"very little opportunity for new 'colonies'"* is.
3. **The causal path runs through a delayed, channel-mediated awareness.** *"Man can 'know' something intellectually a long time before he 'knows' it as part of his total being"*; the emotional modalities are vision and hearing; *"It might be that television… was the most important factor"*; *"It was harder to doubt that his earth was one 'colony' after 1969."* So the chain is resource-loss → (lagged, audiovisual) awareness → anxiety, not resource-loss → anxiety.

### P1.5 — The decoupling guard holds, but pass 1 restated it circularly and dropped its precondition and its mechanism.
