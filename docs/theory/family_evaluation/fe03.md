## ⭐⭐⭐ FE03.1 — `M1.A.9a` **found in its primary source**, with both counterfeits named `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 3, in full:

> "Americans are sometimes referred to as a culture that values 'individuality' and the Japanese as a
> culture that emphasizes 'togetherness.' **This should not be equated with the meaning of these
> words in family systems theory.** … **Both 'rugged individualism' and obligatory conformity are
> strongly influenced by the togetherness force. The 'rugged individualist' operates as much in
> reaction to others as the compliant person.** His determination to be independent stems more from
> his reaction to other people than from a thoughtfully determined direction for self. **He has
> trouble being an 'individual' without permanently disrupting his relationships with others. The
> compliant person has difficulty maintaining his relationships with others without giving up his
> 'individuality.' Rugged individualism and compliance, therefore, are two sides of the same coin.**"

And the positive definition, footnote 1:

> "Sociobiologists use the word 'individuality' to describe the… capacity of an animal to be a
> separate and competent individual… To function as part of a society, an animal must **relinquish**
> its 'individuality'… **Individuality as defined by family systems theory, in contrast, refers to
> the capacity to be an individual while part of a group. The human is probably unique in the
> potential to be both an individual and a team player.**"

And the disambiguation that `M1.A.9a` exists to enforce:

> "**Autonomy does not mean selfishly following one's own directives; it means the ability to be
> self-determined. Self-determination could result in the choice to be guided by the best interests
> of the group.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.9a` is `[T]` in the primary source, and every clause of it is
independently supported.**

| `M1.A.9a` clause | Source |
|---|---|
| Two axes, not a scalar | "two sides of the same coin" — the same underlying quantity, opposite expressions |
| Outward impingement — the forceful declarer | "**rugged individualist**… trouble being an individual **without permanently disrupting his relationships**" |
| Inward impingement — the compliant accommodator | "**compliant person**… difficulty maintaining his relationships **without giving up his individuality**" |
| The differentiated position is **both low** | "the capacity to be an individual **while part of a group**"; "**both an individual and a team player**" |
| For self **without being selfish** | "Autonomy does not mean **selfishly** following one's own directives" |
| For other **without being selfless** | "Self-determination **could result in the choice to be guided by the best interests of the group**" |

**Three consequences for the spec.**

1. **The citation changes.** `M1.A.9a` currently cites `kb12` · K12.3 (ASR, no verbatim) with
   `KS24.4` as the two-axis formulation's provenance. Both should be replaced by `FE03.1`, which is
   written, quotable, and states the mechanism rather than the slogan.
2. **`M11.C.19`'s fixture is supplied.** The criterion needs a forceful-declarer arm and a
   compliant-accommodator arm at equal counterfeit magnitude. Here they are, defined as *the same
   magnitude of togetherness force expressed in opposite directions* — which is precisely "equal
   overall counterfeit magnitude" and is why a scalar detector cannot pass.
3. **The failure is defined by what each cannot do**, which is more implementable than the axes as
   currently written. The declarer cannot hold a position **without rupturing the tie**; the
   accommodator cannot hold the **tie without dropping the position**. Both are computable from the
   event log as a joint outcome over (position held?, tie intact?) — a 2×2 in which the
   differentiated cell is *both*, and the two counterfeits are the two off-diagonal cells. That is a
   cleaner operationalisation than a threshold on a hidden state, and it should go into `M5.F.2b`.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE03.2 — Investment is **equal on both sides by construction** — and the asymmetry is a readout artefact `[K]` `[T]`

> "Relationships are in balance because **each person invests an equal amount of 'life energy' in the
> relationship and each retains an equal amount of energy to direct his life separate from the
> relationship. If this is not the case, the relationship will not develop. People gravitate to
> those who are willing to make the same investment.**"

> "**While it may sometimes appear that one person's relationship focus is greater than the other's,
> appearances are deceiving. The person who seems more indifferent is just as dependent on and
> influenced by the relationship as the person who seems preoccupied with it.**"

And the valence rule, in the same passage:

> "**Negative thoughts and feelings serve the same function as positive ones because they also
> represent a relationship focus.**"

*Model consequence:* **a new invariant, and a readout trap the model can produce.**

`M1.B.8` requires `investment` to be **directed** and valence-blind. Valence-blindness is confirmed
in writing. But *directed* is now underspecified: `FE03.2` says the two directions are **equal by
construction on any emotionally significant tie**, and that observed asymmetry lives in the
*expression*, not the quantity. So:

- **New invariant candidate:** for any tie above the emotional-significance threshold,
  `investment(A→B) == investment(B→A)` to a stated tolerance, asserted with the rest of `M6`. The
  directed representation stays — the two ends still differ in what they *do* with it — but a run in
  which a pursuer accumulates more investment than the distancer is a **failing implementation**.
- **`M11.F.6`'s readout-trap list gains the sharpest entry yet**: the apparently indifferent partner.
  An implementation that infers investment from emitted move frequency will report exactly the wrong
  answer, and the model should be able to demonstrate that it does.
- It also supplies the **mechanism** behind `M2.A.0c`/`M2.A.0e`'s pairing rule. Spouses pair at
  comparable `basic_level` *because* "people gravitate to those who are willing to make the same
  investment" — so pairing is selection on **investment propensity**, and level-matching is the
  consequence rather than the rule. That is one mechanism doing two jobs, which `M10.A.2` prefers.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE03.3 — The relationship control loop, with a **two-sided error signal**, fully specified `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "**Each person carefully monitors the other for signs of change, signs of 'too little' or 'too
> much' involvement.** The signals monitored are primarily auditory and visual ones and include
> **body postures, tone of voice, facial expressions, and actual word content. Signals interpreted
> as signs of 'too little' involvement automatically trigger actions designed to restore a 'sense'
> of adequate attachment and signals interpreted as signs of 'too much' involvement automatically
> trigger actions designed to restore a 'sense' of adequate separation.**"

> "The perception (or misperception) of lack of sufficient separation can trigger feelings of being
> **crowded, trapped, controlled, smothered, or absorbed.** The perception (or misperception) of lack
> of sufficient connection can trigger feelings of being **isolated, unsupported, unloved, or
> rejected.**"

*Model consequence:* **this is `M4.C` → `M4.D` written as a closed-loop controller, and the model
should implement it as one.** Deviation from a set point, on a **signed** error, driving a
corrective move whose direction is the sign of the error. Everything the spec has — `M4.C.1`'s
appraisal, `M4.D.2`'s propensity, `PURSUE` and `DISTANCE` — is the same loop stated as separate
pieces.

Three things it adds:

- **The error is computed on the *perceived* value**, "real or imagined", which is where
  `M4.C.5`'s perception-side readout and `M9`'s belief layer enter the control path rather than
  sitting beside it. This is the concrete form of `FE02.7`'s missing edge.
- **The observable channel is named and it is narrow** — posture, tone, facial expression, word
  content. `M1.F.4a`'s register constraint and `FE02.12`'s arthritis case both operate on exactly
  this channel, which is why removing physical presence removed the load.
- **Two symmetric feeling-sets** for the two error signs. These are readouts, not states
  (`M1.A.0`), and they give `M11` something to assert: a crowded/trapped signature at negative
  error, an isolated/rejected signature at positive, and **neither should be derivable from the
  move type alone**.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE03.4 — Both misattributions are errors of the **same kind in opposite directions** — third instance `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Even though one person's actions may be totally governed by his subjective view of the
> relationship, those actions constitute an objective reality for the other person.** The other
> person's response to that objective reality may also be subjectively determined, but that response
> now constitutes an objective reality for the first person. **A person who feels rejected and
> characteristically blames that feeling on his own sense of inadequacy usually is underemphasizing
> an objective reality to which he is responding. A person who feels rejected and blames it on others
> usually is underemphasizing his own subjective distortion of reality. Systems thinking incorporates
> both sides of this 'equation.'**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE01.1`'s new-requirement candidate is now carried by three independent
passages in three chapters** (`FE01.1` the projection cycle, `FE02.12` the arthritis couple,
`FE03.4` stated as a general rule), and this one **fuses it with `FE01.5`'s definition of
`systems_perspective`**:

- The inward-blamer under-weights **field→self**.
- The outward-blamer under-weights **self→field**.
- Systems thinking is the conjunction — exactly `FE01.5`'s "focus simultaneously… if one emphasizes
  one side, a systems perspective is lost."

So the asymmetric belief error and `systems_perspective` are **the same two-term quantity read at
different points**: perspective is the balance of the two attributions, and the misattribution is
which term is being dropped. That means the model needs **one** mechanism, not two — `M10.A.2`
satisfied — and `M1.A.18a`'s two-sided readout becomes computable as *which side of the equation
this agent's attributions are ignoring*. **Requirement candidate, and it should be written as an
amendment to `M4.C.6` and `M1.A.18a` rather than as a new ID.**

## ⭐⭐ FE03.5 — The scale, with numbers the project did not have `[K]` `[#]`

Footnote 8:

> "In developing the concept of the scale of differentiation, **Bowen used from 0 to 100** to describe
> the increments of difference between people. **A very poorly differentiated person, for example,
> would be in the 0 to 10 range. The 'average' human being is not very autonomous and 40 might be
> considered a median for the species.** Bowen recognized that **nobody functions with complete
> autonomy or 100**, but he defined that level to emphasize that a well differentiated person is not
> completely differentiated. **By present standards, a well differentiated person might be considered
> over 60 and a very well differentiated person over 70** on the scale."

*Model consequence:* **`M10.C.2`'s calibration-target list gains entries, with care.**

| Figure | Status |
|---|---|
| 0–100 range | confirms `M1.A.2`; already held |
| **median ≈ 40 for the species** | **new** — the project had `KS05.4`'s "~90% in the lower half" but no central tendency |
| very poorly differentiated = **0–10** | new, and it bounds `M1.A.4d`'s capacity floor at 25 from below |
| well differentiated **> 60**, very well **> 70** | new |
| 100 unreachable | confirms `M2.A`'s `Dr Halim` at 55 being the highest agent in the instance |

**Three cautions, all of which must travel with the numbers.** (a) Kerr hedges every one —
"might be considered", "by present standards" — so these are `[#]` *stated quantities* and not
measurements; `M0.5`'s parameter rule is unaffected. (b) They are **band edges on a construct with
no instrument**, which `M10.C.2b` already forbids presenting as precision. (c) A median of 40 with
the behavioural transition at 50 (`M1.A.3`) means **the median human is below the transition** —
which is consistent with `M1.A.18c`'s population requirement and with `KS05.4`, and is worth
stating explicitly in `M2.A`, because the reference family's levels (29–44 for members) sit squarely
around this median and that is now a sourced choice rather than an arbitrary one.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.6 — Objectivity includes a **recursive stability** clause — and it modulates `M4.C.8` `[K]` `[T]`

Figure 2's caption:

> "**The term objectivity describes the fact that a person can think about a communication from
> another in a way that is not influenced by his subjective, feeling, and emotional responses to that
> communication. Objectivity also describes the fact that a person can think about subjectivity,
> feelings, and emotions without triggering more subjectivity, feelings, and emotions.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M4.C.8` is currently unconditional and should not be.**

`M4.C.8` requires that directing attention at the feeling channel **increase** its activity — the
volcano. `FE03.6` says the *second* property of objectivity is precisely that attention to feeling
**does not** trigger more of it. These are the same operation at two levels of the same variable.
So:

**`M4.C.8`'s amplification MUST be gated by objectivity** (`systems_perspective`, per `M1.A.18`, or
its per-tie attenuated value per `M1.A.18d`) — high at low objectivity, approaching zero at high.
Without the gate the model cannot represent a coach or a differentiated member *thinking about* the
emotional process, which is the entire content of `M1.E.7c`'s category supply and delayed
self-observation. As written, `M4.C.8` would make every act of reflection an escalation.

This also explains `M1.E.7c.4`'s six-month tape rule from the other side: the delay lets acute
anxiety decay so that objectivity is high enough for attention **not** to amplify.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.7 — The sink is chosen; the **amount is not** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**An 'emotional cocoon' and a conflictual relationship can be at the same level of differentiation.
> At a given level, there is just as much focus on one another and just as much energy consumed,
> irrespective of the type of interaction.** The relationship dilemma has simply been managed with
> different responses to the same basic pressures. **The intensity of attachment and degree of
> problem in a harmonious relationship, in other words, can be just as great as in a conflictual
> relationship. In one instance the problem has been bound in harmony and in the other it has been
> bound in disharmony.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M6.I.1`'s conservation stated as a claim about observables, in writing.**
The budget is set by level; the *allocation* across sinks is free. This is the single clearest
statement of the invariant in the corpus and it should be quoted at `M6.I.1`.

It is also `M11.F.6`'s **apparently-fine-marriage** readout trap with its mechanism: harmony is not
the absence of the quantity, it is one of the places the quantity goes. And it gives `M11` a shape
check — two arms at matched `basic_level` driven to different sink allocations must show **equal
total** bound anxiety and **different** locations, which is `M11.C.13`'s count-and-location scoring
applied inside a single family.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.8 — Conflict **externalises** and thereby protects the individuals `[K]` `[T]`

> "**To the extent that the anxiety generated by the emotional interdependence is acted out in the
> interaction (the opposite of surface calm), the individuals do not absorb it within themselves.
> This externalization of anxiety provides some protection for each person from the development of
> clinical symptoms. When conflict prevails, it is the relationship that is symptomatic.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.11a`'s internalise/externalise axis, applied to the sinks rather than to
the symptom channels.** The spec has the axis on `symptom_load`'s three channels — physical and
mental internalise, social externalises — and nothing connecting it to `M1.D.1`'s sink allocation.
Here it is: **the marital-conflict sink is the externalising one, and choosing it lowers per-person
symptom load while raising the tie's.**

That makes `M11.C.12`'s result (curing a symptom raises conflict) and this one the same equation
read in opposite directions, and it means the model's sink allocation and its symptom channel are
**coupled** rather than independent. Worth stating; it is a constraint, not a new mechanism.

"When conflict prevails, **it is the relationship that is symptomatic**" also gives `M1.B` a
readout the spec lacks: symptom load at the **tie** level, which `M1.D.2a` already requires for
distance and which should be the same field.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.9 — Lock-in is **non-monotone**: past a point the symptom destabilises `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 18:

> "**If the dysfunction of a family member becomes excessive, his poor functioning can have more of a
> destabilizing than stabilizing impact on the family. In such instances, if the dysfunctional person
> leaves the family, the family's anxiety level may actually decrease.**"

Footnote 27, the same for the individual:

> "**At high levels of intensity, symptoms can have more of a destabilizing than stabilizing function
> for both the individual and the family.** Severe chronic illness can place so many demands and
> constraints on the patient and the family that **people are more drained than stabilized by its
> presence.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.D.2a` MUST be amended, and `M11.C.22` with it.** As written, symptom
lock-in is unconditional: once a member carries load, family anxiety falls and the configuration
resists removal. `FE03.9` says the relation **inverts above a threshold** — and this is the
mechanism behind `KS20.8`'s Gilmore result (the family relaxed when he was removed), which the spec
currently files under `M7.D.2a` as a *consequence* of lock-in. It is the opposite: it is what
happens **past** lock-in.

So the sink's contribution to family anxiety is an inverted-U in symptom severity, and
`M11.C.22`'s removal arm should show **relief only in the high-severity arm** and resistance in the
moderate one. That is a two-arm direction test, which is the shape `M0.4` requires, and the current
single-direction criterion would pass on a monotone implementation that is wrong.

This is `M11.F.6`'s two-sidedness reaching the model's newest mechanism — the fourth requirement in
this run to need the same correction.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.10 — Both parties lose **flexibility** even when the transfer is zero-sum `[K]` `[T]`

> "The one who adapts more '**loses**' self to the relationship and the one who adapts less '**gains**'
> self from the relationship. 'Losing' and 'gaining' of self are relative to one another. **In
> reality, both people 'lose' in that, by participating in preserving the relationship balance
> through adaptations to relieve anxiety, they each become part of a less flexible relationship
> system.** Adaptations to relieve anxiety may favor the functioning of one person more than another,
> but **the process puts constraints on both people.**"

And the variable is named, repeatedly:

> "the relationship loses some of its '**emotional reserve**' or **flexibility**"; "Very well
> differentiated relationships… have a **high level of 'emotional reserve' which makes it very
> adaptive to stress. Intense and protracted stress, however, can exceed the limits of the reserve**
> and a clinical symptom may then develop."

*Model consequence:* **a second quantity alongside `M6.I.4`'s conserved pseudo-self, and the spec
has no place for it.**

`M6.I.4` makes the exchange zero-sum. `FE03.10` says the exchange **also** consumes a tie-level
resource that neither party receives — flexibility — and that this resource is what **gates symptom
onset** under stress. That is exactly what `M4.D.5a`'s accommodation stock accumulates, with the
sign reversed.

**Recommendation: derive, do not add.** Per `M10.A.2`, `emotional_reserve` should be a **readout**
— capacity as a function of `basic_level`, minus `M4.D.5a`'s monotonically-growing accommodation
stock — and the symptom threshold should be evaluated against it. This costs no new state, gives
`M4.D.5a` a consequence it currently lacks (it accumulates and nothing reads it), and produces
`FE03.21`'s severity ladder for free.

## ⭐⭐ FE03.11 — Genes set traits; **differentiation sets how much a trait governs functioning** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 7:

> "**Genes appear to influence neither the strength of a person's togetherness orientation nor his
> capacity to be an individual. While some personality traits might have a genetic basis, the degree
> to which a trait governs a person's functioning depends on the level of differentiation or
> individuality.**"

*Model consequence:* **this resolves `FE02.19`'s contested passage and confirms `M1.A.11b`'s
shape.** `FE02.19` claimed the emotional process may reach gene expression, which appeared to
threaten `M1.A.11b`'s requirement that constitution be exogenous. `FE03.11` states the relation
precisely and it is a **gating** one: constitution supplies the trait, level supplies how much the
trait governs behaviour.

That is structurally identical to `M1.A.14c`'s sibling-position rule — position carries both an
adaptive and a maladaptive expression and `basic_level` selects which, gating rather than adding.
**Three mechanisms now share one shape** (sibling position, symptom channel, personality trait),
which is a strong argument for implementing the gate once and applying it, and for `M11.C.9`'s
mid-scale peak being a general prediction rather than a sibling-position quirk.

## ⭐ FE03.12 — Fantasy content is a **reliable, inverted** readout of the balance `[K]` `[T]`

> "when a husband is responding emotionally to the perception of too much involvement or pressure
> from his mate, he may have fantasies of **hurting, demeaning, or running away** from her… If
> circumstances change, however, and impose some physical distance on the relationship, or if the
> mate emotionally withdraws, **the emotional response to too much involvement may quickly subside.
> Fantasies may then shift to a preoccupation with positive images of the mate and sexual
> involvement. This correlation of psychic state with the relationship balance can be so predictable
> that the content of feelings and fantasies can be used as a fairly reliable indicator of the status
> of that balance.**"

*Model consequence:* another `M11.F.6` readout trap, and an unusually sharp one because the sign is
**inverted from the naive reading**: hostile fantasy indicates *too much* closeness, warm fantasy
indicates *too much* distance. Any affect-derived measure of tie quality will read backwards. It is
also a second instance of `FE03.3`'s controller — the fantasy is the error signal made visible — and
`M9`'s belief store is where it would live.

Note the honest limit: "**fairly** reliable indicator", clinical impression, no series.

## ⭐ FE03.13 — Relationships **deteriorate by default**, at a rate set by level `[K]` `[T]` `[#]`

> "**Relationships, like unstable chemical elements, tend to deteriorate. The time of onset, the
> rate, and the magnitude of the deterioration are strongly influenced by the level of
> differentiation.** The lower the level, the higher the probability that deterioration will begin
> very early and progress rapidly. There often is a quite tangible point when the first signs of
> less tolerance and more irritability appear. **Some relationships maintain a relaxed state for only
> a few weeks. Once deterioration begins, it can progress to the point of disruption within just a
> few months.** Other relationships, of course, endure a lifetime of stresses."

Figure 6's caption states the same as a monotone trend: "**the average level of chronic anxiety
tends to increase over time.**"

*Model consequence:* **a default dynamic the spec does not have.** `M1.B.4` fixes `bond_energy`
decay at ~zero and `M1.B.12` uses `dyad_age` only to raise the cost of a pole flip. Nothing makes a
tie's *generated* anxiety rise with time in configuration. `FE03.13` requires it, with the rate
inverse to `basic_level` and — usefully — with **two `[#]` anchors at the low end**: relaxed for
weeks, disruption within months.

This matters for `M11.C.17`'s no-drift arm. Without a default deterioration term the coach-free arm
is *flat*, which passes the criterion trivially; with it, the arm should show **downward** drift in
tie state while `basic_level` stays flat, which is a much stronger and more falsifiable claim.
**Requirement candidate.**

## ⭐ FE03.14 — The symptom ladder: acute-and-reversible → chronic-and-structural `[K]` `[T]` `[D]`

> "**Very well differentiated relationships**… spawn clinical symptoms **only when the relationship
> is significantly stressed by external events**… **Moderately differentiated relationships**…
> require **less additional stress**… for acute symptoms to appear. **When the anxiety associated
> with the stress subsides, the symptoms disappear. In a poorly differentiated relationship, the
> pressure to adapt is so constant and significant that chronic symptoms tend to be a feature of the
> relationship balance.** Additional stress increases the severity of the chronic symptoms. **In very
> poorly differentiated families… it is common for several family members to have chronic symptoms.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.1` is currently weaker than the source.** The criterion asserts that
the lower-`basic_level` family reaches symptom threshold *sooner*. `FE03.14` says the difference is
not only in timing but in **kind**: at high level symptoms are acute and remit with the stressor;
at low level they are chronic and persist as part of the balance. And the count changes — "several
family members" at the bottom.

So `M11.C.1` should assert three directions across the arms, all of which follow from
`FE03.10`'s reserve formulation with no new mechanism: **onset sooner**, **remission slower or
absent**, and **more members symptomatic**. That is a strictly stronger test at no extra cost.

## ⭐ FE03.15 — Distance is universal, and blocking it makes anxiety **reappear** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**One response to the pressure to adapt, however, is a feature of all relationships. This response
> is emotional distance.** … With distancing, **anxiety 'disappears' and is manifested in people's
> avoidance of one another and of potentially upsetting subjects. If people are pushed together or
> try to discuss emotionally charged issues, the anxiety reappears.** While distance can provide some
> stability to a relationship, **it is a compromise. Comfort achieved by emotional closeness is
> balanced against comfort achieved by emotional distance.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.D.2a` in 1988, with every clause intact** — distance binds rather than
destroys, the binding is *readable as the avoidance itself*, and prevention returns the anxiety to
the people. The spec cites `KS04.1` (2019) and `K04.1` (ASR); this is earlier and written.

It also names **topic avoidance as part of the distance mechanism** — "avoidance of one another
**and of potentially upsetting subjects**" — which puts `M1.B.10`'s `taboo_set` inside the distance
channel rather than beside it, exactly as `FE02.9` requires.

## ⭐ FE03.16 — The overfunctioner can lose **more** self than the underfunctioner `[K]` `[T]` `[X]`

> "**In fact, there are times when the overfunctioner loses more self to the process than the
> underfunctioner. The overfunctioner may eventually feel more pressure to adapt to the
> underfunctioning of the underfunctioner than the underfunctioner feels to adapt to the
> overfunctioning of the overfunctioner.**"

*Model consequence:* **flag against `M6.I.4`.** The invariant makes one party's functional gain equal
the other's loss, with the over-functioner as the gainer. `FE03.16` says the *adaptive pressure* can
run the other way while the functional positions stay put — so **self-transfer direction and
functional position are not locked together**.

This is consistent with `FE02.11`'s phobia case, where the overfunctioner is the one who breaks, and
with `M11.C.21`'s inversion on projector collapse. It is not a contradiction of `M6.I.4` — the
conserved quantity still moves one way at a time — but it means an implementation that reads
transfer direction **off** the functioning balance will be wrong in the cases that matter most.
Record as a constraint on how `M6.I.4` is computed, and carry it to pass 2 for reconciliation
against `L03.1` and `KS07.2`.

## ⭐ FE03.17 — Withdrawal from the relationship system, graded by level `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Very poorly differentiated people may eventually shun all relationships to avoid the discomfort
> that is associated with them. The 'street people' are in this category.** They avoid and are
> avoided by others because of the problems generated by enduring relationships. '**Insulation**'
> from their exquisite sensitivity to the emotional environment **is achieved through chronic
> psychosis, alcoholism, and drug addictions. Slightly better differentiated people may become
> 'relationship nomads.' When the process gets too intense, they change relationships.** People with a
> little more differentiation but who are still quite vulnerable **may avoid intense involvements and
> successfully maintain a relationship network of less intense relationships**, ones that allow more
> distance."

## FE03.20 — Boredom, and satisfaction diverging from attachment `[K]` `[T]`

> "As differentiation decreases, people look more to one another to provide a sense of well-being,
