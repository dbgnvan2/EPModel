# ⚠ INDEPENDENCE AUDIT — read before trusting any chapter count

Chapter counts overstate evidential weight. Bowen reports the **same clinical material** across many
papers, so agreement between chapters is often one study reported twice.

**The NIMH live-in cluster (1954–59) covers Ch01, Ch02, Ch03, Ch04, Ch05, Ch07, Ch08, Ch16 (in part)
and Ch19.** Nine chapters, one residential project. Ch08 adds Menninger/Topeka 1949–54; Ch09 onward
draw on Georgetown private practice; Ch11 on the 1965 multiple-family pilot; Ch21 on Bowen's own
family (n = 1); Ch13, Ch17, Ch18 and Ch20 carry no clinical series at all.

**C5 is the casualty.** Four chapters, one setting, two case series, one of which (Ch02/Ch04) is
double-counted — and **Ch05 states the opposite**, that the family *leaders* move first, usually the
overadequate mother.

**✅ C5 better resolved, 2026-08-26.** The project's reconciliation was *two roles, not one contested
slot* — the leader drives, the peripheral member performs the visible change. Bowen's own account is
simpler and accommodates both observations without needing two roles:

> "**a more differentiated leader began to emerge. Instead of blaming the other person, he focused on
> himself.** … **After several weeks, the leader's wife began the same slow process** … **The
> differentiation pattern is one in which leadership shifts back and forth, until the entire family is
> safely out of the danger zone of undifferentiation.**" **`[B]`** `FE11.6`

**It is a relay.** "Leaders first" and "peripheral member first" are one alternating process sampled
at different moments, and neither is the rule. C5's weakness stands as an *evidential* judgement —
four chapters, one setting — but the apparent contradiction with Ch05 is dissolved.

## ⭐ The one place this corpus has independent witnesses — added 2026-08-26

Everything above is Bowen reporting his own clinical material, so the audit can only ever count
**settings**. **One source escapes that.** Kerr & Bowen, *Family Evaluation* (1988): Bowen wrote the
Epilogue without sight of Kerr's chapters, and says so in its opening paragraph.

> "**Dr. Kerr has written a major portion of the book, without my knowledge of its content. This has
> been purposeful.**" **`[B]`** `FE11.0`

Six claims are stated **separately by both men** in that volume:

| Claim | Kerr `[K]` | Bowen `[B]` | Bears on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Projection is **mutual**, not one-way | `FE07.4` fn 22 | `FE11.3` — "qualities are transferred in the process of **each describing the other**" | `M7.E.1c`, `M9` |
| The general-systems objection | `FE00.1`, `FE01.14` | `FE11.2` | `M11.F.3` |
| Three-outcome transmission | `FE04.19` | `FE11.4` | `M7.E.1a` |
| Dogmatism is negative evidence for self | `FE04.4`, `FE06.13` | `FE11.19` | `M5.F.2a` |
| The family is not the system's boundary | `FE10.9` | `FE11.14` | `M1.D.8`, `M2.3` |
| Basic and functional levels separable **by intervention** | `FE04.6` | `FE11.5` | `M1.A.5` |

That is not a large number, and it is **the project's first clean two-witness convergence**.

**⚠ The limit, stated plainly.** Independence of *drafting* is not independence of *training*. Kerr had
been in Bowen's programme for twenty years, and Bowen says so in the same paragraph — "**He probably
knows more about my theoretical, therapeutic, and organizational orientation than any other person.**"
Two observers from **one school**, writing without sight of each other. Better than co-authorship;
weaker than two schools. Score it as **one setting, two observers** — which is still a category the
rest of this table does not contain.

## C1 — Relief opposes progress ⚠ **SCOPE DISPUTED AT PASS 2** *(was 11 chapters; Ch14's and Ch22's contributions are withdrawn or contradicted)*

> **Read before using.** Ch22 contains a **direct counterexample** — reducing cut-off reduces symptoms
> *and* makes therapy more productive. Ch14's "gets worse before it gets better" was built from a risk
> clause, not an observation. Ch22's "motivation budget" mechanism was invented by pass 1.
> The finding likely survives as a narrower claim — **symptom relief obtained *without* structural
> change opposes progress** — but pass 3 must re-derive its scope from the chapter texts, not from
> these summaries.

The single most corroborated finding in the corpus, and it **inverts the coach's objective function.**

- Ch02/03: when the parents change their functioning, the patient becomes *more* disturbed.
- Ch06: symptom relief *reduces* motivation for differentiation.
- Ch07: the marriage stays harmonious "as long as the disabled spouse does not recover."
- Ch10: symptom relief and differentiation are independent axes that can move oppositely.
- Ch11: symptom relief **and increased togetherness** are why families quit — the reward the system
  delivers early is the very quantity the work exists to reduce.
- Ch12: the wife's drinking remits, and her regaining self is followed by intense marital conflict.
- Ch18: helping-programme intensity **impairs recipients** — the same finding at societal scale.
- Ch20: offering solutions "worked very well" short-run while building dependence and blocking
  staff from developing responsibility for their own solutions.
- Ch14: he abandoned a format that produced "high praise… but no basic change". *(The "accepts a
  temporary increase" half is withdrawn — it is a risk clause, not an observation.)*

**Model consequence.** The coach cannot optimise for family anxiety or symptom load. The correct
trajectory gets worse before better, so any short-horizon greedy policy rejects the right move. Needs
two outcome axes and a coach objective defined on the structural one.

## C2 — Reciprocal functioning is a conserved signed transfer on the dyad *(**6**: 03, 04, 05, 06, 09, 12 — Ch07 withdrawn at pass 2: sixteen of its propositions rewrite Ch06's near-verbatim, so it is not an independent witness. ~4 independent settings.)*

Not two independent per-person traits. One gains exactly what the other loses.

Properties, accumulated across the seven: no stable midpoint (03, 04, 05); either party can occupy
either pole (03, 05, 06 — the same woman held opposite poles in two marriages); the pole is assigned
**by acts**, notably decisions, which is why these families avoid deciding (04); the assignment
**hardens with time in configuration** (05, 12); reversal is **direction-asymmetric** — far easier to
tone down the overfunctioner than to raise the dysfunctioner (12); the deficit is **absorbed by the
symptom** (07); and transfer magnitude scales **inversely with basic level** (09), with the first
real numbers — husband at 55 drawing from a wife at 15, both basic ~35.

**Model consequence.** `functioning_balance` must become bistable and hysteretic rather than a linear
scalar, with an asymmetric reversal cost and a stickiness term.

## C3 — Anxiety is conserved with substitutable discharge channels *(5: 01, 08, 09, 10, 18 — Ch11 withdrawn at pass 2; and Ch01/Ch08 share the NIMH ward, so this is fewer independent studies than chapters)*

Blocking one channel reroutes the flow; it does not reduce it.

Ch11 is an unintentional controlled experiment — the ward closed channels one at a time and each
closure produced the next displacement, never dissipation. Ch08 measured it: staff became *more*
helpful and the incident **rate stayed flat** while the incidents relocated. Ch09 names the allocation
explicitly — a fixed quantity of "immaturity" spread across marital conflict, spouse dysfunction, and
transmission to children, with overflow. Ch01 documents the target queue.

Ch18 supplies the input list — undifferentiation, **cutoff with families of origin**, and stress.
**Corrected at pass 2: the sink count is THREE in every chapter**, with emotional distance an
always-on universal baseline explicitly *outside* the set and outside the budget (Ch09, Ch12, Ch16,
Ch18 all agree). There was never a three→four transition — pass 1 manufactured it.

**Model consequence.** The conservation invariant already adopted is correct and now well sourced.
Adds an overflow term and the **adversarial test**: a coach that supplies help should produce the
same incident count in different places, not fewer.

## C4 — The investment seesaw: the marital tie gates the child's symptom *(5: 02, 03, 04, 05, 06)*

Ch04 has the sharpest formulation — parents more invested in each other than in the patient, the
patient improves; either more invested in the patient, the patient "immediately and automatically"
regresses. Ch05 times it: one unilateral stand, symptoms gone in **three days**, collapsed at **one
month** because the marital relationship had not changed. Ch06 adds the qualifier that the load is
**directed parental attention**, not parenting content — a psychologically "correct" project does the
same damage. Ch04 supplies the adversarial test: with marital distance high, *all* management
approaches fail equally.

## C5 — The peripheral / inadequate member moves first, and it is a precondition *(4: 01, 02, 03, 04)*

> **⚠ Weakest convergence in the file.** Four chapters, **one setting, two case series**, and Ch02/Ch04
> are the *same* series. **Ch05 states the opposite** — "The family leaders were the first to begin
> working on differentiation of self", usually the **overadequate** mother. Keep Ch05's own
> reconciliation: **the leader drives the process, the peripheral member performs the visible position
> change. Two roles, not one contested slot.**

Ch04 is the hardest form: six families, six attempts, every one failed. *Corrected at pass 2:* the six
failures are attributed **in-text to the mother's non-relinquishment**; the emotional-divorce sentence
is a separate claim with **no case evidence attached**. And the gate is on the **primary relationship
position** — contact-level closeness is granted freely.

**Model consequence.** Two changes. Propensity for the differentiating move is **not** monotonic in
functioning — my design has it rising with FD, which predicts the wrong mover. And the repertoire
needs **gating**: some moves are unavailable until a structural precondition holds elsewhere.

## C6 — The outside position is the intervention, not the professional's skill *(6 chapters / **5 independent** — Ch01 and Ch19 are the same NIMH residential project: 01+19, 09, 10, 17, 20)*

Ch10 generalises it explicitly — "would probably proceed with any third person… no matter what the
subject matter" — and states the converse: the therapist's neutrality and a family member's
self-control are **the same operation from different positions**. Ch01 shows the coach is capturable
by topology while holding excellent intellectual understanding. Ch19 closes the escape hatch — the
doctor's **silence is captured too**, given "the weight of a professional opinion", so abstention is
not an exit. Ch20 supplies the detector: the coach's earliest signal of rising system anxiety is its
**own** urge to become critical.

**Model consequence.** Rewrite acceptance test 7. The coach needs no special mechanism and its
detriangling must not be a high-FD parameter; it is a position, and the configuration decides. But it
does need **its own drifting internal state** — six chapters now make the coach's reactivity an
observable rather than a constant. Ch17 goes furthest: the observer *changes the configuration*, so a
costless external coach models something Bowen says does not exist.

## C7 — Coupling does not decay with distance or separation *(4: 02, 06, 08, 10 — MECHANISM REVISED at pass 2: separation is edge-rewiring with the old edge dormant, not a slow-decaying edge on an isolated node. See `_PASS2_CORRECTIONS.md`.)*

Ch02 is the earliest and most direct source, found only on the second read: **"The surface
distance controls a deeper interdependence on each other."** Distance is a *regulator over an
undiminished coupling*, not an attenuation of it. Then: a 22-year-old away at college with vacation
contact only responded to parental change "almost as
dramatically" as a preadolescent at home (06). Reunite an institutionalised triad and "the old
emotional fusion is immediately operative again" (08). Cutoff is a move inside the system, never an
exit — the runaway fuses into a new family and reproduces the pattern (10).

**Model consequence.** Independent confirmation, from sources decades older, of the bond-energy term
proposed in §4.3. Sets its decay rate near zero. Kills any distance- or contact-frequency-based
conductance: **access gates which moves are available; it does not attenuate coupling.**

## C8 — Calendar time, not contact frequency *(**2: 11, 14** — Ch22 withdrawn at pass 2, its comparison varies frequency and focus together; and Ch14's version is the weakened retelling of Ch22's. The one-year honeymoon is mis-filed here — it belongs to the ward's open-system conversion.)*

Monthly ≈ or > weekly in both. "It takes a certain amount of time on the calendar… not decreased by
increasing the frequency of appointments." About **four years** for a motivated family; the honeymoon
effect decays after about **a year**. Ch14's comparison is the stronger one because the group with
*no* formal therapy did as well. Ch22 gives the dosage band directly — monthly to about six one-hour
appointments a year, with **1–2 a year failing** — and names the real rate limit: the person's own
between-session effort, not contact with the coach.

## C9 — The differentiating move is a state machine with a reaction ladder *(3: 13, 21, 22)*

Three chapters independently describe the same multi-step sequence, at increasing resolution.

Ch13: define self → immediate opposition ("selfish and mean and does not love the others") → hold
course → a **final intense attack** → if the mover stays calm the opposition calms and pulls up, and
then another, and another. Abort branches: **defend, counterattack, go silent** — anger is the abort
condition.
Ch21 gives the ladder verbatim — **"You are wrong" → "Change back" → "If you do not, these are the
consequences"** — a mandatory next-day follow-up, and the diagnostic rule that **absence of reaction
means the move did not land**.
Ch22 adds the preconditions — but **on the mover's engagement axis, not on family anxiety**: serious
illness and death, the *highest*-anxiety events, are **enabling**; what backfires is the mover's own
"emotional confrontation". Calm is a delay, not a gate.

**Model consequence.** I-POSITION is not one move with a propensity. It is a sequence with a
precondition, a scripted escalation peak, a calm-check gate, a follow-up window with revert-on-timeout,
a whole-family payoff, and contagion to later movers. Ch21 adds three efficacy gates that multiply it: **secrecy** (for latency and undivided ownership, not
information leakage), **outside-ness** (three inputs, not one), and **ally displacement** — an ally
opens a new peripheral triangle, which Bowen calls "my undoing". *Corrected at pass 2: he never says
an ally **cancels** the move.*

## C10 — An act's identity depends on the actor's hidden state, not on the act *(3: 13, 20, 21)*

Confirmed at pass 2, missed entirely by pass 1.

Ch13: "to 'stand up to' means to attack and shock the other… and to get away with breaking rules" —
the same act is a differentiating move at one level and an assault at another.
Ch20: accepting **blame** versus taking **responsibility for the part self plays** — "a fine line",
discriminated by "the inner orientation of self".
Ch21: until the mover is partly outside the system, an identical technique is "either hollow
meaningless words or a hostile assault on the system, **and an emotional system knows the difference**."

**Model consequence.** A move's effect cannot be a function of the move type and the tie alone. It is
multiplied by a hidden state of the actor that the *receivers* can read but the actor may not.
That is the same gate as `outside_ness`, and it applies to family members and the coach alike.

**The scale is sourced — in Ch16, and it does not match the model.** 0–100 with profiles at 0–25 /
25–50 / 50–75 / 75–100, a **qualitative regime change at 50**, and 75–100 withdrawn as "more
hypothetical than real". The repo's `C ∈ [10, 80]` with a linear clip has no threshold at all.
Ch04, 05, 07 and 14 give no scale numbers. **Ch09 (1966) states the endpoints AND all four quarters**,
plus the 55 / 15 / basic-35 arithmetic — so the quartile history is **non-monotonic**: present 1966
(Ch09) → absent 1972 (Ch21, which has **no regime change at 50** and an explicitly *gradual*
lower/upper transition) → present again 1976 (Ch16). Ch16 is the **only** source for the band numbers,
and Ch17 has none. Ch13's 55/60 figures are explicitly disclaimed. Ch17 adds the population skew —
~90% in the lower half — and the warning that the top profile is extrapolation.

**Two caveats that must travel with any use of the number.** Bowen *abandoned* the clinical scale
because readers wanted it without the concept (Ch16), and *stopped* his own scale-level research to
prevent misuse (Ch17). The scale is a construct with no external validation offered anywhere in this
book.
