# Ch14 — Family Therapy After Twenty Years

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** *Not stated.* The chapter carries no dateline, venue, or "presented at" note. It can only be dated internally: family therapy "came on the psychiatric scene in the mid 1950s" and the chapter reviews "its first two decades"; the summary says the movement "began almost twenty years ago"; the latest work cited is Bowen 1974a; forward-looking statements say "Sometime within the next decade" and "in the next decade." Internal evidence therefore places it around 1975–76, but Bowen does not say so.
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):**
  - Two overlapping data sources. (a) The **field**: Bowen's own participation as "one of the originators of the family movement," from his family explorations begun "in the late 1940s from a psychoanalytic orientation," plus the GAP Committee on the Family survey — "a detailed questionnaire completed by some 300 family therapists from all the professional disciplines and all levels of experience," published March 1970, whose "basic pattern of theory and practice is still very much as it was then."
  - (b) His own **clinical series**: the two-person (spouses + therapist) method has "had wide clinical use on several thousand families by the staff and trainees in a large family training center." Additional data from a multiple-family-therapy adaptation begun 1966, from psychiatric residents and mental-health professionals coached on their families of origin from 1967 onward, and from a family-therapy course for freshman medical students and their spouses.
  - Note the comparison group Bowen explicitly claims: residents who did family-of-origin work while in *no* formal psychotherapy, compared against residents who had "formal weekly family therapy" with their spouses. He calls this "a good sample for comparison."
- **Genre: theory / technique / both — and roughly what proportion.**
  Both, plus a large third component this template does not have a slot for: **field history and meta-commentary on the discipline**. Rough proportion by volume: ~40% history/survey of the field (origins, the A–Z scale, a catalogue of eleven-odd rival methods), ~20% theory (the systems theory of emotional functioning and its concepts, deliberately compressed because "the total theory has been described in other publications"), ~40% technique (background for family systems therapy, the two-person method, teaching, family-of-origin coaching). The theory section is unusually thin *by Bowen's own choice*; the technique sections and the field-history sections carry the new material.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Symptomatic families are out of emotional contact with each other.** "Most families with symptoms are out of emotional contact with each other and are not aware of what others are thinking and feeling." Observed across the general family-therapy caseload.
- **Anxiety and isolation covary.** "The higher the level of anxiety and symptoms in a family, the more the family members are emotionally isolated from each other." From family research.
- **Spouses who have lived together for decades do not know how the other thinks.** In two-person sessions it is "common to hear these comments about increasing fascination at discovering what goes on in the other after having been in the dark so long." Observed in the several-thousand-family series.
- **Spouses can hear each other through the therapist but not directly.** "they can listen and hear when the other spouse talks to the therapist in a way they could never listen when talking to each other." Same series.
- **Symptom substitution across members.** "There are situations in which an improvement in the former 'patient' is followed by serious symptoms in another family member." General clinical observation.
- **The family actively recruits a diagnosis.** "The family members point to the sickness in the patient and try to confirm this by getting the therapist to label the patient as the sick one." Observed *inside* the therapy sessions, not only at home.
- **Nagging recurs automatically when anxiety rises, against the mother's own knowledge and intention.** "a mother in her calmer periods can know that nagging makes the child's problem worse. She may resolve to stop the nagging, only to have it recur automatically when anxiety rises." Child-focused families.
- **Family group therapy produces large, fast improvement that then stalls and reverses.** Communication opens, "family symptoms subside and the family can report much more fun and togetherness"; then over longer term "the family begins to act out the same problems they had at home," adequate children get bored and stop attending or go silent, and the format collapses to parents-plus-one-child or the two parents.
- **Deliberate behavioural change by one member reliably changes the others — and reliably decays.** "When any central member of the family can observe and come to know his own part in the family, and he can purposely change his part, the others will immediately change in relation to it." But others "rather quickly catch on" and adapt, the process "can become 'game playing'," and "When the effort lapses, the family system returns to its former level."
- **Parents + symptomatic child together: symptoms improve, the family problem does not.** "the aggressive mother becoming less aggressive, the passive father less passive, and the child's symptoms much improved. The family would terminate with high praise for family therapy but with no basic change in the family problem."
- **The child's symptoms subside *faster* when the child is absent from the sessions.** Observed after 1960.
- **In a two-person system under tension a third person is drawn in, immediately and automatically.** "Emotional tension in a two-person system immediately results in the twosome involving a vulnerable third person."
- **With three family members plus therapist, the emotional issues cycle and evade the therapist.** "the emotional issues cycled between the family members and evaded the therapist's efforts to interrupt the cycles."
- **A differentiating spouse is attacked by the other with togetherness pleas, and typically falls back.** "The other spouse increases the pressure on togetherness demands, which commonly results in the first spouse falling back into the old togetherness... a number of false starts."
- **Residents who worked on their families of origin became better clinicians and improved with their own spouses and children — without any formal therapy.** Discovered in 1967 and 1968 respectively.
- **A twenty-year state-hospital patient was discharged within twelve months** of his medical-student son beginning ~four solo visits a year, aimed at relating "through the psychosis to the man beneath the symptoms."
- **Monthly sessions produce results as good or better than more frequent sessions.** From experiments spreading a given amount of therapy time over a longer calendar.
- **Group psychotherapists stayed out of the family movement.** "There were no group psychotherapists among the originators of the family movement," and the group-therapy-derived family therapists remain in separate meetings and separate journals — Bowen treats this separateness as a datum about the nature of the family movement.
- **Family therapists come disproportionately from a particular childhood.** "suggestive evidence that family therapists come largely from childhood situations in which they had more than average awareness of relationship disharmony, some ability to see both sides of an issue, and some motivation to modify the situation."
- **The professional field itself failed to converge.** Two decades on, "Family therapists represent such a diversity in theory and therapeutic method that it is difficult to find a frame of reference for either the common denominators or differences in the field."
- **[p2] The parents-plus-child series has a narrow stated scope.** Not child-focused families in general: "These were families with adolescent behavior problems and school problems in the youngster." Pass 1 recorded the outcome and dropped the population.
- **[p2] Only one observation in the chapter is attributed to research.** "From family research we have learned that the higher the level of anxiety and symptoms in a family, the more the family members are emotionally isolated from each other" — and the isolation → low responsible communication → gossip chain hanging off it. Every other empirical claim in the chapter is clinical experience, personal recollection, or the GAP questionnaire. The 1968 residents comparison, the chapter's strongest empirical claim, carries no research attribution at all.
- **[p2] The residents' comparison group is defined by exclusion only.** Verbatim: residents "who were going home to visit their families of origin and who were not in any type of formal psychotherapy." The chapter says nothing about what contact they *did* have; it names only the teaching conference that generated the impasse reports.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Emotional system vs. intellectual system** → two distinct functional systems in the human. The emotional system "includes the automatic forces that govern protoplasmic life... instinct, reproduction, the automatic activity controlled by the automatic nervous system, subjective emotional and feeling states, and the forces that govern relationship systems"; it "governs the 'dance of life' in all living things" and "is deep in the phylogenetic past and is much older than the intellectual system." Acts on: all behaviour, including relationship behaviour. A **feeling** is "the derivative of a deeper emotional state as it is registered on a screen within the intellectual system" — i.e. feelings are downstream readouts, not the driver.
- **Fusion between emotion and intellect** → the master variable. "The degree of fusion in people is variable and discernable. The amount of fusion in a person can be used as a predictor of the pattern of life in that person." Acts on: susceptibility to being governed by automatic forces, and on the person's fusion into others.
- **Emotional illness as dysfunction of the emotional system** → "In the more severe forms of emotional illness, the emotions can flood the intellect and impair intellectual functioning but the intellect is not primarily involved in emotional dysfunction." Acts on: where the model should locate pathology — in the emotional layer, with intellectual impairment as an overflow effect, not a cause.
- **Differentiation of self scale** → "principles for estimating the degree of fusion between the intellect and emotions." Composed of **solid self** ("within self, which is stable under stress, and which remains uninfluenced by the relationship system") and **pseudo-self** ("determined by the relationship system and which can fluctuate from day to day, or year to year"). Pseudo-self is *traded with the environment*: "can be increased by a congenial relationship and emotional approval and decreased by a negative relationship or disapproval." Acts on: life pattern, adaptive pattern, and the entire predictive apparatus.
- **Triangles** → "the way any three people relate to each other and involve others in the emotional issues between them"; "the molecule or building block of any relationship system." "A two person system is basically unstable. In a tension field, the two people predictably involve a third person to make a triangle." Four or more people = "a series of interlocking triangles"; in a large system "the emotional issues may be acted out between three people, with the others relatively uninvolved, or multiple people clump themselves on the poles of the emotional triangle." Two governing variables: level of differentiation, and level of anxiety/emotional tension.
- **Triangle position dynamics** → in calm, "the triangle consists of a two-person togetherness and an outsider. The togetherness is the preferred position." In tension, "the outside position is preferred." The triangle "is rarely in a state of optimum emotional comfort for all three. The most uncomfortable one makes a move to improve his optimum level of emotional closeness-distance. This upsets the equilibrium of another who attempts to adjust his optimum level. The triangle is in a constant state of motion." All moves "are automatic and without intellectual awareness."
- **Triangle propagation / detriangling** → "When one person in the triangle can control self while still remaining in emotional contact with the other two, the tension between the other two subsides. When it is possible to modify the central triangle in a family, the other family triangles are automatically modified without involving other family members in therapy." Acts on: the whole family, via one triangle.
- **Togetherness force vs. individuality force** → "A relationship system is kept in equilibrium by two powerful emotional forces that balance each other." Togetherness is "powered by the universal need for emotional closeness, love, and approval"; individuality by "the drive to be a productive, autonomous individual, as determined by self rather than the dictates of the group." The mix is set early: "The mix of togetherness and individuality into which the person was programmed in early life becomes a 'norm' for that person," and *is* the level of differentiation. "The greater the need for togetherness, the less the drive for individuality."
- **Togetherness deprivation → symptom** → "The greater the need for togetherness, the harder it is to keep togetherness forces in equilibrium without depriving certain family members. Discomfort and symptoms develop when togetherness needs are not met. The automatic response to anxiety and discomfort is to strive for more togetherness. When this effort fails repeatedly, the family member reacts in ways characteristic for that person." The reaction menu Bowen lists: "dependent clinging, seductiveness, pleading, acting helpless, denial of need, acting strong, dictatorial postures, arguing, fighting, conflict, sexual acting out, rejection of others, drug and alcohol abuse, running away from the family, involving children in the problem."
- **Families arrive already exhausted.** "When a family seeks psychiatric help, they have already exhausted their own automatic mechanisms for achieving more togetherness."
- **Nuclear family emotional system** → the routing mechanism. "Depending on the relationship patterns each spouse developed in their families of origin and the patterns they continue in marriage, the adaptive patterns in the nuclear family will go toward marital conflict; toward physical or emotional or social dysfunction in one spouse; toward projection of the parental problems to one or more children; or to a combination of all three patterns."
- **Family projection process** → "the patterns through which parents project their problems to the children... exists to some degree in all families."
- **Multiple generation transmission process** → "the over-all pattern of the family projection process as it involves certain children and avoids others and as it proceeds over multiple generations." Note the two-part structure: *selection* of children, and *propagation* across generations.
- **Sibling position** → Toman's profiles, "except Toman did not include the predictable ways that profiles are skewed by the family projection process." So: baseline profile by ordinal position, *modified* by projection intensity onto that child.
- **The family as a role-assignment system** → "each member of the system, on cue, says his assigned lines, takes his assigned posture, and plays his assigned role in the family drama as it repeats hour by hour and day by day. This process operates without intellectual awareness."
- **Anxiety → isolation → gossip mechanism** (a chain, stated for both families and social systems) → high anxiety produces emotional isolation; isolation lowers responsible communication; that raises "irresponsible underground gossip about each other in the family, and the confiding of secrets to those outside the family."
- **Secrecy as recruitment into the emotional network** → "Through pledging a confidence, a person becomes part of the emotional network around the family problem." This is the mechanism by which the therapist gets absorbed.
- **Assortative mating on life style** → "People marry spouses who have identical life styles in terms of togetherness-individuality."
- **Individuality emerges from underneath, in a calm field** → the method "proceeds on the assumption that the forces of individuality are present beneath the emotional reactiveness around togetherness, that the individuality forces will slowly emerge in the favorable emotional climate of the therapy triangle, and that togetherness forces will automatically readjust on a higher level of adaptation with each new gain in individuality."
- **The individuality force is fragile once surfaced** → "The individuality force emerges slowly at first and it takes very little togetherness force to drive it back underground for fairly long periods."
- **Societal isomorphism** → "The emotional forces in a triangle operate the same in society as in the family," though "the specific mechanisms involved in this have been elusive and hard to define."

**[p2] The concept set — the single most important thing pass 1 failed to count.**
- The section "The Theoretical Concepts" enumerates **exactly six**, in this order: (1) differentiation of self scale — "a cornerstone of the theory"; (2) **triangles**; (3) nuclear family emotional system; (4) family projection process; (5) multiple generation transmission process; (6) sibling position. Nothing else is presented as a concept.
- **Emotional cutoff and the societal emotional process are absent as named concepts**, in a chapter that internal evidence dates ~1975–76 and that cites Bowen 1974a (his societal paper) elsewhere.
- Their absence is deliberate and stated: the six "describe some over-all characteristics of human relationships, the functioning within the nuclear family system (parents and children), the way emotional problems are transmitted to the next generation, and the transmission patterns over multiple generations. **Other concepts about details in the extended family and the ways family patterns are interlinked with larger social systems will be added to the theory at a later time.**"
- And the section is explicitly a compressed restatement, not an update: "Since the total theory has been described in other publications (Bowen 1966, 1971) the concepts will not be described in detail here." The theory in this chapter is the 1966/1971 theory.
- **Triangles are listed second, immediately after the scale.** Pass 1 recorded the content of the triangle concept but not its rank.
- The **nuclear family emotional system names three channels plus a mixture** — marital conflict; physical or emotional or social dysfunction in one spouse; projection to one or more children; "or to a combination of all three patterns." **Emotional distance is not a fourth sink here.**

## A3. Predictive claims

Each stated as a conditional. Evidence flag in brackets.

- **If anxiety rises in a family, then triangling becomes more intense.** "The higher the anxiety, the more intense the automatic triangling in the system." [asserted]
- **If differentiation is lower, then triangling is more intense; if higher, then people have more control over the emotional process.** [asserted]
- **If anxiety is low enough, then the triangle may be clinically invisible.** "In periods of low anxiety, the triangling may be so toned down it is not clinically present." [asserted — important: absence of observed triangling does not mean absence of the triangle]
- **If a two-person system is under tension, then a third person will predictably be pulled in.** [asserted as a prediction of the theory; Bowen also reports it operationally — the method is *designed* to make the therapist that third person]

- **If the central family triangle is modified, then the other family triangles are automatically modified** — without those members entering therapy. [asserted with clinical support; this is the load-bearing claim behind treating a whole family through two people]
- **If fusion between emotion and intellect is greater, then (a) the more life is governed by automatic emotional forces "despite man's intellectual verbalization to the contrary," (b) "the more the individual is fused into the emotional fusions of people around him," (c) "the more man is vulnerable to physical illness, emotional illness, and social illness," and (d) "the less he is able to consciously control his own life."** [asserted as postulate]

- **If the need for togetherness is greater, then the drive for individuality is less.** [asserted as inverse relation]
- **If togetherness needs are not met, then discomfort and symptoms develop.** [asserted]
- **If the drive for more togetherness fails repeatedly, then the member emits his characteristic reaction** from the list above. [asserted]
- **If anxiety and symptoms in a family are higher, then members are more emotionally isolated from each other.** [from family research]
- **If isolation is greater, then responsible intra-family communication is lower and underground gossip / outside confiding is higher.** [from family research]

- **If the avowed intent of secrecy in individual psychotherapy is higher, then the greater the chance patient or therapist will gossip about the other, "all done in strictest confidence."** [asserted — a counterintuitive, falsifiable claim]
- **If a person is a "gossip" in a larger social system, then he came from an anxious gossipy family.** [asserted, retrodictive]
- **If parental anxiety is higher in a child-focused family, then the child-focused process is more intense.** "The higher the anxiety in the parents, the more intense the process." [asserted]
- **If a central family member observes and purposely changes his own part, then the others will immediately change in relation to it — and if the effort lapses, the system returns to its former level.** [clinical experience across "almost every experienced family therapist"]

## A5. Terminology

- **Family movement** — Bowen's own coinage-in-use, defined here deliberately against popular usage: "the theoretical thinking, the family research, and family therapy as they have evolved together... This is in contrast to the more popular use of the term 'family therapy' as it is used to connote a treatment method." He is reclaiming scope: theory and research are constitutive, not preliminary.
- **Family therapy** — two competing definitions are set side by side. Popular: "any psychotherapy session attended by multiple family members" — i.e. defined by *who is in the room*. Bowen's: "the effort to modify the family relationship system, whether that effort is with one or with multiple family members" — i.e. defined by *how the therapist thinks*.
- **Family systems therapy** — "began after the theoretical concepts were better defined. It is more accurate than previous terms, but it is not well understood by those not familiar with systems concepts."
- **Systems therapy** — "now used more often to refer to the process either in the family or in social systems." A widening of scope beyond the family.
- **Couples therapy / marital therapy** — Bowen treats these as near-empty terms: "The terms convey nothing about the problem for which the therapy is used, or the theory or method of therapy." General use "implies merely that both spouses attend the sessions together."
- **Conjoint family therapy** — "psychotherapy sessions attended by family members from two or more generations."
- **Psychoanalytic marital therapy** — a term he says is barely used but would be one of the more precise ones, since theory, method, and technique would all cohere.
- **Child-focused family** — "a well defined family problem rather than a therapy approach": "one in which sufficient family anxiety is focused on one or more children to result in serious impairment in a child."
- **Designated patient / identified patient** — "The mere use of the term implies an awareness of the basic process in the family, in the therapy, and in society."
- **"Healthy unstructured state of chaos"** — Bowen's own 1957–58 label for the state of the new field; "healthy on the premise that clinical experience would bring an awareness of the theoretical dilemma implicit in family therapy."
- **A-to-Z scale** — a scale of *therapists*, not families. A = theory and practice identical to individual psychotherapy, family sessions as adjunct technique. Z = "think in terms of systems, relationships, emotional fields, and breakdown in communication," "think family" for all problems, aim at "restoring communication, improving relationships in the family, and toward helping family members toward higher levels of differentiation." Bowen: "The author is at the extreme Z end of the scale."
- **Experiential vs. structured** — a later reclassification of the field, "a modification of the A to Z scale." Experiential: "Know and express your feelings and the process will break down the unhealthy structure that interferes with your life." Structured: "Problems are the result of a poorly structured life. The surest approach is the modification of the structure which will automatically result in free and spontaneous relationships." A structured method "contains a built-in blueprint... knows the problems to be encountered during therapy, it has a methodology for getting through the difficult areas, and the method knows when it approaches its goal."
- **Theory / method / technique** — defined hierarchically and used as a weapon against loose usage: "theory is determined by the way the therapist thinks about the nature of the family problem; method is determined by broad principles for implementing the theory into a therapeutic approach; and techniques are the specific ways or strategies for implementing the method." Followed by: "Terms such as theory, hypothesis, assumption, formulation, and concept are used loosely and inaccurately. It is not uncommon to hear someone say, 'I have a theory,' when it would be more accurate to say, 'I have an idea.'"
- **Emotional system / intellectual system / feeling** — see A2. A "feeling" is specifically *not* primitive here; it is a derivative registered on an intellectual screen.
- **Fusion** — used in two senses that Bowen links: fusion *between emotion and intellect within a person*, and fusion *of the person into the emotional fusions of people around him*. He treats the second as a consequence of the first.
- **Solid self / pseudo-self** — see A2. "An index of pseudo-self is the degree to which people act, pretend, and use external appearance to influence others and to feign postures that make them appear more or less adequate or important than they really are."
- **Life style** — used here as a synonym for level of differentiation: "varying degrees of need for togetherness, which constitutes the life style (level of differentiation of self) for that person." This is a term doing a job elsewhere done by "basic level of differentiation."
- **Togetherness / individuality** — the two balancing forces. Note "individuality," not "individuation," is the force; "individuation" is used for what happens in the family under Bowen's MFT structure.
- **Triangle** — "the molecule or building block of any relationship system." Explicitly distinguished from the oedipal triangle.
- **Detriangle** — used as a verb for the family-of-origin work: "the task is to detriangle old family triangles as they come to life."
- **Reversal / paradoxical comment** — "a technique of picking up the opposite side of the emotional issue for a neutralizing comment."
- **Coach / supervisor / teacher** — proposed replacements for "therapist." "The coach is probably the best in conveying the connotation of an active expert coaching both individual players and the team to the best of their abilities."
- **People / person / family member** — deliberate replacements for "patient," including in the therapist's private thinking.
- **Process vs. content** — an operational distinction: "It is necessary to listen to content in order to follow process but to keep the focus on process." Losing the process focus *is the diagnostic sign* of entanglement.
- **"Magic of family therapy"** — Bowen's own phrase for "this process of externalizing the thinking of each spouse in the presence of the other."
- **Sculpting / simulated family** — see B1.
- **Network** — the friendship-plus-kin field around a fragmented family.
- **[p2] Terms that do NOT appear.** "Emotional cutoff" (or cutoff in any sense), "societal emotional process", "societal regression", "emotional divorce", "undifferentiated family ego mass", "functional level of differentiation", "basic level of differentiation", "chronic anxiety". The last is notable: the chapter's anxiety is undifferentiated as to acute vs. chronic throughout.
- **[p2] "Multiple generation transmission process"** — this chapter's spelling. Not "multigenerational transmission process."
- **[p2] "Individuation"** is used only for what happens to a family inside Bowen's multiple-family format ("individuation is impaired or blocked"); the force is always "individuality." Pass 1 noted the distinction; the point is that "individuation" is a *therapy-outcome* word here, not a theory word.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **Sibling position** is a named concept, an "extension and modification of sibling position profiles as originally defined by Toman (1961)," with the projection-process skew added. It "provides important clues in predicting areas of family strength and weakness for family therapy."
- **The symptom-bearer is produced, not found.** "There is an emotional process in the family through which the family helps to create and maintain the 'illness' in the 'patient.' The process is more intense when anxiety is high. The process also operates in the family therapy sessions."
- **The healthiest member is the operative lever.** "It is a slow, laborious task to improve the functioning of the weakest family member. It is many times more effective to work through the healthy side of the family." Bowen developed an approach "to work with the 'healthiest family member' and to exclude the 'sick' family member from the therapy. It has been possible to do an entire course of family therapy with the focus on family health without ever seeing the 'sick' family member."
- **Identifying the healthy member is itself hard.** "The search for the most responsible, most resourceful, and most motivated part of the family can be elusive. It is best determined from knowledge of the family emotional process and the functioning patterns in the past and present generations, in collaboration with the family. The potential source of family strength can be submerged in an emotional impasse with a nonproductive family member." Note the collaborative determination — the family participates in choosing.
- **In conflictual marriages, the choice can be delegated to the couple:** the therapist says "they should decide who was healthiest, and he would do the next sessions with the healthiest alone."
- **Assuming responsibility is asymmetric by position.** "It is far more difficult for the impaired family member to begin to assume responsibility than it is for healthier family members."
- **The standard therapeutic unit is the two most important family members plus the therapist** — "For practical purposes, this means husbands and wives." Requirement: "two people in the same generation with a life commitment to each other."
- **The child is removed** from the child-focused case as early as possible; occasionally seen "to get the child's view of the family, but not for 'therapy.'"
- **Roles are assigned and rehearsed.** Each member "on cue, says his assigned lines, takes his assigned posture, and plays his assigned role in the family drama."
- **Sculpting names functional positions explicitly**: family members "assume positions such as bossy, meek, clinging, and distant."
- **The pursuer/distancer role reverses over the course of differentiation.** After the first spouse passes the nodal point, "the second spouse begins a similar differentiating effort to change self, and the first spouse becomes the promoter of togetherness." The togetherness-promoter role is *positional*, not personal.
- **The togetherness-oriented spouse terminates therapy.** Middle-term results come "when symptoms have subsided and the togetherness-oriented spouse exerts pressure to discontinue."
- **Adequate children exit family group therapy first.** "The more adequate children become bored by the repetition of issues they have heard before and they begin to find reasons not to attend. If forced to attend, the formerly talkative children can become silent."
- **The person who moves first** in family-of-origin work is a single motivated member, and the move is often covert: residents "go home to secretly try out the knowledge on their families."
- **The father is a viable entry point even when institutionalised** — the son relates "through the psychosis to the man beneath the symptoms."
- **Therapists themselves have a family-of-origin profile** (see A1): more-than-average awareness of relationship disharmony, ability to see both sides, motivation to modify.
- **[p2] The child is not fully removed from the system, only from the therapy role.** "In other situations, the child is seen occasionally to get the child's view of the family, but not for 'therapy.'" The child remains an information source. Pass 1's B1 had this; the ledger's summary ("removes the child entirely") does not, and is wrong.
- **[p2] The two-person unit has an admission criterion beyond the same-generation/life-commitment rule:** "It works best for people who are capable of calm reflection." A capacity gate on the participants, not on the relationship.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

**Framing and stance**
- Refuse to diagnose. "The therapist tries to avoid diagnosing the patient, and to focus on the family emotional process that creates the patient." Diagnoses are "avoided, even in the therapist's private thinking."
- Substitute vocabulary: use *people*, *person*, *family member* for *patient*; use *supervisor*, *teacher*, *coach* for *therapist* in work with families, retaining conventional terms for institutions and writing.
- Maintain a working mix of old and new terminology for records and insurance. "Some therapists explain the situation to the family that medical model principles are necessary for records but a different orientation is used for the therapy."
- Take clinical responsibility for the whole family rather than one patient. If a second member becomes symptomatic as the first improves, keep the single therapist for the total problem rather than referring out.
- Keep the patient at home and work on overall family anxiety, rather than separating patient from family.
- Operate from the premise "that family members want to be responsible and helpful to each other and that it often requires very little help to shift the family climate from a hurtful to a helpful one."
- Aim at "helping the family to be responsible for its own, including the 'sick' one."

**Selecting the unit**
- Work through the healthy side. Identify the most responsible / resourceful / motivated part of the family from multigenerational functioning patterns, in collaboration with the family.
- In cyclical blaming marriages, refuse to continue the cycle; tell the spouses to decide who is healthiest and see that one alone.
- In child-focused families: "defocus the child as quickly as possible, to remove the child from the therapy sessions as early as possible, and to give technical priority to getting the focus on the relationship between the parents, at the risk of a temporary increase in the child's symptoms."
- Put the two most important family members into the room with the therapist, "which makes the therapist a target for family efforts to involve a third person."

**Managing self in the triangle — the core technique**
- "The most important aspect of the therapy depends on the therapist's emotional functioning, his ability to stay neutral in an emotional field, and his knowledge of triangles."
- Find and hold an operating emotional distance: "the point I can 'see' the emotional process flowing back and forth between them"; "The right distance is the point it is possible to see either the serious or the humorous side."
- Actively counterbalance the family's affective drift: "If the family goes too serious, I have an appropriate humorous remark to defuse the seriousness. If the family starts to kid and joke, I have an appropriate serious remark to restore neutrality."
- Use a calm tone of voice and "a focus on facts rather than feelings."
- Use the **reversal** — "picking up the opposite side of the emotional issue for a neutralizing comment." Worked example: wife detailing her critical, nagging, bossy mother with husband agreeing → therapist says "I thought you appreciated your mother's devotion to you," converting seriousness to a chuckle.
- Stay constantly active in proportion to tension: "The greater the tension in the family, the more it is necessary for the therapist to stay constantly active to affirm his neutral position."
- Anticipate triangling moves before they occur, from knowledge of triangle characteristics.
- Treat one's own entanglement as detectable and correctable — recognise it and deal with it when it occurs.

**Running a session**
- Structure every exchange as **each spouse talking directly to the therapist**, in a factual, calm voice.
- "It is talking *about* emotional process, rather than the communication of emotional process."
- Loop: husband comments to therapist → therapist asks wife what she was thinking when she heard it → therapist asks husband what was going through his thoughts while the wife was talking. "This kind of interchange might go back and forth for an entire session."
- If a comment is too minimal, ask as many questions as needed to elaborate the thinking into a clear presentation, then turn to the other spouse.
- Other openers: ask for "a summary of private thoughts about the family situation since the last session," or "the most recent thinking about a particular family situation."
- "The therapist asks for thoughts, ideas, and opinions, and avoids asking for feelings or subjective responses."
- On sudden tears or emotion: stay calmly on course; ask "what was the thought that stimulated the tears," or ask the other what they were thinking when the feeling started.
- If feeling mounts and a spouse addresses the other directly, read it as building tension: "The therapist increases the calm questions to defuse the emotion and to focus the issue back to him."
- "The therapist is always in control of the sessions, asking hundreds of questions and avoiding interpretations."
- "By considering each new family as a research project, the therapist always has so many questions there is never time to ask more than a fraction of them."
- Occasionally guess aloud what might be going on, then ask questions to get the family's ideas about the guess; or name an area of investigation he thinks might help, "as a way of telling the family what he is thinking and a way of enlisting their effort in the exploration."

**Promoting differentiation**
- "The therapist can facilitate the differentiating process by focusing questions on this new area of family issues, by focusing on responsibility for self, and by avoiding any connotation that he is siding with the more righteous sounding togetherness pleading."

**Teaching**
- Use didactic teaching for families going long-term, to give "a way of understanding the problem, an awareness they are responsible for progress, and a framework in which they can direct their energy on their behalf."
- Use teaching statements "cautiously until after the family is calm."
- "In the later stages of therapy, all kinds of conferences and didactic sessions can be helpful."

**Family-of-origin coaching**
- Send spouses "home for frequent visits with their families of origin."
- The method: "a detailed family history for multiple generations in the past and developing a personal relationship with all important living relatives. This activates old family relationships grown latent with neglect. Then, with the advantage of objectivity and the knowledge of triangles, the task is to detriangle old family triangles as they come to life."
- Handle the inevitable impasses in conference discussion, "to help understand the problem and make suggestions for the next trip home."
- Coaching cadence roughly monthly for an actively working person; less for those with access to teaching sessions or living at a distance.
- Case-level instruction, verbatim in effect: visit the hospitalised father *alone*, whenever home, and "try to relate through the psychosis to the man beneath the symptoms."

**Multiple family therapy (Bowen's version)**
- Work with each family separately, dividing time between three or four families, "avoiding communication or emotional exchange between the families."
- 30-minute mini-sessions with each of four families while the others are nonparticipant observers; monthly is the current majority practice.

**Confidences**
- Adopt "some kind of working rule about not keeping secrets," and find ways to communicate secrets in the family sessions.
- Be "a responsible person who knows the difference between underground secrets and valid, responsible, private communication and who respects this difference."

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not label the patient as the sick one**, even when the family works to get you to; and do not carry diagnoses in your own private thinking.
- **Do not conclude the patient should be separated from an "innately pathogenic" family.** Bowen names this as the conventional therapist's move and rejects it.
- **Do not keep individual secrets, and do not gossip to outsiders about the family.** Both are named as detrimental: "it can be as detrimental on the one side to blindly keep individual secrets as it is detrimental on the other side for the therapist to gossip to outsiders."
- **Do not become entangled in secrets and confidences** — effectiveness is lost.
- **Do not facilitate or encourage a therapeutic relationship with the therapist.** "to specifically avoid actions and techniques that facilitate and encourage the therapeutic relationship with the therapist."
- **Do not work transference.** Transference issues, "formerly considered critical for resolution of problems, were avoided."
- **Do not make transference-type interpretations** about the family's triangling attempts: the goal is "to avoid transference type interpretations to the family about it."
- **Do not interpret at all, largely.** "asking hundreds of questions and avoiding interpretations."
- **Do not ask for feelings or subjective responses** — ask for thoughts, ideas, opinions.
- **Do not structure family members to talk directly to each other.** "The therapist avoids a structure in which family members talk directly to each other." Explicitly reversing standard practice.
- **Do not take sides**, and do not let the family believe you agree with either of them (the mother-in-law example).
- **Do not side, even implicitly, with "the more righteous sounding togetherness pleading."**
- **Do not focus on content.** "If he finds himself focusing on the content of what is being said, it is evidence that he has lost sight of the process and he is emotionally entangled on a content issue."
- **Do not go silent.** "If he cannot think of anything to say, he is emotionally entangled."
- **Do not use the reversal when deeply involved** — it will be heard as sarcasm or hostility and fail.
- **Do not attempt didactic teaching with a very anxious family** — inevitable distortions and impasses follow.
- **Do not allow emotional exchange between families in multiple family therapy** — "Emotional exchange between the families encourages group process, which overshadows family process, and individuation is impaired or blocked."
- **Do not keep parents and child in sessions past the point of usefulness**, and do not hand the child to a separate therapist: "This can result in parents becoming complacent, expecting the problem to be solved in the child's 'therapy.'"
- **Do not run the two-person method on twosomes without same-generation life commitment.**
- **Do not use the differentiation scale as a psychological instrument** "by people not familiar with the theory and the variables in a relationship system."
- **Do not rely on the popular "use of self"/intuition route.** Bowen's structured stance: "A structure-oriented therapist makes decisions based on theory and he stays on course in spite of his feelings that might oppose it."
- **[p2] The feelings prohibition is stated here without exception or stage.** "The therapist asks for thoughts, ideas, and opinions, and avoids asking for feelings or subjective responses." No later-in-therapy exemption is offered anywhere in the chapter. Recorded exactly because an earlier chapter states it as a graded rule — see P3.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition for any differentiation move: a calm field.** "Moves toward differentiation of self are usually not possible in a tension field." Therefore anxiety reduction precedes everything.
- **Precondition for teaching: the family must already be calm.** Teaching statements "are used cautiously until after the family is calm." Later stages permit conferences and didactic work freely.
- **Precondition for the family-of-origin rationale to land:** the same — "extended family concepts make little sense when people are anxious."
- **Motivation window (a trap).** Anxiety blocks extended-family work; but "After symptoms subside, it is harder for people to find motivation for serious work with their extended families." And "Once a family starts formal family therapy sessions, it is harder to find motivation for serious work with the families of origin." So the window is squeezed from both sides, and starting formal therapy narrows it further.
- **Priority ordering inside a session:** "The process of keeping the therapist emotionally neutral gets first priority in the therapy." Triangling pressure "is more intense early in the therapy and at periods when anxiety is higher than usual."
- **Child-focused sequence:** defocus the child → remove the child from sessions as early as possible → get the focus onto the parental relationship, accepting a temporary symptom increase in the child.
- **Differentiation sequence (the explicit staged course):**
  1. Family anxiety subsides; spouses become "more capable of reflection."
  2. Individuality forces surface in *one* spouse: he focuses on his own part, decreases blaming, accepts responsibility for changing self.
  3. The other spouse raises togetherness pressure; the first "commonly" falls back.
  4. "a number of false starts," the differentiating one gradually gaining strength, the other raising the tempo of togetherness pleas.
  5. The differentiating one proceeds calmly without defending, counterattacking, or withdrawing → the attack subsides → "the differentiating process passes through its first major nodal point." "It may require a year or two."
  6. "a period of calm and a new, higher level of adjustment in both."
  7. The second spouse begins the same effort; the first becomes the togetherness promoter.
  8. "New cycles usually take less time and the steps are not as clearly defined as in the first step."
- **Family-of-origin sequence:** multigenerational family history → develop a personal relationship with all important living relatives → this "activates old family relationships grown latent with neglect" → detriangle the old triangles as they come to life → impasse → conference → next trip home.
- **Calendar time is irreducible.** "When the differentiation of self is the goal, it appears to take a certain amount of time, on the calendar, for motivated people to modify their life styles." Frequency can be lowered without loss; elapsed time cannot.
- **Historical sequence of Bowen's own method development** (given as a sequence of forced revisions): individual therapy with a family orientation (late 1950s) → refined enough by 1960 to be called "family therapy with one family member" → parents+child format, found wanting → since 1960, parents only, child excluded → two-person triangle method "well formulated by the mid 1960s" → 1966 multiple-family adaptation → 1967 family-of-origin turning point → 1968 discovery that family-of-origin work alone matched formal weekly family therapy.
- **[p2] The direct-communication reversal is given NO date.** It is presented only as "a major change from earlier methods," with no year and no statement that the earlier method was his own. Every other revision in the chapter is dated (1960, mid-1960s, 1966, 1967, 1968). This is the one undated one, and it is the one he calls "this one technique."
- **[p2] Precondition on the two-person method itself:** participants must be "capable of calm reflection" — placed alongside the same-generation and life-commitment requirements, so it is a third entry condition, not a prognosis.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **The therapist gets triangled.** Signs Bowen names as *evidence* of entanglement: focusing on content; being unable to think of anything to say; the reversal landing as sarcasm. Consequence: loss of effectiveness, distortions, impasse.
- **The therapist becomes part of the emotional webwork through secrets** → "his effectiveness as a therapist is lost."
- **Family group therapy over the long term** → the family reproduces its home problems in the room; adequate children disengage or go mute; the format collapses; "Very impaired families may continue for a long time, using the therapy much as an individual psychotherapy patient uses therapy for support."
- **Terminating either too late or too early.** "A fair percentage of families tend to terminate at the point of feeling good about the family. If they terminate before they reach the impasse of longer term therapy, they may terminate feeling that little was accomplished."
- **Parents + child format** → symptom relief with "no basic change in the family problem," and high family satisfaction concealing it. Bowen flags the praise itself as a failure signal.
- **Referring the child to a separate therapist** → "parents becoming complacent, expecting the problem to be solved in the child's 'therapy.'"
- **Behaviour modification** → others adapt, "the process can become 'game playing'"; the initiator must keep consciously initiating; "When the effort lapses, the family system returns to its former level."
- **Encounter / marathon / sensitivity methods** → "For some, the methods can result in temporary periods of good feeling and exhilaration, which are called growth. For others, the sessions are followed by increased disharmony." Bowen calls the movement "antithetical to the efforts of the majority of family therapists."
- **Lacqueur-style multiple family therapy** → effective for support and discharge, but "least effective in helping individual family members toward defining a self."
- **Inter-family emotional exchange in MFT** → group process overshadows family process; "individuation is impaired or blocked."
- **Network therapy over time** → "a fair percentage lose interest, attendance at meetings dwindles, and continuation requires enthusiasm by the therapist and those who organize the network." Plus logistical burden and the expertise needed to manage large divergent meetings.
- **Teaching an anxious family** → therapist "becomes deeply entangled in the family emotional system with inevitable distortions and impasses in the therapy."
- **Symptom substitution** when improvement in one member is followed by symptoms in another — and the conventional error of referring the second member elsewhere.
- **The medical model / insurance requirement** intensifies the family problem by forcing a diagnosis.
- **Direct spouse-to-spouse communication in session** raises tension "even when the emotional climate is calm."
- **Renaming without changing** → "Changing the terms does not change the situation." Bowen names this as a real risk of his own terminology reforms.
- **Field-level failure mode (the largest one in the chapter):** therapists rushed into practice without the research and theory that produced it. "Family research and theoretical thinking that had given birth to family therapy were lost in the new rush to do therapy." "Many dropped out after initial therapeutic failure."
- **Two incompatible theories in one head** → "There are problems in using two different kinds of theories for the same over-all phenomenon" — one for intrapsychic forces, another for interpersonal ones.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

Each rule of technique converted back into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.

- **"Structure each spouse to talk to the therapist, not to each other"** ⇒ *Direct dyadic communication between fused spouses is itself an anxiety amplifier; the pathway through a neutral third party is a lower-gain channel.* Implied further: the information content of what each spouse says is not the problem — the affective loop is. A model needs a *channel gain* that differs by route (direct dyad vs. routed through an outsider), not merely an information-transfer link.
- **"Ask for thoughts, never feelings"** ⇒ *Intellectual-system activity and emotional-system activity are separately addressable, and querying one suppresses the other.* Attention allocated to cognition mechanically lowers emotional-system dominance in that moment.
- **"Talking *about* emotional process is therapeutic; communicating emotional process is not"** ⇒ *Observation of the process by a participant is itself a state change in the system* — the observer position is a real functional position, not a metaphor.
- **"The therapist must stay neutral, and neutrality must be actively re-affirmed in proportion to tension"** ⇒ *A triangle's third position is unstable and continuously pulled; neutrality decays unless work is done against it.* Model implication: third-position neutrality is a decaying quantity with a restoring action, not a boolean role.
- **"If the therapist cannot think of anything to say, he is entangled"** ⇒ *Entanglement has an observable proxy in the third party's own functioning.* The system's state is legible from the outsider's internal state — which is exactly what makes a coach agent's own state a measurable variable.
- **"Put the two most important members in the room so the therapist becomes the triangling target"** ⇒ *The triangling drive is not optional and will find a target; the only choice is which target.* Therefore a family with no available outsider will triangle a child, a symptom, or an institution.
- **"Modify the central triangle and the others change automatically"** ⇒ *Family triangles are coupled, hierarchically, with one dominant; change propagates outward from it without direct contact.* Strong, testable, and the single most consequential structural assumption in the chapter.
- **"Remove the child; the child's symptoms subside faster"** ⇒ *The child's symptom is sustained by the parental anxiety focus, and the child's physical presence in the room maintains the focus.* Presence itself is a term in the equation.
- **"Accept a temporary increase in the child's symptoms when defocusing"** ⇒ *Withdrawing the projection focus destabilises before it stabilises;* the system overshoots on the way to a better configuration.
- **"Work through the healthiest member; a whole course is possible without ever seeing the sick one"** ⇒ *Change enters the system at the point of highest functioning and propagates to the point of lowest, not the reverse.* And: the impaired member's participation is not required for the impaired member's improvement.
- **"Refuse to diagnose, even privately"** ⇒ *The family's designation of a patient is an active process seeking confirmation from outside; withholding confirmation weakens it.* The label is a load-bearing structural element the family is trying to get externally certified.
- **"Never keep a secret; never gossip"** ⇒ *Information asymmetry is the mechanism by which an outsider is absorbed into the emotional system.* A confidence is not neutral data — it is an edge added to the network.
- **"Use a reversal to defuse; it fails when you're involved"** ⇒ *A neutralizing intervention's effect depends on the sender's own emotional position, not on its content.* The same utterance has opposite effects depending on the state of its source. Model implication: intervention effect must be gated on the actor's own differentiation/neutrality state.
- **"Teach only after the family is calm"** ⇒ *Cognitive uptake is gated on anxiety;* the intellectual system is not addressable above some anxiety threshold. This is the same assumption as "no differentiation moves in a tension field," applied to learning.
- **"Coach monthly; monthly is as good or better than weekly"** ⇒ *The rate-limiting step is what happens between sessions, in the real relationship system, not in the session.* The session is a supervisory checkpoint on the family's own work: "The families are able to accept responsibility for their own progress and to use the sessions for the therapist to supervise their efforts."
- **"A certain amount of calendar time is required"** ⇒ *Differentiation has an intrinsic time constant independent of intervention intensity.* Dose cannot be compressed.
- **"Avoid the transference; work the existing intense relationships instead"** ⇒ *A new intense relationship with the therapist would be a functional substitute for the family relationships and would relieve the pressure that drives change.* The therapist as a source of togetherness supply *competes with* differentiation.
- **"Do not allow emotional exchange between families in MFT, but do let them observe"** ⇒ *Observation without emotional participation transmits learning; emotional participation transmits group process and blocks individuation.* Two distinct channels with opposite effects — a clean modelling distinction.
- **"The method requires same-generation partners with a life commitment"** ⇒ *Sustained differentiation work requires a relationship the participants cannot cheaply exit.* Exit cost is a precondition for change.
- **"Keep the patient at home rather than separating"** ⇒ *Removal relocates but does not reduce the family's anxiety;* the symptom follows the process, not the person.
- **"Consider each family a research project; ask hundreds of questions"** ⇒ *The therapist's curiosity is functionally identical to neutrality* — a questioning stance is structurally incompatible with taking a side.
- **"Behaviour change that must be consciously maintained reverts"** ⇒ *There are two layers — a surface action-behaviour layer that responds fast and reverts, and an emotional-intensity layer that changes slowly and holds.* Any simulation needs both, with different time constants, or it will predict permanence from a behavioural intervention.
- **[p2] "Defocus the child, accepting the risk of a temporary symptom increase"** ⇒ *the correct move has a variance cost, not a guaranteed dip.* The technique presupposes that withdrawing the projection focus has an **uncertain** short-run sign — sometimes worse, usually better — not that worsening is a necessary phase. A model that hard-codes an overshoot on `DEFOCUS_CHILD` asserts more than the source does; a model that gives the move a wider outcome distribution than its alternatives asserts exactly what the source does.
- **[p2] "The method works best for people capable of calm reflection"** ⇒ *reflective capacity is both a precondition of the intervention and an early product of it,* which makes the entry condition self-reinforcing: families below the threshold cannot be moved by this method at all, and the method's first job is to raise the same quantity it requires. A hard gate with a bootstrap problem.
- **[p2] "The theory covers the nuclear family and generational transmission; extended-family concepts come later"** ⇒ *the extended-family layer is, on Bowen's own account here, a technique without a theory.* Anything the model builds on that layer is extrapolation from clinical practice, not from the stated concept set — worth marking as such rather than treating extended-family mechanics as equally sourced.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

This is a retrospective chapter and it is dense with self-correction. Recorded exhaustively.

**Predictions Bowen made and now reports as failed**
1. **The "healthy unstructured state of chaos" (1957–58) was named "healthy on the premise that clinical experience would bring an awareness of the theoretical dilemma implicit in family therapy, and awareness would result in efforts to clarify the dilemma."** He now states flatly: **"This has not evolved to the degree it was predicted."** A named, dated, failed prediction about his own field. Some later generations did work toward theoretical order, but "A majority of family therapists see family therapy as a method based on conventional individual theory or as an intuitive, experiential method."
2. **He initially misattributed the 1967 residents' improved clinical skill to cohort quality.** "At first, I thought this was related to the quality of residents that year, but the residents said it was experience with their own families that made the difference." The correction came from the subjects, not from him.

**Premises he explicitly reversed**
3. **The therapeutic relationship as the vehicle of growth — abandoned.** "In the years prior to family research, the author had operated on the premise that the more reliable method for emotional growth was the working out of psychopathology as it was expressed in the relationship with the therapist. **Now this basic premise was changed.**" He notes the magnitude: "A change of this magnitude, for one trained in psychoanalysis, is so great that many say it is impossible."
4. **Transference — from critical to avoided.** "Transference issues, formerly considered critical for resolution of problems, were avoided until more family members could join the sessions."
5. **Direct family-member-to-family-member communication — from goal to hazard.** "The therapist avoids a structure in which family members talk directly to each other... **This one technique is a major change from earlier methods in which emotionally distant family members were encouraged to talk directly to each other.**" Note that he lists exactly that encouragement as a defining feature of family group therapy, which he says accounts for "a high percentage" of family therapy — so this reverses the field's dominant practice as well as his own earlier one.
6. **Parents-plus-child format — abandoned in 1960.** The average outcome ("twenty-five to forty appointments... about a year") produced symptom improvement "but with no basic change in the family problem." "This experience led to rethinking the theory and new techniques." Parents were then asked "to accept the premise that the basic problem was between them, to leave the child out of the sessions, and to try to focus on themselves. The results were excellent and this technique has been continued since 1960."
7. **Psychoanalytic marital therapy — used and then left.** He rates it "one of the most effective long term methods of family therapy" and says "The author used it a number of years before moving to a systems approach to the entire family relationship system." A method he still calls effective, abandoned on theoretical grounds rather than results.
8. **Frequent sessions — not required.** "There have been experiments to spread a given amount of therapy time over longer periods of time with less frequent appointments. A majority of multiple family therapy sessions are now held monthly, with results as good, or better, than with more frequent sessions."
9. **The nuclear family as the primary site of work — displaced by the family of origin.** The 1968 comparison found residents doing family-of-origin work while in *no* formal psychotherapy "had made as much progress with spouses and children as similar residents in formal weekly family therapy with their spouses." He calls this "the beginning of a new era in my own professional orientation," and later: the extended-family approach "bypasses the nuclear family and the infinite emotional detail in close-up relationships. It appears to produce better results than the more conventional family therapies."
10. **Weekly formal therapy for residents — he had been recommending it since the early 1960s** ("I had been suggesting family therapy for residents and their spouses instead of individual psychotherapy or psychoanalysis"), and the 1968 finding undercuts it. The prior recommendation is what makes the comparison group exist.

**Terminology replaced, and why**
11. *patient* → *people / person / family member*, in order "to neutralize the family emotional process to create the 'sick patient'."
12. *diagnosis* → avoided, "even in the therapist's private thinking."
13. *therapy / therapist / treatment* → *supervisor, teacher, coach* — and he reports this replacement as **incomplete and hardest**: "It has been more difficult to replace the concepts of 'treatment,' 'therapy,' and 'therapist' and to modify the omnipotent position of the therapist to the patient... It has been most difficult to find concepts to replace the terms therapy and therapist in work with the families and to retain them for relating to the profession."
14. He warns against his own reform: **"Changing the terms does not change the situation, but it is a step in an over-all direction. When the therapist has changed himself, the old terms begin to seem odd and out of place."** The direction of causation is therapist-first, vocabulary-second.
15. *family therapy* → *family systems therapy* → *systems therapy*, each renaming tracking a widening of scope; and he concedes the newer term "is not well understood by those not familiar with systems concepts."
16. He acknowledges his own usage is deviant: "family therapy with one family member... is consistent with his orientation but **can be considered inaccurate by most family therapists**."

**Concepts of others he modifies or rejects**
17. **Toman's sibling-position profiles are corrected:** developed from "normal" families, "remarkably close to the observations in this research, **except Toman did not include the predictable ways that profiles are skewed by the family projection process.**"
18. **The oedipal triangle is rejected as a general model:** psychoanalytic theory postulates it "without specifically naming it," "but the concept deals primarily with sexual issues, and it is awkward and inaccurate to extend this narrow concept into a broad one."
19. **The medical model is rejected for this domain** while conceded as "this cornerstone of sound medical practice."
20. **Confidentiality is explicitly reopened:** "A basic principle of medicine and individual psychotherapy requires that the physician and psychotherapist not divulge confidential information. **Family therapists are forced to reevaluate this principle.**"
21. **The A-to-Z scale is itself superseded** by the experiential/structured classification: "This is a modification of the A to Z scale in 'The Field of Family Therapy.'"

**Where he says the field went astray**
22. **The 1957–58 rush.** "Dozens of new people, attracted by the promise of therapy, and with little knowledge of the family research that had led to the development of family therapy, rushed into the field and began their own versions of family therapy." And: **"Family research and theoretical thinking that had given birth to family therapy were lost in the new rush to do therapy."**
23. **Group psychotherapy filled the theoretical vacuum by default,** not by fit: "In 1957, when new therapists began developing their own version of family therapy, without much knowledge of family research, the already defined methods of group psychotherapy offered more guidelines than any of the other existing methods." Consequence: "A high percentage of family therapy should more accurately be called family group therapy."
24. **"Systems" has been degraded:** "In the past decade, the term 'systems' has been misused to the point of simplistic meaninglessness."
25. **Basic theoretical vocabulary is misused:** "Terms such as theory, hypothesis, assumption, formulation, and concept are used loosely and inaccurately. It is not uncommon to hear someone say, 'I have a theory,' when it would be more accurate to say, 'I have an idea.' It would be improbable that anyone could have a theory about marital relationships that is not part of a larger theory."
26. **Type of therapy is being defined by seating arrangement rather than theory:** "Designations of the type of therapy is determined more by the configuration of family members who attend the sessions than by the theory."
27. **The two-theory problem is the field's central unresolved defect:** "This resulted in one theory for the intrapsychic forces and another for the interpersonal forces. A majority of therapists still use this combination of theories... There are problems in using two different kinds of theories for the same over-all phenomenon."
28. **Encounter/marathon/sensitivity groups** are "antithetical to the efforts of the majority of family therapists," resting on "partial theoretical notions that suppressed feelings are responsible for symptoms."
29. **He lost an argument about naming and records it:** "The author objected to the title 'The Field of Family Therapy,' for the 1970 survey of the family field on the grounds that it did not recognize the thinking and research that helped create the field. A majority of the Committee members insisted on this title on the grounds that it best represented the field as it exists."
30. **He keeps the 1960 self-criticism in force.** The six-blind-men analogy he used in 1960 is "accurate today," and he applies it to himself: "The author's effort at developing a systems theory represents the serious effort of another 'blind man'."

**Internal tension within the chapter (not flagged by Bowen)**
31. He asserts family group therapy achieves striking short-term results *because* the therapist facilitates family members talking to each other — and then makes the prohibition of exactly that the signature technique of his own method. The reconciliation he offers is temporal: the group-therapy route buys fast symptom relief and stalls; his route is slower to feel good and goes further. But he does not reconcile "communication increases → symptoms subside" with "direct communication increases tension."
32. He says "Family therapists are less likely to consider family members hurtful to each other" and that "it often requires very little help to shift the family climate from a hurtful to a helpful one" — against a chapter whose central claim is that change requires years of calendar time. The two statements are about different levels (climate vs. life style) but he does not say so.
33. He states the individuality force emerges "in the favorable emotional climate of the therapy triangle" as an *assumption* the method proceeds on, and elsewhere states the same thing as an observed finding.
34. **[p2] The chapter's primary method is outside the chapter's own theory.** The extended-family / family-of-origin approach occupies the longest technique section, is called "the turning point in the method," and is said to produce "better results than the more conventional family therapies." The theory section, four pages earlier, states that concepts "about details in the extended family… will be added to the theory at a later time." The technique he most advocates is the one his enumerated concepts do not cover, and he does not remark on it.
35. **[p2] Bowen does not name himself as the source of the earlier direct-communication practice.** He attributes the encouragement of direct talk to "earlier methods" and to family group therapy's borrowing from group psychotherapy. In this chapter it reads as a correction of the field. See P3 — his own earlier text says otherwise.
36. **[p2] The parents+child failure is stated flatly here.** "The family would terminate with high praise for family therapy but with **no** basic change in the family problem." Recorded as a flat negative, with no hedge, in a chapter whose neighbouring sentences are heavily hedged. See P3 for what the earlier text said.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- **On his own vantage point:** "Each therapist is emotionally invested in his own approach and therefore has some degree of bias in the way he views the total field. With awareness of the differences, the author will present **one version** of the way the field has evolved."
- **On describing others:** "No one is ever really accurate in describing the work of another."
- **On the field's origins:** "There is **some evidence** to support the thesis that the family focus evolved slowly..." vs. "**Most of the evidence favors** the thesis that the family movement developed within psychiatry." And "**A psychoanalytic principle may have accounted for** the family movement remaining underground."
- "**I believe** this may account for the heavy influence of group psychotherapy on family therapy."
- "**I believe** this was the first time it was discussed as a definite method at a national meeting."
- "The pattern **suggests** there were others who never reported their work."
- "There is **suggestive evidence** that family therapists come largely from childhood situations in which..."
- "It is **impossible to know** how many individual therapists now do occasional family interviews."
- **On theory-building in general:** "The number of choices in the selection of pieces for such a theory is **almost infinite**." "the development of workable theories are still far in the future. Large areas of specific knowledge are lacking." **"Into the far distant future, man must be content with his lack of knowledge and discrepant, partial theories."**
- **On systems concepts:** "systems concepts are **poorly defined** in areas that apply to man and his functioning." "the 'elephant' of systems thinking is far bigger and more complex than the simpler 'elephants' of the past."
- **On the theory's completeness:** "In developing any systems theory, it is **not possible to develop concepts to cover each piece** of the total puzzle." "Other concepts about details in the extended family and the ways family patterns are interlinked with larger social systems **will be added to the theory at a later time**."
- **On the differentiation scale:** it estimates, it does not measure — "this difference **can be estimated** from clinical information"; "It is **not a scale to be used as a psychological instrument** by people not familiar with the theory"; "The degree of pseudo-self varies so much it is **not possible to make a valid estimate** of solid self except from estimating the life patterns over long periods of time"; predictions hold only "**within broad limits**."
- **On triangles:** "The triangle **appears** so basic that it **probably** also operates in animal societies."
- **On the theory's status:** it is repeatedly called *postulate* — "Emotional illness is **postulated as** a dysfunction of the emotional system"; "The theory **postulates that** far more human activity is governed by man's emotional system than he has been willing to admit"; "The concept **postulates** the triangle... as the molecule."
- **On extended-family results:** "There are **some speculations** about the more rapid change in working with the extended families than with the nuclear family. It is **easier to 'see' self**..." — the explanation is offered as speculation. And "This approach is so different **it is hard to compare results** with other approaches. It **appears to produce** better results."
- **On the multiple-family-therapy result:** "The difference **appears related to** the ability to 'hear' and learn from the other families without reacting emotionally."
- **On time:** "it **appears to take** a certain amount of time, on the calendar."
- **On child-focused families:** "**There is no single high-road to success** in these families. Finding a way through the problem depends on the therapist's concept of the problem and his skill."
- **On method vs. therapist:** "**The type of approach is not a positive index of success in therapy.**" "There are Indian scouts better qualified to lead an expedition through the wilderness than inexperienced novices with scientific instruments." And in the summary: "There are skillful therapists who would be masters with any therapeutic method. **In this sense, family therapy is still more of an art than a science.**" This is a substantial concession — it concedes that method choice may not be the active ingredient.
- **On the structured claim being a belief, not a finding:** "The structure-oriented therapists **believe** that knowledge and structure, in addition to experience, **will eventually produce** a better result."
- **On his own clinical judgement:** "**In my opinion**, this process of externalizing the thinking of each spouse in the presence of the other is the epitome of the 'magic of family therapy'."
- **On his own best distance:** "**My best** operating emotional distance from the family..." — framed as personal, and generalised only as "Each therapist has to find his own way to maintain emotional neutrality." He says the same of the medical model ("Each therapist has to find his own way to oppose, or neutralize, or deflect") and of confidences ("Each family therapist has to find his own way").
- **On guessing, in technique and in a case:** the therapist may "**guess** what might be going on in the family"; in the medical-student case, "**I was guessing** that the father might be able to leave the hospital by the time the son graduated."
- **On family-of-origin work with no living relatives:** "**Reasonable results are possible** with those who believe they have no living relatives" — offered without mechanism.
- **On society:** "the specific mechanisms involved in this have been **elusive and hard to define**. The author has made **one serious effort** at this... Sometime within the next decade, systems theory **has promise for some contributions** in this new area."
- **On the whole enterprise:** "He has presented his theoretical-therapeutic system as **one of the many ways** that family and social systems may be conceptualized."
- **On the future:** "**If** the present trend in systems thinking continues, we can **reasonably expect** even more striking developments in the field in the next decade."
- **Softeners on the mechanisms themselves:** "usually not possible in a tension field"; "the triangle is **rarely** in a state of optimum emotional comfort"; "**commonly** results in the first spouse falling back"; "**Most** families with symptoms are out of emotional contact."
- **[p2] On the child's symptom increase — the hedge pass 1 dropped.** "**at the risk of** a temporary increase in the child's symptoms." Not "with," not "followed by," not "there is." The strongest available reading is that he accepts a possibility, not that he reports an event.
- **[p2] On direct communication — a softener carrying real weight.** "Even when the emotional climate is calm, direct communication **can** increase the emotional tension." *Can*, not *does*. Pass 1's A3 and the ledger both rendered this as a flat conditional ("then emotional tension increases"), which the text does not support.
- **[p2] On the extended-family results, three hedges stacked in two sentences.** "This approach is so different **it is hard to compare results** with other approaches… It **appears to** produce better results than the more conventional family therapies."
- **[p2] On the theory's own boundary:** concepts for the extended family and for family/social-system linkage "**will be added** to the theory at a later time" — a stated incompleteness in exactly the two areas the chapter's own technique and its closing section occupy.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **A theory's job is specified, and it includes handling its own exceptions.** "A theory of behavior is an abstracted version of what has been observed. If it is accurate, it should be able to predict what will be observed in other similar situations. **It should be able to account for discrepancies not included in the formulations.**" A stated criterion for theory quality — usable as a validation standard for the simulation itself.
- **A stated design principle for building the theory:** "an effort has been made to make each concept harmonious with overall view of man described here, and **above all to avoid concepts that are discrepant with the over-all view**." Also: "Without a framework, one can emerge with multiple concepts, each accurate within itself, that do not fit together."
- **The theory is explicitly modular and extensible in resolution:** "Each concept describes a separate facet of the total system. **One may have as many different concepts as desired to describe smaller facets of the system.**"
- **The concepts are scoped as a set:** "These concepts describe some over-all characteristics of human relationships, the functioning within the nuclear family system (parents and children), the way emotional problems are transmitted to the next generation, and the transmission patterns over multiple generations." Extended-family detail and linkage to larger social systems are named as *not yet in the theory*.
- **The autonomy paradox, stated carefully.** "This in no way changes what man is or has always been. He is as autonomous as he has always been, and he is as 'locked in' to those about him as he has always been. **The family focus merely points to ways that his life is governed by those about him.**" The change is in the observing lens, not in the object — a direct statement that the theory is a change of measurement, not of ontology.
- **Anxiety about the ultimate implication.** "when this simple concept is extended to its ultimate, then all mankind becomes responsible for the ills of all mankind... **man becomes anxious about the notion of changing himself** to help modify the ills of mankind. It is easier for man to fight his wars, inflation, social ills, and pay his money for noneffective corrective action, than to contemplate changing himself."
- **A sharp asymmetry between capability and willingness:** "**it is relatively easy for family members to modify their part in the creation of emotional illness once they clearly see what has to be done, but this does not decrease initial anxiety and evasive action at the mere contemplation of it.**" The barrier is at the contemplation step, not the execution step.
- **Biofeedback is cited as proof of principle** that conscious control over automatic functioning is achievable: "It is possible for man to discriminate between the emotions and the intellect and to slowly gain more conscious control of emotional functioning. The biofeedback phenomenon is an example."
- **The family is described as the exemplary system for study:** "The family presents a near perfect model of a 'system' in operation."
- **Family therapy is the most-recorded psychotherapy:** "with its emphasis on open communication within the family, has been the most observed, audiotaped, filmed, and videotaped of all the psychotherapies."
- **Humour is a technical instrument, not a personal style.** "The human phenomenon is usually as humorous and comical as it is serious and tragic." The therapist's calibrated distance is defined by the ability to see either face, and the humorous remark is a specific tension-regulation move.
- **Sculpting's mechanism is described in a way that is directly implementable:** "The therapist helps the family members decide on the functioning position of each family member in relation to the others, following which the family members are put into physical apposition" — positions named as *bossy, meek, clinging, distant* — giving "both a cognitive and feeling experience." "The sculpting may be repeated during therapy for awareness of change and progress." A repeatable state-measurement instrument.
- **Simulated families produce real affect from role-play:** "People who participate in simulated families discover an uncanny sense of realness to the role played situation."
- **Network theory's stated premises are a small independent model:** "people have distorted ideas about problems in others, that distortions are often worse than reality, that friends become distant during stress, and open discussion of problems can stimulate more real relationship activity and helpfulness to network members. Experience with networks tends to support the premises."
- **Minuchin is described as having achieved full determinism of technique:** "theoretical concepts so well formulated that he has **automatic therapeutic moves for any clinical situation**." Bowen presents this approvingly as what a structured method looks like — a direct description of a rule-driven agent.
- **Bowen names the difference between his and Minuchin's method precisely:** Minuchin's therapy "is designed to modify the feedback system of the relationship system through which the whole family is modified"; Bowen's is "designed to modify the relationship system by **modifying the part the individual plays** in the relationship system." Both "avoid focus on the intrapsychic forces." Same target, different actuator: system-level feedback vs. single-agent role change.
- **Spouses become better talkers as a side effect.** "Spouses experience a challenge in being as expressive and articulate as possible. People who have formerly been nontalkers gradually become talkers."
- **Gains show up at home faster than in session:** "Expressions of emotional closeness and increasing affection for each other occur at home. This occurs faster than when the effort is directed at emotional expression in the sessions." Other home-level gains: "the ability to deal calmly with children, the ability to listen to others for the first time, and new experiences about being able to work together calmly."
- **Sessions can look boring and still be working.** "Therapists accustomed to emotional exchanges can find these sessions dull and uninteresting, but the families are interested and motivated to attend."
- **Secret family-of-origin experiments are normal.** Residents "began to see themselves in their own families and to go home to **secretly** try out the knowledge on their families. This was followed by **reports of inevitable emotional impasses**." The impasse is treated as expected, not as failure.
- **Theory becomes real only through personal application:** the residents' own explanation — **"Family theory is just another theory until you see it work with your own family."**
- **Motivation is gated on symptoms for the general population but not for professionals:** "Results are the same, except there are few other people who seek family therapy until they have symptoms."
- **A gradient of tractability for family-of-origin work is given explicitly:** "It is easiest with highly motivated people with intact families that have drifted apart. At the other extreme are those who are repulsed with the idea of contacting extended family and those whose families are extremely negative. In between are all different levels of motivation and families with varying degrees of fragmentation and distance." Death of parents is not disqualifying "if there are other surviving relatives."
- **[p2] The mechanism he offers for the multiple-family advantage is *non-reactive hearing*, not observation.** "The difference appears related to the ability to 'hear' and learn from the other families **without reacting emotionally** and to learn." The gain is attributed to the absence of emotional participation, i.e. to the same variable as the outside position, not to a distinct observational-learning channel. Pass 1 read this as an observation channel; the chapter's own wording makes it a reactivity condition. (See P3 — the earlier chapter gives a different mechanism.)
- **[p2] A selection criterion, stated once and never developed:** the two-person method "works best for people who are capable of calm reflection." Since reflective capacity is elsewhere in the chapter an *output* of low anxiety ("After the family anxiety subsides and the spouses are more capable of reflection"), this makes the method's entry condition the same quantity as its early result.
- **[p2] The chapter contains no negative-prognosis statement.** Nowhere does he say a family cannot change. The nearest approach is the tractability gradient above and the calm-reflection criterion — both about difficulty, neither about impossibility. Recorded because an earlier chapter does say it (P4).

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

Chronological placement for this pass: Ch14 sits between **Ch22** (presented Oct 1971, rewritten
Oct 1974) and **Ch16** (~1976). Internal evidence — "its first two decades," Bowen 1974a as the
latest citation, "within the next decade" — puts it ~1975–76. The chapters it can be checked

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

**1. Pass 1 missed the concept count, which is the chapter's most important evolution datum.**
Ch14 enumerates **exactly six** concepts — scale, triangles, nuclear family emotional system,
family projection process, multiple generation transmission process, sibling position. These are
**the same six as Ch21** (1967/70), in a different order. **Emotional cutoff and the societal
emotional process are absent as named concepts** as late as ~1975, and their absence is explicit:
"Other concepts about details in the extended family and the ways family patterns are interlinked
with larger social systems will be added to the theory at a later time." Pass 1 quoted that
sentence in X3 as a modularity remark and never counted the list. The `_CONVERGENCES` terminology
timeline records Ch14 only as "communication direction reversed · nuclear family displaced"; the
concept-count row is missing and Ch14 is the datum that fixes the six→eight transition to
**after ~1975**, i.e. between this chapter and Ch16. Added at A2 [p2].

**2. The "temporary increase in the child's symptoms" is a risk clause, not an observation, and
pass 1 turned it into a trajectory.** Pass 1's A3 wrote a single conditional — remove the child
→ symptoms subside faster "at the cost of" a temporary increase. The chapter does not join those
clauses. The increase appears once, in a different section, as "**at the risk of** a temporary
increase," inside a statement of technical priorities. There is no frequency, duration, incidence
or case. Verified against the corpus: **the phrase "temporary increase" occurs exactly once in
all 22 chapters — here.** The only *observed* course Bowen ever reports for the child-excluded
format, in Ch10 as well as Ch14, is faster subsidence. This propagates: ledger **L14.3/L14.2**
states he "**accepts** 'a temporary increase in the child's symptoms', and reports symptoms then
subside faster," and derives from it "an intervention whose correct trajectory **gets worse before
it gets better**." That derivation is not supported. `_CONVERGENCES` **C1** carries the same claim
in its Ch14 line. C1 itself survives — the *other* half of the Ch14 evidence (praise without basic
change) is solid — but the "gets worse before better" mechanism must be withdrawn from Ch14's
contribution to it. Corrected at A3, A4, X2, B5 [p2].

**3. Pass 1 hardened two hedges into flat conditionals.** (a) A3: "If family members are structured
to talk directly to each other, **then** emotional tension increases." Text: direct communication
"**can** increase the emotional tension." (b) A3: the extended-family conditional is stated without
the two hedges that surround it in the text — "it is hard to compare results" and "it **appears
to** produce better results." Both corrected at X2 [p2]. This matters because the model is being
asked to treat routing gain as a mechanism; the source licenses a tendency, not a law.

**4. Pass 1 dropped the scope of the parents-plus-child series.** Not child-focused families in
general: "families with adolescent behavior problems and school problems in the youngster." The
25–40-appointment figure and the "no basic change" finding attach to that population only. A4/A1 [p2].

**5. Pass 1 recorded the residents' comparison as "no formal psychotherapy" without noting what the
chapter omits.** The chapter's own words are exact and pass 1 quoted them correctly. What pass 1
did not do is register that Ch14 gives no account of the contact these residents *did* have, while
the earlier published account of the identical episode does. See P3.4. This is the single largest
practical correction for the model, because the ledger's bolded "**no** formal psychotherapy" is
being read as "no intervention."

**6. Pass 1 read the multiple-family-therapy mechanism as an observation channel.** The chapter
attributes the advantage to "the ability to 'hear' and learn from the other families **without
reacting emotionally**" — a reactivity condition, not a distinct observational-learning path.
Pass 1's B5 built "two distinct inter-family channels with opposite signs" partly on this. The
two-channel reading is sourced from Ch11, not from Ch14; Ch14's own mechanism is narrower. X3 [p2].

**7. Ledger wording to fix: "He now removes the child entirely."** He does not. "The child is seen
occasionally to get the child's view of the family, but not for 'therapy.'" A6 [p2].

**8. Did pass 1 read later theory back into the chapter?** Checked and, with one exception, no.
Pass 1's A2 correctly lists **three** nuclear-family channels (not Ch18's four) and correctly omits
cutoff. The exception is negative rather than positive: pass 1 failed to *flag* the absences, and
in `_LEDGER` L14.3 used the phrase "the nuclear family is displaced as the primary site of work" —
which is right about technique and wrong about theory, since the nuclear family is precisely what
the chapter's six concepts *do* cover and the extended family is what they explicitly do not.
X1.34 [p2].

## P2. What is NEW here, relative to chapters preceding it chronologically

1. **The calm-field extension of the direct-communication claim.** Ch11 says attempts to "talk it
   out" "only stir up emotional reactiveness." Ch14 extends the claim to the case where there is
   no reactiveness to stir: "**Even when the emotional climate is calm**, direct communication can
   increase the emotional tension." That is a new and stronger assertion — a property of the
   channel itself rather than of the state it is used in. It is also the assertion the model's
   route-dependent gain term rests on, and it appears for the first time here.
2. **The A-to-Z scale of *therapists*, and its successor the experiential/structured axis.** No
   earlier chapter classifies practitioners on a scale. Note it is a scale with letter endpoints
   and no numeric anchors, derived from a 300-respondent questionnaire, and Bowen places himself
   at one extreme of it.
3. **Field history as a data source.** ~40% of the chapter. The 1955–56 eruption, the March 1957
   Ortho meeting, the 1957–58 rush, the "healthy unstructured state of chaos" and its failed
   prediction, the group-psychotherapy-by-default account. None of this exists elsewhere in the corpus.
4. **The therapists' own family-of-origin profile** — "more than average awareness of relationship
   disharmony, some ability to see both sides of an issue, and some motivation to modify the
   situation." New, and it is a claim about who becomes an outside third.
5. **The terminology-reform programme with its own failure mode stated.** *patient* → *person*,
   *therapist* → *coach*, diagnoses avoided even privately — plus the warning that "Changing the
   terms does not change the situation," with the causal direction given: the therapist changes
   first, the vocabulary follows. The word **coach** is defined here for the first time in the
   corpus: "an active expert coaching both individual players and the team to the best of their
   abilities."
6. **The confidentiality reappraisal as a general principle.** "Family therapists are forced to
   reevaluate this principle," with symmetric hazards named (blindly keeping secrets vs. gossiping
   to outsiders) and the recruitment mechanism stated: "Through pledging a confidence, a person
   becomes part of the emotional network around the family problem."
7. **The secrecy→gossip prediction generalised beyond the family** — to individual psychotherapy
   ("the higher the avowed intent of secrecy… the greater the chance") and to social systems
   ("irresponsible secret files about each other"). Counterintuitive and falsifiable; new here.
8. **The medical-student case.** Twenty years hospitalised, ~four solo visits in a year, discharged
   at exactly twelve months. The instruction — relate "through the psychosis to the man beneath the
   symptoms" — is new, and so is the demonstration that the mover can be a *child* of the target
   with no nuclear-family work at all.
9. **The catalogue of rival methods** (sculpting, simulated families, network therapy, co-therapy,
   TA/games/gestalt, behaviour modification, Lacqueur MFT, encounter groups) with stated failure
   modes for each. Nowhere else in the corpus.
10. **Two remarks that constrain the whole modelling enterprise**, both new: "The type of approach
    is not a positive index of success in therapy" and "family therapy is still more of an art than
    a science." Also new: the theory-quality criterion "It should be able to account for
    discrepancies not included in the formulations."
11. **The "capable of calm reflection" admission criterion** for the two-person method.

## P3. What CHANGED from earlier chapters

Each item names the chapter, quotes both sides, and states the delta. Items 1–3 are the substantive
findings; 4–9 are drifts in his own reported figures and hedges.

**1. Ch03 (~1960) → Ch14: the diagnostic sign of "talking to the therapist" is inverted.**
Ch03, verbatim: "Our first basic rule is that **the family work on its own problem** in the hour
while the therapist observes from the sideline… There are several ways in which the family attempts
to avoid the anxiety. **The most frequent is for the decision-making family member to engage the
therapist in conversation.** In addition to avoiding the family problem, it tends to encourage an
individual relationship with the therapist, and the decision-making family member thus becomes more
dependent on the therapist." Bowen's remedy in Ch03 is to "structure himself **out** of the
'working-together effort' of the family."
Ch14, verbatim: "The principal technique of this method is a structure for **each spouse talking
directly to the therapist**… The therapist avoids a structure in which family members talk directly
to each other."
**Delta: the same two behaviours swap valence completely.** In Ch03, family-to-family talk is the
work and family-to-therapist talk is the primary avoidance mechanism that builds dependence. In
Ch14, family-to-therapist talk is the work and family-to-family talk is "evidence of building
emotional tension." Ch14 describes this as "a major change from earlier methods in which emotionally
distant family members were encouraged to talk directly to each other" — which understates it. It
was not a change in seating; it was a reversal of what the same act *means*. Bowen does not
acknowledge that his own earlier text assigned it the opposite sign.

**2. Ch03 (~1960) → Ch14: the three-person format changes from the productive configuration to the
discarded one.** Ch03, verbatim: "It has been our experience that when father, mother, and patient
are present together and the therapist is able to maintain the therapy structure, the family soon
encounters conflict and disagreement. **This results in high anxiety, action, and progress in
therapy. Any two members of the father-mother-patient threesome can successfully avoid anxiety
issues and the therapy becomes more intellectual, more sterile, and less profitable.**"
Ch14, verbatim: "From earlier family therapy with three family members present, the emotional issues
cycled between the family members and evaded the therapist's efforts to interrupt the cycles. This
method is designed to put the two most important family members into therapy with the therapist."
**Delta: Ch03 says the twosome is sterile and the threesome is where progress happens; Ch14 makes
the twosome the standard method and cites the threesome's cycling as the reason for abandoning it.**
*Honest qualifier, which Bowen does not supply either:* Ch03's threesome is father–mother–psychotic
adult daughter; Ch14's is parents plus an adolescent with behaviour and school problems. Different
populations. But the periods overlap (Ch03 ~1960, the parents+child series pre-1960), and Ch03's
claim is stated as a general property of the threesome, not of psychosis. This is the corpus's
clearest case of a retrospective account contradicting Bowen's own earlier text. Ledger entry
**L03.4** — "three chapters now assert this… it should be treated as load-bearing" (the
presence-determines-anxiety finding, corroborated across Ch02/Ch03) — is directly opposed by Ch14
and should carry the conflict.

**3. Ch11 (≥1971) → Ch14: the direct-communication change is undated in the retrospective, and was
dated in the original.** Ch11, verbatim: "Very early in my family psychotherapy **I** discouraged
spouses from attempting to talk more at home; and **after about 1962**, I stopped suggesting that
they talk directly to each other in the family therapy sessions."
Ch14: "a major change from earlier methods" — no year, no first person, no admission that the
"earlier methods" were his.
**Delta: Ch11 shows he had suggested direct talk himself, dates the change to ~1962, and had already
published it four years before Ch14 presents it as the distinguishing feature of the method.** Ch10
independently confirms the earlier practice was his: "One of the early techniques of family group
therapy encouraged the expression of feeling between family members… But many families overreacted
to feeling expression and suddenly terminated therapy after a few sessions." So the reversal is
real, was his own, and is older than Ch14 makes it sound.

**4. Ch22 (Oct 1971 / rewritten Oct 1974) → Ch14: the 1968 comparison loses its dosage and its
hedge.** Ch22, verbatim, on the same residents: a conference "of some fifteen to twenty trainees
**who met once a week**… The amount of time devoted to any one trainee amounted to no more than
**fifteen to thirty minutes every month or two**. There was no more than token 'private' time with
any resident." And: "**Observations suggested that** trainees who were not in any type of
psychotherapy made **as much progress**…"; "The observations appeared valid by the most searching
criteria I could use at the time"; the finding is dated "1968 **and 1969**" and followed by "five
years of clinical investigation."
Ch14, verbatim: "**In 1968, I discovered that** these residents had made as much progress with
spouses and children as similar residents in formal weekly family therapy with their spouses. There
was a good sample for comparison."
**Delta, three parts.** (a) *Dosage removed:* Ch14's "not in any type of formal psychotherapy" is
true and, standing alone, misleading — weekly conference attendance plus 15–30 minutes of personal
coaching every month or two is a real intervention, just a small and differently-labelled one. The
finding is **dose reduction, not intervention removal**, and the model must not read it as the
latter. (b) *Hedge removed:* "Observations suggested" becomes "I discovered." (c) *Claim
strengthened downstream:* Ch22 says "as much or more progress"; Ch14 later escalates to "It appears
to produce better results than the more conventional family therapies," having first conceded "it is
hard to compare results."

**5. Ch10 (~1970) → Ch14: his own figure for the parents+child series moves.** Ch10: "The average
good result comes after about a year—**thirty-five to forty-five sessions**." Ch14: "The average
good outcome of such therapy would come in about **twenty-five to forty** appointments that covered
about a year." Same series, same "about a year," a different band five years later, no note of the
revision. Ch10 also says "**postadolescent** child"; Ch14 says "adolescent."

**6. Ch10 (~1970) → Ch14: the same series' failure verdict loses its hedge.** Ch10: "The family is
usually pleased with the result on termination of therapy, but the therapist **may** see no basic
change in the family pattern." Ch14: "The family would terminate with high praise for family therapy
but **with no** basic change in the family problem." A qualified clinical impression becomes a flat
finding in the retelling. Ch10 also says "the **nagging** mother is calmer"; Ch14 says "the
**aggressive** mother becoming less aggressive."

**7. Ch10 (~1970) → Ch14: family group therapy's plateau widens downward.** Ch10: "The maximal
benefit from family group therapy sessions is usually achieved in **twelve to twenty** sessions."
Ch14: "The maximum results with short-term family group therapy come within about **ten to twenty**
sessions." Minor, but recorded because both are offered as observed regularities and `_CONVERGENCES`
uses these bands as calibration anchors.

**8. Ch11 (≥1971) → Ch14: the multiple-family effect size drops from a number to an adverb.**
Ch11: "The surprise was the unusually rapid progress of the families. This was estimated to be about
**50 percent faster** than comparable clinical problems in other families." Ch14: "The average family
makes **a little faster progress** than those in one-hour sessions for single families."
**Delta: a quantified ~50% becomes "a little," unremarked.** The comparators are not identical —
Ch11 compares to "other families," Ch14 to one-hour single-family sessions — but Ch14 offers no
number at all and does not mention the earlier estimate. Ledger **L11.2** carries the 50% figure as
a finding; it should now be flagged as contradicted by his own later report.
*And the mechanism changes with it:* Ch11 rejects the families' own explanation and proposes "it is
easier to really see and know your own problem when you watch it in other people." Ch14 proposes
"the ability to 'hear' and learn from the other families **without reacting emotionally**." The first
is an observational-learning mechanism; the second is a reactivity mechanism.

**9. Ch11 (≥1971) → Ch14: scheduling moves further out, consistently.** Ch11: monthly "as much, and
possibly even more, progress," which "led me to reduce the frequency of appointments with all
families… to **once every two weeks**. An increasing number are seen once a month." Ch14: "**A
majority** of multiple family therapy sessions are now held monthly." A continuation, not a
contradiction — and it strengthens C8. Ch11's "**about four years**" for a motivated family and
Ch14's "**an average of five years**" for long-term families are different populations and should
not be treated as a revision.

**10. Ch11 (≥1971) → Ch14: the feelings prohibition hardens.** Ch11: "Early in the course of
therapy, my questions are designed to elicit the intellectual process… **Only much later in therapy,
and in special situations do I ask for a reading from her subjective, inner feelings.**" Ch14: "The
therapist asks for thoughts, ideas, and opinions, and **avoids** asking for feelings or subjective
responses," with no stage exemption anywhere. A staged rule becomes an absolute one. For the model
this is the difference between a permanently disabled move and a move gated on therapy phase.

**11. Ch09 (~1965) → Ch14: the triangle is promoted into the theory.** Ch09 excludes the triangle
from the major theoretical concepts as having "more to do with therapy than the basic theory" while
calling it the building block. Ch14 lists it **second**, immediately after the scale, and keeps the
building-block language: "the molecule or building block of any relationship system." Consistent
with his own Ch14 claim that the two-person triangle method "was well formulated by the mid 1960s" —
therapy first, theory after. This is a case where the retrospective **checks out**.

**12. Ch10 (~1970) → Ch14: "since 1960" for the child-excluded format checks out.** Ch10: "Another
technique has been used for **more than ten years** with only minor changes: The parents begin

independently supports the faster-subsidence claim: the child's symptoms subsided "much sooner than

**13. Ch08 (~1962) → Ch14: "since 1960 he has spoken of 'family therapy with one family member'" is

**14. Ch18 (early 1973) / Ch16 (~1976) → Ch14: the sink count reads three, not four.** Ch18 names

## P4. What was DROPPED

1. **Emotional cutoff.** Absent as a concept and as a word, despite Ch18 (early 1973) making "degree
   of emotional cutoff with families of origin" one of three inputs to intensity, and despite the
   chapter's longest technique section being about restoring contact with families of origin. The
   mechanism is being used and not named.
2. **The societal emotional process.** Present only as a two-paragraph closing section that cites
   Bowen 1974a, concedes "the specific mechanisms involved in this have been elusive and hard to
   define," and defers: "Sometime within the next decade, systems theory has promise for some
   contributions in this new area." Ch13 and Ch18 — both written earlier — contain a developed
   societal-regression account with calibration figures. None of it is carried forward.
3. **All scale arithmetic.** Ch09's 55/15/basic-35, Ch13's ten-points-in-twenty-five-years, Ch21's
   named divisions (0, 50–75, above 60, 75, mid-90). Ch14 has the scale as a cornerstone concept and
   **not one number**.
4. **Any attempt at measurement.** Ch11 reports that "The research staff on this project has tried
   hard to define change in a way that can be measured and quantified." Ch14 does not mention this
   effort, and offers no criterion for "as much progress," "better results," or "a little faster."
5. **The negative prognosis.** Ch11: "some families, because of the intensity of their ingrained life
   patterns and their initial basic level of differentiation of self, **will never be able to change
   significantly**." Ch14 has no such statement — its nearest equivalents are a tractability gradient
   and a calm-reflection criterion, both about difficulty.
6. **Basic vs. functional level of differentiation.** The two-level structure that Ch07, Ch09, Ch12,
   Ch17 and Ch21 all carry is absent. Ch14 has solid self vs. pseudo-self, which is the *composition*
   distinction, not the basic/functional *dynamics* distinction.
7. **Chronic vs. acute anxiety.** Ch13 makes chronicity the trigger for regression; Ch18 makes
   symptom onset depend on chronicity, not instantaneous level. Ch14's anxiety is undifferentiated.
8. **The specific reciprocity mechanism** — overadequate/inadequate, the equal-and-opposite transfer,
   the organ analogy (Ch03, Ch05, Ch07, Ch12, Ch16). Ch14 has pseudo-self "increased by a congenial
   relationship… and decreased by a negative relationship," which is the trading rule stripped of its
   conservation, its bistability and its hysteresis.
9. **Sibling position detail.** Ch14 keeps the concept and the Toman correction but gives no profiles
   and no example.
10. **The ranked avoidance ladder** of Ch03 (engage the therapist → talk to the patient about the
    psychosis → criticise the patient → small talk → silence). Only the top rung survives, and with
    its sign reversed (P3.1).

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Anxiety ↑ → triangling intensity ↑; differentiation ↓ → triangling intensity ↑ → `Triangle` → triangling activation is a two-input function of system anxiety and participants' differentiation, not a fixed structural property.
- In calm, triangle = 2-person togetherness + 1 outsider, togetherness preferred; in tension, outside position preferred → `Triangle` → the *sign of the preference* flips with anxiety; positions must carry a preference value that inverts above a tension threshold.
- "The most uncomfortable one makes a move... this upsets the equilibrium of another" → `Triangle` / `move:ADJUST_DISTANCE` → continuous relaxation loop: each tick, the least-comfortable member moves, perturbing another. Never settles. Implement as perpetual motion, not as convergence.
- Triangling moves are "automatic and without intellectual awareness" → `Person` → default move selection is unconscious; awareness is a separate, acquirable capability that gates the alternative.
- Modify the central triangle → all other family triangles change automatically → `Family` / `Triangle` → **triangles are coupled with one dominant**. Needs a designated central triangle and a propagation rule that changes peripheral triangles without direct interaction. This is the single highest-leverage structural claim in the chapter.
- One person controls self while staying in emotional contact with both others → tension between the other two subsides → `move:DETRIANGLE` → requires *two* conditions simultaneously (self-control AND maintained contact); either alone should fail. Withdrawal must be modelled as a distinct, non-working move.
- Solid self (stable under stress, uninfluenced by relationship system) vs. pseudo-self (fluctuating, tradeable with approval/disapproval) → `Person` → two separate quantities. Pseudo-self needs an update rule driven by relationship approval; solid self must be near-invariant on the simulation's timescale.
- Pseudo-self varies so much that solid self cannot be validly estimated except over long periods → `test` → any measurement the simulation exposes should be noisy on short horizons and only converge over long runs. A model that reports a clean differentiation number per tick contradicts Bowen.
- Fusion ↑ → vulnerability to physical, emotional, AND social illness ↑ → `Person` → one latent variable, three symptom channels. Do not model physical illness as unrelated.
- Togetherness force and individuality force balance; greater togetherness need ⇒ lesser individuality drive; the mix *is* the level of differentiation → `Person` → represent as a single scalar with two named poles rather than two independent parameters.
- Togetherness need unmet → discomfort/symptom; automatic response is to seek *more* togetherness; repeated failure → characteristic reaction → `Person` / `move:SEEK_TOGETHERNESS` → a positive-feedback loop with a fallback to a per-person reaction repertoire. The reaction list (clinging, acting strong, conflict, substance abuse, running away, involving children) should be a per-person trait, drawn once.
- Families arrive at therapy having "already exhausted their own automatic mechanisms" → `event-kind:HELP_SEEKING` → help-seeking should trigger only after the internal repertoire has been exercised and failed, not on symptom onset.
- Nuclear family routes anxiety to exactly one of: marital conflict / dysfunction in one spouse / projection to children / a combination → `Family` → a routing rule with three channels plus mixture, parameterised by each spouse's family-of-origin patterns.
- Family projection process "exists to some degree in all families" → `Family` → never zero; a baseline projection intensity for every family.
- Multigenerational transmission "involves certain children and avoids others" → `Family` / `Person` → selection is non-uniform across siblings and must be explicit, not spread evenly.
- Sibling position profiles skewed by projection intensity → `Person` → sibling position gives a base profile; projection load applies a distortion term. `unmodelled` if the model has sibling position but no projection-skew modifier.
- People marry spouses with identical togetherness-individuality life styles → `Family` / `event-kind:MARRIAGE` → mate selection must be assortative on differentiation, effectively exactly.
- Anxiety ↑ → emotional isolation ↑ → responsible communication ↓ → underground gossip and outside-confiding ↑ → `Relationship` / `Family` → a three-stage chain, with *outward* leakage of communication as the terminal state. Give relationships both a responsible-communication and an underground-communication channel.
- Pledging a confidence makes the confidant part of the emotional network → `Relationship` / `coach` → secret-sharing creates a network edge; the coach agent must have a rule refusing that edge.
- Direct dyadic communication raises tension even when calm; routing through a neutral third lowers it → `Relationship` → channel gain differs by route. This is the mechanism behind the whole two-person method and is currently likely `unmodelled`.
  **[p2] Two amendments.** (i) The source says direct communication "**can**" increase tension — so route gain is a **distribution shift, not a sign flip**: the direct route has higher variance and a worse mean under fusion, not a guaranteed increase. (ii) Ch14 also reports the opposite-signed effect from the *same* channel: family group therapy increases direct communication and symptoms subside, with "much more fun and **togetherness**." Both are true because they land on different outputs. `readout` → **the direct route must raise tension and lower symptoms and raise togetherness simultaneously.** A model with one wellbeing scalar cannot represent the chapter's own two observations at once, and the two-axis requirement (C1/Ch10) is what makes them consistent.
- **[p2] The concept set is six, and the extended-family layer is technique without theory** → `framing` / `unmodelled` → Ch14 (~1975) still enumerates the Ch21 six and explicitly defers extended-family and society-linkage concepts. Anything the model builds on the extended-family layer or the societal dial is sourced from Bowen's *practice* and from Ch13/Ch16/Ch18, not from this chapter's stated theory. Mark the provenance rather than blending it.
- **[p2] The 1968 result is dose reduction, not intervention removal** → `coach` / `test` → the comparison group had a weekly conference and 15–30 minutes of directed coaching every month or two (Ch22).
