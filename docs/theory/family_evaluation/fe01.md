# FE01 — Toward a Natural Systems Theory

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third-person throughout — "**Bowen** was one researcher who
was able to take this step back", "**Bowen's** professional interest in the family began…",

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE01.1 — The projection cycle, with its **amplifying loop** and its **asymmetric misattribution**, in one passage `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

This is the fullest statement of the mechanism in the whole corpus, and it contains two things the
spec does not carry.

**The loop:**

> "one family member would become anxious or worried about what he or she perceived as a problem or
> potential problem in another family member. **As this 'anxious one' became preoccupied (in
> fantasies, verbalizations, etc.) with the appearance and behavior of the person perceived to have
> a problem, the 'problem one' would typically exaggerate the very demeanor, attitude, or appearance
> that the 'anxious one' was worried about. This exaggeration of 'the problem' would increase, of
> course, the anxiety of the 'anxious one.'** An escalating cycle of anxiety and problem behavior
> would then ensue and result in the 'anxious one' getting into more of a caretaking position than
> he or she wanted and the 'problem one' getting into more of a patient or child position than he or
> she wanted."

> "**Each person became an emotional prisoner of the way the other person functioned and neither was
> able to change his or her functioning enough to stop the process.**"

**The misattribution, and it is asymmetric by position:**

> "**Family members, however, usually viewed their anxiety as being 'caused' by the patient's
> attitudes and behavior and rarely viewed the patient's behavior as a reflection of their own
> anxiety-determined functioning. The patient, in turn, tended to perceive himself as an inadequate
> or defective person and as indeed the 'cause' of the family problems.**"

*Model consequence:* **two requirements, one of them new.**

1. The over/under-functioning coupling (`M1.B.5`) is **positive feedback**, not a static allocation:
   each party's move *increases* the input driving the other's. `M11.C.12`'s symptom-relief result
   and `M7.D.2a`'s lock-in both follow from a loop of this shape. Note also that neither party wants
   the position they end in — "**more of a caretaking position than he or she wanted**" — so the
   configuration is **not** preference-driven, which is what makes it survive both parties wanting
   out. This is the mechanism behind `M4.D.1d`'s conflicting urges.

2. **The belief layer's error is asymmetric by position, and the spec does not say so.** `M9.1`
   permits belief to differ from ground truth and `M9.2` decouples truth value from emotional
   function, but nothing states the *shape* of the error. Here it is: the caretaking pole attributes
   causation **outward**; the symptomatic pole attributes it **inward, to a defect in self**. Both
   are wrong, and they are wrong in **opposite directions**. That is `M11.F.6`'s two-sidedness at the
   belief layer, and it is directly implementable — the misattribution's sign follows the pole of
   `M1.B.5`. **New requirement candidate.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE01.2 — What *drives* the polarisation: **anxious focus on a difference** `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

The spec has `M1.B.5`'s bistability and `M1.B.6`'s relative flip, but nothing that says what pushes
a pair toward the extremes. This does:

> "**The degree of polarized extremes that these reciprocal traits reached was influenced by the
> degree to which family members defined the differences between them as a problem and anxiously
> focused on 'correcting' those differences.** In the process of this focusing, **each person would
> be driven to become a certain way in relationship to the other person that was different from the
> way he or she was with people outside the family.**"

The list of reciprocities, verbatim:

> "**overadequate and inadequate** (one did everything right and could cope and the other did
> everything wrong and could not cope), **decisive and indecisive** (one made all the decisions and
> the other felt incapable of making any decisions), **dominant and submissive** (one led and the
> other followed), **hysterical and obsessive** (one was a fountain of feelings and the other was a
> constrained internalizer of feelings)."

*Model consequence:* **the driving term for `M1.B.5` is named, and it is the same quantity
`M4.C.8` already carries** — *attending to a channel amplifies it*. Here the channel is a
**difference between two people**, and anxious attention to it drives the pair apart along that
axis. `M4.C.8` was extracted from `K12.6` as a statement about feelings; `FE01.2` shows it is the
general form. **One mechanism, two instances — that is `M10.A.2`'s least-number-of-pieces criterion
satisfied.**

The second sentence is an independent requirement and the spec already has it: the polarisation is
**per-tie, not a personal trait** — different "**from the way he or she was with people outside the
family**". Confirms `M1.B.5`'s prohibition on a fixed personal trait and `M1.A.14b`'s general rule.

The four named reciprocities also give `M1.B.9`'s `areas_of_joint_activity` concrete content: they
are four **different axes**, and a pair can be polarised on one and not another.

## ⭐⭐ FE01.3 — The mirror-opposite rule: an **anti-symmetric conservation**, stated as predictive `[K]` `[T]`

> "This reciprocal functioning could be so precise that **whenever a significant personality
> characteristic was found in one family member, it was predictable that its mirror opposite
> characteristic would be found in another family member.** The two opposite characteristics would
> so reinforce each other that **it was impossible to understand adequately the intensity of a
> particular trait in one person out of the context of the intensity of the mirror opposite trait in
> another person.** The activeness of one person, in other words, was reflected in the passiveness
> of another, and vice versa."

*Model consequence:* `M6.I.4`'s conservation stated as a **falsifiable prediction** rather than an
invariant — find a marked trait, predict its mirror in a named other. It is also the cleanest
statement anywhere that the quantity is **paired and signed**, which `M11.C.24`'s dissolution test
depends on.

**Caution on scope, and the chapter supplies it itself** (footnote 6): "By virtue of studying
families with schizophrenia, Bowen was seeing **an extreme version** of the reciprocal relationship
processes. **The fact that they were extreme is what made them so readily visible.**" So the
*precision* claimed here is a property of the observed population, not of families generally —
which is exactly what differentiation was later introduced to account for (`FE01.11`). Do not read
"so precise that it was predictable" as a claim about the whole scale.

## ⭐⭐ FE01.4 — Closeness and distance are an **oscillation with no owner** `[K]` `[T]`

> "the existence of **cycles of distance and closeness. People would move together, move apart, move
> together, move apart like an accordion. It did not appear that any one family member 'caused'
> these cycles, although characteristically someone got blamed for them.** Rather than being created
> by one person, these cycles were **perpetuated by the functioning of each family member.** The
> cycles were **orchestrated with such precision and predictability** within various family
> relationships that any explanation for them based on the psychological makeup of individual family
> members seemed awkward and inadequate."

*Model consequence:* three things.

- The pursue/distance pair is an **oscillator**, not a pair of set points, which is why `M5.E.8`'s
  polarity inversion after a successful sequence is observable at all and why `M5.E.6` requires a
  damped oscillation rather than a spike-and-settle.
- "**characteristically someone got blamed**" is `M1.A.18a`'s blame readout arising *from the
  oscillation itself* — no one caused it, and someone is always held to have. A cheap emergent
  readout: blame attribution should appear in the log at the extremes of the cycle with no
  corresponding causal event.
- It is a second witness for `M12.1`'s withdrawn **permission scalar** staying withdrawn: the cycle
  is driven by every member's functioning, not gated by an excluded third.

## ⭐⭐ FE01.5 — `systems_perspective` **defined**, and the definition is two-sided `[K]` `[T]`

The best definition of `M1.A.18` in the corpus, and it comes with its own failure mode:

> "One has **gained or regained** an 'outside' perspective when he is able to **focus simultaneously
> on the influence of each family member's thinking, feelings, and behavior on the family
> 'atmosphere' and the influence of the 'atmosphere' on each individual's thinking, feelings, and
> behavior. If one emphasizes one side of this equation over another, then a systems perspective is
> lost.** Emphasizing one side is like forgetting that **mass depends on gravity as much as gravity
> depends on mass.**"

And the reason it is hard:

> "**People have a strong tendency to regard their own thoughts and feelings and what they think are
> other people's thoughts and feelings as occurring independently of what is transpiring between
> them.** This tendency appears to be what makes it so difficult for people to get sufficiently free
> of their own ruminations to be able to observe a larger process."

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.18` gains a definition, a readout and a loss condition from one
passage.**

- The **definition** is a *conjunction of two attributions held at once* — self→field and
  field→self. That is the same conjunctive shape as `M1.A.9a`'s two-axis identity, and it means
  `systems_perspective` should be **computed as the weaker of two terms**, not as a scalar. An agent
  that attributes everything to the field (helplessness) fails as surely as one that attributes
  everything to itself.
- "**gained or regained**" is direct support for `M4.C.4` — it is losable.
- It supplies the missing half of `M4.C.6`. `M4.C.6` requires an agent with perspective to attribute
  part of a received event to **its own prior emission**; that is the self→field direction only.
  `FE01.5` requires the other direction too. **`M4.C.6` MUST be made two-sided.**
- `M11.F.6`'s general rule gains a fifth independent instance.

## ⭐⭐ FE01.6 — Symptom emergence needs **more than the relationship system**, and a symptom can be **protective** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 7, and it is one of the most consequential passages in the chapter:

> "Whether a serious clinical problem emerges **depends on more variables than those of the family
> relationship system.** If a person with **very little emotional autonomy**, for example, has
> **ideal life circumstances, he may never demonstrate any overt symptoms** normally associated with
> clinical schizophrenia. He may, for example, have a support system that is **quite available to
> him, but does not pressure him.** While that may result in his being in a childlike position for
> his whole life, the low stress level would make the more obvious clinical signs and symptoms…
> less likely to appear. **If another person with little autonomy develops a serious alcohol or
> eating problem, the presence of these problems may 'protect' him from developing other types of
> clinical syndromes.**"

*Model consequence:* **three requirements, and the first is a direct hit on the estimator.**

1. **`M1.A.4b` is stated in the source.** "Ideal life circumstances → no overt symptoms despite very
   little autonomy" is precisely *an easy life reads as differentiation*, which `M1.A.4b`'s
   load-weighting exists to defeat. `M1.A.4b` was written as a modelling judgement; it is `[T]`.
   **The grade rises.**
2. The **support system that is available but does not pressure** is a distinct configuration the
   model cannot currently express: a tie with high `bond_energy` and **low routed load**. `M1.D.6`'s
   `access_vector` is the nearest thing and it is family-level. Worth recording as a tie-level
   attribute — *availability without demand* — since it is the condition under which a low-autonomy
   agent runs symptom-free for a lifetime.
3. **Symptom channels are not only substitutable, they are mutually protective.** `M7.D.2` requires
   substitutability; `FE01.6` adds that an established symptom **suppresses the others**. That is
   `M7.D.2a`'s lock-in seen from inside the individual rather than from the family, and it means the
   three-channel vector should not be independent — occupancy of one lowers the hazard on the rest.
   **New requirement candidate**, and it makes `M11.C.22` testable at the person level as well as
   the family level.

## ⭐⭐ FE01.7 — "**anxious man versus calm man**" replaces "rational man versus animal man" `[K]` `[T]`

> "So often when humans resort to violent and brutal behavior, we say that they are acting '**like a
> bunch of animals**.' … Perhaps it would be more accurate to say that the periodic emergence of
> man's brutal self reflects **the shift from a calm and orderly human being to an anxious and
> disorderly one.** The same thing happens in a troop of chimpanzees… **So a dichotomy that may be
> more accurate than 'rational' man versus 'animal' man would simply be 'anxious' man versus 'calm'
> man. Obviously, this is not really an either-or dichotomy, because there are all degrees of
> anxiety and associated anxiety-determined behaviors.**"

*Model consequence:* **the regression axis is anxiety and it is continuous — stated as such, and
stated as a *correction* to the intellect-versus-instinct framing.** This matters for `M1.A.3`,
which implements the transition at 50 as *a licence over joint decisions* rather than a general
suppression of intellect: `FE01.7` is the general principle behind that choice, and it says the
underlying variable is graded, not a switch. It also underwrites `M4.D.3a`'s complexity ordering —
regression slides selection down the ordering continuously.

## ⭐ FE01.8 — Emotional autonomy is **low by default**, and that is the claim `[K]` `[T]`

> "Probably the major implication is that **people have less autonomy in their emotional functioning
> than is commonly thought.** Freud emphasized the fact that human beings were often motivated by
> unconscious forces rather than rational thinking. Human beings, in this sense, lacked autonomy
> from their **inner selves**. If the family is an emotional unit, however, then people often
> function in ways that are a reflection of what is occurring around them. **They have precious
> little autonomy from their environment.**"

*Model consequence:* `M4.D.1a`'s mixing weight — "at low level the person is nearly all automatic" —
is the implementation of this sentence, and `M10.A.1a` derives agency as the solid-self fraction
rather than parameterising it. `FE01.8` is the corpus statement that the *default* is low, which
matters for initialisation: `M1.A.18c` already requires most agents to start near zero on
`systems_perspective`; this says the same of agency.

## ⭐ FE01.9 — Relationship system **describes**; emotional system **explains** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Systems is a descriptive term. It does not account for what is occurring, for what 'drives' the
> process.** Bowen eventually dealt with this problem by making a distinction between the family
> **relationship system** and the family **emotional system. The relationship system was a
> description of what happened, and the emotional system was an explanation for what happened.**"

> "Saying that the human relationship process is **rooted in instincts**, has much in common with
> what occurs in other forms of life, and has a function in evolutionary terms is a step toward
> accounting for what occurs."

*Model consequence:* second written witness for `M1.A.0` — *emotional* means **instinct**, and the
word marks the explanatory layer rather than the descriptive one. `M1.A.0` currently cites two ASR
sources under a no-verbatim constraint; this is a quotable primary. **Upgrade the citation.**

It also names a real distinction for the model's own architecture: the event graph and tie states
are the *relationship system* (description); the instinctual drives that select moves are the
*emotional system* (explanation). The engine implements both and should not conflate them in
readouts.

## ⭐ FE01.10 — Anxious reactivity **blocks learning**, by forcing premature closure `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "there is a strong tendency in all of us to **try to make it fit with what we already know**… It
> is as if the brain experiences some sort of '**cognitive tension**' when confronted with ideas or
> observations that are 'supposed' to fit together but do not. **It seems to require a fairly
> nonreactive brain, a brain that does not automatically react to force ideas together, to avoid
> this sort of premature intellectual closure**… This type of mental foreclosure, while reducing the
> 'cognitive tension,' also **obliterates the chance to learn.** One molds new observations and
> information to fit with preexisting assumptions about the world rather than allowing the new
> information to be a stimulus to examine basic assumptions."

*Model consequence:* **this is `M4.C.2`'s gain function applied to `M1.E.7`'s landing condition, and
it closes a gap.** `M4.C.2` requires that above threshold, event content is *defended against rather
than absorbed*. `M1.E.7` requires `systems_perspective` to rise only on a *landed* contact. Nothing
currently connects them. `FE01.10` states the connection directly: **a reactive agent cannot take in
information that conflicts with what it already holds**, so landing must be gated by the recipient's
current anxiety, not only by `M1.E.7d`'s occasion and `M1.E.7`'s binder failure.

Note the sharp edge: foreclosure is **anxiety-relieving** ("while reducing the cognitive tension"),
so it is selected for by exactly the short-horizon proxy `M4.D.6a` forbids. The same forbidden
gradient appears here at the epistemic level.

## ⭐ FE01.11 — Differentiation was introduced **to explain unequal intensity** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 6:

> "**Bowen later developed the concept of differentiation of self which accounted for the fact that
> the processes being described, while present in all families, did not operate with equal intensity
> and predictability in all families.**"

*Model consequence:* the cleanest statement of what differentiation *is for* in the architecture —
it is the **modulator on pattern intensity**, not a separate process. This is `M1.C.3a` exactly
(routing capacity is a function of members' level, not of the triangle) generalised to every
pattern in the model. It is also an argument for `M10.A.1`'s derivation policy: patterns should
share one modulator rather than each carrying its own.

## ⭐ FE01.12 — The subjectivity screen **thickens with closeness to self** `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "Although the subjective 'screen' we place between us and the world around us is always to be
> reckoned with, no matter what we are observing, the **'screen' appears to get 'thicker' as we get
> closer to observing ourselves. The closer we get to ourselves, the greater the pressure to see
> what we want to see or, at least, to see what we have always seen.**"

> "Probably the most important subjectively determined block to observing human behavior has been
> the… difficulty in **seeing the part oneself plays in the functioning of others.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.18d`'s per-tie attenuation has a general mechanism, and it is not
about ties.** `M1.A.18d` says the effective value of `systems_perspective` on a tie is attenuated by
that tie's emotional intensity, and cites two cases (Kerr's marriage, Mr. S.). `FE01.12` gives the
underlying variable: **attenuation is by closeness to self**, of which a loaded tie is one instance.
Implementation stays as `M1.A.18d` states it; the citation improves and the mechanism generalises.

The second sentence is `M4.C.6` again — the *specific* thing the screen hides is one's own
contribution — which is why reappraisal is self-attribution rather than re-interpretation.

## ⭐ FE01.13 — A family does **not** traverse the scale in one generation `[K]` `[T]` `[D]`

> "It is, rather, an **outcome**. It is the outcome of a biologically rooted process that has many
> participants and that has **gradually taken shape over a long period of time. A family does not
> change from very good functioning to very poor functioning in one generation.**"

Footnote 2 gives the mechanism as a **graded chain**:

> "he eventually accounted for the intensity of the symbiotic attachment in one generation on the
> basis of a **gradual buildup of that intensity over many generations.** The mother of a
> schizophrenic patient… had a symbiotic relationship with her own mother **only slightly less
> intense** than the one with her child. The mother's mother had a symbiotic attachment to her
> mother **only slightly less intense** than with her child, etc."

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.6(a)`'s traversal constraint gains a second, independent, earlier
source.** The spec currently cites `KS11.1` (2019) for "eight to ten generations or as few as three
or four", recorded as a shape constraint only. `FE01.13` states the lower bound directly — **not one
generation** — in 1988, in writing. It also supplies the *per-step* shape: "only slightly less
intense" at each hop, which is a **small-increment** constraint on `M7.E.1a`'s three-outcome
distribution, and it does so **without** naming a rate. `M10.C.3`'s prohibition on a per-generation
rate is untouched.

## FE01.14 — The general-systems objection, second statement, more specific `[K]` `[T]`

> "General systems ideas grew out of the assumption that similar mathematical expressions and models
> were applicable in biology, the behavioral and social sciences, as well as in the physical
> sciences… The development of these models was **strongly influenced by man-made systems such as
> the simple domestic thermostat, steam and electric machines, rocketry, and computers.** At this
> point in our knowledge, any extension of general systems concepts to the family… **must be limited
> to saying the family operates in ways that are often similar to the dynamics of physical systems.
> The forces that create the similarity are not part of general systems theory and most of the basic
> concepts of family theory are not in general systems.**"

And the definition of *natural*:

> "**The principles that govern a natural system are written in nature and not created by the human
> brain.** … We did not design human relationships anymore than the elephant or gibbon designed
> their family relationships."

*Model consequence:* strengthens `M11.F.3` with `FE00.1`. The named list — *thermostat, steam
engine, rocketry, computers* — is pointed, and a simulation belongs to it. **This must be quoted
in `M11.F.3` rather than paraphrased**, because it is the sharpest form of the objection the corpus
contains and softening it would be exactly what `M11.F.3` forbids.

## FE01.15 — The "**as if**" clause — the epistemics a simulation actually needs `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 14:

> "this distinction does not imply that a theory of human behavior that strives to be objective…
> is a theory that is about the way the world really is. **While we can attempt to develop theories
> that are consistent with all available observations about nature, we can never be sure what nature
> is really like. We can only say that nature operates 'as if' a particular theory is accurate.** In
> addition, this distinction between objectivity and subjectivity is **not to imply that one is
> 'good' and the other 'bad.'**"

*Model consequence:* **the framing `M11.F` needs and does not currently have.** The model is an
*as-if* computation, and the authors state that this is the correct status for the theory itself,
not a concession forced on the simulation. It converts `M11.F.3`'s objection from a wound into a
shared position: theory and simulation are both "as if", and the question is only whether each is
consistent with the observations. **`M11.F` requirement candidate**, and it pairs with `FE00.1`'s
derived/imposed test as the two halves of an honest statement of what this project is.

## FE01.16 — Theory yields to observation, never the reverse `[K]` `[T]`

> "**It has always been the task of science to modify theories and models to fit observations as
> opposed to modifying or ignoring observations to preserve existing theories.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.F.1` — the model is a thing to be tested — with a written 1988 source
alongside the interview material. Also the standing rule of this project's own extraction passes:
pass 2 withdrew nineteen pass-1 findings, in every case because the extraction had been shaped to
fit the model.

## FE01.17 — The barrier between "normal" and "schizophrenic" is **built by helping** `[K]` `[T]`

> "There is a tendency in all of us to want to see schizophrenia… as somehow separate from
> ourselves… **The 'normals' often benevolently minister to the 'schizophrenic' ones and, in this
> effort to be helpful, often firm up the barrier, further isolating the schizophrenic person and
> family. This barrier is of our own making, not nature's act.**"

> "**We create the schizophrenia we see around us. We create it by virtue of the way we function
> everyday.** We continually make decisions and do things that **tend to impair as well as promote
> the functioning of others.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.13`'s help-relocates-not-reduces criterion, stated as a mechanism rather
than an outcome — the helping act itself *increases* the recipient's isolation. That is a positive
sign on a specific edge, and it is what `M1.E.5`'s negative-signed burden-transfer term and
`M1.E.3`'s responsibility absorption implement. The second quote is `KS05.17`'s symmetry
requirement — every agent both impairs and promotes — thirty-one years earlier.
