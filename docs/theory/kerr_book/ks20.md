# KS20 — Gary Gilmore and His Family

**Citation rule:** cite `KS20`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Evidence class.** Reconstructed from two published books — Mikal Gilmore's *Shot in the Heart* (1995) and Mailer's *The Executioner's Song* (1979). Kerr never met the family. Grade as `KS19`: family facts `[T]`, mechanisms `[D]`, causal attributions `[K-ext]`. Its value is as an **extreme-end validation trajectory** — the lowest-differentiation family in the book.

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ KS20.1 — **"Family of extinction"** — the model's absorbing state, named and sourced `[T]`

Citing Bowen theorist Roberta Holt (2011), quoting Bowen (1976):

> "**[Dying out families] referred to families that had patterns of low fertility, in the presence of extremely poor functioning, characterized by physical, social, and emotional dysfunctioning for many members of the family.**"

> "**The family of Frank and Bessie Gilmore has no descendants.**"

The four sons: Gary executed at 36; Gaylen dead at ~27 after being beaten and stabbed; Frank Jr. lived with his mother "**for the next seventeen years, until the day she died**", with a promising relationship apparently sabotaged by her; Mikal, "**the sibling that I assess to have developed the highest level of 'self'**", one marriage lasting a few years, no children, and "**the first to acknowledge his inability to have a sustained intimate relationship.**"

> "**The levels of 'self' are so low and the emotional interdependence so extreme that most family relationships are woefully unstable and program far more undifferentiation than differentiation into the offspring.**"

*Model consequence:* **`KS11.9`'s extinction clause given a name, a citation and a worked instance.** This is the model's **absorbing state at the bottom of the scale**, and it has a specific signature the model can produce: **low fertility co-occurring with poor functioning**, which is not obvious — one might expect the opposite.

`M7.C.1`'s life stages govern partnering and mortality; this requires that **reproduction itself be suppressed at very low differentiation**, via relationship instability rather than by a fertility parameter. That is implementable from `M1.B`'s tie dynamics and it makes the extinction outcome **emergent**, which is the right shape.

Note also the **gradient across four siblings** — executed, killed, caretaker-for-life, and highest-functioning-but-cannot-sustain-a-relationship. Even the best outcome carries the signature. `M11.C.6`'s projection-line decline should be assertable over a sibling set, not only across generations.

## ⭐⭐⭐ KS20.2 — **The intervention triangle**: Bessie interrupts, and the conflict transfers to the parents `[T]` `[M]`

The family's recurring sequence, from Frank Jr.'s account (Figure 20.2):

1. Gary commits some disobedient act; **Frank Sr. beats him** — "razor strap, belts, and fists". "**Gary would yell, which only made the beatings worse.**" Gary "**would feel outraged at the unfairness of it all.**"
2. "**At some point Bessie would intervene, demanding Frank Sr. stop the beating.**"
3. "**when Bessie intervened, the conflict would shift from between Gary and Frank Sr. to between the parents.** Frank Sr. would beat Bessie, who would scream and yell."

> "**Frank Jr. thought that the real problem was between his parents, each wanting the other's attention.**"

*Model consequence:* **a complete, repeating triangle-position transfer driven by a third party's intervention** — and it is the *rescuing* move that performs the transfer. `KS03.4` gave two routes for an outsider to become an insider; **this is a third**: the outsider intervenes on behalf of the suffering party, and the conflict relocates to the tie between intervener and aggressor.

That is directly implementable and it is a **cost the model should show**: the intervention succeeds locally (the beating stops) and relocates the conflict rather than reducing it — `M11.C.13`'s help-relocates claim, at the tick level and inside a family rather than between family and community.

Frank Jr.'s reading — the parents each wanting the other's attention — makes the child's suffering the **medium** of a marital exchange, which is `M11.C.2`/`KS04.7` and the reason `M1.C` must let a triangle's leg carry traffic that belongs to another leg.

## ⭐⭐ KS20.3 — **"I love you no matter what you do"**, with the legal system as amplifier `[T]` `[M]`

Bowen's clinical observation, quoted: parents of severe acting-out offspring "**consistently convey a message to that offspring of 'I love you no matter what you do'**… **No matter how severe the punishments inflicted on the child, it does not dilute the basic message.**"

The behavioural record: Frank Sr. hired a private detective to disprove a vandalism charge; hired a lawyer who defended a car theft as unwitting accompliceship; when a judge finally sentenced Gary to reform school, "**Frank Sr. cussed out that judge and was thrown out of the courtroom.**" His standing advice: "**Don't admit anything.**" During an escape from reform school, "**Bessie lied to authorities to cover for him.**" And after the murders, Frank Sr. long dead, the pattern persisted in the extended family: Vern and Ida "**were not mad at him and still wanted to help him.**"

> "**The court's opinion was that Gary's parents refused to see him as he really was.**"

> "**permissiveness was punctuated by Frank Sr.'s harsh efforts to control Gary. This pattern prevailed through all of Gary's growing-up years.**"

And the societal coupling, made explicit: this occurred during the period Bowen's court review found "**the courts were tending toward overly permissive decisions, similar to the decisions the parents of juvenile delinquents were also making.**"

*Model consequence:* **`KS06.7`'s Madison finding and `KS14.4`'s cascade converging on the same shape, now with the institution in the loop.** Three requirements:

1. **Punishment and permissiveness coexist and do not cancel.** The severity of the beatings did not alter the message. So the model **MUST NOT** let a harsh move offset an accommodating one — they act on different channels, which is why `KS14.4`'s oscillating policy is worse than either extreme.
2. **The accommodation extends beyond the household.** Legal defence, lying to authorities, the aunt and uncle's continued support after two murders. `KS19.3`'s accommodation stock is **family-wide and outlives its originators.**
3. **The societal dial and the family pattern reinforced each other in the same period.** `M1.D.7`'s coupling running in both directions (`KS14.4`), with a dated instance.

## ⭐⭐ KS20.4 — Gary and Nicole: **fusion as the only source of well-being**, and its rupture as the trigger `[T]` `[M]`

> "**It was not until he met her and they developed an initially stable connection that Gary developed a strong sense of emotional well-being. He had gotten relief from the anxieties generated by his dealings with other human beings during periods of solitary in the prisons, but that nowhere near matched the contentment he derived from the relationship with Nicole.**"

The matching: "**Gary tended to violate others' emotional boundaries; Nicole allowed others to violate her boundaries. Both acted out of their profound hunger for a romantic/sexual relationship to reinforce them.**" And "**Bowen theory would say that her lack of 'self' was equal to Gary's.**"

Nicole's own account of her side: "**It is easier to let things happen in a relationship than to tell a guy to leave you alone.**"

The rupture and its consequence, in Gary's words: "**This is a pain I can't take**"; "**It hurt so bad, it was becoming physical. I could hardly walk, sleep, or eat. Booze did not dull it, and it was steadily getting worse. Every day grew into calm rage. I opened the gate and let it out.**"

The murders followed **one week** after she left, on consecutive nights, of two men who posed no threat, during robberies of small sums — "**One shot for me, one shot for Nicole.**"

And the equivalence Kerr states: "**Bowen theory assumes that the degrees of emotional fusion with Bessie and later Nicole were equal.**" Gary's two tattoos: "**Mom**" and "**Nicole**".

*Model consequence:* three things, all model-relevant.

1. **`M1.A.9a`'s two axes at maximum contrast and equal magnitude** — violating boundaries versus permitting violation — stated by Kerr as *equal* lack of self. `M11.C.19`'s equal-magnitude arm, again, in the most extreme available case.
2. **The nuclear-family fusion was transferred whole to the partner.** `M1.B`'s substitution mechanism (`KS09.6`'s "later substitutes for the parents"), with Kerr asserting the two fusions were of **equal degree**. That is an assertable conservation claim: the unresolved attachment is not diminished by transfer.
3. **Rupture of the sole fusion object produced externalised catastrophe within days.** `KS19.5`'s Kaczynski case had removal of *contact* producing remission; this is removal of the *fusion object* producing the opposite. Both are the same variable — the model must produce both signs depending on whether what is removed is a load or a support.

Kerr's own summary is the causal claim, and it is stated carefully as a trigger rather than a cause: "**The inevitable disruption of the relationship of two very needy people with unrealistic expectations of themselves and each other was the critical factor in triggering the murders.**"

## ⭐⭐ KS20.5 — Psychotic and psychopathic as **one continuum**, internalising versus externalising `[T]` `[M]`

Kerr takes up the epigraph's question — a "bridge from the psychopathic to the insane" — and answers it in the theory's terms:

> "**Bowen theory views both extreme degrees of psychotic symptoms and antisocial actions as reflecting severe levels of emotional dysfunction. They are disorders of the emotional system**, in Gary and in his family emotional system."

> "**Bowen theory holds that psychotic people internalize their anxiety in the form of delusions and hallucinations (which in some instances may direct them to kill someone), and socially acting-out people habitually externalize the anxiety into the relationship system (sometimes in the form of doing violence to others).**"

And the consequence he draws for responsibility:

> "the legal system currently views people who commit murder while in a psychotic state as **less responsible** than people deemed to be psychopaths… **However, the almost unfathomable intensity of emotional forces in this case makes me think that the dividing line here is not nearly as clear-cut as medicine and the legal system have assumed.**"

*Model consequence:* **`KS10.5`'s internalised/externalised axis doing its most important work.** The two diagnostic categories are **the same level on the same scale, differing only in discharge direction** — which is exactly `M1.A.11`'s substitutable channels, and it means the model needs **no diagnostic categories at all**.

`KS19.11` said outcome *form* is not predictable; this says the two forms most often treated as different *kinds* are one kind with a sign. That is a strong and testable structural claim, and it is the same finding as `KS19.11`'s contrast (Kerr's brother's suicide versus Kaczynski's killings) stated as a principle.

The responsibility remark is Kerr's own and is flagged as opinion — `[K-ext]`, and it should **not** enter the model, which per `KS01.5`/`M11.F` makes no culpability findings.

## ⭐ KS20.6 — Two poorly differentiated parents, assembled from the record `[T]`

**Frank Sr.:** "**at least six brief marriages that ended in divorce before he met Bessie**", a first child born out of wedlock whom he "**never saw or supported**", a second son handed to his own mother after which he "**went eighteen years without seeing that son… or his own mother**", "**no extended family that were of any significance to him during his adult years**", and "**a relationship nomad**" until 1938. Married Bessie days after meeting her again by chance, having previously told her he was marrying someone else the next day.

**Bessie:** fifth of nine in a strict Mormon family, "**deep insecurity and much self-blame**", "**a pattern of emotional cutoff surfaced between Bessie and her family**", left Provo in her early twenties, "**felt the judgment and scorn of her family, especially when she returned home for brief visits.**"

> "This information… **adds up to these being two poorly differentiated spouses.**"

*Model consequence:* `M2.A.0c`'s matched pairing at the bottom of the scale, with **both** parties showing the same signature — **severe extended-family cutoff** — arrived at independently. That is `KS07.6`'s isolation stressor as an *inherited* rather than produced condition, and it means the couple began with no external supportive network at all, which `KS05.15` names as one of two modifiers besides basic level.

The "relationship nomad" label is `KS13.2`'s strategy-2 pattern applied to the *parent*, so the model should show the pattern transmitted: Frank Sr. nomadic across marriages, Gary nomadic across institutions and then fatally dependent on one tie.

## ⭐ KS20.7 — Sibling differentiation by triangle position, four ways `[T]` `[D]`

- **Gary** — "**my special son, the one I have to love the most**" (Bessie). "**It is common for the offspring who becomes the most dysfunctional in a sibling group to be 'special' to the mother. The mother perceives that child to need more of her love than the others.**"
- **Frank Jr.** — from infancy "**Bessie relied increasingly on Frank Jr. to help take care of Gary. This was a pattern that powerfully shaped the older boy's life. He would be the one that would take care of their mother until she died.**" He also did Gary's chores "**in an effort to prevent the beatings**". "**The development of 'self' for Frank Jr. was blocked by a functioning position in the family of taking care of others to the detriment of his own life moving forward.**"
- **Gaylen** — similar estimated basic level to Gary, different discharge: "**Gaylen sought solace in alcohol and womanizing. Gary sought solace in being a criminal.**"
- **Mikal** — "**beaten by his father only once, partly because Bessie protected him**… **a bit less caught in the family problem**", but "**the legacy of his level of intense involvement manifested in the inability as an adult to sustain a relationship with a woman.**"

*Model consequence:* **four siblings, four positions, four outcomes, one family** — the fullest instance of `KS09.1`'s same-family-different-triangles in the corpus. Two specifics:

- **"Special to the mother" is a positive-valence marker of the projection target.** With `KS09.7`'s idealised child, that is now two independent statements that the target may present as favoured. A readout keyed to criticism would miss both.
- **Frank Jr.'s position was assigned in infancy and never released** — caretaker of the sibling, then of the mother, for seventeen years to her death. That is `KS12.2`'s functional-position assignment producing a life course, and `KS11.4`'s sibling reciprocity in its costly direction.

Kerr's estimate that Gary and Gaylen had "**fairly similar basic levels… but their anxieties played out in different ways**" is `KS19.11`/`KS20.5` again: level predicts vulnerability, not form.

## ⭐ KS20.8 — Anxiety fell when the symptomatic member was removed `[T]`

> "**Not surprisingly, the family tensions dropped significantly during Gary's time in reform school. Bessie was the only family member looking forward to Gary's return.**"

And on his return: "**The peace in the family lasted only a few days.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.11`'s removal test with the ordinary, non-catastrophic sign** — removal of the *focus* member relieves the system, and the relief is real. `KS10.4`'s shock wave had removal of a *stabiliser* devastating the system; this is the complement.

The one dissenting party — the mother, who alone wanted him back — is the mechanism: the projection tie is the one that loses. So the model should show **removal producing opposite-signed effects on different members**, keyed to their position relative to the removed member, rather than a uniform family-level shift. That is a genuinely testable refinement of `M11.C.11`.

## ⭐ KS20.9 — The dinner table: any of the three can start it `[T]` `[M]`

Bessie has cooked a large meal; the family is seated; Gary is late. Bessie says, "**I wonder where Gary is at.**"

> "**Frank Sr. visibly reacted to her comment, likely feeling he was supposed to fix the problem. When Gary finally did get there, Frank Sr. started yelling at him. It was very upsetting to Bessie and she and Frank Sr. got into a vicious fight. During the fight Bessie turned the dinner table over, spilling all the freshly made food onto the floor.**"

> "**This added twist illustrates Frank Sr.'s exquisite sensitivity to Bessie's upsets. Had he kept his cool and not jumped on Gary, the family might have derived some enjoyment from the meal. The point is that anxiety in any of the three members of the parental triangle can activate its escalation. People's sensitivities to each other are so great that it is often difficult to know where it starts.**"

*Model consequence:* a **six-word trigger** producing a full escalation, and Kerr's point is the model's: **entry into the cycle is symmetric across all three positions.** `M1.C`'s triangle must not have a privileged initiator.

It is also a clean counterfactual — "had he kept his cool" — which is `KS08.1`'s interception at the point where it did **not** happen. The same tick, the same three agents, one withheld move away from a different outcome. **A short, complete, two-arm fixture.**

## ⭐ KS20.10 — The rebellion is not differentiation — restated at the extreme `[T]`

> "**Gary's anxiety spiked during adolescence as he moved out into the world armed primarily with pseudo-self and little solid self. His rebellious, defiant character was clearly a way to establish some sort of anxiety-relieving identity for himself. It is not differentiation but a reactive oppositional state.**"

Gary's own accounts: "**It is all real, and I know I did it… but I don't feel responsible. It's as if I had to do it, it was a compulsion… I didn't know I was going to shoot him until I did it. I did not feel I had control of my actions.**" And: "**It has been a lifetime of lonely frustration. I have allowed weak habits to develop that have left me somewhat evil. I don't like being evil and I desire not to be evil anymore.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS04.5`'s adolescent-rebellion finding and `KS05.7`'s knee-jerk opposition as a **counterfeit emotional boundary**, at the far end of the scale. The first-person account of *absent agency* — "I did not feel I had control of my actions" — is `KS02.1`/`KS12.11`'s graded-agency claim reported from the low end, and it is the model's mixing weight near zero.

Kerr does not use this to excuse; he uses it to locate. That distinction is exactly `M11.F`'s no-culpability-without-absolution pairing (`KS16.11`).

## KS20.11 — Emotionally driven without feeling for the victim `[T]`

Gary: "**I was capable of murder. I can become totally devoid of feelings for others, unemotional. I know I am doing something wrong, but I still go ahead and do it.**"

> "**Bowen theory would say that Gary's actions were emotionally driven, but feelings for the victims were absent.**"

MacLean (1990), cited: "**the neocortex can act somewhat like a coldly reasoning, heartless computer**" — the kind that lets monkeys "**scheme their way like gangsters into another troop, murder the dominant male, and perform infanticide in the presence of the distressed mothers.**"

*Model consequence:* **the sharpest available demonstration that `M1.A.0` is load-bearing rather than terminological.** An act can be maximally *emotional* in the theory's sense — instinct-driven, anxiety-discharging — while entirely devoid of *feeling*. A model that represented the anxieties as feeling-states could not produce this agent at all.

It also confirms `KS00.3`'s guide/motivate split at its most disturbing: the cortical system supplied planning and the subcortical supplied the drive, with no affective brake between them.

## KS20.12 — The mold metaphor, and the closing epistemic claim `[T]`

> "**Bowen theory helps to explain Gary Gilmore and his family in some unique ways — it applies to this case in the way a mold fits perfectly over an object that was casted from it.**"

> "**I conclude by posing the question of whether complex family life can indeed be reduced to two basic life forces and four patterns of emotional functioning. I have asked myself this repeatedly in my almost fifty years of studying Bowen theory.**"

> "**The many individual factors assembled by researchers over the decades that have been found to correlate with the development of various clinical conditions — such as smoking and cancer — are important to understand but do not provide a complete picture… The inclusion of variables that Bowen theory describes can help move our understanding of human problems toward a more complete picture.**"

*Model consequence:* the claim is **additive, not exclusive** — Bowen variables *add* to the picture rather than replacing established correlates. That is the right framing for `M11.F` and it is consistent with `KS04.9` (anxiety necessary, never sufficient) and `KS15.12` (validation by absence of inconsistency with accepted research).

The mold metaphor is worth flagging as a **methodological caution rather than a strength**: a framework that fits a case perfectly in retrospect is not thereby predictive. Kerr's own honesty — asking the reductionism question repeatedly over fifty years — is the right note, and the project should carry the caution explicitly when Part III cases are cited.
