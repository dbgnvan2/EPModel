# KB04 — Anxiety and Emotional Reactivity in Therapy

**Citation rule:** findings cite the interview (`KB04`), not a speaker. Kerr is Bowen's successor and equally authoritative; questions are ~99% Kerr. ASR source — **no verbatim quotation**. See `_SOURCE_QUALITY.md`.

---

## ⚠ K04.1 — FOUR ways of absorbing anxiety, and it reconciles with the three sinks rather than contradicting them

The interview enumerates **four**: distance between the spouses, marital conflict, sickness in a spouse, projection to children — "these are the four main ways in a nuclear family."

The book counts **three** and puts distance outside (Ch16, Ch09, Ch12, Ch18), and the 1979 lecture counts three while naming distance first and separately.

**These answer different questions, and the difference is the finding.** KB04 asks what *absorbs, manages, deactivates anxiety* — distance qualifies, and is listed first. The 1979 lecture asks what *ends up with symptoms* — "I list the three ways they deal with it **because these are the three things that end up with symptoms**", and distance does not qualify.

*Model consequence:* `M6.I.1`/`M6.I.2` are right about the **symptom budget** and incomplete about anxiety. Distance **absorbs anxiety without producing a symptom** — which is exactly why it sits outside the symptom budget and why a family can discharge into it indefinitely and read as untroubled. The spec should say both, not just the second.

## K04.2 — Reactivity is a manifestation of anxiety `[T]` — and the drum

> reactivity is "a kind of a manifestation of the anxiety"; both framings are theoretically correct and "essentially the same"; but "the notion of reactivity gives us something **we can get our hands on and see** better than this vague notion of anxiety."

Kerr offers a drum analogy — tightening the skin is the anxiety level, the noise when struck is the reactivity — and Bowen accepts it as "specifically accurate… on target" while saying he does not like it.

*Model consequence:* **the strongest support yet for `M1.A.1`** (reactivity derived, not stored), and it adds the mechanism: anxiety is the standing tension, reactivity is the response to a stimulus. That is directly implementable as `reactivity = f(anxiety_level, stimulus)` and it fills the gap the 1979 lectures left silent (A3).

## K04.3 — The symptom envelope, stated as an interaction `[T]`

> "You can have anxiety so low that even the **most impaired** family is relatively free of symptoms. You can have anxiety so high that even the **best adjusted** family can develop symptoms, and all of us are in between."

*Model consequence:* symptom onset is a joint function of differentiation and anxiety with no threshold on either alone. This is the positive form of the decoupling guard (`M1.D.7`, Ch18) and constrains `M11.C.1`.

## K04.4 — Anxiety-first is a hard ordering, not a preference `[T]`

> "we use two modalities of therapy, and one is decrease the anxiety, and two is differentiation of self, and **you don't start differentiating the self until you have the anxiety down.** So number one is get the anxiety down."

*Model consequence:* corroborates Ch22 · `L22.1` — contact reduces anxiety and *permits* differentiation — and makes it operational as a **sequencing gate** rather than a modulation. Belongs in `M5.C` alongside the existing gates.

## K04.5 — Anxiety binding is a distinct mechanism from the four `[T]`

Named binders: religiosity, over-meticulousness, picketing, obesity, ulcers, obsessions, compulsions, addictions. "In the binding of anxiety, it is **non-operational** at that time… It is still there, and it might come bubbling out."

And a warning that is C1 in miniature: "if you have a good anxiety binder, what's wrong? **Don't try to cure it.** You might have to give it up."

*Model consequence:* a mechanism the model lacks — anxiety held out of circulation without being discharged into a sink and without producing a relational symptom. It is conserved (`M6.I.6`) but inactive, and removing the binder releases it.

## K04.6 — Which sink a person routes to is inherited, not chosen `[M]`

> "the character of the symptom is **programmed into the person out of the multigenerational past**, and that determines whether this person will focus on the other spouse… whether one of the spouses is programmed to accept responsibility and get sick."

*Model consequence:* the model currently selects a sink from current state. This says the *disposition* is a per-person inherited attribute set before the marriage. New field, and it interacts with `M7.E`.

## K04.7 — Acute and chronic separate on real versus imagined threat `[T]`

> acute "comes suddenly… more in the realm of the **real** threats"; chronic "comes more out of the **imagined** threats. It's the poorly differentiated people that has all these fears and concerns of what might be."

Plus: "It's fairly common for people to come through catastrophic things and adapt well… get anxious **after it's over**."

*Model consequence:* sharper than the book's chronicity finding (Ch18 · `L18.2`). Gives the two anxieties different *sources* rather than only different time constants, and predicts a post-event onset the model does not produce.

## K04.8 — Differentiation, not event magnitude, governs disaster response `[D]`

On a proposed study of population response to disaster: "it's **not the size and the massiveness** of the disaster, the flood, the earthquake, the fire… it has to do with the **level of the people**." Good-functioning people adapt, poor-functioning become symptomatic, "and all gradations in between."

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.1` restated as a claim about **exogenous** events, and a caution for `M1.F` — an exogenous event's magnitude must not dominate the response.

## K04.9 — Media as an anxiety amplifier, with a worked case `[T]` — this grounds `media_amplification`

On Three Mile Island: the incident was "pretty well contained. But the **anxious media** got it, and you know what happened. Now, if an **un-anxious media** could have reported more factually, without all this emotional elaboration, we would have had a different world… **Same thing happens in a family.**"

*Model consequence:* the `media_amplification` dial has been graded `[I]` on the basis that Ch18's six downward channels do not include media. **KB04 grounds it directly**, with a mechanism (emotional elaboration of a contained event) and an explicit family analogy. Regrade, and note the mechanism is *amplification of an event's emotional framing*, not new events.

## K04.10 — Declaring detachment is evidence of attachment `[T]` — C10 again, with a signature

> "One of the biggest pretends you can have is to tell somebody, 'I'm not going to get into your problem.' **Well, that in itself is an indicator of involvement.**" And: someone genuinely neutral "**they don't say 'I'm not going to get in it,' they're just not in it.**"

Also on the reversal/paradox: it "**can't be a way of getting you out, but it can be a way of communicating that you are out**", and performed from inside it lands as "sarcastic, nasty, or you're cold and indifferent."

*Model consequence:* C10 corroborated from a fifth setting, and it supplies an **observable**: announcing detachment is a negative indicator of `outside_ness`. A move that *signals* a hidden state cannot *create* it.

## K04.11 — The coach's loss-and-recovery loop, worked `[M]`

A session with a terminally ill man: the therapist finds himself discussing cancer cells and catches it — "your head is now chasing cancer cells in his body and **you have lost your value**." The recovery is a factual relationship question — what is he doing to help his wife adapt after his death — which produces laughter from both and "one of the best sessions I've had."

Related readout: "**when you're caught up in the emotional system, you can't be humorous**."

*Model consequence:* `L20.2` gives the coach's self-state as a detector; KB04 adds a **recovery move** and a second observable — the return of humour. Both are cheap to implement and neither requires estimating anyone's differentiation.

## K04.12 — Having no therapeutic goal produced better outcomes `[T]`

> "the families I had in **research** did better than families I had in **therapy**… Now I would see this as the therapist being **outside the problem**, and people do better. So almost every family I see is a kind of a research family. I'm not in there to help, I'm in there to try to **understand**."

*Model consequence:* the strongest statement in any source that **the coach's objective cannot be to reduce family anxiety** (C1, `L06.2`). It also reframes the coach's objective function as *understanding*, which is a position rather than a goal — consistent with `M11.C.7`.

## K04.13 — System overload lands on the weakest link `[M]`

> "when the **family organism has adapted as far as it can go**. Every resource is strained, and somebody piles more anxiety on, **somebody dies. Gets sick, probably dies. The weakest link.**"

Said against a therapist proposing to expose family secrets in an already-symptomatic family: "Its decks are awash."

*Model consequence:* a capacity ceiling the model does not have, and a rule for where failure lands. Bears on `M11.C.11` and on the coach's cost model — an intervention into a saturated system is not neutral.

## K04.14 — Psychosis tracks family anxiety continuously `[D]`

> "the hallucinations, the delusions, everything could beautifully **parallel the level of anxiety in the family**. Once that anxiety starts to come down, those hallucinations come down with it."

*Model consequence:* `L06.1` — symptom as continuously-driven output — at the severe end of the range, which is where the book's evidence is weakest.

## ⚠ K04.15 — Late de-emphasis of triangles, and it needs care

> "**I don't do much with triangles anymore.** You don't talk about them as much as you used to. I used to get all into them triangles… **no difference how many triangles are out there**, really, and this has been a **recent change**."

Reduced to: "number one is reduce the anxiety… number two is work toward differentiation of self."

*Reading, and the caution:* this is about **therapeutic technique**, not about whether triangles exist — the same interview uses the triangle to explain how detachment reduces anxiety. But it is a late statement from a source that also calls "triangle" his "most unfortunate term" (Ch17 · `L17.4`), and the model makes `Triangle` a first-class object with its own state.

**✅ RESOLVED — it is a statement about coaching technique, and the sources give the mechanism.**

1979 Tape 5, directly: the intensity of the relationship patterns "**is determined by level of differentiation and principally anxiety**" — "**well differentiated, these patterns will be mild, they'll be there in periods of anxiety and they'll go away**, and as we go down into lower levels of differentiation, **these patterns are more intense**."

So **raising functional differentiation reduces how much anxiety a triangle routes.** The coach therefore has no need to work the triangles: reduce the anxiety, work toward differentiation, and triangle activity falls out as a consequence. That is the whole of "no difference how many triangles are out there."

Corroborated by Ch17 · `L17.1` (triangles inoperative when calm), Ch18 · `L18.2` (patterns intensify with anxiety and vanish when calm), 1979 Tape 1 (a calm triangle is a twosome plus an outsider; tension recruits), and `KB04` itself — one member staying detached "and the anxiety between them disappeared."

**The `Triangle` object stays.** It is the structure anxiety routes *through*; differentiation changes how much it carries. Encoded as `M1.C.3a`–`M1.C.3c`, the last of which separates *detriangling self* from *detriangling another* — two operations the sources give one name.

## K04.16 — Self-regulation is a separate channel from relationship regulation `[M]`

Biofeedback, meditation, yoga, jogging — "anything that focuses **within a person** and is directed at containing body activity **rather than other people in the system**." With a stated failure mode: "the problem I've found with meditation is that people get involved in a **relationship system** in meditation, which then destroys much of its value."

*Model consequence:* a second, non-relational route to lowering anxiety, which the model has no representation of — and a failure mode where it collapses back into the relationship system.
