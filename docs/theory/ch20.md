# Ch20 — Toward the Differentiation of Self in Administrative Systems

## 0. Provenance
- Stated date / venue (from the text itself): "a summary of my paper at the 1972 Georgetown Family Symposium." The programme title was "Toward the Differentiation of Self in the Georgetown 'Family'." He gives his own more accurate title as "My Own Efforts to Practice Principles of the Differentiation of Self in My Administrative Functioning as Head of the Family Faculty and the Family Programs at Georgetown University." The summary itself is written later — "Now, after two years, I hope that more can hear the subject of that paper."
- Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period): Not a clinical study. The data source is Bowen's own administrative functioning as head of the Georgetown Family Faculty and family teaching/training programmes, which "have grown slowly around me and my work since 1959" — so roughly 1959 to 1972/74. Secondary data: the family research staff of the 1950s, his clinical work with families, his own family, and the symposium audience itself ("some 1100 people," with his family faculty present). Observations are self-reported and retrospective; no counts, cases, or measurements.
- Genre: both, but weighted toward technique-as-self-management. Roughly 40% theory (the claim that work and family systems share emotional patterns and differ only in intensity; the mechanism by which unresolved parental attachment loads onto work relationships) and 60% technique (the operational rules Bowen says he applies to himself as an administrator: focus on own functioning, listen to process not content, detriangle, restrain the critical urge, define self). The technique half is unusual in that the *only* target of intervention is the writer himself — there is no patient and no therapist role.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- Relationship patterns formed in childhood for adapting to the parental family reappear in every later relationship — social and work relationships included. → observed across "all areas of relationships"; asserted from his own administrative and clinical experience.
- Work and social relationship patterns look *identical* to family patterns, differing only in intensity, with work/social intensity usually lower. → observed in work systems generally.
- But there are exceptions in which the intensity of relationships in a *work* system approximates the intensity of the original family. → observed in work systems containing lower-differentiation people.
- A wide spectrum of people find work relationships more useful than social relationships for fulfilling emotional "needs." → general population claim.
- These intense work relationships are usually covert rather than overt to a casual observer. → observed in work systems; explains why the phenomenon is easy to miss.
- Organisations of the kind he heads — a professional group grown around one person's work — are "most vulnerable" to emotional alliances and intense emotional processes that make them more like a family. → observed in the class of organisations resembling the Georgetown Family Faculty.
- "A good percentage of such organizations do not continue for many months or many years before there are major splits and disruptions in the central organization, just as there are in poorly differentiated families." → observed across such organisations; the split is located in the *central* organisation.
- Anxiety in the faculty is transmitted downward to trainees: as emotional tension in the system rises, the faculty becomes overcritical of trainees. → observed repeatedly in the Georgetown training programmes.
- Bowen himself first detects the rise not in the faculty but in his own urge to become critical of the faculty for being critical. → self-observation, offered as the reliable early indicator.
- As tension mounts, a specific cluster of behaviours increases in frequency: emotional issues between people, people withdrawing from the group or becoming silent, cliques or alliances forming between faculty members, and talk or gossip about an absent member. → observed in the faculty group.
- In the early family research, Bowen's own habit of thinking about staff and family problems and offering solutions "worked very well," yet produced staff dependence on him and no development of staff responsibility for their own solutions. → observed in the 1950s research staff.
- His symposium presentation itself was "no more than partially successful": feelings were high, there had been "a high level of overpositive systems therapy enthusiasm," some of the audience "heard" the message and most were "reacting emotionally without hearing." → observed in an audience of ~1100.
- **[p2] Emotional "needs" not met inside the family are shifted to relationships outside it — work relationships specifically.** "A wide spectrum of people find work relationships to be more useful than social relationships for fulfilling emotional 'needs.'" Better-differentiated people keep needs "sufficiently contained within the family that there is little need to shift emotional needs to relationships outside the family." → a routing claim about where undischarged need goes, at the boundary of the family rather than inside it.
- **[p2] Correction on evidentiary status.** The faculty→trainee sequence is announced as a case — "A clear-cut issue in the Georgetown Family Faculty will illustrate a way that anxiety in the faculty can be transmitted to trainees" — and then **no issue is ever described**. The entire passage runs in the habitual conditional: "the faculty *would* become overcritical," "I *often* noticed," "my effort *would* be." There is no dated event, no named person, no count, no outcome. The chapter contains **zero cases**.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Transfer of basic relationship patterns.** Patterns developed in childhood to adapt to the parental family → are reused unchanged in all later relationships → acting on social and work relationships as well as family ones. Intensity is the only stated dimension that varies.
- **Unresolved emotional attachment to parents as the load.** Lower differentiation → higher level of unresolved emotional attachment to parents → makes the work-system-approximates-family-intensity exception more pronounced. The attachment is the reservoir; the work system is where it gets discharged.
- **Denial-plus-distance as the adult adaptation.** In order to function as adults, people deny the attachments and move toward emotional distance from the parents → achieved by internal mechanisms while living close to parents, or by physical distance, or by a combination of the two. Three named routes, one outcome.
- **Cutoff displaces intensity outward.** Those who use physical distance in "cutting-off" from the parents → tend to have the most intense relationships with those *outside* the family. The intensity does not disappear; it relocates.
- **Two motive systems for work.** The better differentiated person has goal-directed interests that motivate his life work → work is driven by task. The less differentiated person tends to seek relationships at work that satisfy emotional needs → work is driven by need. These are presented as substitutable drivers of the same activity.
- **Administrative amplification.** Administrative policy, and bosses who encourage a "happy family" attitude in the work situation → further intensify the process of seeking work relationships in lieu of family relationships for fulfilment of emotional needs. Organisational policy is thus an input to individual emotional process, not merely a container for it.
- **Containment in better-differentiated families.** Better differentiation → the emotional "needs" of family members are kept sufficiently contained *within* the family → little need to shift emotional needs to relationships outside the family. Containment is the family-level variable that governs outward spillover.
- **Decision basis as the differentiation signature.** Better-differentiated parents are more contained, more sure of themselves, clearer about the responsibility of self and responsibility to each other → important family decisions are based more on the reality of the situation than on the emotion of the moment. The identical mechanism runs in bosses: less-differentiated bosses decide on the feeling of the moment rather than on principle and reality.
- **One-member modification propagates.** If there is an emotional issue in the organisation, the key member is playing a part in it → if he modifies the part he is playing → the others will do the same. Stated as a principle used in his own family, his clinical work, and his administrative functioning — i.e. explicitly carried over across system types.
- **Over-responsibility / under-responsibility as a paired defect.** Being overresponsible for others in some areas is accompanied by being irresponsible in one's own functioning in other areas → and the combined state produces dependence in the others and blocks their development of responsibility for their own solutions. The two halves are presented as one condition, not two.
- **Downward transmission of anxiety.** Rising emotional tension in the system → faculty becomes overcritical of trainees. The trainees are the lower-status, dependent tier; the symptom lands there. (Structurally parallel to anxiety landing on a child, though Bowen does not draw that parallel here in those words.)
- **Individual-to-group transmission.** Emotional issues in the life of an individual faculty member → are transmitted to the group. But a well-functioning faculty can handle this without administrative intervention — so the group has an absorption capacity that is a function of its own functioning level.
- **"The language of the triangles" as a readout.** Rising tension → increased *incidence* of withdrawal, silence, cliques, alliances, and gossip about absent members. The mechanism is inferred from frequency, not from content.
- **[p2] The work system as an extra-familial sink for emotional need.** Emotional needs not contained within the family → shifted to relationships outside it → work relationships preferred over social ones → intensity concentrated there, and covert. Three amplifiers are named for this sink specifically: low differentiation, physical cutoff from parents, and "happy family" administrative policy. This is the same routed-flow shape as the nuclear-family sink list, but the destination is *outside* the family entirely.
- **[p2] Reading correction — the imported/added direction is the reverse of pass 1.** "In addition to principles that are well known in good administrative functioning, such as clearly defined contracts, there have been some guiding principles from family research back in the 1950s, and from subsequent clinical work in families." Grammatically the **good-administration principles are the base** and the **family-systems principles are the addition**. Contracts are one named *example* of the base class, not "the one imported mechanism."
- **[p2] The over/under-responsibility defect is stated interpersonally as well as intrapersonally.** "I discovered I was being overresponsible for the staff in some areas, and that I was in fact being irresponsible in my own functioning in other areas" is the intrapersonal half; but the sentence it explains is "everyone was dependent on me for suggesting solutions and the staff was not developing in the direction of assuming responsibility for their own solutions" — the leader's excess and the staff's deficit are the *same* transfer. The reciprocal-functioning shape, in an administrative dyad.

## A3. Predictive claims

Each stated as a conditional. Evidence flag in brackets.

1. If a person developed relationship patterns to adapt to the parental family in childhood, then those same patterns will be used in all other relationships throughout life. [asserted; no evidence offered]
2. If a relationship system is a work or social system rather than the original family, then its emotional process will be less intense — except in the identified exception cases. [asserted]
3. If a person has a lower level of differentiation and a higher level of unresolved emotional attachment to parents, then the intensity of his work relationships is more likely to approximate the intensity of his original family. [asserted]
4. If a person achieves emotional distance from parents by physical distance ("cutting-off"), then he tends to have the *most* intense relationships with those outside the family. [asserted; presented as an observed tendency]
5. If a person is better differentiated, then goal-directed interests motivate his life work; if less differentiated, then he tends to seek relationships at work that satisfy emotional needs. [asserted]
6. If administrative policy or a boss encourages a "happy family" attitude at work, then the process of seeking work relationships in lieu of family relationships is further intensified. [asserted]
7. If parents have better levels of differentiation, then emotional needs are contained within the family and there is little need to shift them to outside relationships. [asserted]
8. If a boss is less well differentiated, then he is more inclined to make decisions based on the feeling of the moment rather than on principle and reality. [asserted; explicitly paralleled to less-differentiated parents]
9. If an organisation is of the type that grows around one person's work, then it is most vulnerable to becoming involved in emotional alliances and intense emotional processes that make it more like a family. [asserted]
10. If such an organisation is poorly differentiated, then within months or years there will be major splits and disruptions in the central organisation. [asserted; "a good percentage of such organizations"]
11. If emotional tension in a training system increases, then the faculty will tend to become overcritical of the trainees. [asserted from repeated self-observation; the only claim in the chapter with a described observational history]
12. If there is an emotional issue in an organisation and a key member modifies the part he is playing in it, then the others will do the same. [asserted; called a principle, used in three system types]
13. **If any one key member of an organisation can be "responsibly responsible for self," then the problem in the organisation will resolve.** [asserted; **[p2] not unconditional — pass 1 called it that and was wrong.** The antecedent is "**can be** responsibly responsible for self," a capability gate, and the doubled adverb plus the "blame" hazard below show Bowen treats the antecedent as rarely met. The claim is unconditional in *scope* — whole organisation, one member — not in *condition*.]
14. If self can do a reasonable job of defining the problem, and if self can make some progress in modifying self, then the problems within his sphere of responsibility will work toward *automatic* resolution. [asserted; closing sentence, and a slightly weaker restatement of 13 because it is scoped to the sphere of responsibility]
15. If a person works toward differentiation of self in a work situation, then his effort can be effective *in the area in which he has administrative responsibility* — he does not have to be the boss or head of the total organisation. [asserted]
16. If the faculty is functioning well, then it can handle an individual member's transmitted emotional issue without administrative intervention. [asserted]
17. If a leader thinks about others' problems and offers them solutions, then it may work well in the short run, but everyone becomes dependent on him and the staff does not develop in the direction of assuming responsibility for their own solutions. [asserted from the 1950s research experience — the closest thing to reported evidence]
18. If a differentiating effort is announced in advance rather than done in action from careful private planning, then it will not be successful. [asserted as a precondition for success]
19. If a person in the responsible position accepts "blame" or "fault" instead of responsibility for the part self plays, then the principle fails — this is named "one of the biggest hazards." [asserted]
20. **[p2] A third, slogan formulation of the headline claim appears, and it is the one he attacks:** "One of the biggest hazards in a principle that says, 'Be responsible for yourself, and the emotional issue will resolve itself,' has to do with the inner orientation of self." So the chapter states its central principle **three times** — strong (13), slogan (20), hedged-and-scoped (14) — and hangs the biggest named hazard on the slogan form. [asserted]
21. **[p2] Efficacy-equivalence claim, from the closing thesis summary:** "the principles toward the differentiation of self in work situations can be as effective as they are in the family." Not merely that the same patterns occur — that the same *interventions* work equally well. [asserted]

## A5. Terminology

- **Differentiation of self** — used here without redefinition, and explicitly extended: "differentiation of self principles apply in all areas of relationships whether it be within the family, or in social or work relationships." Its operational content in this chapter is: being "as much of a 'self' as is possible for me," focusing on own functioning, and permitting others latitude to develop their selfs.
- **Levels of differentiation** — treated as a property that varies across *work* situations exactly as it does across families: "It is accurate to think of all levels of differentiation of self in work situations just as in families." Bosses are graded on it as parents are.
- **Administrative system** — a work relationship system with a defined head and defined responsibilities. Bowen's thesis is that it "might be similar to a family, but it is not a family," even though people call it one.
- **"Family" (in scare quotes)** — the term as misapplied to a work system. He flags that the tendency to call work systems families is what produced his original title, and he treats the usage as a symptom, not a description.
- **"Happy family" attitude** — an administrative posture, encouraged by policy or by a boss, that intensifies the use of work relationships for emotional need fulfilment. Named here as a *pathogenic administrative practice*.
- **Cutting-off** — achieving emotional distance from parents by physical distance. Here it is the predictor of the *most* intense outside relationships. (In this chapter it is a route to distance, one of three; not yet named as a formal concept.)
- **Unresolved emotional attachment** — to the parents; the quantity that lower differentiation carries more of, and the thing denied and distanced from.
- **Contained / more contained people** — used of better-differentiated parents; the property that keeps emotional needs inside the family. Appears to do the job "emotional self-containment" or low spillover does elsewhere.
- **Responsibility of self / responsibility to each other** — a distinction better-differentiated parents are "clearer about." Two separate things, not one.
- **Overresponsible / irresponsible** — a paired defect in the leader: overresponsible *for others* in some areas, irresponsible *in his own functioning* in others.
- **"Responsibly responsible for self"** — the deliberately doubled phrase for the state that resolves organisational problems. The doubling seems to guard against the blame-substitution failure named later.
- **"The language of the triangles"** — the observable behavioural vocabulary that signals rising system anxiety: emotional issues between people, withdrawal, silence, cliques, alliances, gossip about an absent member. Explicitly to be read by *incidence*, not content.
- **Detriangle** — used as a verb of self-action: "I have failed to state my position, or to detriangle myself from some emotional issue between other faculty members." Note it is paired with "state my position" as two omissions of the same kind.
- **Process vs. content** — the standing discrimination: "My effort is always to avoid an unwitting focus on the content of issues, and to focus on the process."
- **Responsibility vs. "blame"/"fault"** — "There is a fine line between accepting the responsibility for the part self plays in a situation and accepting the 'blame' for it." Blame is the counterfeit of responsibility, and it comes from an "inner orientation of self."
- **Clearly defined contracts** — cited as an example of principles "well known in good administrative functioning." **[p2] Correction: the sentence runs the other way.** "In addition to principles that are well known in good administrative functioning, such as clearly defined contracts, there have been some guiding principles from family research…" — the good-administration principles are the base, the family-systems principles are the addition, and contracts are one *example* of the base class, not an enumerated import.
- **[p2] "Be responsible for yourself, and the emotional issue will resolve itself"** — the headline principle in slogan form, quoted by Bowen in order to attach its "biggest hazard" to it. Its verb is *resolve itself*, i.e. no actor performs the resolution.
- **[p2] "Inner orientation of self"** — the hidden state that decides whether an outwardly identical act is responsibility or "blame." Bowen locates the discriminator inside the person, not in the act.
- **[p2] "A reasonable level of differentiation" / "a reasonable job in defining the problem"** — the target is explicitly *satisficing*, twice, not maximal. Against this sits one "best possible level of differentiation." The goal state is a band, not an optimum.
- **[p2] "Responsibly responsible for self"** — note the antecedent verb: "*can be* responsibly responsible." Capability, not choice.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The head / key member.** Bowen occupies the position of head of the Family Faculty and the family programmes. The organisation "grew slowly around me and my work," which is what makes it family-like and vulnerable.
- **One key member is sufficient.** "Any time one key member of an organization can be responsibly responsible for self, the problem in the organization will resolve." The role is not defined by formal authority alone — see next point.
- **The differentiating person need not be the head.** "In work situations, the person who works toward the differentiation of self does not have to be the boss or the head of the total organization. His effort can be effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility." So the effective unit is *sphere of responsibility*, not organisational apex.
- **Three tiers in the observed system:** head (Bowen) → faculty → trainees. Anxiety travels down: faculty becomes overcritical of trainees; Bowen becomes critical of the faculty. The trainees are the terminal recipients and are not described as acting back.
- **The symptom-bearer position** is occupied by the trainees — the least powerful tier — and the *initial symptom* is described as the faculty's overcriticism, i.e. the symptom is first visible as behaviour of the tier above.
- **The absent member.** Gossip about an absent faculty member is a named tension indicator, and Bowen reports catching himself doing it — the head is not exempt from the position.
- **Parents as administrators.** Better-differentiated parents "handle 'administrative' decisions in the family with the same principles used by good administrators in work situations." The parental role is explicitly framed as an administrative office.
- **Colleagues vs. employees.** Bowen notes his professional colleagues "are far more important to me than most employees would be in other situations" — i.e. the emotional weighting of the relationship, not the formal role, sets the difficulty.
- **The person who moves first is the self who is doing the observing.** In every example, the sequence starts with Bowen noticing his own reaction, not with an intervention on another.
- **[p2] The symptom is read at the TRANSMITTING tier, not at the receiving one.** The declared projection target is the trainees, but no trainee symptom is ever described. "The initial symptom would begin to show when the faculty would become overcritical of trainees" — the observable belongs to the *projector*. In the family chapters the child carries the readout; here the parent-analogue does. A projection process can be legible entirely from the sender's behaviour.
- **[p2] Sphere of responsibility is a permissive scope, not a boundary.** "In work situations, the person who works toward the differentiation of self does not have to be the boss or the head of the total organization. His effort can be effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility." Bowen says a non-apex actor *can be effective within his own area*; he nowhere says effects **stop** at that boundary — and the headline claim says one key member resolves "the problem in the organization." Pass 1 read the sphere sentence as bounding propagation. It does not; it relaxes the authority precondition.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

Note: there is no therapist and no patient in this chapter. The "technique" is entirely self-administered by a person occupying a leadership office inside the system. Read the agent below as *the leader acting on himself*, which is exactly the coach/leadership-office case.

## B1. What the therapist does

Actions an agent could take, in Bowen's operational terms:

1. **Do the differentiating effort in action, not by explanation.** "I wanted to make this point more by demonstration than by explanation."
2. **Plan privately and in advance.** "If a differentiating effort is to be successful, it has to take place in action, as a result of careful private planning, and without previous announcement of one's plan."
3. **Define the goal as three simultaneous things:** "to be as much of a 'self' as is possible for me, to always focus as much on me and my functioning as possible, and to permit the others as much latitude as possible toward developing their selfs."
4. **Clarify and then discharge one's own defined responsibility.** "My effort went into clarification of my responsibility as head of the research, and functioning responsibly there, without assuming responsibility for others."
5. **Assume, as a working premise, that one is playing a part in any organisational emotional issue** — and then look for the part.
6. **Modify the part self is playing**, rather than acting on the others.
7. **Monitor for the initial symptom** — in this system, the faculty becoming overcritical of trainees.
8. **Use one's own critical urge as the detector.** "I often noticed this first when I would tend to become critical of the faculty for being too critical of the trainees."
9. **Restrain the urge to become critical**, and redirect that time into work on self.
10. **Listen to "the language of the triangles"** — count the incidence of emotional issues between people, withdrawal from the group, going silent, clique/alliance formation, and gossip about absent members.
11. **Score incidence, not content.** "The goal is to listen to the incidence of these phenomena, rather than focusing on the content of what is said."
12. **Focus on process, avoid an unwitting focus on the content of issues.**
13. **Catch self in the act** — "Sometimes I am able to 'catch' myself when I am passing along a bit of news about an absent member."
14. **Take stock of own functioning and modify it**, using the areas he already knows are weak.
15. **Restore lost contact.** He names losing contact with certain faculty members (through absorption in his own work) as one of the recurring lapses to correct.
16. **State one's position** where he has failed to.
17. **Detriangle self from emotional issues between other faculty members.**
18. **Stay aware of emotional issues in an individual member's life that are being transmitted to the group** — while not necessarily intervening (see B2).
19. **Apply the well-known good-administration principles too** — e.g. clearly defined contracts — alongside the family-systems principles.
20. **Keep working on self indefinitely.** "I am sufficiently satisfied with the result to keep always working on myself."
21. **Work within one's own sphere of administrative responsibility** if not the head of the whole organisation.
22. **[p2] Aim at "a reasonable level," not a maximum.** Both the differentiation target and the problem-definition step are qualified as "reasonable"; the acceptance criterion for continuing is "sufficiently satisfied … to keep always working on myself."
23. **[p2] Apply the same principle across system types without modification.** "This principle has been used through the years in my own family, in my clinical work, and in my administrative functioning." The transfer is asserted, not adapted.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not announce the plan in advance.** Success requires action out of private planning "without previous announcement of one's plan."
- **Do not explain instead of demonstrating.** He deliberately chose demonstration over explanation.
- **Do not become critical of the group for its criticism.** Bowen names his urge to criticise the faculty for being overcritical as the thing to restrain — criticism of the criticism is the same process one level up.
- **Do not focus on the content of issues.** "My effort is always to avoid an unwitting focus on the content of issues."
- **Do not think for others and offer them solutions.** It "worked very well" but built dependence and blocked staff from assuming responsibility for their own solutions.
- **Do not assume responsibility for others** while clarifying and discharging one's own.
- **Do not be overresponsible for others in some areas** — it comes packaged with being irresponsible in one's own.
- **Do not pass along news about an absent member** (gossip) — he treats catching himself at it as a corrective event.
- **Do not intervene administratively when the group can handle it.** "Even in those situations, if the faculty is functioning well, it can handle this problem without administrative intervention."
- **Do not accept "blame" or "fault."** The biggest named hazard: "It is easy for a person in such a position to say the situation is his 'fault' and to accept the 'blame' without being responsible."
- **Do not encourage a "happy family" attitude in the work situation.** Named as an administrative practice that intensifies the pathological process.
- **Do not treat the work system as a family.** "My thesis is that it might be similar to a family, but it is not a family."
- **[p2] Do not focus on content — but note what this is and is not.** The prohibition is on where the observer *directs attention* ("rather than focusing on the content of what is said"; "avoid an unwitting focus on the content of issues"). Bowen nowhere says content carries no information. See P1 for why pass 1's stronger gloss must be withdrawn.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition for any differentiating effort:** careful private planning, then action, and no prior announcement. Announcement precedes failure.
- **The detection sequence is inward-first:** rising system tension → faculty overcritical of trainees → *Bowen's own urge to criticise the faculty* → which is where he actually notices it. The self-reaction is the earliest reliable signal available to him, later in the causal chain but earlier in the detection order.
- **On detecting the signal, the ordered moves are:** (1) restrain the critical urge; (2) assume he is playing a part in the anxiety process; (3) devote time to work on himself; (4) take stock of own functioning; (5) identify the specific lapse (lost contact / unstated position / undone detriangling); (6) modify it.
  - **[p2] Scoping correction.** Steps (1)–(3) are one verbatim sentence tied to the critical-urge detection ("At that point my effort would be to…"). Steps (4)–(6) come *after* the language-of-the-triangles paragraph and are introduced as "The main goal **in these situations**" — plural, covering **both** detection routes. So (1)–(3) are specific to the critical-urge trigger; (4)–(6) are the common tail. Pass 1 rendered all six as one chain.
  - **[p2]** The lapse set is not diagnosed, it is *recalled*: "Often I already know the areas in which my functioning has not been as responsible as it should be." The three named lapses are pre-existing known weaknesses, checked against, not discovered.
- **Alternative detection route:** listen to the language of the triangles — track the *rate* of withdrawal, silence, cliques, alliances, gossip; this can be run continuously rather than waiting for the symptom.
- **Ordering of responsibility work:** clarify own responsibility *first*, then function responsibly within it — only then does declining to assume responsibility for others become a coherent move rather than neglect.
- **Escalation is a last resort:** if the group is functioning well, no administrative intervention. Intervention is gated on group functioning, not on the presence of an issue.
- **Resolution is the last step and is not performed:** the problem "will resolve" / "will work toward automatic resolution" once self has defined the problem and modified self. The agent does not act on the resolution.
- **Time course:** the Georgetown system took from 1959 onward; the effort "has always left much to be desired"; the stance is permanent ("keep always working on myself"). Against this, the propagation claim is fast — "Very quickly I learned that … if I could modify the part I was playing, the others would do the same."
- **Audience-level precondition (from the symposium itself):** where feelings are high and enthusiasm is "overpositive," most listeners will react emotionally without hearing. Bowen reports still "trying to shift the feelings toward reality even during my presentation" and having to wait two years for the message to be hearable.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Announcing the plan** → the differentiating effort will not be successful.
- **Delivering the message into a high-feeling, "overpositive" system** → "Some of the audience 'heard' the message but most were reacting emotionally without hearing"; the overall result was "no more than partially successful."
- **Thinking for the staff and offering solutions** → everyone becomes dependent on the leader and the staff does not develop toward assuming responsibility for their own solutions. Note the trap: this failure mode "worked very well" by its immediate results.
- **Being overresponsible for others** → is accompanied by being irresponsible in one's own functioning elsewhere. The over- and under- are one failure, not two.
- **Becoming critical of the group for being critical** → identified as Bowen's own participation in the anxiety process, i.e. the leader amplifies rather than absorbs.
- **Losing contact with faculty members through absorption in own work** → a named lapse in his own responsible functioning that contributes to system tension.
- **Failing to state one's position** → same class of lapse.
- **Failing to detriangle from an emotional issue between two others** → same class of lapse.
- **Passing along news about an absent member** → participation in the tension pattern rather than observation of it.
- **Accepting "blame"/"fault" instead of responsibility** → "one of the biggest hazards" of the be-responsible-for-yourself principle; it arises from "the inner orientation of self," and it produces the appearance of responsibility without the substance.
- **Focusing on content instead of process** → described as happening "unwittingly," i.e. it is the default drift, not a deliberate error.
- **Organisational failure mode:** a work system that becomes family-like through emotional alliances → major splits and disruptions in the central organisation within months or years.
- **Administrative failure mode:** encouraging a "happy family" attitude → intensifies members' substitution of work relationships for family relationships.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

This section converts each rule of technique back into the claim about system functioning it presupposes.

1. **"Modify the part you are playing and the others will do the same"** assumes every persistent emotional issue in a group is *jointly maintained* — no issue exists that a given key member is not participating in. It further assumes participation is modifiable unilaterally, and that a unilateral change propagates to other members without being requested.
2. **"One key member responsibly responsible for self resolves the organisation's problem"** assumes the system has a *strong coupling to at least one node* — that the emotional state of the whole is sufficiently determined by one member's functioning that changing it alone moves the aggregate. It also assumes resolution is automatic (no negotiation, no agreement, no communication step required).
3. **"His effort can be effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility"** assumes only that *effectiveness does not require apex authority* — influence follows responsibility scope rather than formal rank, so a non-head actor is a legitimate mover. **[p2] Withdrawn from pass 1: it does NOT assume the resolution effect stops at the sphere boundary.** Bowen makes no containment claim, and the headline claim (2) explicitly resolves "the problem in the organization" from one member. The model still needs a sphere attribute — to say *who may move*, not *how far the effect travels*.
4. **Do not announce the plan** assumes that a *declared* change of stance triggers the system's counter-reaction (the group re-absorbs or resists it), whereas an undeclared change of functioning does not. This implies the group reacts to stated intentions differently from enacted behaviour — announcement is itself an act with emotional consequences.
5. **Demonstration over explanation** assumes explanation does not transfer differentiation; only observed behaviour does. Combined with (4), this makes *behaviour* the only channel that carries the change.
6. **Restrain the critical urge** assumes criticism is an act of *fusion*, not correction — that criticising the critics adds to the anxiety load rather than reducing it. So the model should treat critical acts as anxiety transmitters even when their content is accurate.
7. **Read incidence, not content ("the language of the triangles")** assumes the *rate* of certain behaviours is a valid proxy for system anxiety, independent of what any of them is about. This is a directly modellable claim: anxiety is observable as a frequency over a fixed behaviour set (emotional issues between people, withdrawal from the group, silence, clique formation, alliance formation, gossip about an absent member). **[p2] Correction: "the content field is noise" is my gloss, not Bowen's claim.** He prescribes where the *observer* should direct attention ("rather than focusing on the content of what is said"), and separately warns that content-focus happens "unwittingly." He never asserts that content is uninformative — and the "blame" hazard (15) requires reading an *inner orientation* that no rate can supply. The safe modelling claim is: **rate is sufficient for the anxiety readout**; it is not that content is unusable in principle.
8. **Gossip about an absent member is a tension indicator** assumes triangling is *the* standard discharge route for two-person tension, and that the absent third is a structural requirement, not an accident of who is out of the room.
9. **Do not think for others / do not offer solutions** assumes that a solution supplied from outside *displaces* the capacity to generate one, and that dependence accumulates. Functional capacity is therefore not fixed per person; it is depressed by another's over-functioning. This is the reciprocity claim in an administrative setting.
10. **Overresponsible-for-others implies irresponsible-in-own-functioning.** **[p2] Pass 1 read this as an *intrapersonal* conserved budget. The text supports an *interpersonal* reciprocity at least as strongly, and probably more.** The clause it explains is that the staff "was not developing in the direction of assuming responsibility for their own solutions" — the leader's excess responsibility and the staff's deficit are one transfer, which is the reciprocal-functioning shape seen in the marital chapters, in a leader→staff dyad. Bowen reports the co-occurrence of over- and under-responsibility once, in himself, and does not state it as a law. Both readings are inferences; the interpersonal one has the better textual anchor. Implement the transfer, and treat the intrapersonal budget as unsourced.
11. **"Clarify my responsibility, function responsibly there, without assuming responsibility for others"** assumes responsibility is *partitionable* and that the partition can be made explicit. It also assumes that without an explicit partition, the default drift is toward taking on others' portions.
12. **Anxiety in the faculty is transmitted to the trainees** assumes anxiety flows *down* a status gradient and lands on the dependent tier, which shows the initial symptom by being over-evaluated. Routine evaluation is not the problem; evaluation *under tension* is. So the same act has different effects depending on system anxiety level — an act's valence is state-dependent.
13. **The leader's own urge is the earliest detectable signal** assumes the leader is *inside* the anxiety field, not observing it from outside, and that his internal state is coupled to the system state with a shorter lag than the group behaviour he could otherwise watch. A coach agent modelled as an external observer loses this.
14. **A well-functioning group handles a member's transmitted issue without administrative intervention** assumes groups have an *absorption capacity* that scales with the group's own functioning level. Intervention should therefore be gated on group functioning, and intervening in a group that could have absorbed it is itself a form of over-functioning.
15. **"Blame" is the counterfeit of responsibility** assumes there are two internally similar but functionally opposite states — accepting responsibility for the part self plays vs. accepting fault for the situation — and that the counterfeit produces none of the resolution effect. The model cannot score "took responsibility" from the utterance; it needs the underlying orientation. Bowen calls the difference "a fine line," so the discrimination is hard even for the person doing it.
16. **Patterns from the parental family are reused in all relationships** assumes each person carries a *portable* relationship pattern, parameterised by differentiation level, applied to whatever system he is in — with intensity scaled by the system type. This is the strongest general claim for a simulation: one pattern engine, per-context intensity multipliers.
17. **Better-differentiated parents keep needs contained within the family** assumes emotional need is a quantity that must go *somewhere*; if not met inside, it is exported to work or social relationships. Nothing is destroyed, only relocated.
18. **Cutoff produces the most intense outside relationships** assumes the same conservation with a sign: distance from parents does not lower total attachment intensity, it redirects it, and the more absolute the physical cutoff, the more concentrated the redirection.
19. **"Happy family" administrative policy intensifies the process** assumes system-level policy is an input to individual emotional functioning — the container shapes the process. A simulation therefore needs an organisation- or family-level policy variable distinct from any member's traits.
20. **Bosses are graded on differentiation exactly as parents are** assumes the *same* decision mechanism (reality-and-principle vs. feeling-of-the-moment) runs in both offices. Bowen's inverse statement — that better-differentiated parents make family "administrative" decisions like good administrators — makes the mapping bidirectional. The leadership office is a general construct, not a family-specific one.
21. **"Permit the others as much latitude as possible toward developing their selfs"** assumes differentiation in others cannot be produced, only *un-obstructed* — the leader's contribution is removing his own interference, never supplying growth.
22. **Continuous, indefinite effort with results that "always left much to be desired"** assumes differentiation is asymptotic: it never completes and has no terminal state. **[p2]** He does not say it regresses if the work stops — that was pass 1's addition; the chapter says only that the effort continues.
23. **[p2] "Blame" and responsibility are outwardly identical and gated by an inner orientation** assumes what Ch21 assumes about outside-ness: **the efficacy of a differentiating act is multiplied by a hidden state of the actor that the act itself does not reveal**, and the system responds to the hidden state rather than to the act. Two independent chapters, two different hidden gates, the same architecture. So the model needs *a class* of hidden efficacy multipliers on self-directed moves, not a one-off blame flag.
24. **[p2] Emotional need not contained in the family is exported to work** assumes the sink list for undifferentiation **does not close at the family boundary**. Where the nuclear-family chapters allocate across marital conflict / spouse dysfunction / child projection (/ emotional distance), this chapter routes the residue *out of the family altogether*, into work relationships, and makes that outward sink modulable by organisational policy.
25. **[p2] The target is "a reasonable level," reached by "some progress"** assumes the objective is a **satisficing band, not an optimum**. A coach that maximises a differentiation scalar is optimising something this chapter does not describe.
26. **[p2] The initial symptom is the projector's behaviour, not the target's** assumes the readout of a projection process may sit at the sending node. A model that detects projection only by inspecting the recipient's symptom load misses the earliest observable this chapter names.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Title correction as an implicit revision.** Bowen replaces the programme title ("Toward the Differentiation of Self in the Georgetown 'Family'") with a much longer, more literal one, and explains that he used "family" only because *other people* apply it to work systems. He then explicitly rejects the equation: "it might be similar to a family, but it is not a family." So the chapter's title concept is set up and then partly withdrawn.
- **Tension between the general claim and the qualifier.** He says social/work patterns are "identical to relationship patterns in the family, except in intensity," and one sentence later admits exceptions in which work intensity "approximates the intensity in the original family." If intensity is the only difference and intensity can converge, the family/work distinction can collapse for some people — yet his thesis is that a work system "is not a family." The reconciliation is not stated.
- **Strength drift in the central principle. [p2] Corrected and enumerated — pass 1 said "two hedges" and then listed three, and missed a fourth softening in the verb.** The principle appears **three times**:
  1. *Strong, mid-chapter:* "Any time one key member of an organization can be responsibly responsible for self, the problem in the organization will resolve." Scope = the organisation. Actor = any one key member. Outcome verb = *will resolve*.
  2. *Slogan, quoted in order to attack it:* "Be responsible for yourself, and the emotional issue will resolve itself." This is the form to which he attaches "one of the biggest hazards."
  3. *Closing:* "if self can do a reasonable job in defining the problem, and if self is able to make some progress in modifying self, the problems within his sphere of responsibility will work toward automatic resolution."
  Form 3 adds **four** weakenings against form 1: (a) a new antecedent — self must first define the problem; (b) the second antecedent is downgraded to "some progress"; (c) the consequent is scoped from "the organization" to "his sphere of responsibility"; (d) the verb weakens from "will resolve" to "will **work toward** automatic resolution." Pass 1's report of a materially weaker restatement is **confirmed**, and understated.
- **[p2] The sphere sentence and the headline claim are never reconciled.** "His effort can be effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility" sits two sentences before form 3, and both are in the closing paragraph — yet form 1 promised organisation-wide resolution from a single member. Whether the effect is organisation-wide or sphere-bounded is left open by the text. Pass 1 resolved it in favour of sphere-bounded; that was a choice, not a reading.
- **A method that worked well is discarded anyway.** Offering solutions to staff "worked very well." He abandons it for a second-order reason (it prevented development of responsibility in others), which supersedes an immediate-results criterion with a developmental one.
- **Self-assessment against the principle.** The principle predicts resolution; his reported result is that his effort "has always left much to be desired," and the symposium demonstration was "no more than partially successful." He does not treat this as disconfirming.
- **[p2] A method that "worked very well" is not described as discarded.** Pass 1 said he abandoned solution-giving. The text says he *discovered* the over/under-responsibility pattern and redirected his effort into clarifying his own responsibility. No abandonment is stated. The correction is real; the word "discarded" is mine, not his.
- **[p2] The announced case that is never delivered.** "A clear-cut issue in the Georgetown Family Faculty will illustrate…" is followed by a generic habitual pattern and no issue. This is the only place in the chapter where evidence is promised, and it is the only place where the promise is not met.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- "The over-all result was no more than partially successful."
- "I was still trying to shift the feelings toward reality even during my presentation."
- "Now, after two years, I hope that more can hear the subject of that paper." — an admission that the original delivery did not land.
- "there are exceptions to this in which the intensity of relationships in work systems approximates the intensity in the original family."
- "The result of my effort, in my own family, and at Georgetown, has always left much to be desired."
- "I am sufficiently satisfied with the result to keep always working on myself." — a deliberately low bar.
- "Toward the differentiation of self" (the title) — directional, not achieved.
- "to be as much of a 'self' as is possible for me" — bounded by the person's own capacity.
- "There is a fine line between accepting the responsibility for the part self plays in a situation and accepting the 'blame' for it." — an acknowledged discrimination problem, not a solved one.
- "A good percentage of such organizations" — deliberately unquantified.
- "One of the biggest hazards in a principle that says…" — he flags a weakness in his own prescription.
- Scare quotes throughout on "family," "needs," "happy family," "heard," "self," "blame," "fault," "catch," "administrative," "cutting-off," "the language of the triangles" — each marks a term he is using with reservation.
- **[p2] "Any time one key member … *can be* responsibly responsible for self"** — the modal is the hedge. The claim is about members who can meet the standard, and the chapter's own hazard section says the standard is easy to counterfeit.
- **[p2] "a reasonable level of differentiation"; "a reasonable job in defining the problem"; "some progress in modifying self"** — three satisficing qualifiers in a chapter with no scale.
- **[p2] The habitual mood of the whole central passage** — "would become overcritical," "I often noticed," "my effort would be," "Frequently I get so involved," "Other times I have failed." Every claim in the anxiety-transmission section is a recurring tendency, not an event.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The chapter is itself a demonstration inside the system it describes.** He delivered a paper about differentiating from his own faculty *with that faculty in the audience*, alongside 1100 others. The act and the content are the same object — a differentiating move performed publicly on the system, which is exactly the case rule (4) in B5 says should not be announced. The paper is arguably the announcement, and it was "no more than partially successful."
- **Anxiety in the audience is treated exactly like anxiety in the family.** "A high level of overpositive systems therapy enthusiasm" is described as something to be shifted "toward reality" — i.e. *positive* affect at high intensity is treated as an anxiety state, not as a good outcome. High enthusiasm and high anxiety are the same variable here.
- **The intensity ranking is counterintuitive and specific.** Colleagues are described as "far more important to me than most employees would be in other situations" — so the difficulty of differentiating in a work system scales with the *personal* importance of the members, not with organisational rank or formal dependence.
- **Organisations that grow around one person are a distinct risk class.** "The Georgetown Family Faculty … have grown slowly around me and my work since 1959. This is the kind of an administrative system that is most vulnerable." A structural property of the organisation's history predicts its emotional vulnerability.
- **The split lands in the "central organization."** Not at the periphery, not in the newest members — the disruption is located at the core. Worth modelling as: failure appears at the highest-intensity, most-central relationships first.
- ~~**He imports one non-family mechanism explicitly:** "clearly defined contracts"…~~ **[p2] Reversed.** The sentence is "**In addition to** principles that are well known in good administrative functioning, such as clearly defined contracts, **there have been some guiding principles from family research**." Good administration is the base; family systems theory is the addition; contracts are one *example* of the base, not an enumerated import. The bidirectional mapping still stands — better parents make family "administrative" decisions "with the same principles used by good administrators" — but pass 1 had the direction of supplementation backwards, and inflated an example into "the single point."
- **A three-item lapse list recurs as his own diagnostic checklist:** lost contact with members / failed to state position / failed to detriangle. These read as a fixed, enumerable set of leader-functioning defects — directly implementable as a leader-state vector.
- **The evaluation function is state-dependent.** "The faculty does routine evaluation of the progress of trainees, but when the emotional tension in the system increases, the faculty would tend to become overcritical." The same institutional act becomes a symptom purely as a function of system anxiety.
- **Covertness is a stated property of intense work relationships:** "often more covert than overt to the casual observer." An observer-facing simulation should distinguish an observed intensity from a true intensity.
- **Two failure modes of the same principle sit adjacent:** the leader who does nothing about himself, and the leader who takes on "blame." The second is the one Bowen names as the biggest hazard — i.e. the failure mode of the *prescription* is more dangerous than the failure mode it was written to correct.
- **[p2] The one chapter set inside an economic institution contains no economic variable.** Employees, bosses, contracts, training programmes, an institution with a payroll — and not a single mention of money, pay, budget, hiring, firing, promotion, or any material resource. Even "clearly defined contracts" is invoked for role definition, never for compensation. If a per-person material variable were going to appear anywhere in this corpus, it would be here, and it does not.
- **[p2] The three-part thesis is stated explicitly at the close**, and its third clause is the strongest: (i) emotional issues in administrative organisations "have the same basic patterns" as in the family; (ii) it is "as accurate to think of varying levels of differentiation in work situations as it is in the family"; (iii) "the principles toward the differentiation of self in work situations **can be as effective as they are in the family**." (iii) is an efficacy-equivalence claim, not merely a structural analogy, and it is the claim that licenses reusing one behaviour engine across contexts.

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

Ordered by how much downstream weight the error was carrying.

**1. "Content is explicitly designated noise" — withdraw the word "noise."**
This is the most consequential error, because L20.3 in the ledger uses it as *"an independent
argument for the deterministic core: if content is noise and rate is signal, the model does not
need language to produce the observable."* An architectural decision is resting on it.
What Bowen actually writes is two attention prescriptions: "The goal is to listen to the
**incidence** of these phenomena, rather than focusing on the content of what is said," and "My
effort is always to avoid an **unwitting** focus on the content of issues, and to focus on the
process." Both tell the *observer* where to look. Neither says content carries no information —
and the chapter's own biggest-hazard passage requires reading an "inner orientation of self" that
no incidence count can deliver. The defensible claim is **rate is sufficient for the anxiety
readout**; "content is noise" is a stronger claim the chapter does not make. The architectural
conclusion survives on the weaker premise, but the premise must be restated in the ledger.

**2. Sphere-of-responsibility was read as a propagation boundary. It is a permission.**
Verbatim (verified): *"In work situations, the person who works toward the differentiation of self
does not have to be the boss or the head of the total organization. His effort can be effective in
the area in which he has administrative responsibility."* Pass 1's B5#3 turned this into "the
system decomposes into spheres with boundaries that the resolution effect respects," and L20.1
turned it into "propagation bounded by it." Bowen makes no containment claim. He is relaxing an
*authority precondition* — you need not hold the apex to be an effective mover — and the sentence
sits alongside a headline claim that one key member resolves "the problem in **the organization**."
The model still wants a sphere attribute, but it gates **who may move**, not **how far the effect
travels**. The containment question is genuinely open in this chapter.

**3. Predictive claim 13 was called "unconditional." It is not.**
The antecedent is "Any time one key member of an organization **can be** responsibly responsible
for self." That is a capability gate, doubled adverb and all, and the chapter goes on to name the
counterfeit of exactly that capability as "one of the biggest hazards." The claim is unconditional
in *scope* (whole organisation, single member) and heavily conditioned in *antecedent*. Pass 1
conflated the two.

**4. The imported-mechanism reading is backwards.**
Pass 1 (X3, and L20.1 in the ledger): "the only imported mechanism is 'clearly defined contracts'"

administrative functioning, such as clearly defined contracts, **there have been some guiding
principles from family research back in the 1950s**."* Good administration is the base; family
systems theory is the addition; contracts are one *example* of the base class. This matters for
Q-TRANSFER, below.

**6. A quantity was paraphrased in A4, which is the one section where that is forbidden.**

Pass 1 said "two hedges added" and then listed three. There are four weakenings between the strong

## P5. Assigned open questions

**Q-SPHERE — verified, with one pass-1 conclusion withdrawn.**
Verbatim, confirmed exactly: *"In work situations, the person who works toward the differentiation
of self does not have to be the boss or the head of the total organization. His effort can be
effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility."* The headline claim is also
verbatim: *"Any time one key member of an organization can be responsibly responsible for self, the
problem in the organization will resolve."* Pass 1's report of a weaker sphere-scoped restatement at
the close is **confirmed and understated** — four weakenings, not two (see X1 [p2]).
**But the sphere sentence does not support the attribute pass 1 derived from it.** It relaxes an
authority precondition; it says nothing about effects stopping at a boundary, and it coexists,
unreconciled, with a claim of organisation-wide resolution from one member. Keep a sphere attribute
as a **move-permission scope** (a non-apex actor is a legitimate mover); do **not** implement
propagation containment on this chapter's authority.

**Q-COACHSTATE — verified, with two qualifications.**
Verbatim: *"I often noticed this first when I would tend to become critical of the faculty for being
too critical of the trainees, instead of being helpful."* Prescribed sequence, verbatim: *"At that
point my effort would be to restrain my urge to become critical of the faculty, to assume that I was
playing a part in the anxiety process, and to devote my time to work on myself."* Three named lapses,
verbatim: *"Frequently I get so involved with my own work that I tend to lose contact with certain
faculty members. Other times I have failed to state my position, or to detriangle myself from some
emotional issue between other faculty members."*
Qualifications: (a) "**often** noticed this first," and the language of the triangles is offered as
"**another** way of detecting" — so this is one of two routes, not the privileged one; (b) the lapse
list is **recalled, not diagnosed** — "Often I already know the areas in which my functioning has not
been as responsible as it should be."
**The argument that the coach agent needs its own drifting internal state survives intact**, and is
now supported by three chapters with three different signal sources (Ch10 system behaviour, Ch19 own
error direction, Ch20 own impulse). Ch20 is the only one where the signal precedes any behaviour of
the coach's own, which is what makes it a *state* rather than a *readout*.

**Q-READOUT — the observable set is verified; "content is noise" is not.**
Verbatim: *"As tension mounts there is an increasing incidence of emotional issues between people;
people tend to withdraw from the group or become silent, or form cliques or alliances between faculty
members, or talk or gossip about an absent faculty member. The goal is to listen to the incidence of
these phenomena, rather than focusing on the content of what is said."* Plus: *"My effort is always to
avoid an unwitting focus on the content of issues, and to focus on the process."* The fixed set and
the rate-as-readout claim are exact.
**Content is nowhere designated noise.** Both statements prescribe where the *observer* directs
attention; neither asserts that content lacks information, and the chapter's own blame hazard depends
on reading an "inner orientation" that no rate provides. Since this is being used architecturally, the
premise must be restated as: **the anxiety readout can be computed from event rates over a fixed
behaviour set, without content.** That is sufficient for the deterministic-core argument and is what
the text supports.

**Q-BLAME — verified in substance; two of the three phrases are inference, not text.**
Verbatim: *"It is easy for a person in such a position to say the situation is his 'fault' and to
accept the 'blame' without being responsible. There is a fine line between accepting the
responsibility for the part self plays in a situation and accepting the 'blame' for it."* "A fine
line" is exact. **"Looks identical" and "produces none of the resolution effect" are not in the text**
— the first is a reasonable reading of "fine line," the second follows only because the principle's
antecedent ("being responsible") goes unmet. State them as derived. The discriminator is named: "the
inner orientation of self." Structurally this is the same hidden-state efficacy gate as Ch21's
outside-ness (see P3).

**Q-TRANSFER — carried unchanged: essentially everything. Modified: nothing. Excluded: nothing.**
Carried across without modification: basic relationship patterns; levels of differentiation ("as
accurate to think of varying levels of differentiation in work situations as it is in the family");
the decision rule (feeling-of-the-moment vs. principle-and-reality) in bosses as in parents; splits
and disruptions "just as there are in poorly differentiated families"; triangles and "the language of
the triangles"; anxiety transmission down a status gradient; over/under-functioning; process-vs-content;
and the one-member-modifies principle, which he states runs in all three system types — "This
principle has been used through the years in my own family, in my clinical work, and in my
administrative functioning." He goes further than analogy at the close: the principles "**can be as
effective as they are in the family**."
Two scaling qualifications, not modifications: work intensity is lower overall ("except in intensity"),
with stated exceptions; and the work system "might be similar to a family, but it is **not** a family."
**Nothing is excluded, and nothing is adapted.** The only *addition* runs the other way — the
family-systems principles are used "in addition to principles that are well known in good
administrative functioning, such as clearly defined contracts." So the direction of import is
family→work, and the non-family material is the pre-existing base, not a patch.

**Q-VALIDATION — none.** No measurement procedure, no instrument, no external criterion, no
inter-rater check, no comparison group, no count of anything but the audience. All observation is
Bowen's self-report of his own functioning, in the habitual mood, with the one announced case never
delivered. Consistent with Ch16 (scale development abandoned) and Ch17 (scale research stopped;
pseudo-self trading "results in false readings when one attempts to estimate levels of
differentiation"). Ch20 adds nothing on this axis and asks for nothing.

**Q-MATERIAL — none, and the absence is itself the finding.** This is the only chapter set inside an
economic institution — employees, bosses, a payroll, training programmes, contracts — and it contains
no money, pay, budget, hiring, firing, promotion, or resource variable of any kind. "Clearly defined
contracts" is invoked for role definition, never for compensation. "Colleagues … far more important to
me than most employees would be in other situations" ranks ties by **emotional weight**, explicitly not
by economic relation. If a per-person material variable existed anywhere in this corpus it would be
here; it is not.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Patterns from the parental family are reused in all relationships, differing only in intensity → `Person` → one pattern/differentiation parameter drives behaviour in every context the person is in; context supplies an intensity multiplier, not a different rule set.
- Work/social systems are less intense than the family, with exceptions → `Family` / `unmodelled` → needs a context-type intensity scalar (family ≈ 1.0, work < 1.0) that can be raised toward 1.0 for low-differentiation, high-unresolved-attachment persons.
- Lower differentiation + higher unresolved parental attachment → work intensity approaches family intensity → `Person` → attachment-to-origin should be a per-person state that modulates the out-of-family intensity multiplier.
- Denial + distance (internal mechanisms, physical distance, or both) as the adult adaptation → `Person` / `Relationship` → three distinct distancing modes with the same functional result; the model likely has only one.
- Cutoff by physical distance → most intense outside relationships → `Person` / `Relationship` → cutoff must *redirect* intensity outward, not remove it; a cutoff that merely zeroes a tie is wrong.
- Emotional need contained in the family vs. exported to work → `Family` → a containment/spillover budget at family level: unmet need must be routed somewhere, not discarded.
- Better differentiated → goal-directed work motivation; less → work-relationship-seeking → `Person` → two motive modes selectable by differentiation level.
- "Happy family" administrative policy intensifies need-substitution → `Family` / `unmodelled` → a system-level *policy* variable, distinct from any member's traits, that scales emotional-need routing. The model probably has no organisation-level policy slot.
- Less-differentiated bosses decide on feeling of the moment, not principle and reality — same as parents → `Person` / `Family` → decision rule should be selected by differentiation level and applied identically wherever the person holds an office.
- Parents handle family "administrative" decisions with good-administrator principles → `Family` → validates a general **leadership office** construct rather than a family-specific parent role.
- The differentiating person need not be the head; his effort "can be effective in the area in which he has administrative responsibility" → `Family` / `unmodelled` → **[p2] revised.** The leadership office needs a *scope* attribute that gates **which agents may attempt the differentiating move** (a non-apex member with a sphere is a legitimate mover), replacing a global leader flag. **Do not** implement effect containment at the sphere boundary — the chapter makes no such claim and asserts organisation-wide resolution from one member. Containment is an open question, not a finding.
- "One key member responsibly responsible for self → the organisation's problem resolves" → `coach` / `Family` / `test` → the headline mechanism: a single node's functioning change propagates to aggregate system state without any explicit interaction. Needs an explicit propagation rule and a test that it actually fires.
- "If I modify the part I am playing, the others will do the same" → `Relationship` / `move:MODIFY_OWN_PART` → a move whose target is self and whose effect lands on others.
- Overresponsible for others ⇄ irresponsible in own functioning (one paired defect) → `Relationship` / `Person` → **[p2] revised.** Implement primarily as the **reciprocal-functioning transfer** on the leader→staff tie (the leader gains responsibility, the other does not develop it) — the same signed transfer as C2, in an administrative dyad. The intrapersonal conserved budget with a hidden deficit side is a weaker reading of one sentence about himself; keep it flagged as unsourced rather than building on it.
- Offering solutions works immediately but builds dependence and blocks others' responsibility development → `Relationship` / `move:OFFER_SOLUTION` / `test` → a move with positive short-term and negative long-term effect; a good adversarial test case for any scoring function that rewards immediate improvement (P7).
- "Permit the others as much latitude as possible toward developing their selfs" → `coach` → the coach can only remove interference; it must have no move that directly raises another agent's differentiation.
- Rising system tension → faculty overcritical of trainees → `Family` / `event-kind:ANXIETY_TRANSMISSION` → anxiety flows down a status gradient and the symptom appears in the dependent tier as over-evaluation by the tier above.
- Routine evaluation becomes overcriticism only under tension → `Relationship` / `test` → an act's valence is state-dependent; the same action should score differently at different system anxiety levels.
- The leader's own critical urge is the earliest detectable signal → `coach` → the coach agent must be modelled as *inside* the anxiety field with its own reactive state, not as an external observer. A stateless coach loses the chapter's primary detection channel.
- "The language of the triangles": incidence of emotional issues, withdrawal, silence, cliques, alliances, gossip about the absent → `Family` / `Triangle` / `event-kind` → a fixed observable behaviour set whose *rate* is a readout of system anxiety. Directly implementable as an anxiety proxy. **[p2] Premise restated:** the readout is computable **from event rates alone, without content** — which is what the deterministic-core argument needs. Bowen does not claim content is noise, and the blame/responsibility discrimination below requires a state that no rate supplies.
- Gossip about an absent member as a triangling indicator → `Triangle` / `event-kind:GOSSIP_ABSENT_THIRD` → the absent third is structurally required, not incidental.
- Process vs. content discrimination → `coach` → the coach must score events by type/rate, never by content. Any content-matching in the coach is a modelled failure mode Bowen names explicitly.
- Leader lapse checklist: lost contact / failed to state position / failed to detriangle → `Person` / `coach` → an enumerable leader-functioning state vector with three named deficits, each with its own corrective move.
- `move:STATE_POSITION`, `move:DETRIANGLE_SELF`, `move:RESTORE_CONTACT`, `move:RESTRAIN_CRITICISM`, `move:CLARIFY_OWN_RESPONSIBILITY` → `coach` / `move` → all self-directed; none acts on another agent.
- A well-functioning group absorbs a member's transmitted issue without intervention → `Family` / `coach` → an absorption-capacity variable scaled by group functioning; intervention should be *gated* on it, and intervening below the gate is itself over-functioning.
- Accepting "blame"/"fault" instead of responsibility is the biggest hazard, and the line is fine → `coach` / `test` / `unmodelled` → two states that look alike and behave oppositely; the model needs to distinguish them, and the coach must not credit the counterfeit. A strong adversarial test: a "took responsibility" utterance that is actually blame must not produce resolution. **[p2] Generalised:** this is the same architecture as Ch21's outside-ness gate — **a hidden actor state multiplying the efficacy of an outwardly identical self-directed move, with the system responding to the state rather than the act.** Implement as a *class* of hidden efficacy multipliers on self-directed moves (`inner_orientation` here, `outside_ness` in Ch21, receiver readiness in Ch17), not as a one-off blame flag.
- Differentiating effort must be enacted from private planning, never announced → `coach` / `move` → announcement is a distinct act with its own (negative) system effect; a move that declares intent should differ from the same move performed silently.
- Demonstration transfers, explanation does not → `coach` → behaviour is the only channel that carries differentiation between agents.
- Organisations grown around one person are the most vulnerable class, and splits land in the central organisation → `Family` / `unmodelled` → structural history as a risk variable, and failure appearing first at the highest-intensity central ties.
- High "overpositive" enthusiasm treated as an anxiety state to be shifted toward reality → `Family` / `unmodelled` → positive affect at high intensity should raise the anxiety measure, not lower it. Most implementations get this backwards.
- Intense work relationships are "more covert than overt to the casual observer" → `Relationship` / `unmodelled` → distinguish true tie intensity from observed tie intensity.
- Differentiation is asymptotic — no terminal state, "always left much to be desired," effort is permanent → `Person` / `test` → no agent should reach a completed differentiation state; assert an upper bound is never attained.
- Clearly defined contracts → `unmodelled` → **[p2] revised.** One *example* of "principles that are well known in good administrative functioning," which are the **base** to which family-systems principles are added — not a mechanism imported into family theory. Nothing in a family model corresponds to it, and the chapter does not require one.
- **[p2]** Emotional need not contained within the family is exported to relationships **outside** it, work preferentially → `Family` / `unmodelled` → the undifferentiation sink list is **not closed at the family boundary**. Ch09/Ch10 give three intra-nuclear sinks, Ch18 four; this adds an extra-familial sink whose size is modulated by low differentiation, physical cutoff, and organisational policy. A household-closed allocation cannot represent it — corroborates L18.1's argument against a nuclear-only model from a different direction.
- **[p2]** "Any time one key member … **can be** responsibly responsible for self" → `coach` / `policy` / `test` → the propagation rule fires on a **capability gate**, not on a role. Note this is the fourth chapter asserting single-node sufficiency (Ch06, Ch12, Ch13, Ch20) and all four qualify the node — *motivated*, *significant*, *principled*, *key*. Any test of "one member resolves the system" must run on a qualified node and must **fail** on an arbitrary one.
- **[p2]** The initial symptom is the **projector's** behaviour, not the target's → `readout` / `event-kind` → a projection process must be detectable from the sending node's act rate, not only from the recipient's symptom load. The model currently reads symptom at the recipient.
- **[p2]** The target is "a reasonable level," reached by "some progress" → `coach` objective → **satisficing band, not maximisation.** A coach whose objective is `max(C)` optimises something this chapter does not describe; pair with C1's constraint that the objective cannot be anxiety or symptom reduction either.
- **[p2]** The differentiating effort requires "careful private planning" and no prior announcement → `move:I-POSITION` → third independent statement of the secrecy gate (Ch20, Ch21, Ch22), with the object being **the plan**, distinct from Ch10's announcement of a specific withdrawal to the other party. Resolves the L21.3 flag; the two rules do not conflict and both should be implemented, on different objects.
- **[p2]** No material, economic or financial variable anywhere, in the corpus's only workplace chapter → `unmodelled` / discipline → recorded so a later pass does not invent one. Tie weight is set by emotional importance, explicitly not by economic relation ("colleagues, who are far more important to me than most employees would be in other situations").
- Colleague importance, not formal rank, sets differentiation difficulty → `Relationship` → difficulty of a differentiating move should scale with the emotional weight of the tie, not with structural position.
