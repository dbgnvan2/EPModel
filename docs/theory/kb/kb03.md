# KB03 — Systems Therapy

**Citation rule:** cite `KB03`. ASR source — no verbatim quotation.

## ⚠⚠ K03.1 — The research attitude outperforms the therapeutic one — now three statements and an independent replication

Third and fourth statements of the finding first recorded at `KB04` · K04.12:

> "the people who did the best in their life courses were those for whom **we had no therapeutic goal**."
>
> "the more I can make it into a **research effort, the better the family does**, and the more I get all uptight trying to make the problem better, **the worse the family does**."

**An experiment.** Residents were instructed to learn rather than cure. Measured: "the most versatile resident would get about **ten hours**" before the family demanded an answer — "Doctor, I've been answering all of these questions so you would eventually tell me what's wrong with me. When are you going to do it?" — and the resident gave in. Most managed two or three.

**And a replication by Kerr, on a different population.** Interviewing cancer families with no intention to fix anything: "the number of ideas they produced in any given session were **greater than in my private practice**… and the cancer people were **doing better, no question about it**, and that changed my private practice significantly."

At the severe end: "if you communicate *I'm going to do therapy and cure you* to a schizophrenic, **the schizophrenia will win the day**."

*Model consequence:* **the best-evidenced claim about the coach in the entire corpus** — four statements, an informal experiment with a measured ceiling, and an independent replication by a second investigator. `M11.C.7` currently tests *position, not skill*. This says something stronger and more testable: **an agent whose objective is to understand produces better outcomes than an identical agent whose objective is to help.** That is a two-arm criterion and it should be added.

## K03.2 — "Therapeutic-itis", and the mechanism behind C1 `[T]`

> Therapeutic-itis is the belief that one can "learn a body of knowledge in professional school and apply it in therapy and help out the poor patient" — "only, I believe, **the problem was created in the first place by the family trying to do good for the patient**, and then in the process of trying to do good for people, you create more problems."

*Model consequence:* the clearest statement of C1's mechanism. The problem is not that help fails; **help is the same operation that produced the problem**. This is why the coach's objective must not be relief (`L06.2`) and why `M4.D.6a` forbids a relief-based reinforcement signal.

## K03.3 — Announcing non-involvement is denial — the fourth instance of one pattern `[T]`

> "When somebody says, 'I'm out of it, you handle it, I don't have anything to do with it' — **that is mostly denial, because they are in it**, and when somebody maintains that posture, then they are in it and **can't get out**."

*Model consequence:* with `KB04` · K04.10, `KB06` · K06.7 and `KB12` · K12.3, this is the **fourth independent form** of the same rule. The generalisation is now safe to state as a model requirement: **any assertion of a differentiated or detached state is negative evidence for it.** That is a readout, it is cheap to compute from the event log, and it belongs in `M5.F` beside `outside_ness`.

## ⚠ K03.4 — Method changes are driven by staff anxiety, not by the patient `[T]`

> "the method of therapy would be determined by the staff member who made the **strongest speech**. Or by a staff member who's **mad at the patient**… I will swear that it's determined by the **anger of the people** at the patient at that point."

And the trigger rule: "as long as somebody's doing well, no problem, no staff conference. **Patient develops symptoms or acts out, and now we've got to have a staff conference and we've got to change the method.**"

His response was pre-commitment: "I wanted the **principle defined way ahead of time**, instead of in the emotionality of the moment", and "never make a decision based on the emergency of the moment unless it was an absolute emergency… **wait until the incident cooled down**."

*Model consequence:* a coach-side failure mode the model does not have — **symptom → institutional anxiety → intervention change**, where the change is caused by the staff's state rather than the patient's. It is `L19.1`/`L20.2` (the professional's drifting state) with a specific trigger and a specific countermeasure (pre-committed protocol, decisions deferred until calm). Both are implementable, and the countermeasure is a natural acceptance criterion.

## K03.5 — The therapist's frame of reference, not technique, determines what they do `[T]`

> "the **questions they ask, the moves they make** in the therapy is determined by **the way they think**. Much more than what they do or their techniques."

Hence his refusal to participate in comparative studies: "the **researcher evaluates through the researcher's frame of reference**", so such studies miss the variable that matters.

*Model consequence:* corroborates `M11.C.7` and sharpens it — the coach's varying parameter is its **frame**, not its move repertoire. It also warns that a readout comparing coach *behaviour* across arms will miss the difference, which bears on how `M11.C.7` is measured.

## K03.6 — A comparative experiment, with a null result `[T]`

Georgetown, early 1960s: for most of a year, clinicians of every orientation — family, group, biological, child, analytic — worked the same cases in front of each other, on the premise that convergence would reveal common denominators.

> the result was that "**each one became more sure that their way of doing it is best**."

*Model consequence:* an informal comparative study whose outcome was **divergence, not convergence**, reported by the person who ran it. It corroborates `L14.4` ("the type of approach is not a positive index of success") and is worth holding beside any claim that method determines outcome.

## K03.7 — Role is locked by venue, not by person `[M]`

A woman who has worked at differentiation for a long time still "gets in the **child role**" at her parents' house; when they visit her, "she gets in the **parental role** and ends up putting parents in a child role."

> "Why in the world can't these three people be together and be **three adults**?"

*Model consequence:* the same three people occupy different positions depending on **whose territory the encounter is on**. The model's triangle positions shift with tension (`M1.C.2`); this adds a second, orthogonal determinant — location — which is cheap to represent and which no source has stated this plainly before.

## ⚠ K03.8 — A second, stricter epistemic standard, in tension with `KB02`

Here he describes a genuine hypothesis-test loop: "this is the hypothesis about what brings this condition about. Now, if we put a certain amount of therapy… **this will result**, and **if it didn't result, either the hypothesis was wrong or something was wrong**" — requiring a change to the hypothesis or the therapy. "**I never changed a method of therapy without** finding some kind of [reason]… the therapy had to fit with the hypothesis."

And: "psychotherapy can one day become a **science**… if we put into the theory only the things that are **facts**… I'm still in that very small minority, and **I'll die in that minority still believing it.**"

*Model consequence:* this sits against `KB02` · K02.3, where he says he needed only to be convinced within himself and accepted the label *unscientific*. **Both are his, and they are not the same standard.** The honest reading is that he held a rigorous standard for the theory–therapy relationship and a permissive one for the foundational premises. Validation item **A6 must reflect both**, not just the permissive half.
