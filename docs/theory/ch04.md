# Ch04 — A Family Concept of Schizophrenia

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** No date line and no conference named. Venue is stated only as a book: "This volume is devoted to papers about the etiology of schizophrenia." Internal date anchors: study "started in 1954"; the three-generation idea "began in 1955 with the statement of our consultant, Dr. Lewis Hill"; "Our research project has been in operation four years"; opening line says "three and one-half years of a clinical research project"; note 3 says Dr Hill "died in February 1958 while this paper was being written." So: written 1958, published as a chapter in an edited volume on the etiology of schizophrenia.
- **Clinical setting and data source:** A clinical research project on a psychiatric ward at a research center, where schizophrenic patients and their parents lived together on the ward. Started 1954 with three mothers and their schizophrenic daughters living on the ward (mother–patient dyads only). After one year the hypothesis was extended and new families were admitted in which both father and mother lived with the patient. Ward accommodates three families at a time. Totals stated: **seven inpatient families** (three mother–patient, four father–mother–patient), max participation three years, min six months, **average eighteen months**; four of the father–mother–patient families lived on the ward "for periods up to two and one-half years"; normal siblings lived with two of the families for up to one year. Plus **seven outpatient families** (father, mother, "moderately disturbed psychotic patient") treated up to two years — **fourteen research families total**. An **additional twelve family groups** were seen in detailed preadmission evaluation only and supplemented the data. Elsewhere the paper repeatedly says "all eleven research families" / "all eleven father-mother-patient families" (4 inpatient + 7 outpatient). Patients were "chronically and severely disturbed," all previously hospitalized for years. Staff: twenty full-time people — three psychiatrists and a social worker as the clinical research team, twelve nurses and attendants on eight-hour shifts seven days a week, plus an occupational therapist and clerical/technical staff. Data capture: nurses record per-shift observations on each individual, on the family unit, and on the family's relation to the environment; every family therapy hour tape-recorded plus three written records (process psychotherapy notes, verbal-content summary, and a **sociogram of the meeting**); daily material rolled up into weekly and monthly summaries.
- **Genre:** Both, heavily weighted to theory. Roughly **85% theory / 15% technique**. Stream B material is incidental — it arrives as by-products of the research operation (how staff achieved detachment, who may confront whom, what changed in families that "have done well in family therapy") rather than as a set of instructions. The paper's declared purpose is the family concept, not the method.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **The mother–patient relationship could not be seen from individual work.** When mothers and schizophrenic daughters were put into a shared living situation, "certain facets of the relationship came into focus that had not been anticipated from work with each individually or from joint interviews with the two together." → three mother–daughter pairs, inpatient ward, first year of the study. This observation is what forced the hypothesis change.
- **The father turned out to be an active part of the mother–patient problem.** The mother–patient relationship was "a dependent fragment of a larger family problem, and that the father played an important part in it." → same three mother–daughter pairs.
- **Marked emotional distance between the parents in every family.** "A constant finding in all eleven father-mother-patient families has been a marked emotional distance between the parents." Bowen names it the *emotional divorce*. It presents across a range: at one extreme a formal, controlled couple with few overt differences, an "ideal" marriage and a reportedly active and satisfying sexual relationship, who could not share personal feelings; at the other extreme a couple who could not be together long without arguments and threats, congenial in social situations, controlling conflict by physical distance, calling the marriage "a terrible twenty-five years." Nine families sat in the middle: consciously aware of differences, avoiding touchy points, seeing the marriage as "a difficult situation to be endured."
- **Parental conflict starts almost immediately on cohabitation, not on marriage.** "In most of the families, the parental conflict began in the first few days or weeks after the marriage," over decisions about routine problems of living together. The sharpest case: an intern and nurse secretly married two years before finishing training, whose relationship was "calm and satisfying until they began to live together."
- **"Arguments over nothing" in two-person joint activity.** Parents described fights arising in golf, a card game, a work project "for just the two of them"; conflict was less when a third or fourth person was present. Couples spent much time visiting others or entertaining; marital tension dropped when one went to visit his or her parents.
- **A closeness–distance–fight cycle.** A father: "Our life was a cycle of too much closeness, too much distance, and fights." He describes that in closeness "I would start acting like a little boy and she would make demands like a bossy mother"; if he stayed helpless "she would purr like a kitten"; if he balked they fought. In the distance phase: "I did my best work when we were far apart… That is when I would get depressed."
- **Inability to make decisions.** "One of the outstanding clinical characteristics of the families is the inability of the parents to make decisions." Decisions at all levels are left "to be decided by time, by circumstance, or by advice from experts." Decisions that are "problems to be solved" in other families become "burdens to be endured." A father: "We can never decide together on anything. I suggest we go shopping Saturday afternoon. She objects. We argue. We end up doing nothing."
- **The mate-choice reasons are the partner's overadequate facade.** Fathers said they admired the mothers' "strength, social confidence, and directness"; mothers said they admired the fathers' "kindness, intelligence, and reliability." Both were reading the facade: one mother — "I was so scared in social groups I would start chattering… Now, after twenty-five years I hear my husband thought I was a brilliant conversationalist"; one father — "I was too scared to do anything but agree and she thought it was kindness."
- **The decision to have a child was the most difficult decision of all** in these families.
- **The pregnancy that produced the patient was different from the pregnancy that produced the normal sibling.** In one mother, from the beginning of the pregnancy her thoughts were "almost totally invested in the developing fetus," expressed as doubts and worries about the child's normality; when emotionally close to the husband, investment shifted back to him and preoccupation with the child dropped; in periods of greatest distance from the husband she wished she could abort. A later pregnancy with a normal child produced the same fantasies "much less intense."
- **A remembered surge of maternal feeling at first sight of the child who became schizophrenic.** "This tiny, tiny, helpless little thing. I am its mother and I am the one who has to protect it and care for it." Contrast with the normal second child: "A new baby is so tiny. It is a wonder that such a tiny thing can grow up and become an adult." **Two other mothers** described maternal feelings of similar intensity at first sight of the child who later became schizophrenic, and the memory persisted as a life-defining emotional experience; similar but less intense feelings with other babies were not noted at the time.
- **All eleven research families show the same structure:** "overadequate mother, helpless patient, and peripherally attached fathers."
- **Two simultaneous, contradictory levels of demand from mother to patient.** Illustrated by a mother who buttered a psychotic son's bread, cut his meat, and poured his milk while simultaneously urging him "on an intellectually mature level" to grow up and do more for himself. "The action story would be appropriate between a mother and small child and the verbal one would fit best between a mother and teenage child." (Bowen notes the patient stopped eating.)
- **The content of a mother's worry about the patient matches her own felt inadequacy.** A mother preoccupied with disease and the inadequacy of her own internal organs worried about her son's bowels, skin, sinuses; her son had multiple physical complaints. Mothers doubting their own physical attractiveness worried about the patients' teeth, hair, complexion, posture, body build, dress, masculine/feminine characteristics — "these patients tended to be exaggerated examples of what the mothers had 'fought against.'" Mothers doubting their own intellectual capacity worried about IQ tests, grades, intellectual functioning — "the patients in these two families appeared intellectually dull."
- **Somatic reciprocation: anxiety in one person becomes physical illness in another.** An overtly anxious mother would describe the patient's symptoms to the ward internist; the patient would agree; the doctor would diagnose and prescribe — "within a few hours, a process could change from anxiety in the mother to pain in the patient that had been diagnosed and was being treated." In a striking series, a mother responded to rapid improvement in a regressed patient with a physical illness of several days' duration within a few hours of each significant change — febrile respiratory infection, laryngitis with severe edema of the vocal cords, gastroenteritis, severe urticaria. Bowen adds that pediatricians report this as a troublesome problem in their practice.
- **Fusion of feeling between mother and daughter.** A mother who had never noticed how much time she spent thinking about the daughter recalled that when the child fell and injured her head, "the mother's own head began to hurt in the exact spot." She decided to "put an invisible wall between us so I can have my life and she can have hers." The daughter confirmed it: she "had never been able to know how she herself felt," depended on the mother to tell her how she felt, discounted her own divergent feelings, and also depended on the mother to know how she looked and whether colors matched. Away at school for long periods she could begin to have her own feelings; on returning home she would again lose the ability. She reported the same intuitive ability to know how the mother felt.
- **Patients are not passive.** "The patient participates so actively in this process that I do not see the patient as a 'victim' of the situation." Patients "philosophically accept this position as a mission for which they were born." One patient: "I was born when my mother needed someone. It could have been my brother or my sister if they had been born when I was."
- **Every household became geared to the patient's demands.** "All the research families have eventually found their homes geared to the demands of the patient. The parents are as helpless in taking a stand against the patient as the patient in taking a stand against parents." Patients are "adept at arousing sympathy and overhelpfulness in others."
- **Father–son closeness efforts uniformly failed.** In the **six families with psychotic sons**, all parents worried about implications of homosexuality if sons stayed attached to their mothers and agreed sons needed close relationships with fathers for masculine identification. All six fathers tried; "every effort failed." The most successful pair spent an afternoon a week together for several years, with the father in the position of "the mother's hired attendant for the son." Another father became a boy scout troop leader hoping his son would join; the mother did not relinquish her attachment and the son never attended a meeting.
- **Normal siblings get drawn in but always withdraw.** Bowen initially thought all siblings were heavily involved. A family story indicated the father and the younger daughter were as attached as the mother and the psychotic older daughter, and the first six months of observation tended to confirm it; over the next two years the younger daughter sided with all three members of the basic triad "but she was never involved to the point that she could not withdraw and leave the family." "Over and over, normal siblings and in-laws have become involved for a time in the family conflict but they have always withdrawn."
- **The patient's presymptomatic social profile.** "She had few close friends. She was more comfortable with adults. She never seemed to know what to do or what to say around other children." After adolescence, "she was much more active and outgoing in school." The normal younger sibling, four years younger, "was unusually outgoing and successful with minimal effort."
- **Psychotic break on the first sustained separation.** The daughter's break came the first year away from home at college: she made it through one semester by "increasing the denial," work became impaired during the first examination period, and psychotic collapse developed over a period of a few days while she was still increasing the denial and redoubling her effort to "do it myself."
- **The father's functioning collapsed after the patient's break.** He "automatically slipped back to inadequate functioning in relation to the extremely overadequate mother. His business began to fail. He went into bankruptcy within a year without any awareness that his bankruptcy could have any relation to the functional interdependency of the central family triad."
- **The mother reframed the daughter's first open rebellion as illness.** "It is because she is sick."
- **No rejecting mothers in the sample, though every patient called the mother rejecting.** Mothers' attention tracked their own anxiety: anxious mothers hover and infantilize, non-anxious mothers give much less attention, and patients experience the drop as rejection.
- **Change in one member is followed by change in the other two.** "A change in one member would be followed by changes in the other two. It was observation of the changes that led to the description of the 'overadequate-inadequate reciprocal functioning.'"
- **The keystone observation on parental closeness.** "When the parents were emotionally close, more invested in each other than either was in the patient, the patient improved. When either parent became more emotionally invested in the patient than in the other parent, the patient immediately and automatically regressed." Further: when the parents were emotionally close "they could do no wrong" in managing the patient — firmness, permissiveness, punishment, "talking it out" all worked; when emotionally divorced, "any and all 'management approaches' were equally unsuccessful."
- **Silent families exist.** "There are families with schizophrenic family members that have little or no conflict and no history of the factors we ordinarily associate with schizophrenia."
- **Observers lose objectivity around these families.** A nonparticipant observer "begins to participate emotionally in the family drama just as surely as he inwardly cheers the hero and hates the villain when he attends the theater," even when trained in handling countertransference. Each family member solicits outside support for his own emotional point of view.
- **Parents demanded the staff take over the family problem.** "The parents were soon asking, and demanding, that the staff 'come into the family problem' and solve it for them." **[p2] The sentence does not end there, and pass 1 truncated it.** The full clause continues "…so that there was never a problem with the staff's 'intruding into' or 'being excluded from' the family situation," and the next sentence gives a two-sided result: "The close 'helping' relationship of the staff made it possible for them to know the families better but it created new technical problems for the treatment program." A stated benefit (access, no boundary conflict) alongside a stated cost.
- **[p2] "Intense" is given an operational definition, and it is thought-allocation, valence-blind.** "The term *intense* describes an ambivalent relationship in which the thoughts of both, whether positive or negative, are largely invested in each other." This is the chapter's own account of what "invested" means, and it counts hostile preoccupation exactly as it counts affectionate preoccupation.
- **[p2] The mother's own discovery of the allocation.** The untangling mother "began to notice how much time she spent in thinking about the daughter. She had never been consciously aware of this before." The quantity is real and out of the holder's awareness until change is already underway.
- **[p2] All the described characteristics are stress-modulated.** "The characteristics of the relationships are most pronounced during periods of stress." Stated once, flatly, as a global qualifier over the whole mother–child / child–mother / father section.
- **[p2] The mother's demand direction inverts with the child's current state.** "When the child is more grown up, the mother infantilizes. When he is childish, she demands that he grow up." This sits beside — and is not reconciled with — the simultaneous-contradictory-channels account (the buttered bread).
- **[p2] The father is recruited on a specific trigger.** "If the mother's effort to 'make the child behave' is not immediately successful, the father will add his weight to her effort." And: "Even though both parents have a conscious wish for the child to grow and develop normally, they both automatically do things to keep the child in the helpless position." The peripheral father is not inert; he is a second stage that fires on the mother's failure.
- **[p2] Symptoms are gated by growth *rate*, not by growth level.** "The relationship might remain fairly calm and erupt into separation anxiety symptoms only during periods of rapid growth in the child."
- **[p2] The mother's threat of a new baby is named as the primary threat *she* poses.** "The mother threatens the child in many ways. Most important is the threat that she might have another baby and desert the child." This is stated immediately before the which-child-is-selected list, and one of those instances is a displacement that did not happen ("the oldest was so pitiful when the second was born").
- **[p2] A unanimous family causal theory that Bowen's own data contradict.** "All the mothers have been concerned about the intensity of the child's attachment to themselves. The mothers see the attachment as due to the fathers' disinterest in the child. **The fathers agree with this.**" Yet all six fathers tried and failed, with the failure attributed in-text to the mother's non-relinquishment. Both parents, in all eleven families, hold the same wrong explanation of their own structure.
- **[p2] The husband's overt opposition masked the wife's own covert fear.** On the decision to have a child: "The husband opposed passively with comments about money and the right time. His opposition obscured her fears that she might not be able to have a normal baby." One partner's stated position carries the other's unstated one.
- **[p2] Pre-adolescent coping channels, named.** "He had handled his wishes to grow up with fantasies of future greatness," and "Our patients used denial and isolation, while still living in the home, to escape the mother's 'magnetic field.'"
- **[p2] The child faces three obstacles in a stated order.** "The child in this dilemma has to deal first with the mother's effort to hold him back and then with his own urge to return to her, before he can get to the problems of the normal adolescent." Two distinct forces — the external hold and the internalised pull — and only then the ordinary task. "Once free of the mother, he faces outside relationships without a self of his own."
- **[p2] The eleven families are ordered on a "scale" of emotional-divorce style, with a stated distribution.** One extreme formal-controlled family, one extreme overtly-conflictual family, and "In the middle of the scale were nine families." The word "scale" appears exactly once in the chapter and this is it.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Family as a single organism.** The family unit is regarded as one organism; the patient is "that part of the family organism through which the overt symptoms of psychosis are expressed." Acts on: the interpretation of any individual symptom.
- **Three-generation process.** Schizophrenia is "a process that requires three or more generations to develop." The chain as stated: (1) grandparents relatively mature; (2) one child in that sibling group acquires "roughly the combined levels of immaturity" of the two parents — and it is the child who had "the most intense early attachment to the mother"; (3) that child marries a spouse of *equal* immaturity but *opposite* defense mechanisms; (4) the process repeats, and one child in the third generation carries a high degree of immaturity while the other siblings are much more mature; (5) that child "may develop clinical schizophrenia in an attempt to adapt to the demands of growing up." Bowen credits the three-generation idea to his consultant Dr. Lewis Hill (1955) and flags this section as his most speculative and least supported.
- **Assortative mating on immaturity.** "It is a consistent clinical experience, among those who work with husbands and wives, that people choose spouses who have identical levels of immaturity but who have opposite defense mechanisms." What each consciously admired in the other was "prominent in the facades of overadequacy."
- **Emotional divorce.** A marked, stable emotional distance between the parents, maintained by varying combinations of formal control and overt disagreement. It is the platform state on which the rest of the machinery runs.
- **Overadequate–inadequate reciprocal functioning.** Both parents are equally immature. One denies the immaturity and runs "a facade of overadequacy"; the other accentuates it and runs "a facade of inadequacy." "The overadequacy of one functions in reciprocal relationship to the inadequacy of the other. Neither is capable of functioning in the midground." Explicitly **functioning states, not fixed states**. When the mother is overadequate she is "dominating and aggressive" and the father "helpless and compliant"; when the father is overadequate he is "cruel and authoritarian" and the mother "helpless and whining."
- **The domination–submission issue and decision paralysis.** On personal issues affecting both, whoever makes the decision *becomes* the overadequate one and the other *becomes* the inadequate one. The decider sees self as forced to take responsibility and the other as a shirker; the other sees self as "forced to submit" and the first as "dominating." The term "domination-submission" was introduced by the inadequate one, who complains most. Because deciding costs a position, all decisions are avoided. When decision paralysis becomes intense, "the mothers more often assume the decision-making function against the passive resistance of the fathers."
- **Anxiety-binding by joint activity avoidance.** Because reciprocal functioning generates anxiety in any teamwork situation, the couple's mechanism is "for each to work independently and to avoid joint activity." Conflict is less when a third or fourth person is present — an explicit statement that a third party absorbs dyadic anxiety.
- **The interdependent triad (father–mother–child), with the child as keystone.** Once the child is born, the mother is securely in the overadequate position toward a human who belongs to her and is *realistically* helpless. "She could now control her own immaturity by caring for the immaturity of another." With her own functioning stabilized, she becomes a more stable figure for the father, who can hold a "more fixed position of aloof distance" — for this father, "similar to his relationship with his own mother." "The child was the keystone." The net: "the functional helplessness of the infant makes it possible for both parents to have a less anxious adjustment."
- **Projection (mother → child).** "The most all pervasive mechanism in the mother-child relationship… used constantly by every mother in every aspect of her relationship with the patient." Sequence: the mother denies her own helplessness and wish to be babied → projects the denied feeling onto the child → perceives the child as helpless and wishing to be mothered → the child, "and even the entire family," accepts the perception as a reality in the child → the mother then mothers that helplessness with her adequate self. Result: **"a situation that begins as a feeling in the mother, becomes a reality in the child."** Bowen flags the term as inadequate (see A5/X2).
- **Introjection (child → self).** "The mother 'projects' her inadequacies to the child and the child automatically 'introjects' the mother's inadequacies." The mother initiates; the child responds rather than initiating.
- **Somatic reciprocation.** "A mechanism in which the soma of one person reciprocates with the psyche of another person." An anxiety in one person becomes a physical illness in another. Bowen locates it as belonging "primarily in the functioning reciprocation of extreme unrealistic overadequacy to extreme unrealistic inadequacy" — i.e. it is the overadequate–inadequate mechanism expressed in the body, and is most common in but not limited to the mother–patient relationship.
- **Two simultaneous levels of demand (action vs verbal).** The mother makes two demands: the *emotional* demand that the patient remain helpless, "conveyed in subtle forceful ways that are out of conscious awareness," and the *overt, verbalized, "hammered home"* demand that the patient become gifted and mature. "The verbal level is usually a direct contradiction to the action level." Bowen states this is "the same dilemma described in different terms by Bateson et al. (1956) in their concept of the double bind."
- **Externalization of the mother's inadequacy as the content of her worry.** "The subjects of the mothers' overconcerns about the patients and the focus of their 'picking on the patients' are the same as their own feelings of inadequacy about themselves… almost any point in the mothers' list of complaints about the patient can be regarded as an externalization of the mother's own inadequacies."
- **Emotional process / emotional demand.** Deliberately descriptive terms for "the emotional responsiveness by which one family member responds automatically to the emotional state of another, without either being consciously aware of the process." It "runs silently beneath the surface between people who have very close relationships" and "operates during periods of conflict and periods of calm harmony." Unconscious "in the sense that neither person is consciously aware of it, but it is not unconscious in our usual use of the term."
- **"Being for the other" → loss of self.** The child enters the arrangement "automatically protecting his own interests by doing the things that will insure a less anxious and more predictable mother." Once the child is "being (helpless) for the mother" and the mother is "being (strong) for the child," they are in "a functional bind of 'being for each other.'" "When the child's self is devoted to 'being for the mother,' he loses the capacity of 'being for himself.'"
- **Functional helplessness, not constitutional helplessness.** "I regard schizophrenia as a functional helplessness in contrast to concepts that regard it as a constitutional helplessness."
- **Arrest of psychological growth, widening gap against physical growth.** "The process in which the child begins to 'be for the mother' results in an arrest in his psychological growth. His physical growth remains normal. Each year there is a wider discrepancy between physical growth and psychological growth."
- **The perpetuated symbiosis, destabilized by biology.** "The symbiosis attempts to make two lives stand still at a particular pleasurable phase in both life cycles." Normal as an early stage; "when it is perpetuated, it becomes a foreign thing, threatened by the biological progression of the very life process of which it was once a part." Each is threatened by change in the other: the child by any sign of aging, sickness, anxiety, weakness, or attitude change in the mother; the mother by growth, sickness, or anything that might stop the child being her baby. "The feelings that each experience in regard to the loss of the other are equated with death."
- **Selection of which child.** "The mother's selection of a particular child for such an intense relationship is determined by her unconscious functioning in the prevailing reality situation."
- **"Peace at any price."** "The research families all have a low tolerance for anxiety. They operate on a 'peace at any price' principle. They quickly compromise important life principles to relieve anxiety for this moment… this 'peace at any price' policy immediately causes greater anxiety for tomorrow, but they continue."
- **Anxiety as the trigger signal.** "Anxiety in the mother is an automatic signal for the patient 'to help the mother' by becoming her baby." "The mothers' efforts to change the patients are timed with anxieties in themselves, and not with the reality situation in the patients."
- **The father as substitute mother, never a primary figure.** The parents behave "very much like divorced parents who share their children." The mother, overadequate, is in charge. The child cannot choose between parents directly but "can harass the mother until she goes away and leaves the child with the father." The father then occupies "the functioning position of a substitute mother" and "still remains a representative of the mother."
- **The psychosis as a failed adaptation.** "The psychosis represents an unsuccessful attempt to adapt the severe psychological impairment to the demands of adult functioning." The patient's "denial of incapacity and her protestations of strength were now expressed in the distorted verbalization, while her helplessness was acted out by the psychosis." Structurally, "the psychosis represented a disruption of the symbiotic attachment to the mother and a collapse of the long term interdependent father-mother-patient triad."
- **A spectrum of unresolved symbiotic attachment with alternative outlets.** "Unresolved symbiotic attachments to the mother vary from the very mild to the very intense… the mild ones cause little impairment… schizophrenic psychoses develop among those with the most intense unresolved attachments." Alternative resolutions named: replacing the original mother with mother substitutes; expressing the functional helplessness as somatic illness; the character-neurotic flight mechanism; seeking distant relationships (what the research patients did). "The psychotic collapse is seen as an effort at resolution that failed."
- **[p2] Investment as a thought budget allocated across targets.** Not stated as a law, but used consistently as one. Intensity is defined as thoughts "largely invested in each other" (valence-blind); during pregnancy "her thoughts were almost totally invested in the developing fetus," and "When she was emotionally close to the husband, her thoughts were more invested in him and there was less preoccupation with the child." Increase toward one target, decrease toward the other, in the same sentence. This is the quantity the closeness seesaw operates on.
- **[p2] The maturity servo.** Two contradictory demands with fixed content (action = stay helpless, verbal = grow up) *and* a state-dependent inversion in which the mother pushes back against whichever direction the child has just moved. The second form is a negative-feedback controller holding the child at a setpoint; the first is a standing double bind. Bowen states both and does not reconcile them.
- **[p2] Two-stage enforcement.** The mother acts first; if her effort is "not immediately successful," the father adds his weight. Enforcement escalates by recruiting the peripheral member, and the recruitment condition is the *failure* of the first stage, not the size of the deviation.
- **[p2] Growth rate as the destabiliser.** The symbiosis is "precariously balanced at best" and is disturbed by *periods of rapid growth*, with calm between them. Bowen states the mechanism separately from the accumulating physical/psychological gap: the gap widens monotonically, but the eruptions are episodic and rate-driven.

## A3. Predictive claims

Format: conditional → evidence status.

1. **If two people of high immaturity with opposite defense mechanisms marry, then at least one of their children will have a very high level of immaturity, and that child may develop clinical schizophrenia in adapting to the demands of growing up.** Bowen explicitly hedges: "It is stressed that this is not a specific proposition about the origin of schizophrenia but that such a pattern has been present in several of the families." *Asserted, with case illustration, self-flagged as speculative.*
2. **If a sibling group produces a child with elevated immaturity, then it is the child who had the most intense early attachment to the mother.** *Asserted from history-taking; the whole three-generation section is flagged as "where thinking is most speculative and supporting data are weakest."*
3. **The immaturity acquired by that child is roughly equivalent to the combined levels of immaturity in the two parents.** *Asserted, no measurement offered.* Notably additive, not averaging.
4. **People choose spouses with identical levels of immaturity but opposite defense mechanisms.** *Asserted as "a consistent clinical experience, among those who work with husbands and wives" — appeal to shared clinical consensus, not to project data.*
5. **If the parents are emotionally divorced, then each will occupy one pole of overadequate/inadequate, and neither can function in the midground.** *Evidence: "in all the families, the parents have definite patterns of functioning in the emotional-divorce situation."*
6. **If one parent makes a decision affecting both, then that parent occupies the overadequate position and the other the inadequate one — so decisions will be avoided.** *Asserted from observation, with a quoted father as illustration.*
7. **If the couple engages in joint two-person activity, conflict rises; if a third or fourth person is present, conflict falls.** *Evidence: reported by "several couples" plus described golf/cards/work-project cases.*
8. **If the mother becomes emotionally closer to the husband during pregnancy, her investment in the fetus decreases; if more distant, investment in the fetus increases (to the point of wishing to abort).** *Evidence: a single mother's detailed account.*
9. **If a mother's anxiety rises, then she hovers and infantilizes; if it falls, she withdraws attention, which the patient experiences as rejection.** *Asserted as a pattern across "every mother in our group."*
10. **If the mother is overtly anxious and comes into direct contact with the patient, then the mother becomes less anxious and the patient becomes more psychotic and regressed.** *Asserted as near-automatic and near-instantaneous. This is the strongest single mechanistic claim in the chapter.*
11. **If the patient actively opposes an emotional demand ("I refuse to let you get me upset"), then the mother becomes overtly anxious or physically ill.** *Asserted; requires "a fairly high level of functional strength" in the patient.*
12. **A patient's response to demands is a function of his functional helplessness and the mother's functional strength:** a very helpless/regressed patient complies immediately with emotional demands and ignores verbal demands; a less regressed patient offers token resistance to emotional demands but disagrees vigorously with verbal ones. *Asserted as a graded rule — effectively a dose-response.*
13. **The degree of negative response in the patient is directly related to the intensity of the campaign to change the "inadequacies" in the patient.** *Asserted.*
14. **If a therapist or other outside figure names the mother's projection to her, then the mother — and even the father and patient — will attack or withdraw, or both. If the patient or the father names it, there is a significant beneficial emotional reaction.** *Asserted from clinical experience. 27. **[p2] The relationship stays fairly calm and erupts into separation-anxiety symptoms only during periods of rapid growth in the child.** *Asserted. Symptom onset is driven by the rate of change, not by the accumulated gap.*
28. **[p2] The characteristics of all these relationships are most pronounced during periods of stress.** *Asserted once as a global qualifier. Every mechanism in the chapter is stress-gain-modulated.*

## A5. Terminology

- **Family unit / family as a single organism** — the unit of observation and of diagnosis; the symptom belongs to the organism, the patient is the part through which it is expressed. Bowen calls this concept "crucial to our way of thinking."
- **"Family-unit" view** — a view of the family available to the psychotherapist meeting the family group, "different from any possible in any, or in all, individual perceptions of the family." Explicitly a *fourth* perspective, not the sum of members' perspectives.
- **Family psychotherapy** — the revised treatment plan "in which the family members attended all therapy hours together."
- **Emotional divorce** — marked, sustained emotional distance between the parents, maintained by any of several styles (formal control, overt conflict, physical distance).
- **Overadequate / inadequate** — explicitly "functioning states and not fixed states." Overadequate = "a functioning facade of strength that is greater than realistic"; inadequate = "a functioning facade of helplessness that is as unrealistic as the facade of strength is unrealistic in the other direction."
- **Overadequate–inadequate reciprocal functioning** — the paired, complementary operation of those two states; the strength of one is a function of the helplessness of the other. Bowen states in note 5 that Wynne's *pseudomutuality* and Jackson's *complementarity* describe the same relationship phenomenon.
- **Domination–submission issue** — the two parties' opposed readings of the same decision event. Bowen notes the term "was introduced by the inadequate one who complains the most" — i.e. it is the family's word, adopted descriptively.
- **Interdependent triad** — the fixed father–mother–child functioning arrangement in which the child is "the keystone." Also called "the basic family triad" and "the central family triad," and its members are "linked in the triangular interdependency." *This is the term doing the work that "triangle" does in later Bowen vocabulary; the word "triangle" as a technical term does not appear here.*
- **Symbiosis / symbiotic attachment** — the unresolved mother–child attachment. Normal as an early stage; pathological when perpetuated. Bowen's original 1954 hypothesis was built on "an unresolved, symbiotic attachment to the mother," and note 1 credits Benedek, Mahler, Hill, Lidz and Lidz, Reichard and Tillman, and Limentani.
- **Projection** — used in quotation marks throughout. Note 6: it "accurately describes a mechanism in the individual, but, in a two-person relationship, it does not describe the reciprocating 'introjection' of the other. The combined term 'projection-introjection' also does not account for all the essential aspects of this complex mechanism." *A term Bowen is visibly dissatisfied with and would replace.*
- **Introjection** — the child's automatic uptake of the mother's projected inadequacy.
- **Emotional demand** — the out-of-awareness, action-level demand, as against the verbal demand. Contrasted directly with "verbal demand."
- **Emotional process** — the automatic responsiveness of one family member to another's emotional state, outside the awareness of both. Chosen descriptively. "Perhaps this can be understood as nonverbal communication, but I have chosen to use these terms descriptively." Bowen distinguishes this unconsciousness from psychoanalytic unconsciousness.
- **Maturity / immaturity** — Bowen states outright he does not like these terms but uses them "descriptively in an attempt to avoid terms with an association automatically connotative of an individual orientation." *This is the slot later filled by differentiation; here it is a deliberately blunt placeholder.* Immaturity is treated as an additive quantity across the marriage.
- **Functional helplessness** — helplessness as a state of functioning ("being helpless") rather than a fixed constitutional property ("is helpless"). Contrasted with "constitutional helplessness."
- **"Being for the other" / "being for himself"** — devoting one's self to the other's emotional needs, at the cost of the capacity to act on one's own behalf.
- **Somatic reciprocation** — the soma of one person reciprocating with the psyche of another.
- **Interested detachment** — the workable objectivity staff attained by emotionally detaching from the family problem; the precondition for defocusing the individual.
- **"Peace at any price"** — the families' operating principle of trading tomorrow's anxiety for today's relief.
- **"Magnetic field"** — a patient's own image for the mother's pull: too close and "she would suddenly be 'pulled into the mother' and lose her own identity"; too far and "she had no 'self' at all."
- **"Invisible wall"** — a mother's own image for the boundary she deliberately erected: "so I can have my life and she can have hers."
- **[p2] Intense / invested** — *defined in the text*: "The term **intense** describes an ambivalent relationship in which the thoughts of both, whether positive or negative, are largely invested in each other." Intensity is share of thought occupied by the other, and it is **valence-blind**. This is the operational content of "emotionally invested" everywhere else in the chapter, including the closeness seesaw.
- **[p2] "Important other"** — what the child becomes to the mother. "The first child came closer to completing 'fulfillment' of the mother's need for an important other person than any other person in her experience," and "the child becomes the 'important other' to the mother." A named slot in the mother's economy that the child fills.
- **Function vs diagnosis** — Bowen states the team is "working our way toward some kind of system that deals with 'function' rather than with the static situation conveyed by a diagnostic label," citing Spiegel's role theory, Jackson's stable/unstable × satisfactory/unsatisfactory classification, and Regensburg's functional classification of the marital relationship.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The fixed triad in all eleven families:** overadequate mother — helpless patient — peripherally attached father.
- **The child is the keystone.** Not a bystander and not a victim: the infant's realistic helplessness is what allows the mother to hold the overadequate position, which in turn allows the father a less anxious relationship with the mother. Remove the child and the parental equilibrium goes.
- **The patient's own position statement:** "I was born when my mother needed someone. It could have been my brother or my sister if they had been born when I was." Position is assigned by the family's need at a moment in time, not by the person.
- **Which child is selected** is "determined by [the mother's] unconscious functioning in the prevailing reality situation." Stated patterns, none exclusive:
  - "A good percentage of mothers will retain the initial relationship with the first child."
  - One mother kept the oldest because "the oldest was so pitiful when the second was born."
  - Some mothers form successive attachments to each new child and finally retain the youngest as "my baby."
  - Some choose from the middle: one mother with five children had successive attachments to the first two and retained the third, "a daughter who looked like the mother."
  - One mother had normal relationships with her first two children and an intense attachment to the third, "born shortly after the death of her own mother." *(A death in the previous generation as the selection trigger.)*
  - "The birth of a physically deformed child might come closer to 'fulfillment' of the mother's emotional needs than a normal child."
- **In the parental generation, the vulnerable one was the one most attached to his/her own mother.** In the illustrating family: the father was one of eight children, "much less mature than his siblings," "mother's favorite" (which the grandmother either denied or implicitly conceded); the mother was "the oldest daughter" and "the one in her sibling group who had the most intense attachment to her mother."
- **Two opposite adolescent resolutions in the parental generation.** The father "attained his area of adequacy outside the home" (aloof from his mother, applied himself to school and business, more successful than siblings and colleagues, shy and uncomfortable in close relationships, never rebelled, maintained "a distant, compliant relationship" with his parents). The mother "gained her area of adequacy in the home" (changed suddenly from a shy dependent girl who could do nothing without her mother into "a socially poised and resourceful young woman who could run the home without help"). Both denied their immaturity and functioned adequately in a chosen domain; both were lonely and aloof.
- **The mother is the initiator, the child the responder.** "The mother actively initiates her emotional and verbal demands and the child is more involved in responding to the mother's demands than in initiating his own demands."
- **The mother is in charge of the child; the father can only get access if she permits it.** "The parents follow a pattern very much like divorced parents who share their children." The child has no direct voice in choosing but can harass the mother until she leaves the child with the father. The father then functions as "a substitute mother" and "still remains a representative of the mother."
- **The father who moves first (in therapy) is the father.** In the successful case, the shift ran: mother–daughter conflict occupies the first months → the father gradually participates → conflict shifts to the mother–father relationship → the father takes stands → the mother becomes more anxious, challenging and aggressive → the father "assumed a position as head of the family, in spite of her marked anxiety, tremulousness, and protest" → within a few days the mother became "a kind, motherly, objective person": "It is so nice finally to have a man for a husband. If he can keep on being a man, then I can be a woman."
- **The patient contests the reorganization.** "The patient tried to win back her lost symbiotic partner, but the mother remained firm and the patient made some solid progress."
- **Who may confront whom.** A therapist or outside figure naming the mother's projection triggers attack or withdrawal from mother, father *and* patient. The same content from the patient or the father produces "a significant beneficial emotional reaction." Position in the system determines whether an intervention lands.
- **Normal siblings occupy a temporary, exitable position.** They and in-laws take sides for a time and always withdraw; "the basic family triad remains, linked in the triangular interdependency."
- **The normal sibling's trajectory is contrastive.** The four-years-younger sister was "unusually outgoing and successful with minimal effort," against a patient who "had few close friends, was more comfortable with adults, never seemed to know what to do or what to say around other children."
- **Sex composition:** six of the families had psychotic sons. The initial three inpatient dyads were mothers and schizophrenic *daughters*.
- **[p2] The father–child tie has two distinct levels with two distinct gates.** Verbatim, one sentence apart: "his emotional divorce from the mother remains rather constant, but **he can have a close relationship to the child any time the mother permits it**" — versus — "it has not been possible for a father to have a **primary** relationship with the patient until he has first been able to change his own emotional divorce with the mother." A *close* relationship is available on the mother's permission and puts the father in the substitute-mother / "representative of the mother" position. A *primary* relationship is gated on the marital state and was never achieved. The "most successful" of the six sons cases — an afternoon a week for several years — is an instance of the first, counted as a failure of the second.
- **[p2] The father is peripheral by default and recruitable on demand.** He adds his weight when the mother's effort to make the child behave fails. Peripherality is a resting position, not an incapacity.
- **[p2] Both parents hold the same wrong account of the structure.** Mothers attribute the child's attachment to the fathers' disinterest; "The fathers agree with this." Bowen's own data put the failure on the mother's non-relinquishment. A family-level belief, unanimous across roles and across all eleven families, that points at the wrong node.
- **Excluded structures:** "We have not worked with families with complicated family histories involving the death of a parent, divorces, remarriages, or multiple neuroses and psychoses in the same sibling group."

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **See the family group together.** The revised plan, "family psychotherapy," is one "in which the family members attended all therapy hours together." The unit of treatment is the unit of pathology.
- **Attain interested detachment first.** "Clinical staff members have been able to gain workable objectivity by detaching themselves emotionally from the family problem. When it was possible to attain a workable level of interested detachment, it was then possible to begin to defocus the individual and to focus on the entire family at once."
- **Defocus the individual; focus on the whole family at once.** Bowen's two images: shifting a microscope "from the oil immersion to the low power lens," and "moving from the playing field to the top of the stadium to watch a football game." Broad patterns of form and movement obscured in close-up become clear, and "the close-up view could then become more meaningful once the distant view was also possible."
- **Discard psychiatric terminology; use simple descriptive words.** "To facilitate the shift to family-unit thinking, we have attempted to discard as much of the 'second nature' psychiatric terminology as possible and to force ourselves to use simple descriptive words." Language discipline is treated as a technique, not a stylistic preference.
- **Track function, not diagnosis.** Work toward "some kind of system that deals with 'function' rather than with the static situation conveyed by a diagnostic label."
- **Work toward changing the parents' emotional divorce as the primary target.** The successful case runs through the marriage, not through the patient: get the peripheral father to participate, let the conflict move from mother–patient to mother–father, support the father in taking stands.
- **Let the family confront itself.** When the patient or father names the mother's projection there is "a significant beneficial emotional reaction." The therapist's job is to make that possible rather than to deliver the interpretation.
- **Expect and hold through the mother's escalation.** When the father took stands, the mother "became much more anxious, challenging, and aggressive toward him"; the change required him to hold "in spite of her marked anxiety, tremulousness, and protest."
- **Wait out the fragmentation of the material.** Personal material about mate choice "is usually fragmented and distorted until after they are comfortable in family psychotherapy."
- **Structure the setting so the family functions as it would at home.** "The ward administration was adapted, as nearly as possible, to permit the family to function as it would at home." Parents assumed principal responsibility for the patient's care; staff made services available at the family's request.
- **Record at multiple levels.** Nurses record per shift on the individual, on the family unit, and on the family's relation to the environment; each therapy hour is taped plus process notes, verbal-content summary, and a sociogram; daily → weekly → monthly summaries.
- **Read the action story separately from the verbal story.** Bowen cites Dysinger (1957) as having attempted to isolate the action story in one of the research families. The technique implied: score the two channels independently.
- **Watch what the family does around symptom change, not only around symptom presence.** The mother's serial physical illnesses tracked *improvement* in the patient.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

> **[p2] Read this section with a correction in hand.** The chapter contains almost no imperatives. Pass 1 converted Bowen's *observations* into *prohibitions*, which is a real distortion for the coaching model, because an impossibility and a prohibition implement differently — one removes the move from the repertoire, the other leaves it available with a penalty. Specifically: "it has not been possible for a father to have a primary relationship…" is an **impossibility claim**; "It is easy for the observer to become preoccupied with the conflict" is an **ease claim**; "It is much easier to treat the compliant patient than to attempt to deal with the underlying problem" is a **wry observation**. Only the naming-the-projection item is stated with anything like a consequence attached. The items below are retained as inferences, now labelled as such.

- **Do not aim for objectivity as a nonparticipant observer.** "A nonparticipant observer might aspire to scientific objectivity, but, in the emotional tension that surrounds these families, he begins to participate emotionally in the family drama." Training in countertransference is explicitly stated to be insufficient: "It is difficult for therapists and personnel to remain objective even if they are trained in handling countertransference problems."
- **Do not take sides.** "Each family member solicits outside support for his particular emotional point of view" — every member is actively recruiting the therapist.
- **Do not, as the therapist or any outside figure, tell the mother that her complaints about the patient are her own inadequacies.** "If a therapist or other outside figure suggests this, the mother and even the father and patient will attack or withdraw, or both." The whole family closes ranks; the correct content from the wrong source is counterproductive.
- **Do not let the father pursue a primary relationship with the patient before he has changed his emotional divorce with the mother.** "In our experience it has not been possible for a father to have a primary relationship with the patient until he has first been able to change his own emotional divorce with the mother." Six documented attempts, six failures.
- **Do not solve the family's problem for them when they demand it.** The parents "were soon asking, and demanding, that the staff 'come into the family problem' and solve it for them"; Bowen records that the close "helping" relationship "created new technical problems for the treatment program." **[p2] This item is pass 1's worst anachronism and should be struck as a rule.** Bowen states no prohibition. He states a *mixed* result: the demand meant "there was never a problem with the staff's 'intruding into' or 'being excluded from' the family situation," and the helping relationship "made it possible for them to know the families better **but** it created new technical problems." Pass 1 read Ch05's later finding (L05.2 — proximity of help is a load-bearing negative) back into a 1958 passage that reports an access benefit alongside an unnamed cost. In 1958 the sign of the helping relationship is **undetermined**.
- **Do not become preoccupied with the conflict and turmoil.** "It is easy for the observer to become preoccupied with the conflict and turmoil" — and the silent, conflict-free families "may provide more clues to the process." The loud material is a distraction from the mechanism.
- **Do not treat the patient's compliance as recovery.** The ward internist case: diagnosing and prescribing for the patient's symptoms *completed* the transfer of anxiety from mother to patient. "It is much easier to treat the compliant patient than to attempt to deal with the underlying problem."
- **Do not carry the individual frame of reference in unexamined.** "We have all been trained to think of emotional problems as individual ones. The entire body of psychological and psychoanalytic theory was developed from perceiving the family through the eyes of the patient… It has been difficult to change this automatic way of thinking in ourselves."
- **Do not pick a management style and expect it to be the active ingredient.** Firmness, permissiveness, punishment and "talking it out" all work when parents are close and all fail when parents are emotionally divorced.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

1. **Interested detachment precedes family-unit perception.** Only after staff detached emotionally was it "possible to begin to defocus the individual and to focus on the entire family at once."
2. **The distant view precedes the useful close view.** "The close-up view could then become more meaningful once the distant view was also possible."
3. **Living contact precedes the observations.** The hypothesis change came from the ward, not from joint interviews: facets came into focus "that had not been anticipated from work with each individually or from joint interviews with the two together." Bowen states plainly: "All of the changes in our working hypothesis and treatment approach have been based on clinical observations from the in-patient families."
4. **The father must change his emotional divorce with the mother before he can have a primary relationship with the patient.** A hard precondition, not a preference. **[p2] But only for the *primary* relationship.** A *close* relationship is available "any time the mother permits it," at the cost of occupying the substitute-mother position. Two gates, two levels of the same edge — see A6 [p2].
4a. **[p2] The child's own path out has three stages in a stated order.** First the mother's effort to hold him back, then his own urge to return to her, and only then "the problems of the normal adolescent." Two forces, external and internalised, must be cleared in sequence before the ordinary developmental task even begins.
4b. **[p2] The fusion material is only reportable after the fusion has changed.** The mother–daughter account of feeling each other's feelings comes from a pair "far along in the process of untangling themselves from each other," and the mother "had never been consciously aware of this before." The chapter's best first-person evidence for the core mechanism is available only from cases in which the mechanism was already loosening.
5. **The observed therapeutic sequence in the successful family:** mother–patient conflict occupies the first several months → the father gradually begins to participate in the family problems → the conflict *shifts* to the mother–father relationship → the father takes stands → the mother's anxiety, challenge and aggression rise → the father assumes the head-of-family position → **in a few days** the mother changes character → the emotional divorce disappears; **two months** of mutual investment "as two young lovers" → the patient attempts to win back the symbiotic partner → the mother holds → the patient makes solid progress.
6. **Relapse is expected and is not a reset.** "Under stress, they each fell back to their former ways of functioning, but thereafter it became easier for the father to move into an adequate position and less threatening for the mother to lose the overadequate position." The threshold moves even when the state reverts.
7. **Comfort in family psychotherapy precedes usable material.** Accounts of mate choice stay "fragmented and distorted" until then.
8. **The hypothesis itself was revised on a yearly cadence.** "At the end of one year, the hypothesis was extended"; "The hypothesis and operational approach has been modified, to some degree, every year."
9. **Timescales of the work:** inpatient participation averaged eighteen months (six months to three years); outpatient up to two years.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **The therapist joins the drama.** Untrained *or* trained, the observer near these families "begins to participate emotionally," cheering hero and hating villain. Failure mode: loss of the family-unit view entirely.
- **Naming the projection from outside unites the family against the therapist.** Attack, withdrawal, or both — from the mother, the father *and* the patient.
- **Treating the patient's transferred symptom completes the transfer.** Anxiety in the mother becomes diagnosed and medicated pain in the patient within a few hours. The medical response ratifies the mechanism.
- **Father→son closeness campaigns fail while the marriage is unchanged.** All six attempts failed; the "most successful" produced a father functioning as "the mother's hired attendant." A father's boy scout campaign produced a son who never attended a meeting. **[p2] The stated cause is the mother, not the marriage.** In the only case where Bowen gives a reason: "The mother did not relinquish any of her attachment to the son, and he never attended a scout meeting." Pass 1's "while the marriage is unchanged" is an inference imported from the adjacent precondition sentence, not the chapter's attribution.
- **The mother's anxiety-driven change campaigns produce negative response in the patient proportional to their intensity** — and they are timed to her anxiety, not to anything real in the patient.
- **"Peace at any price" makes tomorrow worse.** The families "quickly compromise important life principles to relieve anxiety for this moment… this 'peace at any price' policy immediately causes greater anxiety for tomorrow."
- **Improvement in the patient produces illness in the mother.** Four named illnesses within hours of each significant change.
- **Success in the patient's first separation attempt is not safety.** "Most of our patients were successful in their first efforts to function without their mothers." The family concluded adolescence had solved the problem; the collapse came at college. The strategy that carried the daughter through a semester — increasing denial and redoubling effort to "do it myself" — was the same strategy operating during the collapse.
- **The parent-side reciprocal collapse after the patient breaks.** The father slipped back to inadequate functioning and went bankrupt within a year, "without any awareness that his bankruptcy could have any relation to the functional interdependency of the central family triad."
- **The mother's defense after the break:** an increased facade of strength (toward the father and the hospital staff), taking responsibility for the hospitalization, and reframing the daughter's first open rebellion as illness — "It is because she is sick."
- **The institution cannot fully align with the theory.** "It has not been possible to run the clinical and the research operation in complete harmony with our theoretical position. Our own limitations and the generally accepted view that it is a disease in the individual make it necessary to retain part of the individual orientation… we may say that we regard psychosis as a family problem, but, in many basic ways, we must relate to it as a disease in the individual."

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

This section converts each rule of technique back into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.

1. **"See the whole family in one room" assumes the symptom is not located in the patient.** If the psychosis were a property of the individual, a family hour would add context but not change the object of treatment. Treating the family together asserts that the *unit that carries the disturbance* is the family, and that the patient's symptom is only its visible surface. → *Model implication: symptom state must be computable from a family-level state, not stored solely on a Person.*
2. **"Detach emotionally before you can see the unit" assumes the family emits a pull that reorganizes anyone in range.** Objectivity is not the default state of an observer near this family; it is an achievement against an active force. The family's field extends past its membership. → *An outsider entering the field acquires a position in it by default; neutrality is a maintained state with a cost, not a starting condition.*
3. **"Every member solicits outside support for his point of view" assumes recruitment is a standing property of the members, not an occasional event.** The system's response to an incoming third party is to attempt to convert it into an ally. → *This is the same machinery as "conflict was less when a third or fourth person was present": an outsider is an anxiety sink, and the family will try to fill it.*
4. **"Do not, as an outsider, name the mother's projection — but a beneficial reaction follows if the father or patient does" assumes an intervention's effect is a function of the intervener's position in the system, not of its content.** Identical content produces closing-of-ranks from outside and change from inside. → *Interventions must carry a source-position attribute; the same message has different effects by sender.*
5. **"The father cannot have a primary relationship with the patient until he changes his emotional divorce with the mother" assumes the father–child link is gated by the parental link, not independent of it.** The father's access to the child is a permission issued by the marital state. Six attempted violations produced six failures and one father functioning as "the mother's hired attendant." → *Relationship edges are not independently settable; changing one requires a prior change in another. There is a dependency ordering on the triad's edges.* **[p2] Sharpened: there are two gates on two levels of the same edge, issued by two different parties.** Contact-level closeness is gated by the **mother's permission** (granted freely, and the father who accepts it becomes "a representative of the mother"). Primary-relationship status is gated by the **marital state**. → *An edge needs at least two attributes — contact and position — with separate gating conditions. Raising contact while position stays gated does not converge on the position; it consolidates the substitute-mother role. A coach that maximises father–child contact is optimising the wrong attribute and will look like it is working.*
6. **"Work toward parental closeness rather than toward better patient management" assumes patient state is a dependent variable of the parental investment balance.** The strongest form Bowen gives: when the parents are close, *all* management approaches work; when divorced, *all* fail equally. Management technique has no independent effect. → *The patient's functioning should be modelled as driven by the relative investment of each parent in the spouse versus in the patient — not by anything done to the patient.*
7. **"Expect the mother to escalate when the father takes a stand" assumes the reciprocal positions are actively defended, not merely habitual.** Loss of the overadequate position is experienced as threat and generates anxiety, challenge, aggression, tremulousness. → *A move by one member imposes a forced change on the other; the resistance is proportional and appears as anxiety in the displaced party.*
8. **"Expect relapse under stress, but with a lowered threshold thereafter" assumes the system has hysteresis.** Position is state-dependent and reverts, but the cost of the new position falls permanently after a first successful occupation. → *There are two quantities: current position and the cost of assuming a position. Only the second is monotone-improving.*
9. **"Do not treat the compliant patient's symptom" assumes symptom location is fluid within the family and that medical validation fixes it in place.** The mother's anxiety became the patient's pain within hours specifically because it was described, diagnosed, and prescribed for. → *Diagnosis is an event that transfers and stabilizes symptom assignment — an external actor can act as a mechanism, not merely as an observer.*
10. **"Discard psychiatric terminology and use simple descriptive words" assumes vocabulary constrains perception.** Bowen states that diagnostic and descriptive terms apply to the individual and that "the entire body of psychological and psychoanalytic theory was developed from perceiving the family through the eyes of the patient." Keeping the terminology reimports the individual frame. → *The representation used to describe the family determines which regularities are visible; the choice of state variables is not neutral.*
11. **"The family-unit view is different from any, or all, individual perceptions" assumes non-additivity.** "Each family member has a perception of the family that is different from the perception of any other family member. Each family member is different in outside relationships from what he is in the presence of other family members." → *Family-level state is not recoverable by aggregating per-person state; and per-person behaviour is context-conditional on who else is present.*
12. **"Let the family function as it would at home; put the parents in charge of care" assumes the pattern is robust and will reproduce itself given room.** The research design bets that the pathology is not situational — that if you stop constraining the family, it will re-enact its own structure in front of you. → *The pattern is an attractor; it regenerates from the family's own dynamics rather than requiring specific triggering conditions.*
13. **"Read the action story separately from the verbal story" assumes two independent, routinely contradictory communication channels.** The verbal channel is "usually a direct contradiction to the action level," and the patient's compliance tracks the action channel while his argument tracks the verbal one. → *Messages need two independent fields; a receiver's response weights them differently depending on his functional strength.*
14. **"The silent families may provide more clues" assumes conflict is a surface phenomenon and not the mechanism.** Emotional process "operates during periods of conflict and periods of calm harmony." Conflict volume is not a proxy for process intensity. → *Do not model the mechanism as a function of observable conflict; conflict is one possible expression, and its absence does not indicate a low-intensity system.*
15. **"Track family change as a triad, expecting change in one member to be followed by change in the other two" assumes conservation within the triad.** The overadequate–inadequate concept was *derived* from watching this. → *The triad has a conserved quantity; one member cannot change position without the others' positions changing.*
16. **"Wait for the family to become comfortable before the mate-choice material is usable" assumes reported history is state-dependent.** The same facts are "fragmented and distorted" earlier. → *A family's self-report is a function of its current anxiety, not a fixed record. History is not a reliable read at high anxiety.*
17. **"The staff must not solve the problem when the family demands it" — and the finding that parents demanded exactly this — assumes the family will attempt to install the therapist in the overadequate position.** The overadequate–inadequate mechanism does not stop at the family boundary. → *The reciprocal-functioning machinery generalizes to any dyad the family forms, including with helpers and institutions.*
18. **[p2] "Read the mother's demand direction against the child's current state" assumes the enforcement is a controller, not a bias.** She infantilises the grown-up presentation and demands growth from the childish one. → *The parental input is a function of the* error *between the child's displayed maturity and a family setpoint, not a constant push. A model with a fixed downward pressure cannot produce this and will not generate the adolescent oscillation Bowen describes ("the growth process repeatedly upsets the equilibrium and the emotional process attempts to restore it").*
19. **[p2] "Expect the father to join when the mother's effort fails" assumes enforcement capacity is recruited on demand rather than distributed.** → *Peripherality is a resting state with a wake condition. The triad's effective pressure on the child is not the sum of two constant terms; it is stage one plus a conditional stage two.*
20. **[p2] "Both parents believe the attachment is caused by the father's disinterest, and they are wrong" assumes the family holds a model of itself that is separable from its actual dynamics.** → *A family-level belief layer distinct from ground truth, with a stated systematic bias: it attributes to the absent member's neglect what is produced by the incumbent member's hold. This is the same gap Ch09 later names as the family "obscuring and misremembering", present here in 1958 with the direction of the error specified.*

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **The 1954 hypothesis is explicitly superseded.** Original: schizophrenia is "a psychopathological entity in the patient which had been influenced to a principal degree by the mother," the basic character problem being an unresolved symbiotic attachment to the mother. After one year: "The psychosis in the patient was now considered to be a symptom of the total family problem." The unit of pathology moved from person to family. The symbiosis concept survives inside the new frame rather than being discarded.
- **Bowen reverses himself on siblings within this chapter.** "At the beginning of the study, I thought all the siblings were heavily involved in the family problem. With increasing experience, I now lean strongly to the belief that the essential process is confined in the father-mother-patient triad." He notes that history and superficial observation supported the earlier view, and that six months of observation of one family "tended to confirm" a father–younger-daughter attachment as strong as the mother–patient one — it took two more years to see that she could always withdraw. *An early-observation artifact that reversed with longer observation.*
- **The "rejecting mother" is rejected as a category, but the patients' testimony is preserved.** "We have not had a rejecting mother in our small number of research families. Every mother in our group has been 'called rejecting' by the patients." Bowen re-explains the phenomenon as anxiety-linked variation in attention. He offers a redefinition: "My impression of the real rejecting mother is one whose baby could never be part of her defense system for her own emotional needs, so that she has to desert the baby to find her gratification elsewhere."
- **"Projection" is used while being disowned.** Note 6 states the term does not describe the reciprocating introjection, and that even "projection-introjection" "does not account for all the essential aspects of this complex mechanism." The mechanism is retained; the label is declared inadequate.
- **"Maturity"/"immaturity" are used under protest.** "I do not like the terms 'maturity' and 'immaturity,' as used later in the paper, but I have used them descriptively."
- **The patient-as-victim reading is explicitly rejected.** "The patient participates so actively in this process that I do not see the patient as a 'victim' of the situation." This sits against the causal chain everywhere else in the chapter, in which the mother initiates and the child responds — Bowen keeps both.
- **Theory and operation are stated to be out of harmony.** "We may say that we regard psychosis as a family problem, but, in many basic ways, we must relate to it as a disease in the individual." An admitted, unresolved inconsistency in the research operation itself.
- **[p2] The two demand channels are described twice, incompatibly, and never reconciled.** Version A (the buttered bread): the two demands are *simultaneous* and *fixed in content* — action says stay a baby, words say grow up, "the verbal level is usually a direct contradiction to the action level." Version B (adolescence): the demands *alternate with the child's current state* — "When the child is more grown up, the mother infantilizes. When he is childish, she demands that he grow up." Version A is a standing double bind; version B is a servo. They are different mechanisms with different predictions, and Bowen asserts both flatly in the same chapter.
- **[p2] The helping relationship with the staff is scored both ways.** It removed a boundary problem and let the staff know the families better; it "created new technical problems for the treatment program." Bowen does not net the two out.
- **Emphasis shifts from conflict to the "silent" family.** After describing families full of "conflict and open disagreement and many stories of injustices and misdeeds," Bowen says the conflict-free families "may provide more clues to the process" — quietly demoting most of his own descriptive material as surface.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- **The whole family concept is framed as one more partial view.** The blind-men-and-the-elephant frame (Saxe 1949) is applied to Bowen's own work: "in the long run, the family concept is the perception of yet another 'blind man.'" And: "the family concept presented in this paper is based on psychological thinking."
- **The three-generation section is flagged as the weakest.** "This area remains as the one where thinking is most speculative and supporting data are weakest." The prediction is prefaced "According to the speculative three-generation idea" and then explicitly limited: "It is stressed that this is not a specific proposition about the origin of schizophrenia but that such a pattern has been present in several of the families." He also attributes the idea to Dr. Lewis Hill, who died before the paper was finished, and adds "I believe the three-generation idea as expressed here is a fairly accurate representation of his thinking."
- **Ward vs home observation is an open question he declines to answer.** "There have been discussions about how our observations made in the ward setting might differ from those obtained by an observer in the home. This is impossible to answer."
- **The constitutional-vs-functional question is left open with evidence acknowledged on both sides.** "There are valid data on both sides of this issue," with note 7 citing Bayley, Bell and Schaefer as investigating whether the character of the mother–infant relationship is determined by inherent qualities in the infant or by factors in the mother.
- **The fusion mechanism is admitted to be unexplained.** "The mechanisms by which either the mother or child can feel the same as the other, or 'be for the other,' are difficult to conceptualize. A number of possible explanations have been proposed in the literature."
- **"Emotional process" is a placeholder.** "Perhaps this can be understood as nonverbal communication, but I have chosen to use these terms descriptively." And "This 'emotional process' is deep and it seems somehow to be related to the being of a person."
- **The sample is repeatedly called small.** "our small number of research families."
- **Sample exclusions are stated.** "We have not worked with families with complicated family histories involving the death of a parent, divorces, remarriages, or multiple neuroses and psychoses in the same sibling group."
- **A "current working conceptualization," not a finished theory.** "The hypothesis and operational approach has been modified, to some degree, every year. In this sense, the family concept, as here presented, could be called our current working conceptualization of schizophrenia as a family problem."
- **Schizophrenia has no etiology in the medical sense under this frame.** "When schizophrenia is seen as a family problem, it is not a disease in terms of our usual way of thinking about disease, nor does it have an etiology in terms of the way those of us in the medical sciences have been trained to think of etiology. However, a family orientation does permit us to talk in terms of the origin and development of schizophrenia."
- **[p2] The chapter's two best pieces of evidence both come from already-changed families, and Bowen does not flag the selection.** The closeness seesaw is stated of families "who have done well in family therapy." The fusion account comes from a mother and daughter "far along in the process of untangling themselves from each other," where the mother had "never been consciously aware" of the thought-investment before. Both the headline law and the headline mechanism are visible only after movement has begun — which is a limit on the *observation channel*, not on the phenomenon, and it means the chapter carries no evidence about what these quantities look like in a family that never changed.
- **[p2] A stated observation-window artifact, with a number.** On the normal sibling: "observations during the first six months tended to confirm this. During the next two years the younger daughter sided with all three members of the basic family triad, but she was never involved to the point that she could not withdraw." Six months of observation gave the wrong answer; two and a half years gave the opposite one. Bowen offers this as the reason he reversed his own belief.
- **Convergence with other workers is claimed as terminological, not substantive.** Note 5: "In many cases the main difference is the use of different terms to describe the same phenomenon" — reciprocal functioning ≈ Wynne's pseudomutuality ≈ Jackson's complementarity.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The conflict-attenuating third party is stated in plain form, pre-theoretically.** "The conflict was less when a third or fourth person was present. Several couples spent much time visiting others or entertaining friends in their home. Marriage tensions would be reduced when one went to his or her parents for a visit." Three distinct anxiety-shedding routes: entertain a third party, visit others, return to one's own family of origin. No triangle terminology is used.
- **The pre-children period has its own oscillator with a described period.** Closeness → fight → distance ("a few hours or a few days") → one starts to make up → closeness. The father's account of what "too close" meant is a within-dyad reproduction of the overadequate–inadequate pattern: "I would start acting like a little boy and she would make demands like a bossy mother… I gave up part of me when I was helpless." And the distance phase is described as *functionally productive but personally costly*: "I did my best work when we were far apart… That is when I would get depressed and call myself names but I could somehow work better then." Bowen also records the pull back: closeness "was like a piece of bait I could not resist. I think it was the longing for closeness in both of us that made us respond so fast."
- **Mother's investment during pregnancy toggles with marital closeness in real time.** "When she was emotionally close to the husband, her thoughts were more invested in him and there was less preoccupation with the child. During periods of greatest distance from the husband, she wished she could abort." This makes the parental-closeness/patient-investment seesaw operative *before birth* — the same law that governs the patient's improvement later.
- **The intense maternal feeling at first sight of the child is a durable, encodable marker.** Three mothers reported it, retained "just as another person remembers the most striking emotional experience in his life," while the comparable feeling with other babies "was not particularly noted at the time." A potential per-child attribute set at a single moment.
- **The mother's content of worry is diagnostic of her own deficit — and the child comes to embody it.** Physical-organ worry → a son with multiple physical complaints; attractiveness worry → patients who "tended to be exaggerated examples of what the mothers had 'fought against'"; intellectual worry → patients who "appeared intellectually dull." The projection is *topic-specific* and produces topic-matched outcomes.
- **A neurosis/psychosis discriminator stated in terms of reality-boundary maintenance.** The good-voice case: the neurotic daughter played along at home "to make her mother feel better" and behaved realistically outside. "In a family with a psychosis, neither daughter nor mother could have recognized the boundary between reality and unreality and both would have acted out the good-voice myth in all their relationships." Severity here is context-discrimination capacity, not symptom count.
- **The self-boundary is location-dependent.** The daughter "away at school for long periods… could begin to have her own feelings. When she returned home she would again lose the ability to know her own feelings." Physical distance from the mother partially restores self-definition and it is lost again on return.
- **The "magnetic field" has two failure poles, not one.** Too close → "pulled into the mother" and identity lost; too far → "no 'self' at all." There is no stable position within the field, only a narrow band.
- **The intuition runs both ways.** The mother felt pain in the exact spot the daughter's head was injured; the daughter "described the same intuitive ability to know how the mother felt." Bowen does not present this as unidirectional projection.
- **The mother's own deliberate boundary move is described in the family's language:** "put an invisible wall between us so I can have my life and she can have hers" — an untangling move initiated by the mother, not by the therapist.
- **Patients are effective at recruiting help from anyone.** "Patients are adept at arousing sympathy and overhelpfulness in others." The mechanism is not confined to the mother.
- **Symmetric paralysis.** "The parents are as helpless in taking a stand against the patient as the patient in taking a stand against parents."
- **Anxious mothers give more attention; calm mothers give less.** The mother's attention output is an anxiety readout, and the child reads the drop as rejection. A stabilized mother will therefore be experienced by the patient as a rejecting one.
- **Two contrasting adolescent "solutions" in the parental generation** — adequacy attained *outside* the home (father) versus *inside* the home (mother) — with both producing outward competence, loneliness, and aloofness. And in both, the change at adolescence was described as *sudden*.
- **Neither future parent had been serious about marriage before they met**; on one level "a 'made only for each other' quality," on the surface "casual or even indifferent"; a year of casual relationship; then they "married suddenly, a few days before the husband was transferred to a job in another state." Relationship became conflictual "as soon as they began to live together."
- **The Freud citation Bowen uses for the mother's investment:** the narcissistic mother, in whose child "a part of their own body comes to them as an object other than themselves, upon which they can lavish out of their narcissism, complete object love" (Freud 1914).
- **A psychotic patient's stated wish maps onto the mother's own:** "I wish I could have a baby of my own… if I could ever have a baby of my own, I would never be lonely again." Bowen presents this as the same need one generation on.
- **The mother's inconsistency is justified by authority-shopping.** When most anxious, "she would force attention on the child and justify her actions by quoting an authority who recommended unlimited love for children. When she was not anxious, she would be relatively neglectful of the child and justify herself by quoting an authority who recommended firmness." The rationale is selected to fit the anxiety-driven behaviour.
- **Bowen states the epistemic goal as a unified concept of man** — "a frame of reference that will enable us to understand the necessary connections between cell and psyche, and perhaps between psyche and the entity we know as soul" — while conceding "we are far from capable of such thinking at this time."
- **A striking meta-claim about the field:** "more progress can be made in understanding why man thinks as he does about schizophrenia than in understanding why the schizophrenic patient thinks as he does."
- **And the closing statement of what changed:** "The schizophrenia did not change; the only change was in the eyes that saw it."
- **[p2] The family-of-origin visit is already an observed anxiety sink, seventeen years before it becomes the method.** "Marriage tensions would be reduced when one went to his or her parents for a visit." Recorded here as a spontaneous behaviour of the couple, with no theory attached and no suggestion that anyone should be sent there. Ch22 makes exactly this move the centre of the coaching programme.
- **[p2] Multigenerational pattern transfer stated as a resemblance, not as a mechanism.** The father, once the child stabilises the mother, "tended to establish a more fixed position of aloof distance from the mother, **similar to his relationship with his own mother**." The pattern he brings into the marriage is the pattern he held in his family of origin — asserted in a subordinate clause, with no concept behind it.
- **[p2] The chapter's one endorsed external predictive claim is somebody else's.** Note 4: "Caplan (1960) points out the shift in parental relationships during a pregnancy. He also suggests that the mother's relationship to the child can be predicted from her fantasies during the pregnancy." Bowen cites it approvingly against his own pregnancy case. It is the only forward prediction in the chapter with a named, dated, external source.
- **[p2] The most-important-threat ordering is the mother's, and it is a rival, not a rupture.** "The mother threatens the child in many ways. Most important is the threat that she might have another baby and desert the child." The chapter's other threat class is *change in the other* (aging, sickness, growth). This one is *a new occupant of the position* — structurally different, and it links directly to the which-child-is-selected list, where an actual second birth left the incumbent in place because "the oldest was so pitiful when the second was born."

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

Chronology note. The terminology timeline places this chapter at **1958**, after Ch01 (1957) and
after Ch02, and *before* Ch03 (~1960) despite book order. So its chronological predecessors are
**Ch01 and Ch02 only**. Everything below is judged against those two.

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

**P1.1 — Pass 1 missed the chapter's own definition of the quantity the whole model hangs on.**
This is the worst omission. Bowen defines investment, in the text, in one sentence: "The term
*intense* describes an ambivalent relationship in which the thoughts of both, whether positive or
negative, are largely invested in each other." Investment is **share of thought occupied by the
other, and it is valence-blind** — hostile preoccupation counts exactly as affectionate
preoccupation. Pass 1 recorded the seesaw, the pregnancy toggle and the "much time spent thinking
about the daughter" line as three separate observations without noticing they are three uses of one
defined quantity. Consequence: pass 1's `M` proposed the seesaw be implemented on an undefined
`investment` scalar. It is not undefined here.

**P1.2 — The seesaw's quantifier structure was flattened.** Pass 1's A3 #16 and its `M` entry read
the law as a difference, `investment(parent→spouse) − investment(parent→child)`. The text is a
**conjunction over both parents**: improvement when the parents are "more invested in each other
than **either** was in the patient"; regression when "**either** parent became more emotionally
invested in the patient than in the other parent." It is a min, not a sum — one parent alone drives
the regression, and averaging the two parents' differences would let a strongly-invested marriage
mask a defecting parent. Implemented as pass 1 wrote it, the rule is wrong.

**P1.3 — B2 manufactured prohibitions the chapter does not contain, and one of them is an
anachronism.** This chapter has almost no imperatives. "It has not been possible for a father to
have a primary relationship…" is an **impossibility**; "It is easy for the observer to become
preoccupied…" is an **ease claim**; "It is much easier to treat the compliant patient…" is an aside.
Pass 1 rendered all of them as rules. Worse, B2's "Do not solve the family's problem for them when
they demand it" reads Ch05's later finding (L05.2 — proximity of help is a load-bearing negative)
back into a 1958 passage that reports the **opposite sign first**: the family's demand meant "there
was never a problem with the staff's 'intruding into' or 'being excluded from' the family
situation," and the helping relationship "made it possible for them to know the families better
**but** it created new technical problems." In 1958 the sign of the helping relationship is
undetermined. The distinction is not pedantic for the model: an impossibility removes a move from
the repertoire, a prohibition leaves it available with a penalty.

**P1.4 — The father–child edge has two gates at two levels, issued by two different parties, and
pass 1 merged them into one.** One sentence apart: "he can have a close relationship to the child
**any time the mother permits it**" — versus — "it has not been possible for a father to have a
**primary** relationship with the patient until he has first been able to change his own emotional
divorce with the mother." Contact-level closeness is cheap and granted by the mother, and the father
who accepts it becomes "a representative of the mother." Primary-relationship *position* is gated by
the marital state and was never achieved in eleven families. The "most successful" of the six cases —
an afternoon a week for several years, the father as "the mother's hired attendant" — is a maximum on
the first axis scored as a failure on the second. Pass 1's B5 #5 and `M` treat this as a single
dependency ordering; it is two attributes with independent gates, and the practical consequence is
that a coach maximising father–child contact will produce a rising metric and no structural change.

**P1.5 — Pass 1's blanket "no scale points, percentages, or numeric thresholds of any kind" is too
strong.** The word "scale" appears once and it carries a distribution: the eleven families are
ordered from one extreme (formal, controlled, "ideal" marriage) to the other ("a terrible
twenty-five years"), with "In the middle of the scale were **nine** families" — a **1 / 9 / 1** split
over the whole sample. It is not a maturity scale and has no numbered positions; the axis is
emotional-divorce **style**, not level. But it is a stated ordering with a stated distribution, and
pass 1 denied its existence while quoting the sentence in A4.

**P1.6 — The two demand channels are described a second time, incompatibly, and pass 1 recorded only
the first version.** Version A, the buttered bread: simultaneous, fixed content, "the verbal level is
usually a direct contradiction to the action level" — a standing double bind. Version B, at
adolescence: "When the child is more grown up, the mother infantilizes. When he is childish, she
demands that he grow up" — a **negative-feedback controller** driven by the child's deviation from a
setpoint. These are different mechanisms with different predictions. Version B is the one that
generates the oscillation Bowen then describes ("the growth process repeatedly upsets the equilibrium
and the emotional process attempts to restore it"); a fixed downward pressure cannot.

**P1.7 — Missed: symptoms are gated by growth *rate*, not by the accumulated gap.** "The relationship
might remain fairly calm and erupt into separation anxiety symptoms only during periods of rapid
growth in the child." Pass 1 has the monotonically widening physical/psychological gap and it has
adolescence as a scheduled stressor, but not the general rule that the eruptions are episodic and
rate-driven. The gap sets vulnerability; d(growth)/dt triggers.

**P1.8 — Missed: the father is recruited on a stated trigger.** "If the mother's effort to 'make the
child behave' is not immediately successful, the father will add his weight to her effort." Pass 1's
A6 presents the father as uniformly peripheral. He is peripheral at rest and is stage two of a
two-stage enforcement, with the *failure* of stage one as the wake condition.

**P1.9 — Missed: the mother's threat of a new baby.** "The mother threatens the child in many ways.
Most important is the threat that she might have another baby and desert the child." Named as the most
important threat she poses, stated immediately before the which-child-is-selected list — and one of
those instances is a displacement that did not occur ("the oldest was so pitiful when the second was
born"). Pass 1's `event-kind:CHILD_SELECTION` entry has the list but not the threat that motivates it.

**P1.10 — Missed: stress as a global gain on everything described.** "The characteristics of the
relationships are most pronounced during periods of stress." One sentence, positioned as a qualifier
over the entire mother–child / child–mother / father section.

**P1.11 — Missed: a unanimous, wrong, family-held causal theory.** "The mothers see the attachment as
due to the fathers' disinterest in the child. **The fathers agree with this.**" Both parents, in all
eleven families, explain their own structure by the peripheral member's neglect — while Bowen's own
data put the failure on the mother's non-relinquishment. Pass 1 has neither the belief nor its
direction of error.

**P1.12 — Provenance: note 4 cites "Caplan (1960)."** Pass 1 dated the chapter from Hill's death in

## P2. What is NEW here relative to Ch01 and Ch02

- **The three-generation process** — first appearance in the corpus. Credited to Dr Lewis Hill (1955),
  self-flagged as the section "where thinking is most speculative and supporting data are weakest."
- **A per-person level with arithmetic, before any scale exists.** Assortative mating on "identical
  levels of immaturity but… opposite defense mechanisms"; the loaded child's immaturity "roughly
  equivalent to the **combined** levels of immaturity in the parents." Additive, not averaging — which
  is what makes the generational chain run. No scale, no units, no zero point.
- **"Emotional divorce"** as a named term, with a style taxonomy and a 1 / 9 / 1 distribution.
- **Overadequate–inadequate reciprocal functioning** as a named mechanism, with the "functioning states
  and not fixed states" qualifier, the sex-symmetric statement of both polarities, and the
  decision→pole assignment rule that makes decision paralysis derived rather than primary.
- **The interdependent triad, with a derivation of its stability.** Ch01 has no triad. Here the causal
  chain is given: the infant's *realistic* helplessness lets the mother hold overadequacy → her
  functioning stops fluctuating → the father can hold a fixed aloof distance → "the functional
  helplessness of the infant makes it possible for both parents to have a less anxious adjustment."
- **Somatic reciprocation** as a named mechanism — "the soma of one person reciprocates with the psyche
  of another" — with four named illnesses in one mother, each within hours of *improvement* in the
  patient.
- **The closeness seesaw in its sharpest corpus form, plus the management-technique nullity.** Close
  parents "could do no wrong"; divorced parents, "any and all 'management approaches' were equally
  unsuccessful."
- **The definition of intensity as valence-blind thought-investment** (P1.1).
- **"Peace at any price"** — a low family-level anxiety tolerance producing short-horizon choices that
  raise tomorrow's anxiety.
- **Functional vs constitutional helplessness**, stated as a position with the evidence conceded on both
  sides.
- **The family-unit view as non-additive** — a fourth perspective "different from any possible in any,
  or in all, individual perceptions."
- **The third or fourth person as a conflict attenuator**, stated pre-theoretically, with three named
  discharge routes: entertain others, visit others, **visit one's own parents**. The last is the
  family-of-origin move that becomes Ch22's entire method, observed here as spontaneous behaviour.
- **The two-channel demand aligned explicitly with Bateson's double bind.**
- **Pregnancy as a phase with its own investment toggle**, resolving at the sight of a live baby.
- **Topic-matched projection** — the mother's own deficit domain (organs / attractiveness / intellect)
  predicts the child's presenting domain.
- **Occupational and financial functioning as a symptom channel** — the father's bankruptcy within a
  year of the patient's break, described as reciprocal to the mother's rise and invisible to him. This
  is the "physical illness, emotional illness, or social dysfunction" triad of Ch07/Ch08 with a
  concrete social-dysfunction instance, in 1958.
- **"Interested detachment"** as a named, achieved, precondition state for the staff.

## P5. Assigned open questions

**Q-SEESAW — verified, and the quantity is defined in the chapter.**
Wording, verbatim: *"The striking observation was that when the parents were emotionally close, more
invested in each other than either was in the patient, the patient improved. When either parent became
more emotionally invested in the patient than in the other parent, the patient immediately and
automatically regressed."*
Four things the model needs, all from the text:
1. **What "invested" is.** Bowen defines it: *"The term intense describes an ambivalent relationship in
   which the thoughts of both, whether positive or negative, are largely invested in each other."*
   It is **share of thought occupied by the target**, and it is **valence-blind** — conflict-laden
   preoccupation is high investment, not low. It is not time spent, not stated affection, and not a
   behavioural rate.
2. **That it is allocated across targets.** Stated in one sentence during the pregnancy case: *"When she
   was emotionally close to the husband, her thoughts were more invested in him and there was less
   preoccupation with the child."* Increase toward one target, decrease toward the other, in the same
   clause. This is the corpus's source for treating investment as a budget (L02.2).
3. **Whose reading it is.** An **inference by the observers**, not a report by the subjects. It is
   introduced as "The striking observation" and stands on months-to-years of ward observation, with the
   couple's high-investment state characterised qualitatively — "for two months they were as completely
   invested in each other as two young lovers." No instrument, no rating, no procedure. Corroborating
   evidence that it is *not* self-reported: the untangling mother "began to notice how much time she
   spent in thinking about the daughter. **She had never been consciously aware of this before.**"
4. **Its logical form.** An **ordinal comparison per parent**, aggregated as a **conjunction**:
   improvement requires `invest(p→spouse) > invest(p→patient)` for *both* parents; regression follows if
   it flips for *either*. Not a difference, not an average (P1.2).
Two limits to carry with it: the observation is drawn from families "who have done well in family
therapy," and the improvement direction is stated only as "improved," while the regression direction
carries the time constant — "**immediately and automatically**."

**Q-GATED — the count is verified; the claim it is attached to is not the one pass 1 recorded.**
Verbatim: *"This was especially true in the six families with psychotic sons… All six fathers tried to
be close to their sons. Every effort failed."* Six families, six attempts, every effort failed — pass 1
is accurate on the numbers.
But the six failures are attributed **in the text** to the mother: *"The mother did not relinquish any
of her attachment to the son, and he never attended a scout meeting."* The emotional-divorce
precondition — *"In our experience it has not been possible for a father to have a primary relationship
with the patient until he has first been able to change his own emotional divorce with the mother"* —
is a separate sentence in the preceding paragraph, stated about the patient across all eleven families,
with **no case evidence attached to it at all**. Pass 1 (and ledger L04.5) welded the two together and
so presented the six failures as the corpus's hardest evidence for precondition-gating on the marital
tie. They are not. They are hard evidence for **permission-gating by the excluded third** — which is
Ch02's L02.1, from the same case series (P3.2).
The precondition claim itself survives as an **unevidenced impossibility claim**, and it is
distinguished from the permission gate by *level*: contact-level closeness is granted freely by the
mother; primary-relationship *position* is what has never been achieved. The "most successful" case
maximises the first and still counts as a failure of the second (P1.4). For the model this is stronger
than a single gate, not weaker — but it must not be implemented as one rule with six confirmations
behind it.

**Q-SOURCE — verified, with one correction.**
Verbatim: *"If a therapist or other outside figure suggests this, the mother and even the father and
patient will attack or withdraw, or both. However, if either the patient or father confronts the mother
with this, there is a significant beneficial emotional reaction."*
Confirmed: identical content, opposite outcome by speaker; and the outside version recruits the whole
family against the speaker, including the two members it would benefit ("**even** the father and
patient"). Corrections: the content at issue is specific — that the mother's complaints about the
patient are externalisations of her own inadequacies — not "projection" generically. The verbs are
asymmetric: outsider "suggests," insider "confronts," so the milder act from outside is worse than the
stronger act from inside (P1.13). And the insider outcome is stated as a "beneficial emotional
**reaction**," not a stated improvement in the patient or the family.

**Q-VALIDATION — no. The chapter offers no external validation and no measurement procedure for any
quantity.**
What it does offer, and none of it is validation:
- A **data-capture apparatus**, described in detail: nurses recording per eight-hour shift on each
  individual, on the family unit, and on the family's relation to the environment; every therapy hour
  tape-recorded plus process notes, a verbal-content summary and a **sociogram**; daily → weekly →
  monthly rollups. Instrumentation, not measurement of a construct.
- One **attempt at an operationalisation by someone else**: "Dysinger (1957) made an attempt to isolate
  the action story in one of the research families." Reported as an attempt; no result given.
- One **ordinal ordering with a distribution**: the 1 / 9 / 1 spread of the eleven families across
  emotional-divorce style (P1.5). No numbered positions, no threshold.
- **Inter-investigator agreement claimed as terminological**: note 5 says the findings are "in close
  agreement" with Lidz, Bateson, Wynne and others, and that "in many cases the main difference is the
  use of different terms to describe the same phenomenon." Convergent vocabulary, explicitly not an
  independent measurement.
- An **acknowledged open empirical question owned by others**: note 7, Bayley/Bell/Schaefer on whether
  the mother–infant relationship is determined by the infant or the mother.
On the differentiation quantity specifically: "maturity"/"immaturity" are used **under protest** ("I do
not like the terms… but I have used them descriptively"), with no scale, no zero point, no bounds and
no procedure — while simultaneously being treated as **additive across a marriage**. A quantity with
arithmetic and no measurement.

**Q-MATERIAL — no per-person material or financial resource variable. Money and occupation appear only
as readouts of functional position.**
Every instance is an *output*, never a constraint on action:
- The father's business is the marker of the second generation's "adequacy attained outside the home"
  — "He became more successful in business than his siblings and colleagues."
- The patient's father: "After a difficult few years, the father did well in his own business… The
  father's business failed during the year after the daughter became psychotic," and, in the analytic
  passage, "His business began to fail. He went into bankruptcy within a year without any awareness that
  his bankruptcy could have any relation to the functional interdependency of the central family triad."
  Financial collapse as the *reciprocal* of the mother's rise, on a slower timescale and in a different
  domain from the patient's symptom.
- Grandparental standing: "highly respected members of the farming community"; "a respected professional
  man in a small town." Social position as background, never as an input to any mechanism.
- The one place money is spoken by a family member, it is described as pretext: the husband "opposed
  passively with comments about money and the right time. **His opposition obscured her fears** that she
  might not be able to have a normal baby."
So: occupational/financial functioning belongs in the model as a **third symptom channel** alongside the
psychiatric and the somatic — which is what it becomes in Ch07/Ch08's "physical illness, emotional
illness, or social dysfunction." There is no per-person stock, no budget, and no cost attached to any
move anywhere in the chapter.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Symptom belongs to the family organism, patient is the expression channel → `Family` → family-level state must be able to carry a disturbance quantity that is *expressed through* a designated Person rather than owned by one.
- Emotional divorce present in 11/11 families, with a range of styles (formal-controlled ↔ overtly conflictual, physical distance) → `Relationship` → marital distance is a persistent scalar with a *style* attribute; distance can be maintained by conflict or by formality, and the two are equivalent for outcome purposes.
- Overadequate–inadequate reciprocal functioning; explicitly functioning states, not fixed → `Relationship` → a signed position variable on a dyad, zero-sum, with no stable midground; each partner's functioning level is a *function of* the other's, updated per interaction.
- Interdependent triad with the child as keystone; change in one member followed by change in the other two → `Triangle` → the father–mother–child triad needs a conserved-quantity constraint so a position change in one node forces compensating changes in the other two.
- Parental closeness ↔ patient functioning seesaw ("when either parent became more emotionally invested in the patient than in the other parent, the patient immediately and automatically regressed") → `Triangle` + `Person` → make the patient's functioning a direct function of `investment(parent→spouse) − investment(parent→child)`, with a fast time constant ("immediately and automatically").
- Management approach has zero independent effect; effective only as a function of parental closeness → `test` → an adversarial test: any intervention applied to the symptom-bearer while marital distance is high must produce no improvement, regardless of intervention type.
- Assortative mating on equal immaturity with opposite defenses → `Person` + `event-kind:MARRIAGE` → mate selection should pair on equal level, opposite polarity of the overadequate/inadequate defense.
- Child's immaturity "roughly equivalent to the combined levels of immaturity in the parents" → `Person` → an *additive* (not averaging) inheritance rule for the selected child. Note this makes the quantity grow across generations, which is the engine of the three-generation claim.
- Three-or-more generations to develop; one child per sibling group absorbs the load while the others are "much more mature" → `Family` + `test` → a multigenerational test: run 3 generations, assert exactly one child per sibling group carries the elevated value and that its siblings' values do not rise.
- Selection of which child (first, youngest, middle, resembles mother, born near a grandparent death, physically deformed) "determined by her unconscious functioning in the prevailing reality situation" → `event-kind:CHILD_SELECTION` → **partly `unmodelled`.** Bowen gives no rule, only a list of instances. Needs a selection function keyed on family state at conception/birth, including a *death in the previous generation* as a trigger.
- Projection: a feeling in the mother becomes a reality in the child, and the *content* matches the mother's own felt inadequacy → `move:PROJECT` + `Person` → the projection needs a topic field drawn from the projector's own deficit, and it must write a real state change into the receiver, not a label.
- Somatic reciprocation: anxiety in one becomes physical illness in another, within hours; triggered by *improvement* in the other → `event-kind:SOMATIC_RECIPROCATION` → **`unmodelled`** in most designs. A channel by which one node's anxiety discharges as another node's illness, including in reverse direction on improvement.
- Two-channel demand (action/emotional vs verbal), routinely contradictory; receiver weights the channels by his own functional strength → `move:DEMAND` → messages need two fields; low-functioning receivers comply with the action channel and ignore the verbal, high-functioning receivers argue with the verbal channel and can resist the action one.
- Resisting an emotional demand ("I refuse to let you get me upset") makes the demander overtly anxious or physically ill → `move:REFUSE_DEMAND` → a defined move with a cost paid by the *other* party; available only above a functional-strength threshold.
- Anxiety in the mother is "an automatic signal for the patient 'to help the mother' by becoming her baby"; contact reduces her anxiety and increases his regression → `move:ABSORB` → an explicit anxiety-transfer move triggered by the other's anxiety level, not by the actor's own state.
- Mother's attention output tracks her anxiety; the patient reads the drop as rejection → `Person` + `Relationship` → attention is an anxiety readout, and the *perceived* relationship quality must be derived from the rate of change of attention, not its level. A stabilizing mother is perceived as rejecting.
- Decision-making assigns positions: whoever decides becomes overadequate, so decisions are avoided; under intense paralysis the mother decides against passive resistance → `move:DECIDE` → decisions carry a position cost, which makes avoidance rational for the agent; add a paralysis threshold above which the overadequate party takes the decision unilaterally.
- Two-person joint activity raises conflict; a third or fourth person lowers it → `Triangle` + `move:TRIANGLE_IN` → the third party as an anxiety sink is present here without the word. Also gives three specific outlet moves: entertain others, visit others, visit family of origin.
- Closeness–fight–distance cycle with a stated period ("a few hours or a few days") and a stated pull back into closeness → `Relationship` → the marital dyad needs an oscillator, not a static distance; and distance carries a productivity bonus with a mood penalty ("I did my best work when we were far apart… I would get depressed").
- Peace at any price: anxiety relieved now at the cost of higher anxiety tomorrow → `Family` → a discount-rate parameter on anxiety handling; low anxiety tolerance produces short-horizon choices that raise future anxiety.
- Arrested psychological growth against normal physical growth, gap widening each year → `Person` → two separate age variables per person; the gap is the vulnerability quantity, and it grows monotonically under the symbiotic condition.
- Symbiosis destabilized by biological growth; both parties threatened by change in the other; loss "equated with death" → `Relationship` → an equilibrium that is *inherently* unstable over time because one of its inputs (the child's age) advances unconditionally.
- Adolescence as the destabilizing event; anxiety rises in all three; parents push toward helplessness while verbally demanding growth → `event-kind:ADOLESCENCE` → a scheduled stressor on the triad that inverts the two demand channels.
- Trajectory: helpless child → poorly functioning young adult → helpless patient; first separation attempt often succeeds before collapse → `Person` → a false-recovery phase must exist; a successful first separation must not clear the vulnerability flag.
- Collapse on sustained separation, over "a period of a few days," while the coping strategy (denial, redoubled effort) is still escalating → `event-kind:PSYCHOTIC_COLLAPSE` → collapse is fast relative to everything else in the model and occurs *during* maximal effort, not during withdrawal.
- Reciprocal collapse of the father's functioning after the patient's break (bankruptcy within a year) → `Person` + `Family` → symptom relocation to a different member, in a different domain (occupational), on a slower timescale.
- Spectrum of unresolved attachment with alternative expressions (mother substitutes, somatic illness, character-neurotic flight, distant relationships) → `Person` → outcome should be a graded severity variable with multiple discrete expression channels, psychosis being the highest-intensity failed resolution.
- Father can only reach the child via the mother's permission; harassing the mother buys access; the father remains "a representative of the mother" → `Triangle` → edges are gated: the father–child edge's availability is a function of the mother–child and mother–father edges.
- Father must change the emotional divorce *before* a primary father–child relationship is possible (6/6 attempts failed) → `coach` + `test` → a hard precondition in the coaching agent; a test asserting that a father-child bonding move applied while marital distance is high produces no change.
- Therapist naming the projection → whole family attacks or withdraws; father or patient naming it → beneficial reaction → `coach` + `move:CONFRONT` → the same move must carry different outcomes by sender position. A coach's direct interpretation is a negative-value action.
- The therapist must attain "interested detachment" or be absorbed into the drama; training in countertransference is insufficient → `coach` → the coach is a node in the field with its own anxiety state and can lose neutrality; neutrality is a maintained resource, not a constant.
- Every member solicits outside support for his own view; parents demand the staff solve the problem → `coach` → recruitment pressure on the coach as a standing per-turn force; the family will attempt to install the coach in the overadequate position.
- Diagnosis/prescription completes the anxiety→symptom transfer within hours → `event-kind:MEDICAL_VALIDATION` → **`unmodelled`.** An external actor that stabilizes symptom assignment. Worth modelling as a hazard of intervention.
- Therapy sequence: mother–patient conflict (first several months) → father participates → conflict shifts to mother–father → father takes stands → mother's anxiety spikes → father assumes head-of-family → mother changes in a few days → emotional divorce disappears (two months of mutual investment) → patient tries to recover the symbiotic partner → mother holds → patient improves → `coach` → a staged plan with an explicit patient-side counter-move after the parents reorganize. The patient's attempt to restore the old triad must be modelled or the sequence looks monotone.
- Relapse under stress with a permanently lowered cost of reassuming the new position → `Person` + `Relationship` → hysteresis: state reverts, but the transition cost does not.
- Normal siblings and in-laws enter the conflict and always withdraw; essential process confined to the triad → `Family` + `test` → peripheral members need an exit capability that the triad members lack; a test asserting no sibling becomes permanently fixed in the triad.
- Normal sibling's contrasting profile (outgoing, successful "with minimal effort") vs patient's (few friends, comfortable with adults, lost among children) → `Person` → the differential is large and observable pre-symptomatically; a useful validation target.
- Emotional process operates in conflict *and* in calm harmony; "silent" families with schizophrenic members and no conflict history → `test` → **an adversarial test:** a family with near-zero observable conflict must still be able to reach high process intensity and produce a symptomatic member. Conflict level must not be usable as a proxy for the underlying quantity.
- Self-boundary is location-dependent: away at school she regains her own feelings, returns home and loses them → `Person` → differentiation-of-feeling as a *context-conditional* readout, restored by physical distance and lost on re-entry.
- "Magnetic field" with two failure poles — engulfment when too close, no self at all when too far → `Relationship` → a non-monotone distance function; there is no safe distance, only a narrow band, and both extremes degrade the person.
- Fusion is bidirectional (mother feels daughter's injury; daughter reads mother equally well) → `Relationship` → the fusion channel is symmetric even where the projection is asymmetric.
- Neurosis/psychosis discriminator = ability to maintain the reality boundary between home and outside → `Person` → severity as loss of context-discrimination, not as symptom count.
- Mother's investment in the fetus toggles with marital closeness *during pregnancy* → `event-kind:PREGNANCY` → the parental-closeness/child-investment seesaw is active before birth; the prenatal period sets an initial condition for the child.
- Intense first-sight maternal feeling, remembered for life, present in 3 mothers for the child who became psychotic and absent for the others → `Person` → a per-child bonding-intensity attribute set at a single birth event, durable, and predictive.
- Mother's authority-shopping to justify anxiety-driven inconsistency → `unmodelled` → rationalization as a distinct output that has no effect on state but blocks self-correction. No obvious home in the model.
- Excluded family structures (parental death, divorce, remarriage, multiple psychoses in one sibling group) → `test` → an explicit out-of-sample note: the chapter's claims were never checked against these, so the model should not treat them as validated regions.
- "Maturity/immaturity" used under protest as an anti-individual placeholder → `Person` → the variable exists here with no scale, no zero point, and no measurement procedure. Any numeric differentiation scale in the model is *not* sourced from this chapter.

### [p2] Revised and added

- **[p2] `investment` is defined, and the definition changes its implementation.** Share of thought
  occupied by a target, **valence-blind**, allocated across targets → `Person` → a per-person
  allocation vector over targets, summing to a budget. Conflict must *raise* the investment on a tie,
  not lower it — a hostile preoccupied dyad is a high-investment dyad. Any implementation that derives
  investment from affection, warmth or agreement inverts the sign on exactly the families this chapter
  is about, and it is the quantity the seesaw reads.
- **[p2] The seesaw is a conjunction over parents, not a difference.** Supersedes pass 1's
  `child_state ~ f(investment(p→spouse) − investment(p→child))` → `Family` + `test` →
  `improving iff ∀p ∈ parents: invest(p→spouse) > invest(p→patient)`; `regressing iff ∃p: invest(p→patient) > invest(p→spouse)`.
  A test that fixes one parent at high spousal investment and flips the other must produce regression.
- **[p2] The father–child edge needs two attributes with independent gates.** `contact` gated by the
  mother's permission (freely granted, and accepting it assigns the father the substitute-mother
  position); `position` gated by the marital state → `Relationship`, `Triangle`, `test` → an adversarial
