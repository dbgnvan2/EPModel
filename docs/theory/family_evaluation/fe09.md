# FE09 — Symptom Development

**Citation rule:** cite `FE09`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person; cites Engel, Scheflen, LeShan and Carlsson as
other researchers. `[K]`.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE09.1 — The **five-variable constellation**, and it resolves `FE08.3` into three levels `[K]` `[T]`

The chapter's own closing summary of what determines symptom development:

> "**degree of adaptiveness, level of chronic anxiety, type of mechanisms for binding anxiety in a
> nuclear family, stressful events in the nuclear and/or extended family systems, and degree of
> emotional cutoff from extended family**"

> "**When a constellation of emotional variables… makes a situation 'ripe' for a symptom, any category
> or type of symptom can develop or not develop. The basic emotional processes that contribute to an
> affair can be identical to those that contribute to obesity or to alcoholism. The constellation of
> emotional variables that culminate in acute physical illness can be identical to those that
> culminate in acute psychotic illness.**"

*Model consequence:* **a complete input list for `M7.D.1`'s symptom threshold — and, read with
`FE08.3`, a clean three-level decomposition that dissolves an apparent conflict.**

| Level | Determines | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| **Ripeness** — whether a symptom develops at all | the five-variable constellation | `M1.A.4` level, `M1.A.7a` anxiety, `M1.D.1` sink allocation, `M1.F.6` stressors, cutoff |
| **Category** — physical / emotional / social | learned childhood programming and what the family attends to | `FE08.3`, `FE07`'s "what others focus on" |
| **Specific symptom** within the category | constitution | `FE08.3`, `M1.A.11b` |

`FE09.1` says explicitly that the constellation does **not** pick the category — "any category or
type of symptom can develop". `FE08.3` says learning picks the category and genes pick the symptom.
Those are three different questions with three different answers, and together they are consistent.
**This makes `FE08.3`'s challenge to `M1.A.11b` sharper rather than softer:** the spec assigns the
*category* to constitution, and both chapters of this book assign it to learning.

Four of the five variables map onto quantities the spec already carries. The fifth — **degree of
emotional cutoff from the extended family** — is an *input to the symptom threshold*, not merely a
tie state, and `M7.D.1` does not read it. That is `M1.D.8`'s requirement given a consequence, and it
is the same term `FE08.1` names as quantum-jump condition (2).

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE09.2 — Two event types — and the model has only one `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The event may be the addition of something new that has to be dealt with or the loss of
> something old that was relied on. Both types of events can increase anxiety in the system: the
> first by giving the system more anxiety to manage, the second by depriving the system of an old way
> of managing its inherent anxiety.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.C.1a` covers the first; nothing covers the second.**

The spec models a new tie as a **redistribution of a conserved investment budget** (`M7.C.1a`) —
that is *addition*. `FE09.2` requires a second, structurally different event: **a binding mechanism
becomes unavailable**, which raises anxiety without adding any load at all.

`FE08.6`'s retirement case is exactly this — the distance mechanism was withdrawn by circumstance
and the previously bound anxiety "spilled over" into symptoms — and `M1.D.2a` already requires that
anxiety return to the person "if distancing is prevented". `FE09.2` generalises: **any** binder can
be removed, not only distance, and removal is an event type in its own right. **Requirement
candidate**, and it costs nothing new — the machinery is `M6.I.6`'s conservation with a channel
closed.

It also gives `M11.C.11` a second arm: removal of a *member* and removal of a *mechanism* should
produce comparable disturbance, which is a non-obvious prediction.

## ⭐⭐ FE09.3 — Physical distance is **not** emotional distance — and neither is contact count `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The amount of physical distance that exists between people and their families of origin is not
> equivalent to the amount of emotional distance that exists. People who live several thousand miles
> from their families may be in better emotional contact with them than people who live in the same
> town, on the same street, or in the same house as their family of origin. In addition, the number of
> contacts people have with family does not necessarily reflect the degree of cutoff. Contacts may be
> frequent, but highly ritualized.**"

And the full 2×2, which is the valuable part:

> Certain extended family systems are "**explosive**"… **In the better differentiated 'explosive'
> families, people move apart in the process of pursuing life goals. Though physically distant, they
> maintain good emotional contact. In the poorly differentiated 'explosive' families, people are
> getting away from one another. They are not only physically distant, but they are out of contact
> emotionally.** Other extended families are "**cohesive**"… **In the better differentiated 'cohesive'
> families, people are fairly involved in one another's lives and, for the most part, in comfortable
> emotional contact. In the poorly differentiated 'cohesive' families, people may interact with one
> another regularly, but the level of emotional reactivity is often sufficiently high that people are
> isolated emotionally even though they are in contact physically.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.B.2` and `M1.B.3` in written primary text, plus a complete 2×2 that makes
the readout trap exhaustive.**

|  | **Well differentiated** | **Poorly differentiated** |
|---|---|---|
| **Explosive** (physically dispersed) | distant, **in contact** — moved *toward goals* | distant, **out of contact** — moved *away from problems* |
| **Cohesive** (physically close) | close, **in contact** | close, **isolated emotionally** |

`M11.F.6`'s trap list already contains *geographic distance*; this gives all four cells and shows the
trap runs in both directions — proximity misleads as badly as dispersion. It is also a
**test-design** constraint: no `M11.C` criterion may use physical arrangement as a proxy for tie
state.

Two additions to `M1.B.3`, which currently requires four discriminable tie states (cut off, distant,
resolved low-contact, open conflict):

- **"frequent but highly ritualized"** is a fifth state — many events, low content, high bond
  energy — and it is exactly the state a contact-count readout would score as healthy.
- The explosive/well cell is `M1.F.1a`'s channel distinction at the level of a **life move**: *moving
  toward a goal* versus *running away from a problem*, the same act with opposite drivers. Third
  independent instance (`KS13.5`, `FE08`, `FE09.3`), and it confirms that the event record must carry
  the channel because the act itself is uninformative.

## ⭐⭐ FE09.4 — Cutoff and distance are **the same mechanism**, separated for emphasis `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 10:

> "**Emotional cutoff is not one of the original six concepts in family systems theory. It was added in
> the 1970s along with societal emotional process. Cutoff between the generations was originally
> described by the concept of emotional distance. Cutoff and distance mean the same thing. Cutoff was
> made a separate concept in the theory to emphasize its importance for explaining the intensity of
> the emotional process in a nuclear family and its importance in the conduct of family
> psychotherapy.**"

*Model consequence:* **worth recording against `M5.A.1`, which lists `DISTANCE` and `CUTOFF` as two
of the nine core moves.**

The source says they are one mechanism at two intensities, and that the split is **pedagogical**.
Under `M10.A.2` — the least number of pieces that will hang together — that is an argument for one
move with a magnitude rather than two moves.

**Recommendation: keep both, and say why.** `M11.C.4` requires `CUTOFF` to have a distinct temporal
signature (acute anxiety drops immediately, cost deferred to the next nodal event) that `DISTANCE`
does not, and `M1.D.2a` puts distance outside the symptom budget while cutoff is inside `M6.I.1`'s
three sinks by way of its consequences. Those are behavioural differences the model needs. But the
spec should state that the separation is the *model's* choice for testability, not a claim that the
corpus distinguishes two mechanisms — otherwise it reads as sourced and is not.

## ⭐⭐ FE09.5 — Cutoff's sign **flips with stress** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Cutoff can reduce anxiety and should not be regarded as 'good' or 'bad.' However, while people and
> nuclear families can reduce anxiety by cutting off, people and nuclear families can also increase
> anxiety by cutting off.** … **So while staying away from one's family can reduce anxiety, particularly
> when one is not under much personal stress, loss of viable emotional contact with the family of
> origin can also increase anxiety, particularly when one is under personal stress.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.4`'s now-against-later trade with a *different* conditioning variable,
and it is a better one.**

The criterion currently defers the cost of `CUTOFF` to the **next nodal event**. `FE09.5` states the
condition more generally: the sign of cutoff's effect on anxiety is **negative under low personal
stress and positive under high**. A nodal event is one way to raise stress; it is not the only way,
and conditioning on it makes the criterion narrower than the claim.

**Sharpen `M11.C.4`:** assert the sign flip across a *stress* manipulation rather than only across a
nodal event. That is a two-arm direction test in `M0.4`'s shape and it subsumes the current form.

## ⭐⭐ FE09.6 — Without the systems frame there are **two options**; with it, three `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Declaring one's 'independence' from family is not differentiation of self. It does not resolve
> one's emotional fusion with the past. Most people who claim to be 'independent' of their families
> have 'broken away' from them rather than 'grown away' from them.**"

> "**If one does not see himself as part of the system, his only options are either to try to get others
> to change or to withdraw. If one sees himself as part of the system, he has a new option: to stay in
> contact with others and change self. This does not mean changing self under pressure from the
> togetherness; it means changing self based on a process that comes from within the person.**"

*Model consequence:* **the sharpest statement of `M4.D.3b`'s gate, and it appears to threaten
`M1.A.18c` — but the spec already implements it correctly.**

`M1.A.18` says the perspective is "**graded, not a hard gate**", on the grounds that *no source says a
person with no systems perspective is incapable of a genuine differentiating move.* `FE09.6` comes
closer to saying that than anything else in the corpus: without the frame, the repertoire is
**two** options — change others, or withdraw.

**The reconciliation is already in the spec and should be stated explicitly.** `M5.F.4` requires an
`I-POSITION` selected at low `systems_perspective` to execute as the **assertion form** — which *is*
"try to get others to change", option one. So the third option is not blocked; **it degrades into the
first.** The observable is exactly `FE09.6`'s two options, produced without a hard gate. That is a
good outcome for the design and worth recording at `M1.A.18` as the reason the graded form is right.

The passage also gives the criterion for "grown away", and it is `FE01.5`/`FE03.4`'s two-sided
attribution: "**More objectivity means one is better able to see the ways in which he is part of the
system — the ways in which he affects the emotional functioning of others and the ways others affect
his emotional functioning.**"

## ⭐⭐ FE09.7 — The two cutoff postures need **opposite corrections** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**For some people, being less cut off requires more ability to be responsible for themselves (less
> of a child in their families). For other people, being less cut off requires more ability not to be
> responsible for the emotional functioning of others (less of a 'know-it-all' in their families).**
> Many people have aspects of both postures."

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.9a`'s two axes determining the *correction*, not only the diagnosis.**

`M5.F.2b` requires the counterfeit detector to report **which axis failed**, on the grounds that the
forceful declarer and the compliant accommodator require opposite corrections. `FE09.7` states those
corrections concretely for `REDUCE_CUTOFF` (`M5.B.3`): assume more responsibility for self (inward
axis) or assume less for others (outward axis).

That makes the move **axis-parameterised** rather than uniform, which the spec does not currently
require — `M5.B.3` treats `REDUCE_CUTOFF` as a single operation. **Requirement candidate**, and it is
cheap: the axis is already computed for `M5.F.2b`.

Related, and it is `M5.F.2a` again: "**People rarely reduce cutoff by being 'more honest' with their
families or by 'confronting' them. When people want to 'get it all out on the table,' they are usually
focusing on deficiencies they perceive in others.**" That is `M8.8`'s distinction — announcing a
specific act to the party in that dyad is permitted; announcing the programme carries a penalty —
with the counterfeit form named.

## ⭐⭐ FE09.8 — Treating one level **reinforces** the levels ignored — three worked instances `[K]` `[T]`

> "when a therapist treats a child in individual psychotherapy, he may relieve the child's symptoms,
> but **reinforce the family projection process. A family often relaxes when a child gets into therapy.
> The child's functioning usually improves when family anxiety goes down, but he is viewed even more
> strongly as the one who has problems.**"

> "If a pediatrician concentrates on treating a child's chronic asthma with medication, he may also
> relieve the symptoms, but **reinforce the child's position as a repository of family anxiety.**"

> "When a patient with systemic lupus erythematosus is treated with steroids and her spouse told she
> should 'avoid stress,' **this may encourage the patient's spouse to overfunction even more**… **The
> suggestion has encouraged a family that is 'drowning' in togetherness to 'solve' its problems with
> even more togetherness.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.13`'s help-relocates result with a mechanism the spec does not carry:
the intervention writes the belief.**

`M9.3` requires institutional acts to write the who-is-sick belief with hysteresis that verbal denial
cannot reverse. `FE09.8` says the *therapeutic* act does the same, and that this is how the relief
becomes iatrogenic: symptoms fall, and **the attribution hardens**. That is `M1.E.4`'s
certify-a-defect capability fired as a side effect of `M1.E`'s ordinary work, and it is why
`M11.C.13` must score **location** as well as count — the location here is in `M9`, not in a person.

The lupus instance is the cleanest: a piece of advice that is medically reasonable produces more of
exactly the process generating the symptom. That is a good `M11` fixture because the intervention is
benign by construction.

## ⭐ FE09.9 — The emotional field defined as a **restoring force**, with membership by significance `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 5:

> "A family '**emotional field**' is analogous to a chemical '**structure**.' **A structure is said to exist
> when any movement by a component of the structure automatically triggers a response by the structure
> to restore the component to its previous position.** … **When someone gets 'too close,' he is pushed
> back; when someone gets 'too far away,' he is pulled back.** … **An emotional field includes all the
> members of a group who significantly affect one another emotionally. It can include nonfamily members
> as well as family members. It usually reaches beyond the nuclear family into the extended family.**"

*Model consequence:* two requirements the spec has, confirmed in primary text:

- **`M1.A.12`** — membership is **derived from a threshold over `involvement_weight`** and **MUST NOT**
  be a stored set. `FE09.9` defines the field's boundary by emotional significance, explicitly
  including non-family members. That is the same rule.
- **`FE03.3`'s controller** with a signed error and a restoring response, stated a third time.

Note the physical analogy again — a chemical structure — which with `FE02.18`'s gravity image makes
two, and both sit against `FE00.1`/`FE01.14`'s objection to physics-derived models. Kerr uses them
illustratively; `M11.F.3` should note that the corpus reaches for physical analogies at exactly its
central concepts.

## ⭐ FE09.10 — Bridging a cutoff can remit symptoms — with the **transient-worse-first** signature `[K]` `[T]`

> "**People who are significantly cut off from their families of origin and who develop serious symptoms
> may get complete or at least partial remission of those symptoms based on reestablishing contact with
> the family. Reestablishing contact is not a matter of a few phone calls to parents or a few trips
> home. It is based on a consistent effort made over a long period of time.**"

Footnote 12:

> "**A person's effort to work on his functioning in relationship to his family of origin will initially
> disturb the balance of emotional forces in his nuclear family. This may result in a short-term
> exacerbation of symptoms in himself or others, but it will lead to a long-term reduction in
> symptoms.**"

*Model consequence:* `M5.B.3` (`REDUCE_CUTOFF` lowers anxiety, does not raise `basic_level`) and
`M7.A.2b` (the family of origin is the privileged peripheral system), both confirmed. And footnote 12
is **`FE05.2`'s discriminator for a third time**: a genuine move produces a **transient** worsening
that resolves, and the transience is the signature.

Three statements now carry it — `FE05.2` (footnote 11 of Ch5), `FE07.10` (the migraine sequence),
`FE09.10` — which is enough to make `M5.E.5`'s third-person symptom carry a required **time course**
rather than only a possibility.

Footnote 11 adds the honest failure case, which the model should be able to produce: some people
return and are **not** welcomed, or function worse; "**A family may have the same reasons for
rejecting someone when he attempts to return that it had when it rejected him in the first place.**"

## ⭐ FE09.11 — The family of origin is easier **because the dependence has ended** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Changing oneself while in relationship to the past is a 'highroad' to increasing basic level of
> differentiation. One's parents are the 'original' transference relationships. In addition, it is
> easier to gain emotional objectivity in relationship to people one no longer lives with or depends on
> financially than it is in relationship to one's present nuclear family.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.15`'s `financially_dependent` gate appears for a fourth time, and here as
an *enabler*.**

The spec uses it as a hard gate on `I-POSITION` (`M5.C.1`), `FE04.9` extends it to a precondition on
basic-level change, `FE06.14`'s "cut out of the will" gives the threat its teeth, and `FE09.11` gives
the positive form: **objectivity is available on a tie precisely where material dependence has
ended.** One flag, four uses — good evidence it is the right variable.

**One tension worth recording.** `M7.A.2b` justifies the family of origin's privileged status by
citing `KS06.8` — it is the **highest-load** context, so gains there carry the most evidential weight.
`FE09.11` justifies it by the opposite property — it is **easier** because dependence has ended. Both
can hold (high emotional load, low material dependence) and `M1.A.18d`'s per-tie attenuation is where
they meet, but the spec currently gives only one of the two reasons.

## ⭐ FE09.12 — The **"joined" spouse** becomes a follower `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A person who cuts off from his family of origin and 'joins' his spouse's family often becomes an
> 'emotional appendage' of the spouse's family. He is usually in the position of being a follower (a
> reactor to what transpires) rather than a leader (an initiator of what transpires).**"

*Model consequence:* a structural consequence of **asymmetric** cutoff that the model can produce and
that nothing currently asserts. Two arms differing only in whether one spouse is cut off from their
family of origin: the cut-off spouse must occupy a reactor position — lower `I-POSITION` selection
rate, higher share of responses to others' emissions — with **no change to either spouse's
`basic_level`**.

That is a clean `M11` candidate, it exercises `M1.D.8` and `M2.3` directly, and it is the first place
the corpus states a *positional* consequence of cutoff rather than an anxiety consequence.

## ⭐ FE09.13 — The acute/chronic fork is decided by **whether the focus binds** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**the major stress people usually experience following or in anticipation of an event is not the
> event itself, but an anxiety-generated disturbance in the balance of emotional forces in a family
> system.** … **people usually do not get isolated or overwhelmed by events, but by a relationship
> process that is set in motion by the events.** … **If family anxiety soon returns to 'pre-event' levels,
> the symptom will usually be short-lived. However, if family anxiety becomes bound in a chronic focus
> on the symptomatic one, the dysfunction will become chronic.**"

*Model consequence:* `FE05.9`'s reaction-exceeds-event claim restated, and — more usefully — a
**named branch point** for `FE03.14`/`FE07.9`'s acute-to-chronic transition. The fork is not a
threshold on load; it is **whether the family's focus settles on the symptomatic member**. That is
`M7.D.2a`'s lock-in and `FE07.6`'s fixed-versus-rotating allocation, and it means the acute/chronic
outcome is decided by the *sink allocation dynamics*, not by the symptom's severity.

Directly implementable, and it gives `M11.C.22` a second observable: chronicity should correlate with
allocation persistence, not with peak load.

## ⭐ FE09.14 — The Smith case: a death in the extended family **relieved** a nuclear-family symptom `[K]` `[T]`

In the worked case that closes the chapter, Mrs Smith's father dies by suicide eight months into the
marriage — and:

> "**Interestingly, Mr. Smith's anxiety symptoms were significantly reduced for several months after
> his father-in-law's death. Mrs. Smith was preoccupied with her mother and family and there was
> actually less tension in their marriage.**"

*Model consequence:* a **counterintuitive, directly testable** consequence of `M6.I.6` and
`FE06.1`'s four propositions: a major loss in one spouse's extended family **redirected the focus**
and relieved the other spouse's symptom. It is cell (4) of `FE06.1`'s 2×2 arriving by an unexpected
route — the twosome was stabilised because a third party's attention was drawn away.

The rest of the case is a clean five-variable illustration for `FE09.1`, and its resolution preserves
the distinction `M11.C.17` depends on: "**The rapid improvement in symptoms was related primarily to
the reduction of anxiety and improvement in functional level of differentiation. There may have been
a little change in basic level of differentiation. Significant changes in basic levels of
differentiation, however, usually require more than a few months of therapy.**"

And the ending is `FE04.2`'s motivation rule for a third time: "**After six or seven months of
therapy… neither spouse was too inclined to work on more differentiation. The next stress period
might provide renewed motivation for one or both of them.**" Motivation is lost on comfort, and
`M1.E.7`'s binder-failure condition is what restores it.

## ⭐ FE09.15 — A systems model is a **framework**, not a rule set — and the theory yields to facts `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A systems model is not a set of 'rules' to follow, but a framework for keeping the total picture in
> mind. The goal is for the health professional not to reinforce unwittingly the problem he or she is
> attempting to treat.**"

> "**If the finding of increased dopamine levels in the limbic systems of schizophrenic patients
> (Carlsson, 1976) were inconsistent with family systems theory, which it is not, the theory would have
> to be changed.**"

> "a discussion of family systems variables that contribute to symptom development **should not be
> construed to mean that family process is the cause of any given problem.** … **The emotional system is
> composed of genes, mitochondria, cell membranes, intercellular connections, extracellular fluids,
> organs, tissues, physiological systems, and all the emotional reactions supported by these
> components.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.F.1` (the model is a thing to be tested) with a **named falsifier offered by
the authors themselves** — third instance after `FE01.16` and `FE05.20`. And the last quote belongs in
`M11.F` beside `FE08.10`: **the emotional system is not the relationship system**, so linking
symptoms to it is not a claim that interaction causes them.

Also here, Engel's question — "**Why does this patient have this disease now?**" — which is a better
framing for `M11.C`'s outputs than "what causes X", and Scheflen's eight-level account of
schizophrenia, of which one level is **the mental hospital**: hospitals "**control psychosis but
sustain schizophrenia**". That is `M11.C.13` at institutional scale and it belongs with `FE06.12`'s
amplifying professional system.

## FE09.16 — ⚠ The LeShan claim — record, flag, exclude `[K]` `[X]`

> "Lawrence LeShan (1977) has developed a psychotherapeutic approach to cancer patients who have not
> responded to standard medical treatment. **His results demand attention. Patients who have failed all
> medical regimens available for their particular cancer — 'the medically hopeless' — have a 50% cure
> rate with LeShan's approach.**"

*Model consequence:* **record as `[X]`, exclude, and say why plainly.**

A 50% cure rate in medically hopeless cancer, reported at face value from a 1977 secondary source
with no control group, no series and no follow-up detail, is precisely the class of claim `M11.F.4`
exists to keep out of the model. Nothing in the spec depends on it, and `M11.F.4`'s prohibition on
implementing emotional-state-to-physical-disease mechanisms already covers it.

It is worth recording rather than omitting, for two reasons: it is the strongest disease claim in the
1988 book, and it establishes that the **widening documented at `FE00.3` and `FE08.17` was already
underway** — the hedges elsewhere in this book do not extend to this passage. That is a fact about
the source's internal consistency and it strengthens the case for grading the disease material
`[K-ext]` throughout.
