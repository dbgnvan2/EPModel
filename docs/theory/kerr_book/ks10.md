## ⭐⭐ KS10.1 — The **four social cues** stated a fourth time, with their mechanism `[T]`

> "**Repeated clinical observations suggest that the most common threats people experience are to approval, attention, expectations, and distress.**"

Their operation, per cue:

> "**Approval enhances emotional well-being, and disapproval provokes anxiety.** Approval can be associated with feelings of well-being; disapproval… with feelings of unease and perhaps shame. **The perception of adequate attention from the other can activate the calm and connection system and a reciprocal toning down of the fight-or-flight system.** The perception of **inadequate attention can trigger feelings of rejection, panic, and emotional isolation.**"

> "Such reactions set off a range of emotions in each person, depending on… whether the emotionally competent stimuli are perceived as **approval or disapproval, attention or lack thereof, expectations met or unmet, and upset or equanimity in the other.**"

And a fifth term:

> "**Furthermore, uncertainty about if and when such stimuli will occur intensifies the process.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M4.C.1` should be rebuilt on this.** Four channels, each **bipolar** (the list above gives both poles explicitly), plus an **uncertainty** multiplier. This is the fourth identical statement of the set (`KS01.7`, `KS07.6`, `KS09.4`, here) across four chapters, so it is safe to treat as the theory's appraisal decomposition rather than as one author's list.

Note each cue is bipolar in the *same* two-sided way the rest of the theory is — approval **and** disapproval both act, in opposite directions. So the appraisal function is a **signed** deviation per channel, not a magnitude, which matches `KS06.3`'s contact/distance optimum.

The uncertainty term is new and is separately implementable: variance in the other's response, not just its value. It also explains `KS09.8`'s intermittent-withdrawal case being a stronger programmer than constant involvement.

Kerr flags the list is not closed-by-proof: "**and certainly other cues as well**", "**It is not a complete list, but I emphasize these** because these are the ones that people most commonly identify as needing to work on" (`KS09.1`). Record as **four confirmed, list open**.

## ⭐⭐ KS10.2 — Chronic anxiety is a **property of the field**, not of the person `[T]` `[M]`

> "even though it is individuals that experience anxiety, **chronic anxiety is a consequence of various types of social interaction and, consequently, is most usefully conceptualized as a property of the emotional field** (Henry, 1992)."

> "One individual… may most intensely manifest… anxiety, but **the degree of anxiety that this person manifests is not solely an individual property.** This is an important distinction because commonly **the most anxious person in a system is seen as causing the others' anxiety**, rather than seeing the most anxious person's anxiety as **a reflection of system-generated anxiety.**"

The two determinants, stated as a summary:

> "an individual's level of chronic anxiety is related to **two processes that are not under individual control**: (1) **poorly differentiated relationship interactions** in the relationship system in response to a stress or stressors on one or more members that raise system chronic anxiety, and (2) **the person's functioning position in the system.**"

*Model consequence:* **this is a direct challenge to `M1.A.7`.** The spec makes `chronic_anxiety` a per-person scalar, **fixed in childhood** from that person's own witnessed history, acting as a floor. Kerr says chronic anxiety is (a) a field property and (b) currently determined by **functioning position**, which changes.

These are reconcilable but the spec does not currently state the reconciliation: `M1.A.7`'s fixed childhood value is the person's **programmed reactivity** (`KS09.10`'s first deficit), while the *manifest* chronic anxiety is that reactivity **times** the field and the position. The model needs both, and only has one. **High-priority pass-2 item** — `M1.A.7` may need splitting.

## ⭐⭐ KS10.3 — The hot potato: **the one who is stressed is not the one who absorbs** `[T]` `[M]`

> "**It is important to recognize that the member who is distressed initially by a real or imagined stressor is not necessarily the one who absorbs the system-generated chronic anxiety. The one who absorbs the anxiety depends on the particular patterns of emotional functioning that come into play** to manage the anxiety."

The worked chain: "a mother reacts to her **husband's distance**, distance that has been triggered in reaction to anxiety generated by **problems at his work**, and manifests her reactivity by **anxiously focusing on their six-year-old child. That night the child 'inexplicably' wets the bed. The father, whose anxiety initially tweaked the system, slept peacefully that night.**"

> "**A metaphor for this process is people quickly passing a hot potato to avoid getting burned themselves.**"

*Model consequence:* a **complete three-hop routing trace with an exogenous origin and a symptom terminus**, and the origin agent ends the night *unaffected*. This is the single clearest statement in the corpus that the model's job is **routing**, and it is a compact fixture: work stressor → father `DISTANCE` → mother appraises → mother `TRIANGLE`/child-focus → child symptom, all within one tick-to-tick sequence.

It also states the routing rule: **which member absorbs is determined by the active pattern**, not by proximity to the stressor. `M6.I`'s invariants should be assertable over exactly this trace, and `M11.C` should carry it as a named scenario — it is short enough to test and it fails loudly if the model routes by adjacency instead.

## ⭐⭐ KS10.4 — The shock wave, worked over four years and four nuclear families `[T]` `[M]`

A patriarch and his wife, four adult children's families all local, "**quite involved relationships that were anxiety-reducing and stabilizing**", the parents pouring most into the eldest son's family. Stable for two decades. Both parents die **within six months of each other**.

> "'**My parents died, and the castle crumbled!**'"

In the following years: **two of the four siblings' marriages ended in divorce**; the treated couple went to the verge of divorce and then divorced; **two of their three sons** developed significant clinical problems; several nieces and nephews "in trouble"; the husband went from "verge of being fired" to losing his job; his diabetes destabilised; **he died a few years later.** The daughter, "**who had been less a focus of family anxiety**", finished college, moved away and did well.

> "**The presence of his parents had a calming influence on the whole system.** It is reminiscent of the chaos that occurs in many animal social groups **if the alpha male dies suddenly.** … **much of the potential chronic anxiety of the husband's family of origin was bound by the presence of his parents.** The deep emotional dependence of the siblings on the parents **was not obvious to them while the parents were alive. The functional levels of differentiation of the siblings were sustained by the presence of their parents.**"

*Model consequence:* **the most complete removal/shock-wave case in the corpus**, and it is directly on `M11.C.11` and `M1.A.13`'s `structural_importance`.

Four things the model must be able to produce:
1. **Functional level sustained by a living member's mere presence**, invisible until removal — which is `KS05.1`'s borrowed pseudo-self at family scale, and is the estimator's discriminator (`M11.C.18`) applied to a whole system.
2. **Propagation across nuclear-family boundaries** — the wave hit four separate households, not one.
3. **The unaffected sibling-line member**: the daughter "less a focus of family anxiety" was spared. The wave follows the projection structure, not kinship distance.
4. **A terminal trajectory** — the system never re-equilibrated. `M5.E.6` forbids treating reversion as run failure; this says some regressions simply do not recover, so the model must permit an absorbing state.

Also note the **derivation of `structural_importance` from function, not role** (`M1.A.13a`, from Tape 6): the parents' importance was that they *bound* the system's anxiety, which is measurable, and the eldest son's family — which received most — was among the worst hit.

## ⭐ KS10.5 — Acute versus chronic distinguished by **real versus imagined**, and by boundedness `[T]`

> "**Bowen theory defines anxiety as the reaction of an organism to a real or imagined threat.**"

> "**Acute anxiety is typically in reaction to real threats, and chronic anxiety occurs more in reaction to imagined threats. The response to real threats is generally experienced as time limited. The response to imagined threats is not experienced as time limited because such threats are accompanied by more uncertainty about when they will end.**"

> "Bowen theory distinguishes between **internalized anxiety**, which manifests in one or more locations of the **body (inflamed bowel) or mind (hallucination)**, and **externalized anxiety**, which an individual **acts out in one way or another in the relationship system.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.8`/`M1.A.7`'s acute/chronic split gets a **principled discriminator** rather than a timescale convention — the distinction is *real vs imagined* and therefore *bounded vs unbounded*, and the unboundedness is the mechanism of chronicity. That is better than a decay-rate parameter and it connects to `KS10.1`'s uncertainty multiplier: chronic anxiety is high **because** termination is uncertain.

The internalised/externalised split is a second axis crossing `M1.A.11`'s three channels, and it is what `KS04.13` used (conflict externalises, so the depression lifts). Worth making explicit in `M7.D`.

## ⭐ KS10.6 — An imagined threat **becomes** real: the pursuit loop `[T]`

> "**an imagined threat can become real.** For example, when an anxious response to **a perception that an important other person has become emotionally distant triggers pursuing behavior** to reestablish closeness, and **the pursuing behavior triggers anxiety-driven emotional distancing by the other — it is a self-fulfilling prophecy.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS09.3`'s Merton mechanism at the dyadic level and in the model's own move vocabulary: `PURSUE` on a *misperceived* distance **produces** the distance. This is the tightest possible fixture for `M4.C.5` — perception-side error is the trigger, the emitted move is ordinary, and the loop closes in two ticks.

It also means `M5.A.1`'s `PURSUE`/`DISTANCE` pair is **self-reinforcing under misperception**, which the model should exhibit without being told to.

## ⭐ KS10.7 — Adaptation defined, and the two reasons differentiation protects `[T]`

> "**One definition of adaptation in Bowen theory is that it is the ability to respond to life challenges without participating in an escalation of chronic anxiety in a relationship system.**"

The two reasons a well-differentiated triangle is less vulnerable:

> "**The first** is that if one member… experiences a significant stressor… **that person can usually self-manage without acting in ways that make other members of the system anxious**, for example, by not withdrawing, acting frustrated and angry, or acting helpless. **The second** is that **the other members of the system have enough 'self' not to become unduly anxious about the stressed person's anxiety. They can remain present and accounted for without invading the emotional boundaries** of the somewhat anxious member."

> "**although no one is sufficiently differentiated to be immune to participation in triangles if the stressors are great enough.**"

*Model consequence:* `M11.C.1`'s "differentiation protects" gets **two separable mechanisms** — *emission* damping and *reception* damping — which is a better test than a single divisor. An arm varying only the actor's level and an arm varying only the observers' levels **MUST** both show protection, by different routes. The spec's `/ functional_level` in `M4.C.1` implements only the reception side.

The caveat is `KS02.1`'s ceiling restated: no immunity at any level.

## ⭐ KS10.8 — Attitudes are programmed, and they are **context-bound** `[T]`

> "a person who **perpetually feels that life treats him unfairly** is chronically vulnerable to anxiously reacting to a wide array of life situations." Citing McEwen (2002): "**if we torture ourselves with worst-case scenarios, voluntarily simmering in a toxic broth of dread, we can exhaust our bodies' allostatic systems without ever coming into contact with a predator or adversary, without even leaving home.**"

> "**A person harboring such an attitude has experienced the programming for it in interaction with other people in the past and acts out that programming with the same or other people in the present.**"

The natural experiment: a man "highly vulnerable to considerable self-criticism"; his wife takes a rare out-of-town business trip; "**he experienced a marked decline in his self-critical obsessions, but the obsessions came roaring back when she returned.** He had not realized **how much of his self-criticism, his 'toxic dread,' was linked to worry about what his wife thought of him. His obsessions were much more intense in the context of the marital emotional field than in his work emotional field.**"

*Model consequence:* **a second natural-experiment fixture of exactly `KS07.1`'s shape** — a symptom that remits on removal of one relational condition and returns on its restoration, in **days**. Two independent instances (the cousin's visit, the wife's trip) of the same discriminator, which strengthens `M11.C.18` considerably: this is a *repeatable* observation, not an anecdote.

It also confirms `KS08.2` — an apparently intrapsychic symptom (self-criticism) is **positionally generated** and needs no state of its own.

Bowen's line, recorded: "**That which was created in a relationship can be fixed in a relationship.**"

## ⭐ KS10.9 — Anxiety increases vulnerability; it does not cause `[T]`

> "**heightened chronic anxiety alone does not cause an illness, but it may be one of the most important variables for explaining when clinical symptoms emerge.**"

> "**chronic anxiety does not cause physical and other symptoms; it increases people's vulnerability** to develop symptoms. One way to conceptualize this is that **chronic anxiety disturbs homeostasis in the body, and this disturbance can render people vulnerable** to illness and even death."

> "**Further research will determine if this assertion holds up over time.**"

*Model consequence:* `KS04.9` restated with the **timing** claim isolated — anxiety explains **when**, not **whether**. That is a much more testable proposition and it is the right target for `M11.C.8`-class criteria: the model should predict *onset timing* relative to system events, not incidence.

Note Kerr marks the whole assertion as **awaiting confirmation**. Grade accordingly.

The psoriasis exchange is a clean statement of the framing requirement: "**No, they do not have psychiatric problems, but have anxiety manifesting in physical problems. People with psychiatric problems have anxiety that manifests in mental processes.**"

## KS10.10 — Micro and macro views, both required `[T]`

> "**One is a microscopic view of relationship interactions** to reveal the specific details… **The other is a macroscopic view that examines the total network of relationships in which a given relationship is embedded.**"

> "**The issues family members are reacting to (content) are not the problem; their anxiety-driven level of reactivity related to the larger relationship system (process) is the problem.**"

*Model consequence:* an explicit **two-scale** requirement, and the shock-wave case (`KS10.4`) is what happens when only the micro view is available — the couple had no account of their own collapse until the extended-family deaths were brought in. `M11`'s readouts should therefore report **both** scales for any run, and the model must simulate the extended family even when the question is about the nuclear one. That bears on `M2.A`'s reference-family scope.

## KS10.11 — "You had a part" is useless until the part is **specific** `[T]` `[M]`

The affair case: the first counsellor "**suggested that the wife may have had a part in the affair occurring. She struggled to figure out what her part might have been but could not sort it out. She continued to be in significant distress.**"

After four or five sessions of detailed questions, she identified two specific changes after the baby's birth: an intense focus on the baby, and "**anxiety about whether her husband would pitch in… leading to her critically monitoring his perceived level of involvement.**" Her own summary: "**I think I was hell to live with during that time!**" and then "**I am amazed that I can view this objectively: he had a part and I had a part. I see it.**"

> "she had **accepted intellectually that she must have had a part**… but **until she comprehended the specifics of that part, it had not helped.**"

> "**saying someone has an equal part in a process is not especially helpful until that person can comprehend the specifics of it all.** … **Questions based on systems thinking and occasional clinical vignettes to illustrate are the best help people can get.**"

*Model consequence:* **a precise constraint on `M1.E.7`'s landed contact.** The generic proposition ("you have a part") was delivered, accepted intellectually, and **did nothing** — for months, across a house move and two therapists. What landed was **specific functional facts about her own moves in a named period.**

So a coach contact **MUST NOT** be modelled as transmitting a proposition. It transmits **observations of the recipient's own event history**, and its effectiveness depends on that history being retrievable and specific. That is implementable — the coach's move reads the recipient's own log — and it explains the low landing rate (`KB07` · K07.4): most contacts fail to surface a specific enough fact.

Note also that she "**still held her husband responsible for his actions**" while becoming calmer. Seeing one's own part **does not** require absolving the other, which is `KS01.5`'s no-culpability rule from the participant's side.

## KS10.12 — Anxiety is not a disorder; its evolutionary depth `[T]`

> "**Bowen theory does not view anxiety as a psychiatric disorder. Anxiety exists in all people and, in some form, probably exists in all living things, flora as well as fauna.**"

Kandel (1983) on *Aplysia californica*, ~500 million years back — "processes… that, **despite its not having a complex nervous system, have remarkable parallels to chronic and anticipatory anxiety in human beings.**" McEwen (2002): the human stress-response mechanisms "**first became fully operational in salmon about 400 million years ago.**"

> "**What psychiatry textbooks term anxiety disorders are but one of myriad symptomatic manifestations of overly active evolutionarily ancient anxiety systems.**"

*Model consequence:* supports treating anxiety as the model's **substrate quantity** rather than as a symptom or an outcome — it is always present and always nonzero, which `M1.A.7`'s chronic floor already encodes. The *Aplysia* citation is also the strongest available support for `M1.A.0`'s instinct definition, since the organism has no capacity for feeling in any ordinary sense yet exhibits both chronic and anticipatory forms.

## KS10.13 — The two main variables, named `[T]`

> "**Anxiety is one of two main variables in Bowen theory. The other variable is the degree of integration of self**… **The two variables are distinct but interlocking.** They are referred to as variables because **they significantly affect all the other processes that Bowen theory describes**, such as the activity level of the patterns of emotional functioning."

> "**The variable of integration of self refers to the phenomenon of thoughts and feelings operating as a working team.**"

*Model consequence:* resolves the "second two variables" ambiguity flagged in `_KERR_INTERVIEWS.md` — the pair is **anxiety** and **integration/differentiation of self**, and everything else in the theory is downstream. That is exactly `M1.A.1`'s requirement that a `Person` hold exactly two state variables from which reactive behaviour is derived, and it is now confirmed by the latest source in the theory's own words. Quote in `M1.A.1`.
