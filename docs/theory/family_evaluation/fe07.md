# FE07 — Nuclear Family Emotional System

**Citation rule:** cite `FE07`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person throughout; clinical cases presented as the
author's own practice. `[K]`.

> **The longest chapter in the book and the one that touches the most requirements.** It settles the
> three-sinks question in writing, supplies a **belief-layer readout that identifies which sink is
> active**, and offers a mechanism for **one of the two things `M12.2` records as Bowen not
> knowing.**

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE07.1 — Three sinks, and distance explicitly **not** a fourth `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Three categories of dysfunction occur in nuclear families: (1) illness in a spouse; (2) marital
> conflict; and (3) impairment of one or more children.**"

> "**Emotional distance is not considered a distinct category of dysfunction because it is a feature
> of all relationships and it is intertwined with all the patterns of emotional functioning in a
> nuclear family. However, emotional distance is as important a manifestation of the
> undifferentiation that exists between people as the others.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M6.I.1` and `M6.I.2` in one passage, in written primary text, with the
project's own reasoning.**

The three-versus-four-sinks question has been reopened repeatedly across the corpus — `KB04`'s "four
principal ones" against 1979 Tape 2's "three ways… because these are the three things that end up
with symptoms", resolved at `M1.D.2a` by making distance the substrate rather than a fourth sink.
`FE07.1` states exactly that resolution, and it states **both halves**: distance is not a fourth
category *because it is a feature of all relationships and intertwined with all the patterns*, **and**
it is not thereby less important.

This should be quoted at `M6.I.2`. It is the cleanest statement in the corpus and it retires the
question.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE07.2 — The three sinks are identified by **three distinct beliefs** — a computable readout `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A principal difference between a conflictual marriage that does not produce symptoms and a
> harmonious marriage that does produce symptoms is that in a conflictual marriage each spouse is
> convinced that it is the other spouse who needs to do the changing; in a symptom-producing marriage
> each spouse is convinced that it is one spouse who needs to do the changing. As will be described
> later, in a marriage that produces problems in children, each spouse is convinced that it is the
> children who need to do the changing.**"

*Model consequence:* **the sink allocation and the belief layer are connected for the first time, and
the connection is a clean three-way discriminator.**

| Active sink (`M1.D.1`) | Belief configuration | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| **Marital conflict** | each spouse: "**the other** must change" | symmetric, outward, two targets |
| **Spouse dysfunction** | both spouses: "**one** (the same one) must change" | asymmetric, one shared target |
| **Child projection** | both spouses: "**the children** must change" | symmetric, outward, target outside the dyad |

Three consequences:

1. **`M9` gains a purpose it did not have.** The spec's belief store records who-is-sick (`M9.3`)
   and permits divergence from ground truth (`M9.1`); nothing reads it. `FE07.2` makes the *shape of
   the attribution set* a direct readout of which sink is carrying the load — computable from the
   event log with no new state, since attributions are already what `M1.A.18a`'s blame detector
   counts.
2. **It is assertable in `M11`.** Drive a family into each sink and the belief configuration must
   follow; drive the beliefs and the sink must follow. Either direction is a two-arm direction test.
3. **It fuses with `FE01.1`'s asymmetric misattribution.** The spouse-dysfunction row is exactly
   `FE01.1`'s pair — outward at the caretaking pole, inward at the symptomatic one — and the two
   others are its symmetric variants. One mechanism, three configurations.

Kerr adds the honest hedge: "**This, of course, is rarely an all-or-nothing process in a family. The
patterns tend to be intermixed to some degree.**" So the readout is a distribution, not a label.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE07.3 — ⚠ A mechanism for **one of `M12.2`'s two stated unknowns** `[K]` `[T]` `[X]`

`M12.2` records two things Bowen states he does **not** know, both to be labelled `[I]` wherever the
model decides them:

1. what determines whether a problem stays in the spouse dyad or transmits to a child;
2. what selects which spouse takes the dominant pole at identical levels.

**`FE07` offers a mechanism for both.** For the first:

> "**The particular category or categories of dysfunction peculiar to a nuclear family are determined
> largely by the experience each parent had growing up in his or her family of origin.**" … people
> "**tend to select mates with whom they can replicate the more influential aspects of the
> relationship process that existed in the original family… people gravitate toward their emotional
> mirror images.** … **The patterns of emotional functioning characteristic of a nuclear family evolve
> out of this emotional 'fit' or 'complementarity.'** … **If family anxiety increases, however, the
> basic elements of the emotional complementarity can become exaggerated. The nature of these
> exaggerated elements determines whether the problem emerges as marital conflict, spouse dysfunction,
> or child dysfunction.**"

And for the second:

> "**In relationships where people have emotional significance to one another, males and females
> assume the dominant position with equal frequency. The one in the dominant position is usually there
> by mutual agreement — the product of the emotional fit of a relationship.**"

*Model consequence:* **this must go to the project owner rather than being applied.**

The mechanism is the same for both: **the parents' emotional complementarity, formed in their
families of origin and exaggerated by anxiety.** That is a mechanism, not a magnitude, so it is the
kind of thing the corpus normally supports — and it would let the model *derive* the sink allocation
from `M2.A`'s membership rather than declaring it.

**But the precedence rules bear on this directly and they point both ways.** `FE07` is later (1988)
and written; `M12.2`'s unknowns are Bowen's own statements. The index rule is explicit: *a Kerr
formulation MUST NOT silently overwrite a Bowen one — record both and mark the divergence.* Three
readings are possible and pass 1 cannot choose between them:

- **Kerr answered a question Bowen left open** — in which case `M12.2`'s first clause is superseded
  and the model gains a derivation.
- **Kerr is describing *where* it is decided without saying *how*** — complementarity names the
  locus, not the rule, and Bowen's "I do not know" stands. This reading is supported by the text's
  own hedge: "**determined largely by**".
- **They are answering different questions** — Bowen on the *rule*, Kerr on the *inputs*.

**Carry to pass 2 and to the decision document.** Nothing in `M12.2` changes until it is resolved.

**One part is immediately usable and should be applied regardless: sex-independence.** "Males and
females assume the dominant position **with equal frequency**" is a hard constraint the spec does not
state, and a natural implementation could easily violate it — `M2.A`'s reference family has a named
head-of-household pair and any asymmetry in the pole-assignment rule would show up as a sex effect
across an ensemble. **Requirement candidate: pole assignment MUST be independent of sex, assertable
over an ensemble.**

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE07.4 — The projection loop, as a **five-step algorithm** `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "The pattern '**begins**' (once the pattern is entrenched it can be activated by either mother or
> child) **when anxiety develops in the mother related to some aspect of her child's functioning. She
> may react to something the child actually said or did, to something she imagined the child said or
> did, or to something she fears the child might say or do. The second step is for mother to assign
> meaning or significance to her perceptions of the child. This interpretation is based on her feeling
> state. Her image of the child reflects her anxiety and emotional needs… more than it reflects the
> child himself. The third step occurs when mother treats the child as if her image of him is
> reality. The fourth step is that the child learns (not necessarily consciously) that if he talks and
> acts as if mother's image of him is correct, she is calmer. When she is calmer, he is calmer. The
> final step is when the child internalizes mother's image of him and the circle is completed. Based
> on the internalization, the child does things that are consistent with mother's image of him. Those
> actions are used by mother to justify her image of the child. The mother is not malicious; she is
> just anxious. She is as much a prisoner of the situation as the child.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.E.1d` gains an implementable algorithm, and step 4 is the load-bearing
one.**

The spec requires that the projection be able to initiate with **no signal from the child**
(`M7.E.1d`, sourced to `KS09.3`/Merton). `FE07.4` states that — "something she **imagined**… something
she **fears** the child **might**" — and adds the reinforcement path the spec lacks:

> **Step 4: the child's compliance is reinforced by the mother's calming.**

That is `M4.D.6`'s reinforcement operating **across persons** rather than within one: the child's
learned repertoire is shaped by the *other's* anxiety reduction. The spec's `M4.D.6` reinforces on
the actor's own signal. This is a real gap — without it there is no path by which the child comes to
occupy the image, and step 5's internalisation has nothing driving it.

It also connects to `M4.D.6a`'s prohibition in an interesting way: the child is optimising a
**short-horizon anxiety relief that is not its own**, which is the same forbidden gradient at one
remove, and it is why the outcome is bad for the child while feeling right at every step.

Footnote 22 generalises it: "**A well entrenched symbiotic relationship… is most accurately
conceptualized as a mutual projection process. The mutual projection that occurs in a parent-child
relationship is essentially the same as the mutual projection that occurs in all relationships.**"
So this is **one** mechanism, not a parent-child special case — `M10.A.2` satisfied.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE07.5 — "**I will not**" versus "**You will not**" — the counterfeit detector, reduced to grammar `[K]` `[T]`

> "**If a parent says 'No' in reference to what he (the parent) will do ('I will not') rather than in
> reference to what the child 'should' do ('You will not'), the child cannot use the parent to react
> off emotionally. A child automatically listens to, 'I will not,' just as he automatically does not
> listen to, 'You will not.'**"

With the rebel's mechanism stated as active solicitation:

> "**The child's identity may be so much an 'anti' identity that he will, especially when he is
> anxious, deliberately invite people to tell him what to do so that he can do the opposite. He calms
> himself down by being the opposite of what he perceives others want.**"

And the escalation: "**It is paradoxical that the more unsure of themselves and permissive parents
are, the more they say (and yell) 'No' to the child. The more they say (and yell) 'No,' the more the
child rebels.**"

*Model consequence:* **the cheapest possible implementation of `M5.F.2b`'s axis detector.**

The genuine `I-POSITION` declares what **self** will do; the counterfeit prescribes what **other**
must do. That is `M1.A.9a`'s outward-impingement axis made **syntactic**, and in a model where every
`Event` already carries a sender and a target set (`M1.F.1`), it is computable with no new state:
**does the emitted position's subject refer to the sender or to the target?**

Two further requirements fall out:

- **The counterfeit is not merely weaker — it is *usable* by the recipient.** "the child cannot use
  the parent to react off emotionally" means a "You will not" supplies the rebel with the thing his
  anti-identity requires. `M5.F.2`'s requirement that a counterfeit produce the **opposite sign** of
  effect has its mechanism here.
- **The rebel actively solicits prescriptions.** That is a propensity the spec does not carry: an
  agent whose pseudo-self is an anti-self (`FE04.10`) emits moves designed to **elicit** a directive
  it can oppose. Implementable as a negative-correlation target on the other's positions, and it
  makes `FE04.12`'s "not simply oppose" clause testable.

## ⭐⭐ FE07.6 — Sink **mobility** is protective — a new criterion `[K]` `[T]`

> "**When the family emotional process does not always focus on the same person or on the same
> relationship, it is less likely that the functioning of any one person will be chronically impaired.
> Large discrepancies in the functioning of members of the same family are most likely to occur when
> there is little flexibility in the way the undifferentiation of the family is played out.** For
> example, if family anxiety is bound in an intense focus on one particular child, **this highly
> focused child may function on a schizophrenic level while his siblings function on reasonably
> adequate levels. Had the focus shifted around from child to child, all of the children might have
> grown up with enough emotional separation from the family to escape a schizophrenic level of
> functioning.**"

*Model consequence:* **a variance claim at fixed budget, and a new acceptance criterion.**

Two arms, **identical `undifferentiation_budget`**, differing only in whether the allocation is
**fixed** on one target or **rotates**. The fixed arm must produce a more extreme worst-case
individual outcome; the rotating arm must produce a higher mean and a narrower spread. Nothing in
`M11.C` tests this, and it is the mechanism behind `M11.C.6(b)`'s variance requirement seen from
inside one generation.

It also has a direct consequence for `M2.A.2` — `Nadia` becomes the projection target as an
*outcome* — and for `M1.C.4`'s `activation_memory`: preferential rerouting onto previously-used
circuits is precisely what makes the allocation **fixed**, so the model's own memory mechanism is
what produces the bad outcome. That is a good sign, and it makes `M1.C.4`'s decay rate a
consequential `[I]` parameter rather than a detail.

The chapter states the same mobility for the whole family across time: an early pattern of one
spouse adapting can later shift to marital conflict — "**The principal pattern of binding or
absorbing anxiety has changed, but the basic relationship problem… is the same.**"

## ⭐⭐ FE07.7 — Transmission magnitudes: **±5–10 points a generation, bounded well inside ±30** `[K]` `[#]`

> "**Differences between siblings or between siblings and their parents of only five or ten points on
> the scale of differentiation can result in significant divergencies in the stability of life
> courses.** For example, two parents may raise a schizophrenic son who has a basic level of
> differentiation **about ten points lower** than their levels. The same parents may raise a second son
> who has a basic level **about five or ten points higher** than their levels and, consequently, **about
> 15 or 20 points higher than the brother's level.**"

> "While two people with a basic level of differentiation of self of 45 may bind their chronic anxiety
> predominantly through focusing on what is wrong with the other, **they will still not produce a child
> with a level of differentiation of 75 or 15. The children of these parents may grow up with basic
> levels of differentiation higher or lower than the parents, but not 30 points higher or lower.
> Emotional systems change slowly.**"

*Model consequence:* **magnitudes, which the corpus almost never gives — and they are a *bound*, not
a rate, so `M10.C.3` is untouched.**

`M7.E.1a` requires a three-outcome distribution centred on the parental level ("a little higher, a
little lower, or the same") and gives no scale. `FE07.7` supplies one: **a little ≈ 5–10 points, and
never as much as 30.** That is directly implementable as a bound on the per-generation draw and it is
`[#]` in the strict sense — Kerr states the numbers.

**⚠ One tension to check in pass 2.** `M11.C.6(a)`'s traversal constraint cites `KS11.1` (2019):
"It could take eight to ten generations **or as few as three or four**". At 5–10 points a generation,
a full traversal needs 10–20 generations; three or four would require 25–33 points per generation,
which sits at or beyond `FE07.7`'s stated maximum. The two are not flatly contradictory — 30 is a
bound, not a typical value, and both are shape constraints rather than rates — but a model that
satisfies `FE07.7`'s bound will **not** produce `KS11.1`'s fast case. **Record as a reconciliation
item.** The likely landing is that the fast case is rhetorical in the same way the ten-generation
figure was shown to be (`KB11`, withdrawn at pass 3), in which case `FE07.7`'s bound is the better
constraint because it is stated as a bound and not as an illustration.

## ⭐⭐ FE07.8 — The alternating-symptom couple: a **four-or-five-cycle** fixture for `M6.I.4` `[K]` `[T]`

> "the wife had had moderate symptoms throughout much of the marriage: **labile blood pressure,
> spastic colon, and phobias.** The husband had not had major health problems before he retired. Soon
> after retirement, however, he developed **complications of pulmonary emphysema. When he was sick, the
> wife's blood pressure was normal, her colon was quiet, and the phobias were reduced to the point
> that she could take care of things her husband used to take care of 'because' she was phobic. When
> he recovered, all the wife's symptoms recurred. Eventually the husband got sick again; when he did,
> the wife recovered. This cycle repeated four or five times over four years until, finally, the
> husband died. The wife was the more dominant force in the relationship.**"

*Model consequence:* **the best `M6.I.4` fixture in the corpus** — a repeated, reversible,
antisymmetric exchange **within one dyad**, observed four or five times over four years. Every other
instance in the corpus is a single transition. This is the one that could falsify a
one-way implementation.

The analysis around it also names a case the spec has no room for:

> "**The thinking of the spouse who has symptoms early in a marriage may actually dominate the
> relationship. It is as if this 'dominant-symptomatic' spouse has 'chosen' to support the functioning
> of the mate.** … **The spouse who 'chooses' to support the other appears to be the dependent partner,
> but it is actually this 'dependent' one who 'assigned' the roles.**"

That is `FE06.3`'s impairment-reads-transfer rule and `FE03.16`'s inversion, at once: **symptomatic,
dominant, and the one who set the configuration.** Any implementation that ties dominance to the
`M1.B.5` pole or to symptom absence gets this case backwards.

And the closing sentence is `M11.F.6` in one line: "**There are as many symptoms related to 'too
little' distance as there are related to 'too much' distance.**"

## ⭐⭐ FE07.9 — The colitis case: a **capacity threshold** crossed by successive tie additions `[K]` `[T]`

A dated longitudinal sequence: intermittent cramps and diarrhoea in freshman year → "**The symptoms
disappeared completely after he met his wife-to-be**" → recurrence during the **first** pregnancy and
the son's first months, "**cleared up by the time the child was six months old**" → during the
**second** pregnancy, fever, severe cramping, bloody diarrhoea; ulcerative colitis diagnosed;
"**Despite medical treatment, the disease became chronic.**"

> "**The addition of the first child temporarily increased the level of chronic anxiety and strained
> the family system into emotional imbalance and symptoms. The strain was not so great, however, that
> it exceeded the family's ability to adapt and recover without the presence of a chronic symptom. The
> addition of the second child did exceed the family system's capacity to adapt without the presence
> of a chronic symptom. Chronic physical dysfunction became a permanent feature of the family.**"

*Model consequence:* **three requirements in one case, and it is the clearest demonstration of
`FE03.10`'s reserve.**

- `M7.C.1a` — a new tie is a **redistribution of a conserved investment budget**, not a stressor.
  Here the redistribution happens twice, and the second one crosses the threshold.
- `FE03.10`'s **emotional reserve** — the first addition is absorbed and recovers; the second exceeds
  capacity. That is the reserve being consumed and then exhausted, and it is why deriving reserve as
  *capacity − accommodation stock* is the right shape.
- `FE03.14`'s **acute→chronic transition** happens **at the crossing**, not gradually. The same
  symptom, in the same person, is reversible below the threshold and permanent above it.

Note also the pre-marriage remission — symptoms cleared on meeting the future wife — which is
`M1.A.5c`'s automatic channel raising functional level, and a clean before/after for `FE04.7`'s
borrowing detector.

Kerr's footnote 14 records the honest caveat: other stressors could have contributed, and "**Such
factors were not important in this case**" is the author's judgement, not a control.

## ⭐⭐ FE07.10 — The migraine sequence: suppression and differentiation have **different signatures** `[K]` `[T]`

A worked change-back sequence over several sessions:

1. The wife takes what she thinks is the therapist's advice and tries to "**control**" her feelings.
   Husband: "**This has been the best two weeks of our married life… My wife has never been easier to
   live with.**" Wife: "**I have spent much of the last two weeks in and out of bed with migraine
   headaches… I have been miserable. I have left him alone.**" She had not had migraines since before
   the marriage.
2. She reverts, then achieves the real thing: "**She got more involved with some of her own interests
   and was genuinely less preoccupied with what he said or did not say… She had no headaches, felt
   good, and had more energy for her various projects than she had had for a long time.**"
3. "**Well, the husband reacted. He accused her of no longer caring about him. 'Do you still love me?'
   he asked plaintively… He started to spend a lot of time with an attractive female 'friend' of the
   family, an activity he did little to hide. The wife soon got preoccupied with her husband's
   activities again and started berating him… He shouted back, but gave up the female 'friend.' It was
   just like old times again.**" They ended therapy.

*Model consequence:* **the single best fixture for `M4.D.1c`, and it produces the discriminator as an
observable rather than as a definition.**

`M4.D.1c` requires that `WITHHOLD` is **not** a weak `I-POSITION` and that a `WITHHOLD`-only arm not
produce the `M5.E` resolution. `FE07.10` shows the two arms **in the same couple, weeks apart**, with
different signatures:

| Arm | Mover | Other | Reaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Suppression** (`WITHHOLD`) | **symptom in the mover** — migraines, first in years | delighted, comfortable | **none** |
| **Differentiation** | no symptom, more energy | | **full ladder**, escalating to a third party |

So the two are distinguishable by **where the cost lands**: suppression costs the mover and produces
no reaction; differentiation costs the mover nothing and produces the reaction. That is `M5.E.3`
(absence of reaction means the move did not land) with its complement — *and the withheld move still
had a cost*, which is `M4.D.1b`'s requirement that a withheld move change state, here landing
inside the mover.

Two more requirements:

- **The reaction escalated through a third party** — `M5.E.5` exactly, and the third party was
  *recruited* rather than symptomatic. `M8.6`'s peripheral triangle opened as the reaction.
- **The couple ended therapy at reversion.** `M5.D.6`'s mandatory `FOLLOW_UP` and `M5.D.3`'s abort
  branches: this is an abort at the first opposition, and the pair actively chose it — "**Both
  partners were content to opt for the old level of togetherness.**"

The chapter also names the coach hazard this case sits inside: **the same therapist message is heard
oppositely by the two spouses.** "the spouse who feels most pressured… is more likely to applaud this
point of view than the spouse who feels more isolated… **The one who feels isolated may hear the
therapist's suggestions as aggravating her dilemma.**" That is `M1.F.2`'s `source_position` effect
transposed to the **receiver's** position, and the spec has no term for it. **Requirement candidate:
an event's valence MUST be able to differ by receiver position, not only by sender position.**

## ⭐⭐ FE07.11 — A received event costs the receiver **even when no move is emitted** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Even though the adaptive one may not intend to change in response to this pressure, he is unable
> to ignore the anxious focus. In other words, the adaptive one may not respond externally, but he
> reacts internally (physiologically if not psychologically). He may or may not be aware of his
> internal reactions. The dominant one projects or 'sprays' his or her anxiety and, in the process,
> usually feels calmer; the adaptive one picks up or absorbs the anxiety and, in the process, becomes
> more anxious and more at risk for a symptom.**"

> "**An 'emotional gain' in this process for the dominant one is unloading some of his or her anxiety
> on the other; an 'emotional gain' in this process for the adaptive one is that by absorbing the
> anxiety he does not have to do battle with the dominant one.**"

*Model consequence:* **the receiver-side complement of `M4.D.1b`, and it is what makes the transfer
work.**

The spec's `WITHHOLD` is an *emitter-side* outcome: a move computed and not emitted, which still
changes tie state. `FE07.11` supplies the other half: **an arriving event raises the receiver's load
in full even when the receiver emits nothing.** Without that, an implementation could let a
non-responding agent absorb nothing, and the "spray" would have no destination.

The second quote is why the configuration is **stable**: **both parties gain in the short run.**
That is `M4.D.6a`'s forbidden gradient rewarding *both* poles simultaneously, which is a stronger
statement than the spec's — it is not that one party exploits the other, it is that the arrangement
is locally optimal for each. `M11.C.16`'s short-horizon arm should therefore converge on this
configuration *from both sides*.

## ⭐⭐ FE07.12 — Absorption **protects** the others — conservation stated as a claim `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The degree to which the undifferentiation of a nuclear family system is absorbed in one
> relationship or in the poor functioning of one person is the degree to which other relationships and
> other people are protected from dysfunction. If one parent's functioning is impaired, the other
> parent may function better in reaction to it. If a child's functioning is impaired, his siblings and
> his parents may function better in reaction to that dysfunction. If parents are in conflict, the
> binding of anxiety in the marital relationship frees the children to function better.**"

And the count ladder:

> poorly differentiated — "**several people may be chronically symptomatic. All three patterns… are
> sufficiently active to generate symptoms**"; better — "**the poor functioning of one or two people or
> one or two relationships can absorb the family's inherent problem sufficiently to allow other family
> members to function adequately**"; better still — "**contained primarily within one relationship or
> within the poor functioning of one person**"; well differentiated — patterns present but "**the average
> level of chronic anxiety is so low that the patterns are not usually intensified to the point of
> significant clinical symptoms.**"

*Model consequence:* `M6.I.1`'s conservation restated as **protection**, worked for all three sinks
and directly assertable: raise one sink's allocation and the others' symptom load must fall. It is
also `FE03.14`'s strengthened `M11.C.1` with the **count** dimension made explicit — the number of
symptomatic members is a monotone function of level, which is a third assertable direction.

## ⭐⭐ FE07.13 — Target selection: five situations, and **family myths are unreliable** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**In most instances, it is the position a child is born into that makes his development more or less
> vulnerable to being shaped by parental anxieties. Family stories about whether a child was 'wanted'
> or 'unwanted' have little to do with why a particular child becomes the main target of parental
> anxiety and undifferentiation. Family myths about who was a 'favorite' or who was an 'outsider' are
> also notoriously unreliable indicators of what actually transpired between a child and his family. A
> family emotional system is programmed (based on emotional process of preceding generations) to focus
> its anxiety on certain places in the system. Anxiety that is not bound in the parental generation
> will usually focus on a child.**"

The situations named: **the firstborn** ("simply because he is the first on the emotional scene");
**the firstborn of a particular sex** ("Such families have a generational pattern of focusing either
on girls or boys"); **the last child** ("for no other reason than that he or she is the last");
**a child born during a period of unusual stress** (the third child born as the mother's mother dies);
and **physical defect or temperament** ("A retarded child or one with a congenital defect… can become
a repository for family anxiety").

*Model consequence:* **`M7.E.1c`'s five situations, 1988** — the spec cites `KS09.2` (2019) — plus two
additions:

- **The children are the residual sink.** "Anxiety that is **not bound in the parental generation**
  will usually focus on a child" is an *overflow* rule for `M1.D.1`'s allocation, and it gives the
  three sinks an ordering the spec does not have: the marital and spouse sinks fill first, the child
  sink takes the remainder. That is a strong structural claim and it is testable.
- **Family myths are unreliable, and the model should produce them.** `M9`'s belief store diverging
  from ground truth (`M9.1`) has a specific, named case here: a family holds a "favourite" or
  "unwanted" narrative that does **not** track the actual focus. `M9.5`'s requirement to report
  ground truth and belief separately is exactly what makes this checkable.

Also present, for the third time in this book: the **functional oldest** — "**An anxious focus on a
firstborn can also produce a fairly helpless oldest child. In such instances, a second child develops
to be more like a firstborn. He becomes a 'functional oldest.'**" (`FE02.13`, `M1.A.14a`, `KS12.2`.)

## ⭐⭐ FE07.14 — A nodal event can **permanently shift the family baseline** — a birth-cohort effect `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Sometimes the emotional atmosphere of a nuclear family is permanently changed after certain events
> such as a death of a parent's parent, a serious illness in one parent, a major occupational setback,
> or a significant geographical move. If the family was generally calmer before these events than after
> them, the older children are likely to grow up to have an average level of differentiation somewhat
> higher than the younger children. It is as if the children grew up in two different families. The
> opposite circumstances could also occur where a family is calmed by certain events and the younger
> children fare better (in terms of differentiation) than the older ones.**"

*Model consequence:* **a second mechanism for sibling divergence, orthogonal to `M7.E.1b`'s different
triangles, and it is two-sided.**

`M7.E.1b` explains sibling differences by the *triangle each occupies*. `FE07.14` adds a **temporal**
mechanism: the family's baseline shifts at a nodal event and children born on either side develop at
different levels. That is implementable with machinery the spec already has —
`M1.A.7`'s `programmed_reactivity` is fixed in childhood from **that person's own witnessed history**,
so a baseline shift automatically produces a cohort effect — and it means `M11.C.6`'s variance
requirement has two independent sources.

The two-sidedness matters and must be preserved: a nodal event can **calm** a family as readily as
disturb it, so the younger cohort can fare better. `M11.F.6` again, and it forbids the natural
implementation in which nodal events only ever cost.

## ⭐ FE07.15 — Symptoms in moderation calm; extreme symptoms **destabilise** — third statement `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Symptoms 'in moderation' can calm an individual and a family; symptoms that are more extreme
> usually add to everyone's anxiety.**"

And on children, with the exception spelled out in footnote 27:

> "the presence of symptoms, **particularly if they are life-threatening, can increase a family's level
> of anxiety; however, the presence of symptoms, particularly if they are not life-threatening, can
> also defuse the intensity of other problems in a family.**" … "**If a family's reaction to the symptoms
> of one child has propelled the family into an emotional regression, the family may be more relaxed
> after that child leaves. If that is the case, it is less likely that another child will develop
> symptoms.**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE03.9`'s non-monotone lock-in, stated for a third time in a third chapter.**
With `FE03.9` (footnotes 18 and 27 of Ch3) and `FE05.1`'s hot-potato exception, the amendment to
`M7.D.2a` and `M11.C.22` is now carried by three independent passages. The relation is an
**inverted-U in symptom severity**, and footnote 27 adds the consequence the criterion needs: above
the inflection, removal brings relief **and no substitution**.

## ⭐ FE07.16 — Compatibility becomes incompatibility: the **same trait, exaggerated** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The lower the level of differentiation, however, the more likely it is that the sources of people's
> compatibility will become the very sources of their 'incompatibility.'** … Ways of thinking, feeling,
> and acting that promote an emotional fit between people in the beginning of their relationship
> become exaggerated in response to pressures that develop later… **Qualities that are attractive in
> moderation become unattractive when they are more extreme — charm becomes irresponsibility and
> decisiveness becomes overbearingness.**"

*Model consequence:* `FE05.3`'s amplitude-gating rule applied to the marital tie, and it removes a
mechanism the model would otherwise need. `M1.B.5`'s polarisation does **not** require a separate
"conflict trait" — the polarised trait **is** the attracting trait at higher amplitude. One quantity,
two regimes, which is `M10.A.2`'s criterion.

It also gives `M2.A.0f`'s complementarity a dynamic: the pairing rule selects for a fit, and the same
fit is what fails under load.

## ⭐ FE07.17 — Content varies; **process is universal** — and both routes are named `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Parents can instill a trait, an attitude, a feeling, or a type of functioning in a child that was
> not inherent in the child by treating the child as if that trait… is inherent in him. Parents can
> also exaggerate a trait, a feeling, or a type of functioning that is inherent in the child by
> anxiously focusing on it.** … **The content, in other words, is much less important than the intensity
> of the emotional process that underlies the content. Content varies from family to family and from
> culture to culture; emotional process is the same (varying only in degree of intensity) in all
> families and in all cultures.**"

*Model consequence:* the sharpest form of `FE00.4`'s content/process distinction, and it bears on
`M12.1`'s withdrawn **"content is noise"** item. Note precisely what is said: content is "**much less
important**", **not** irrelevant — and content is what determines *which* trait is instilled or
exaggerated. So the withdrawn mechanism stays withdrawn and the model's position is: **carry the
process, record the content, and never derive an effect size from the content.**

The two routes — **instill what was not there**, **exaggerate what was** — are the projection loop
(`FE07.4`) and `M1.A.11b`'s constitutional channel respectively, and the chapter is explicit that both
occur.

## ⭐ FE07.18 — A child's irresponsibility reflects the **parents'** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**When children are not accepting responsibility for their own actions, this indicates that their
> parents are not accepting responsibility for their own actions. If a child is continually
> irresponsible, this does not reflect a failure by his parents to 'teach' him to be responsible.**
> Characteristically, the parents have devoted considerable energy to trying to convince the child he
> 'should' be responsible; in fact, they have usually harped on the subject… **This focus on what the
> child 'should' do undermines the child's emotional separation from the parents and fosters his
> irresponsibility.** … **If parents shift their focus off the child and become more responsible for
> their own actions, the child will automatically (perhaps after testing whether the parents really
> mean it) assume more responsibility for himself.** … **Children are not wild animals that need to be
> tamed.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.E.3`'s **responsibility as a conserved transferable quantity** operating on
the parent–child tie rather than the coach tie — the family cannot hold what the parent is holding.
That generalises `M1.E.3` off `role = EXTERNAL`, which is where the spec confines it, and it makes
`FE04.21`'s therapy-through-a-relative rule the same mechanism.

"**perhaps after testing whether the parents really mean it**" is `M5.E`'s ladder in miniature, on a
tie the spec does not currently run it on. And footnote 23 fixes the mover: "'**Parents' do not shift
their focus; one parent takes the first step.**" A single mover, not a pair — which matters for
`M11.C.17`'s arm design.

## ⭐ FE07.19 — Parents are the only ones who can lead `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The emotional functioning of the parents is the major determinant of whether anxiety escalates in
> a nuclear family. The parents are the 'heads' of a family and the only ones really capable of
> assuming responsibility for leadership, particularly during a crisis. Sometimes, in the face of
> parents who are floundering, a child will attempt to provide leadership, but he usually lacks the
> authority to be effective.** … **A 'free spirit' can have a calming effect on a family, but he or she
> cannot change the basic direction of the family process.** So when anxiety is high in a family, **the
> parents are as much out of control of their own functioning as the symptomatic child is out of
> control of his own functioning.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.D.4`'s `leadership_office` with an **authority gate** the spec does not
carry: a child's leadership attempt fails **for want of authority**, not for want of capacity. That
is `M1.D.4`'s *sphere of responsibility* as a move-permission scope, applied to a mover rather than
to a move.

It also bears on a convergence the explainer flags as weak. §16 records "the peripheral member moves
first" as **weak and contradicted by Ch05**, whose reconciliation is *the leader drives the process,
the peripheral member performs the visible position change.* `FE07.19` supports the **leader** side
plainly, and adds that the peripheral member's effect is real but bounded — the free spirit calms
and cannot redirect. Worth folding into that reconciliation.

## ⭐ FE07.20 — Mind–body and person–person, named as the two assumptions `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The two assumptions can be summarized as 'mind-body link' and 'person-person link.' The existence
> of a mind-body link and a person-person link means that it is possible for anxiety in one person to
> be manifested as a physical symptom in another person.**"

*Model consequence:* the model's two required couplings named in one sentence — `M4.C.1`'s routed
anxiety is the person-person link, `M7.D.1`'s symptom accumulation from routed load is the mind-body
link, and their composition is what `M11.C.2` asserts. Useful for `M11.F`, because the composed claim
("anxiety in one person appears as a physical symptom in another") is the model's most
counterintuitive output and it should be presented as the composition of two stated assumptions
rather than as a finding.

Kerr's own hedge travels with it: "**most people do not yet accept this interrelationship as fact.**"

## FE07.21 — ⚠ The trauma claim — record, flag, do not implement `[K]` `[X]`

> "**While physical abuse of a child by a parent has a destructive effect on the emotional development
> of a child, the child's life course is more influenced by the lack of emotional separation between
> him and his parents than by the abuse itself.**"

Footnote 25: "**Family systems theory assigns less importance to 'traumatic events' in understanding
an individual's emotional development than it does to ongoing family process. Events may highlight
some aspects of the nature of the process, but the events are not the process.**"

*Model consequence:* **record as `[X]`, do not implement, and say why.**

This is a 1988 clinical position on a question where the wider evidence base has moved a great deal
since, and the chapter offers no series, no comparison group and no measurement in support of it —
the corpus-wide `Q-VALIDATION = NO` applies with full force. The model has no mechanism that requires
it: `M1.F.6`'s stressor spells and `M4.C.3a`'s time-above-floor integrator represent events by their
load and duration without ranking events against process at all.

**`M11.F` requirement candidate:** no output may be presented as adjudicating the relative weight of
discrete traumatic events against ongoing relational process. The model does not distinguish them and
must not be read as having an opinion.

Note that the theoretical point *underneath* the claim is separable and is carried elsewhere without
this baggage: `FE07.17`'s content/process distinction, and `M1.D.8`'s requirement that the family not
be the boundary.

## FE07.22 — Adopted children develop the **adoptive** family's level `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 18:

> "**The levels of differentiation developed by adopted children are governed by the same emotional
> processes that govern the levels developed by a family's biological children. So if a child is
> adopted as an infant, his level of differentiation will be determined by the emotional process in
> his adoptive family. If a child is adopted when he is a little older, his level of differentiation
> will be influenced both by his adoptive family and by the significant emotional attachments he had
> prior to the adoption.**"

*Model consequence:* `FE03.11` confirmed from a different direction — **genes do not set
`basic_level`** — and a clean statement that the determinant is the **emotional field the child
develops in**, weighted by time in it. That is `M1.A.7`'s fixation from witnessed history exactly,
and the graded case (adopted later) is a direct prediction: a partial weighting between two fields.

`M2.A` has no adopted member and none is needed; the value here is as a **falsifier of the genetic
reading** of `M7.E`, which is the misreading `M7.E.1a` most invites.
