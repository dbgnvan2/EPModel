# Ch03 — Family Relationships in Schizophrenia

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** not stated. The chapter carries no dateline, conference, or journal attribution in its body. Internal dating markers only: it opens with Sterne's *Tristram Shandy* as "Two hundred years ago (1762)"; it cites Bowen 1960, Bowen et al. 1959, Wynne et al. 1958, Lidz 1958, Lidz et al. 1957, Spiegel 1957, Jackson and Bateson 1956, Ackerman 1956, Mittelman 1956; and it says "After working at this problem for over three years."
- **Clinical setting and data source:** A clinical research project on a psychiatric ward in a research center. Fathers, mothers, schizophrenic patients, and normal siblings lived together on the ward. **Four in-residence families**, participating up to two and a half years, average one and a half years, with "detailed around-the-clock observations on each family member." Plus **six outpatient families** (father, mother, and a moderately disturbed psychotic patient) treated up to two years. **Ten family groups** total, followed longitudinally. Project staff named in the note: the author, Robert H. Dysinger M.D., Warren M. Brodey M.D., Betty Basamania M.S.S. The first year of the project had a different design: three schizophrenic patients and their mothers on the ward, each in individual psychotherapy.
- **Genre:** both, roughly **45% theory / 55% technique**. The first third is a review of observed family patterns and the family-unit concept; the remaining two-thirds is a hour-by-hour narrative of one outpatient family's course, from which the "basic rules" of family psychotherapy are drawn. The two are not separable — Bowen explicitly says the technique was derived from the observations and then fed back into the theory.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Conflict localizes in the weakest member.** Across the ten research families, a high level of emotional conflict was present; for brief periods it could be spread across several members, but it tended to localize in "the family member who is in the weakest and most inadequate position" — in these families, the schizophrenic one.
- **Diagnosis crystallizes the localization and raises anxiety.** When the conflict was "fixed" in the weakest member by a diagnosis and the designation *patient*, the family problem became more crystallized in that person and family anxiety was "greatly increased."
- **The reverse also happened.** When parents and patient were brought onto the ward together and the designation *patient* was deliberately left ambiguous, the conflict "again becomes more fluid and shifting" and the parents themselves developed intense anxiety and conflict.
- **Parents function differently inside and outside the family.** Parents could be adequate and successful in business and social relationships while being "immature, indecisive, and inadequate" within the family.
- **The conflict stays in a threesome.** The primary participants were father, mother, and patient. Normal siblings, even those living in residence as long as a year, were involved much less than anticipated; they could be intensely involved at times but "could always separate themselves from the conflict" and leave the three to continue cycling.
- **Emotional distance between the parents in all families** — either a formal, positive, controlled relationship with feeling obliterated, or open disagreement managed by physical distance, or a mixture.
- **A constant overadequate/inadequate split between the parents**, with no mid-ground available to either.
- **Highly charged opposing viewpoints about how to relate to the psychotic patient — but not about the normal siblings.** The disagreement may be invisible to the parents themselves until psychotherapy is under way; a mother could go years believing the father agreed with her.
- **Reciprocal, near-immediate change.** When one family member changed, complementing changes appeared in the others "almost immediately." Observed instance: the father's rise to self-assertion → the mother became tremulous, tearful, overtly anxious within days, then calm, objective, firm within two weeks → the emotional divorce resolved → the daughter began significant changes toward more adequate functioning. The whole configuration reverted after a month.
- **Characteristics believed fixed turned out not to be.** The project first tried to define each member's fixed characteristics; observing them change forced abandonment of that framing.
- **Physical illness at the moment of pulling up.** Two fathers reverted to inadequate functioning when they developed minor physical illnesses. In the case family, the father developed an acute intestinal upset requiring an operation just as he was being pressed toward more adequate functioning.
- **Fusion of feeling between mother and patient.** The mother recalled that when her daughter injured her head as a child, her own head instantly hurt in the exact spot. The daughter independently confirmed she had never been able to know how she herself felt — she depended on the mother to tell her, and discounted any feeling of her own that differed. Away at school she could have her own feelings unless someone told her how she must feel; at home the ability was lost again.
- **Differential maternal investment by birth order.** The mother was overinvested in the first daughter before she knew she was pregnant; the second daughter "never occupied this position of importance in the mother's thoughts" and "somehow was able to get along by herself."
- **Families with the most active intrafamily resistance, emotion, and disagreement have done best in psychotherapy.**
- **[p2] Maternal worry about the unborn child rose and fell with the marital distance.** "Her worries about the child were more intense when she was emotionally detached from the father. The worries decreased when she was closer to the father." The investment seesaw is observable *before the child exists as a person* and therefore before any behaviour of the child can be confounding it.
- **[p2] The reciprocity flip has a stated comparative trigger.** "When he had reached a point of self-assertion that was greater than the mother's aggression and domination, she immediately went through a series of dramatic changes." Not "when he asserted" — when his assertion *exceeded* hers. The flip is immediate; the *settling* is what takes days to two weeks.
- **[p2] A cluster of simultaneous changes at the daughter's adolescence.** Over a two-year period: the daughter began to act grown-up and deny the need for the mother, and became outgoing and overactive in a drive for friends; the mother was offered a job and began to work; the father had business reverses, took on more responsibility, and worked longer hours; the parents moved to separate bedrooms. The child's bid for autonomy and the parents' mutual withdrawal-plus-external-investment are reported as *concurrent*, not sequential.
- **[p2] Physical separation did not decouple.** She "looked forward to college and living away from home to bring about an emancipation from the family," and "developed her first acute psychotic break while living away at college." Separation produced the break, not the emancipation.
- **[p2] Fusion returns at full strength whenever anyone occupies the telling-you-how-to-feel position.** "Away at school she could have some feelings of her own *unless a teacher or some such person suggested she must feel a certain way*." A substitute occupant restores the effect. The variable is occupancy of a position, not distance from the mother.
- **[p2] A complete reaction chain in the opening hours, terminating in a move by the peripheral member.** Mother comments on the psychotic behaviour → daughter denies → denial becomes highly emotional contradiction → mother repeats "sick" and proves it with historical material → the daughter's emotion and anxiety rise → at a certain level of anger she "would suddenly shift to paranoid delusions" and scream she would kill the boyfriend → mother yells "Shut up, you will disturb other people" → daughter cries → mother cries and says it is hopeless → the father moves to silence the daughter and says to the mother, "Don't cry. It is not so bad." The father's only entry into the sequence is to suppress the symptom-bearer and comfort the overadequate one, and it is triggered by tears.
- **[p2] Blocking one charged topic reroutes to another of equal charge.** In hospital, "The daughter agreed not to mention the paranoid delusions which upset the mother. She kept the promise but took up another equally sensitive issue which created a scene." The content changed; the scene did not.
- **[p2] The mother made her greatest progress with only two members present** — during the father's two-week hospitalisation, at nine months and sixty hours. This is the chapter's own counter-instance to its rule that any two of the threesome go sterile (see X1 [p2]).
- **[p2] The natural experiment has a matched second half.** The daughter found a job the day after the mother left; "The girl lost her job the week the mother returned, but she immediately found another on her own initiative." Removal and return of the overfunctioner both register.
- **[p2] Decision difficulty is independent of the decision's stakes.** "a father who has no problem in important decisions at work but who can end up in an emotional, paralyzing deadlock when he and the mother try to decide which movie to see."
- **[p2] The peripheral member's non-reaction was helpful.** With the daughter extremely anxious about being fired, "the father's unexcited do-nothing attitude seemed helpful."
- **[p2] Information already known has no effect until self-generated.** The mother concluded that parents should let their children lead their own lives; "Although she must have heard this hundreds of times, she reacted as if she had just discovered a fresh new truth for herself."
- **[p2] The coalition inside the triad inverts over the course.** Opening hours: mother and father act together to quiet the daughter. Closing hours: the mother proposes moving the daughter to individual therapy and "The father and daughter opposed this."

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **The family unit / family as a single organism** → the family is treated as one entity whose sides act against each other, rather than a collection of individuals → acts on how symptoms are attributed and where treatment is aimed. Schizophrenia in the patient is "the symptom manifestation of a problem that involved the entire family."
- **The functioning of one person in relation to another** → replaces fixed personal characteristics with position-in-a-relationship as the unit of description → acts on every trait claim: what looks like a person's nature is their current function relative to others. This is the chapter's central theoretical move, and Bowen dates it to the observed reciprocal changes.
- **Reciprocal functioning** → the process by which all family members participate in concentrating conflict into one of them → acts on the distribution of conflict across members. Explicitly not victimization: "It is inaccurate to say that the patient is a 'victim' in this process."
- **The interdependent triad** (father, mother, patient) → the closed unit within which the conflict circulates → acts to exclude other family members from the process.
- **The emotional divorce** → a fixed emotional distance between the parents, arrived at after early alternating periods of overcloseness and overdistance settling into a "more fixed, and less anxious" distance → acts on the parental dyad, making a close parent–parent relationship unavailable.
- **Overadequate–inadequate reciprocity** → in any joint activity, whoever makes the decision becomes overadequate and the other becomes helpless; both are "equally immature"; neither can find mid-ground → acts on the parental pair, and later (in the case) on parent–child pairs too. Solved by *reducing the areas of joint activity* and increasing individual activity (father to business, mother to home and children), or by avoiding/postponing the decision, or by one "taking the bull by the horns."
- **Custody-sharing of the patient** → because the parents cannot be close to each other, either can be close to the patient *only if the other permits* → acts on the patient's availability to each parent. Functionally like divorced parents sharing children; the mother usually holds "custody," the father is excluded or excludes himself.
- **The re-attachment cycle** → mother acts to keep the patient attached and blames the father for lack of interest → father, believing his poor relationship is his own failure as a father, moves toward the patient → if the patient shows too much interest in the father, the mother moves to intensify the attachment to her → cycle repeats. Variants: mother arranges father–patient time but initiates it, so the father functions as a babysitter; or the patient becomes so hostile that the mother withdraws and the father moves in, but "still functions as a substitute mother," and the mother can win the patient back even after long absences.
- **Scapegoating / fixing the problem in the patient** → a long-term shifting process, participated in by parents and patient alike, that locates the family problem in one person; the diagnostic label is the event that fixes it.
- **The patient as absorber of family anxiety** → "The schizophrenic patients in our families have been like sponges for absorbing family anxiety." When the father was made anxious and inadequate, the daughter "rushed in to bolster the anxious father, except that her help came out in psychotic schemes."
- **Fusion of feeling / the invisible wall** → the mother's and the daughter's emotional lives are joined so the daughter cannot generate her own feeling; the mother's remedy, self-generated in therapy, was to "put an invisible wall between us so I can have my life and she can have hers."
- **Investment redistribution** → when the parents became invested in each other, "neither was overinvested in the patient," and both could be objective toward the patient for the first time.
- **[p2] The reciprocity equilibrium is a partition of AREAS, not a single scalar** → "they eventually find an equilibrium in which one is overadequate in most areas and the other overadequate in fewer areas" → acts per domain of joint activity, with the aggregate position being a count of areas held. This is the same variable that "reduce the areas of joint activity" operates on: scope reduction removes contested domains rather than changing the pole assignment inside any of them. Pass 1 treated these as two separate mechanisms.
- **[p2] The switching rule for the reciprocity** → the pole flips when the inadequate one's self-assertion *exceeds* the other's aggression and domination, and holds only if he then maintains the stand through her anxiety reaction → the mother's own statement of the condition: "If he can keep on being a man, then I can be a woman." Two conditions: exceed, then hold through the counter-reaction.
- **[p2] Concession as attack** → where the parents disagree openly about the patient, "they may alternate their plans for the patient, each one 'letting the other have his way to prove him wrong'" → yielding is a move in the conflict, not an exit from it.
- **[p2] Dependence relocates from person to unit rather than being eliminated** → "When the therapist can avoid individual relationships, there usually develops a dependent relationship with the family unit which is then analyzed." The neutral position does not remove the dependency; it changes what the dependency attaches to, and the attachment target becomes the working object.
- **[p2] Utterance content is instrumental to the reaction it produces** → of the parents' comments during the sexuality phase: "These comments seemed to be directed more for the parents' reactions than for their reality value." Stated as a general reading of what family speech is for, alongside the specific rule about not evaluating historical material.
- **[p2] Financial exhaustion as a binding constraint on the care pathway** → "The family home was mortgaged to finance private treatment. Eventually the family finances were completely exhausted and she was transferred to a state institution." The only place in the chapter where a material resource is stated as *causing* an event rather than co-varying with position.

## A3. Predictive claims

Bowen's evidential status is noted for each. All of it is observational report on ten families; none is statistical.

1. **If a diagnosis and the designation *patient* are applied to the weakest member, then the family problem crystallizes in that person and family anxiety greatly increases.** — asserted from observation across the ten families.
2. **If the designation *patient* is left ambiguous and the family lives together, then the conflict becomes fluid and shifting again and the parents develop intense anxiety and conflict.** — reported as observed on the ward.
3. **If one family member changes significantly, then complementing changes follow in the other members of the triad almost immediately.** — reported as an observed regularity; multiple instances given (father → mother → daughter; daughter improves → mother becomes symptomatic).
4. **If the parent in the inadequate position becomes more assertive and active, then the parent in the overadequate position shifts to the inadequate position.** — stated as the summary formulation of overadequate–inadequate reciprocity; evidenced by the first-family episode and "two families with dramatic changes."
5. **If the parents can resolve the emotional divorce, then the psychotic patient begins to change toward more mature functioning.** — stated in the summary; evidenced by the first-family episode.
6. **If the parents become invested in each other, then neither is overinvested in the patient and both become able to be objective toward the patient.** — one observed instance.
7. **If the psychotic one improves, then it is usually the mother who becomes symptomatic.** — asserted as a pattern ("usually"); one instance narrated in detail.
8. **If a first change occurs and reverts, then thereafter it is easier for the father to pull up to adequate functioning and less threatening for the mother to give up the overadequate position.** — stated twice, once for the first family and once as a generalization over the fathers.
9. **If a parent in the inadequate position attempts to pull up to more adequate functioning, then physical illness is not unusual** (and may return him to inadequate functioning). — two fathers reverted with minor physical illness; the case father was hospitalized.
10. **If only two of the father–mother–patient threesome are present, then the therapy becomes more intellectual, more sterile, and less profitable**; with all three present and the structure maintained, conflict and disagreement arise, producing "high anxiety, action, and progress." — reported as project experience; the basis of a rule.
11. **If the therapist tries to influence a family in a particular direction (e.g. pushing the passive father to assert), then the change is attributed to the therapist and collapses.** — one narrated incident ("The doctor told me I should do it this way") led the project to abandon the strategy.
12. **If therapists become critical of the family member who gets things done, then they end up with passive, complaining families that wait for the therapists to supply answers.** — asserted from project experience, worse with hospitalized families.
13. **If the family leader drops the lead position, then the therapy stalls** — the two-month impasse is attributed to the mother not resuming her leader function after vacation, and resolved when the therapist recognized her position.
14. **A family with the most active intrafamily resistance, emotion, and disagreement does best in psychotherapy.** — asserted, comparative across the ten.
15. **Outpatient families as a group have progressed more rapidly than inpatient families** — attributed to inpatient families becoming entangled with staff, other family groups, and the hospital environment.
16. **The father cannot have a real father relationship to the patient until he deals directly with the mother.** — explicitly flagged as Bowen's own current view ("As I currently see it").
17. **A fairly normal family is one in which parents can function in either the strong or the weak position according to the demands of the situation, without threatening either one.** — offered as a postulate derived from the observed changes.
18. **Absence of the therapist causes much less of a problem in family cases than in individual therapy.** — asserted; consistent with the vacation episode.
19. **[p2] If the inadequate one's self-assertion exceeds the overadequate one's aggression and domination, then the poles flip immediately** — and the flip holds only while the mover maintains his stand through the other's anxiety. Stated as the mechanism of the first-family episode; the comparative ("greater than") is Bowen's own wording.
20. **[p2] If the mother is emotionally detached from the father, then her worry-investment in the child is more intense; if closer, less.** — reported of the prenatal period of the case family. One family, one narrated period.
21. **[p2] If a charged topic is closed off by agreement, then an equally sensitive topic is substituted and the same scene occurs.** — one narrated instance (the hospital promise).
22. **[p2] If a conclusion is reached by the person herself, it acts; the same conclusion heard from outside "hundreds of times" did not.** — one narrated instance, stated by Bowen as the notable feature of it.
23. **[p2] Contradicting claim 10 within the same case: the mother made her greatest progress during the father's absence,** i.e. with only two of the threesome present. Bowen reports this without noting the tension with his own rule.

## A5. Terminology

- **family unit** — the family thought of and related to "as though it were a single unit or single organism." Both an observational stance and a treatment target.
- **family psychotherapy** — psychotherapy directed at the family unit rather than the individual, with all members attending all hours together. Bowen names this as one of the two important concepts of the orientation, alongside the family unit.
- **interdependent triad** — father, mother, and patient, named as the unit within which the family conflict circulates. (Note: this is doing the structural job that "triangle" does in Bowen's later vocabulary; the word *triangle* does not appear in this chapter. It is described as a fixed membership — the three specific people — not as a general relationship form.) **[p2] Fixed membership does not mean fixed positions.** Occupancy of the adequate/inadequate positions and of the inside/outside (close/excluded) positions changes repeatedly, and Bowen states the position shift as his general finding in the summary. See PASS 2 P5 / Q-TRIAD; the "positions do not yet shift" gloss recorded at pass 1 in `_LEDGER.md` and `_CONVERGENCES.md` is wrong and needs correcting there.
- **emotional divorce** — the striking emotional distance between the parents, present in all the families; began with alternating overcloseness/overdistance early in the marriage and settled into a fixed, less anxious distance.
- **overadequate–inadequate reciprocity** — the constant pattern in which one parent takes the decision-making, dominating position and the other becomes helpless and compliant, with no mid-ground; either parent can occupy either position, though an equilibrium develops.
- **functioning of one person in relation to another** — the concept the project moved toward, replacing static diagnostic labels and fixed personal characteristics. Bowen credits Spiegel's role theory as including the idea of function without stressing it.
- **family diagnosis** — a wanted-but-absent alternative to individual diagnoses; Bowen agrees with those seeking one and cites Ackerman's "interlocking pathology" and Mittelman's "reciprocal relationships."
- **disturbed family** — the accurate label for a family whose conflict has become fluid again and whose parents are anxious, as opposed to a family with a designated patient.
- **scapegoat** — the position the patient occupies as the family problem locates in them; Bowen immediately rejects "victim" as a description of it.
- **family leader / the one who motivates the situation / the one in the adequate position** — the parent who makes decisions and gets things done; treated as a functional office that can be occupied by different people over time (the daughter takes it by hour 77).
- **in situ** — analyzing the family's existing intense relationships where they are, rather than allowing them to transfer to the therapist.
- **"schizophrenogenic"** — used once, in scare quotes and dismissively, about actions of a motivating parent that the staff might be tempted to criticize.
- **the top of the stadium / low-power lens** — Bowen's two metaphors for the family-unit viewpoint: it reveals broad patterns of movement and team functioning that the close-up view obscures, and it *enhances* rather than replaces the close-up view.

Terms conspicuously **absent** here: differentiation of self, triangle, projection process, undifferentiated family ego mass, multigenerational transmission, fusion (as a term of art — "fusion of feelings" appears once as plain description), societal regression. Anxiety is used descriptively throughout, never defined or scaled.

**[p2] Absence verified by count over the chapter text.** Zero occurrences of *symbiosis*, *projection*, *ego mass*, *triangle*, *cutoff/cut-off*, *generation*. *Fusion* occurs exactly once, in "this fusion of feelings," as description of the mother–daughter pair. *Differentiat-* occurs twice and in **both cases means "to distinguish"** — differentiating family psychotherapy from individual and from group psychotherapy — never differentiation of self; a keyword search on the stem would produce a false positive here. *Transfer* occurs five times: four are *transference/countertransference*, one is the daughter's physical transfer to a state institution. There is no "transfer of anxiety" in Ch01's sense. *Immature/immaturity* occurs three times, *mature* once ("more mature functioning").

**[p2] A caveat that changes what the absences license.** Bowen states an explicit scope decision: "I shall defocus, as much as possible, the more specific relationship characteristics which have been reported in other papers." Absence of a concept from this chapter is therefore partly a *deliberate editorial exclusion*, not necessarily evidence that he did not hold it in 1960. ## A6. Structure and roles

- **The three positions of the interdependent triad:** father, mother, patient. Bowen states the conflict "seemed to remain pretty much in the father-mother-patient triad."
- **Normal siblings are peripheral by exit.** Even in residence for a year, they could become intensely involved but "could always separate themselves from the conflict." In the case family the younger daughter separated after college, became a schoolteacher in a distant state, and "has made a good adjustment"; she was "the only one who changed during this period" when the family was most prostrate.
- **The most common configuration:** overadequate mother attached to the helpless patient, father peripheral to the intense mother–patient twosome. "The one most striking pattern in the research families has been that of the aggressive mother and the passive peripheral father."
- **Sibling position and maternal investment:** the patient in the case was the older of two daughters; the mother was overinvested in her from before she knew she was pregnant, and the investment was renewed at the second child's birth ("she needs me more than the baby"). The second daughter never occupied that position.
- **Custody:** "The mother usually has the primary relationship or 'custody' of the patient while the father is excluded or permits himself to be excluded."
- **The person who moves first:** in the two dramatic-change families and the first family, "the first to change had been the fathers." The project reasoned from this that therapy might be facilitated by focusing on the father's passivity — and then abandoned that reasoning (see B2/B4).
- **The family leader is whoever asks for treatment.** "The parent in the adequate decision-making position is usually the one who asks about treatment. It is common for other family members to oppose either actively or passively."
- **The lead position circulates.** Over the fifteen months: mother (leader, breadwinner) → dropped after vacation, producing the impasse → mother resumes → mother relinquishes → daughter picks it up and holds it through hour 84, while the father moves toward a more adequate position relative to the mother.
- **Marital origin detail:** the father moved to the town as a single man to establish his business, met the mother through a business acquaintance while she was visiting, brief courtship, he took a few days off for the wedding.
- **Statement of role division:** "It is common for fathers to devote themselves almost entirely to business and for mothers to be completely in charge of home and children" — offered as a *solution* to the anxiety of the overadequate–inadequate reciprocity, by reducing joint activity.
- **[p2] Custody is permission-gated and the permission is revocable in both directions.** "either can have a close relationship with the patient if the other permits." The mother can vacate the inside position — the patient becomes so hostile that "the mother rejects the patient or goes away," the father moves in — and can retake it: "the mother can usually win back the patient, even after long absences." She also vacates it deliberately: in hospital she "decided against visits 'for the good of the daughter,' but she encouraged the father to go regularly." Inside/outside occupancy within the triad is contested, reversible and sometimes voluntarily transferred. See PASS 2 P5 / Q-TRIAD.
- **[p2] The overadequate/inadequate assignment is held per area, not globally** — one parent overadequate in most areas, the other in fewer. A person is not "the overadequate one" but the holder of a set of domains.
- **[p2] The coalition structure inverts.** Opening: mother and father act jointly to quiet the daughter. Middle: mother attacks the father's inadequacy, the daughter holds the lead. Close: the mother proposes individual therapy for the daughter and "The father and daughter opposed this." All three positions and both alliance edges change occupancy over fifteen months while membership never changes.
- **[p2] The younger daughter's exit is by physical and functional departure, and it is the chapter's only reported improvement during the prostration years:** she "separated herself from the family after college," became "a schoolteacher in a distant state," and was "the only one who changed during this period." Set against Ch01's ledger entry (L01.7), where the projection relocates *onto* a well sibling: this chapter reports siblings leaving the field and not being recruited into it.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **Have the whole family present at every hour.** All family members attend all psychotherapy hours together.
- **Basic rule 1 — make the family work on its own problem in the hour while the therapist observes from the sideline** and "attempts to understand and analyze the emotional process between the family members." The goal is to get the family working together on the problem.
- **Analyze the family's avoidance of the structure.** When the decision-making member engages the therapist in conversation to escape the anxiety, the therapist structures himself out of the family's working-together effort, "preferably by analyzing the family effort to avoid the structure."
- **Name the pattern, not the content.** In the twenty hours of mother–daughter accusation cycling, "the therapist confined himself to pointing out the 'proof that I am wonderful — proof that you are terrible' pattern."
- **Comment on what he just observed, in process terms.** Father: "See, I was squelched." Therapist: "I just watched you get yourself squelched."
- **Basic rule 2 — read out individual communications to the group.** Any telephone call, letter, or message from one member aimed at winning the therapist to an individual viewpoint "will be discussed at the next meeting of the family group." Done literally: the daughter's letter from hospital was read at the next hour with the parents.
- **Basic rule 3 — two members minimum.** Family psychotherapy continues with only two members present, but if only one can attend, meetings are discontinued until at least two are available; one member would make it individual therapy.
- **Basic rule 4 — respect and support the one in the adequate position as family leader and negotiator**, "even if his actions appear traumatic or 'schizophrenogenic.'" Recognize the effort of the one who motivates the family.
- **Designate a spokesman for arrangements** (appointments, changes), while telling the family they may designate a different spokesman any time they wish.
- **Refuse to be the source of answers, explicitly.** "Somewhere I may have led you to believe I knew some answers. Actually, psychiatry has never found an answer to schizophrenia, though the premise of family therapy is that the family can find its own answers if they work on it." He then announced he would fall back and become "a nonparticipating, note-taking observer."
- **Poll the family on how they heard him.** When the mother heard the fall-back statement as abandonment, the therapist "asked the others for their perception of his comments"; she was amazed to be alone in hearing it that way. This started family discussions at home about differences in hearing.
- **Refuse to arbitrate hospitalization; hand the decision back with a frame.** "It is the parents' responsibility to determine when you are no longer able to carry on at home. People go to mental hospitals when the family or when society asks for it. Many very upset people continue to live with their families. It is a family decision to determine when the family can no longer remain together at home. Mental hospitals do impose difficult situations for patients but the human organism is capable of growth when it has to adjust to difficult situations." Then: they should make their own decision.
- **Neutralize the diagnostic label by symmetrizing it.** Asked his opinion on "sick": it made no difference to him what label they used; if they wanted *schizophrenic* he would insist the whole family be called schizophrenic; if the parents were *normal* then the daughter must also be called normal. "The issue never came up again."
- **Interrupt overfunctioning by question, not instruction.** When the mother started to cancel her vacation because the daughter presented an "I cannot get along without you" picture: "The therapist asked if the mother was treating the daughter as if she really were helpless."
- **Ask the anxiety-tolerating question about consequences.** During the daughter's job anxiety: "What will happen if she is fired? Will she collapse or disintegrate, or will she learn from the experience?"
- **Refuse a request for private time, on the rule, and name where else it could go.** The daughter asked for ten minutes alone; the therapist refused, said this was family therapy and he would not change the rule, and that if she just had to talk personally she would have to find someone else. She raised the topic in the family hour instead.
- **Refuse to convert to individual therapy** when the mother proposed it for the daughter: he "thought they had not exhausted the advantages of family therapy and he would not change to individual therapy."
- **Detach emotionally enough to work without becoming overinvolved** in the ebb and flow of conflictual emotion.
- **Avoid individual-oriented psychiatric vocabulary** and force the use of simple descriptive words, because familiar terms cause an immediate associative shift back to individual thinking.
- **Extend the working hypothesis only after a clinical pattern has repeated itself sufficiently**, then change the psychotherapy approach to match it.
- **[p2] When reading out a private communication, route the reply back through the family and return the responsibility to the sender.** He read the daughter's hospital letter to the parents, said family matters would be discussed with those able to attend, and added that *if they wished*, they could convey to her that he had received it "and that she was probably more familiar than he with requirements for discharge from the hospital." The reply is not sent to her; it is offered to the family to relay, and its content hands her question back to her.
- **[p2] Accept that a dependent relationship forms on the family unit and analyse it.** Avoiding individual relationships does not produce an undependent family; "there usually develops a dependent relationship with the family unit which is then analyzed."
- **[p2] The therapist's refusal to supply answers has a family-side twin, and it appears in the mother's own speech.** Where she had been "overhelpful and advising," she came to say "Make up your own mind what you want to do and do it," and to the daughter's pleas of helplessness, "It is your life. You decide." Bowen reports the same operation performed from inside the family, without commenting on the parallel.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Must not participate in the intense emotional process in the family.** "It should be obvious that the therapist is not permitted to participate." Taking sides silently is not safe: "Even when the therapist takes sides without expressing it, the other family members can become aware of it and react negatively toward this countertransference participation."
- **Must not permit individual relationships or transference to himself.** The goal is to leave the existing intense relationships inside the family and analyze them *in situ*. Things that encourage the individual relationship were deliberately defined and avoided. The one deliberate experiment in an individual relationship — responding to the daughter as in individual therapy during the impasse — produced immediate liveliness in her, spectator parents, and two telephone calls within the week asking him to decide her job for her. It was abandoned after one hour.
- **Must not try to influence the family in a particular direction.** The deliberate strategy of focusing on the passivity of fathers was eventually abandoned after a father justified his new assertiveness with "The doctor told me I should do it this way." From then on the therapists stuck to analyzing what went on in the families.
- **Must not evaluate the dramatic historical material.** "A therapist can lose his way if he permits himself to become involved in evaluating the dramatic historical material that comes up in such hours."
- **Must not become critical of the family member who gets things done** — this produces passive, complaining families that wait for the therapist to supply answers.
- **Must not take a side on hospitalization.** Agreeing with the parents supports the family's forces to "fix" the problem in the daughter; agreeing with the daughter might delay parental steps to protect the community. The therapist stayed neutral.
- **Must not try to persuade opposing family members into treatment.** "The therapists have been complete failures at this." The adequate parent has ways to overcome the resistance; the therapist does not.
- **Must not agree with the family process to fix the problem in the patient**, and generally avoid diagnostic labels.
- **Must not meet with only one family member.**
- **Must not accept private communications as private.**

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition — theory before technique.** The psychotherapy approach was changed only after the research hypothesis was extended, and the hypothesis was extended only after a clinical pattern had repeated itself sufficiently.
- **Precondition — the staff must first re-learn to think in family-unit terms**, which the chapter treats as the major problem: everyone is trained to think in individual terms, all diagnostic language is individual, and even after some success, familiar terms trigger an immediate slip back.
- **Precondition — a family member must ask.** The adequate parent typically initiates; the therapist offers family therapy or a referral elsewhere for individual therapy and then waits. In the case, the wait was ten days and required the mother to leave home.
- **Precondition for the therapist's freedom to observe** — the parents must be present to deal with the patient's upset. In individual work the psychotic patient can create a crisis that forces the analyst into dealing with rather than analyzing it; with the parents present, the therapist is free to observe "one side of the family organism acting up against the other side."
- **Precondition for a real father–patient relationship** — the father must first deal directly with the mother. Bowen flags this as his own current view.
- **Observed order of change in the successful families:** inadequate/passive parent becomes assertive → overadequate parent shifts to the inadequate position, passing through overt anxiety (days) into calm firmness (about two weeks) → emotional divorce resolves and the parents invest in each other → neither is overinvested in the patient → both become objective toward the patient → the patient begins moving toward more adequate functioning.
- **Expect reversion, and expect it to be productive.** The first new level lasted a month and then the family reverted; thereafter it was easier for the father to pull up and less threatening for the mother to let go. Reversion is not failure of the change but part of its course.
- **Expect a sequence of anxiety-avoiding mechanisms.** When the family attempts to follow the structure, intense anxiety develops. Most frequent avoidance: the decision-making member engages the therapist in conversation. When the therapist structures himself out of that, the next most frequent: the parents talk to the psychotic one about the psychosis, which rapidly turns to criticism of the patient. Others: small talk, silence.
- **[p2] Correction to the above — the ladder is only two rungs deep, and it is role-indexed.** Pass 1 recorded a five-step ranked ladder. The text ranks exactly two items and leaves the rest unordered:
  1. **"The most frequent** is for the **decision-making family member** to engage the therapist in conversation." Bowen adds two side effects — it encourages an individual relationship, and "the decision-making family member thus becomes more dependent on the therapist" — and one duration: "Some families have been able to keep this going with the therapists for weeks."
  2. **"The next most frequent** is for **the parents** to talk to the psychotic one about the psychosis," with the stated reason that discussing personal problems between themselves is extremely difficult while talking to the patient is comparatively easy. **Criticism of the patient is not a third rung**: "this frequently changes rapidly to criticism of the patient" — it is a rapid drift *within* rung 2.
  3. "Other ways the parents avoid anxiety between themselves is by **small talk or by silence**." Introduced as "other ways," with no ranking between them and none against the two above.
  Two further features pass 1 missed. The actor differs by rung — rung 1 is performed by the decision-making member specifically, rungs 2–3 by the parents. And rung 2 is **unlocked by blocking rung 1**: the second set is seen only "when the therapist is able to structure himself out of the 'working-together effort' of the family" — the next mechanism appears because the previous one was closed, not because it failed on its own.
- **Frequency:** the case began at two hours a week, ran two to three hours a week through the conflict phase, was reduced to once a week by the mother at hospitalization.
- **Rule testing is expected.** "Frequently a family member will test this rule by communicating personal information by the telephone."

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **Therapist-directed change collapses and is attributed to the therapist.** The father who said "The doctor told me I should do it this way" when challenged — the single incident that ended the strategy of focusing on fathers' passivity.
- **Therapist-induced inadequacy in a peripheral member cascades.** After the therapist pushed the father to express opinions and the father was squelched, "During the next hour the father was in a more inadequate position than he had been before"; for the first time the possible failure of his new business was mentioned; "During the next hours the daughter became increasingly psychotic, the mother increasingly strong, and the father increasingly peripheral and inadequate." The daughter's attempt to bolster the anxious father emerged as psychotic schemes acted out in town, which led to hospitalization.
- **Criticism of the active parent produces a passive, complaining family** that waits for the therapist to supply answers. Worse in the hospitalized families, where it is easy for staff to become critical of the parents' actions and for the parents then to go inactive.
- **The leader dropping the lead produces an impasse.** After the mother's vacation she did not resume the leader function; the family "sat and looked at the therapist"; no one agreed or disagreed, no one had a problem, they tried to engage the therapist socially. Two months lost. Resolved when the therapist began to recognize her position, whereupon "she moved immediately into the family lead."
- **An individual relationship with one member converts the rest into spectators and pulls the therapist into deciding that member's life.** Two telephone calls in a week asking him to figure out a job decision.
- **Any two of the threesome can successfully avoid the anxiety issues**, making therapy "more intellectual, more sterile, and less profitable."
- **Observer overinvolvement.** A family in daily contact with a psychotic member has a high level of anxiety and emotion, with frequent crises casting one member as victim, another as hero, another as villain. It is easy for the observer to lose objectivity, and each member actively works to recruit a staff ally for his or her emotional point of view.
- **Inpatient families do worse.** They become involved in complex relationships with staff, other family groups, and the hospital environment; outpatient families progressed more rapidly.
- **Physical illness as the failure route out of a pull-up.** Two fathers reverted with minor physical illnesses; the case father had an operation and missed three appointments. "It is not unusual for a parent in an inadequate position to develop a physical illness when he attempts to pull up to more adequate functioning."
- **Anxiety-driven overhelp masquerading as care.** The mother nearly cancelled a long-planned vacation in response to the daughter's helplessness display; in the mother's absence the daughter found a job the next day, and when she lost it she immediately found another on her own initiative. Bowen notes that if the mother had been present "she undoubtedly would have been on the telephone to fight the daughter's battles."
- **Diagnosis as an iatrogenic event.** Applying the label is the moment at which the shifting family process becomes fixed and crystallized in one person and family anxiety greatly increases.
- **[p2] A negotiated topic ban is defeated by substitution.** The daughter kept her promise not to raise the paranoid delusions and "took up another equally sensitive issue which created a scene." Agreeing to close a channel changes the content and preserves the discharge.
- **[p2] Extrusion attempt as the response to a refused projection.** The daughter refused to take the mother's symptoms ("Don't try to dump your troubles on me"); the mother's next move was "You can just move out on your own," and in the following hour she "suggested that the daughter go on in individual therapy," arguing the daughter was now free and on her own. The proposal to remove the patient from the family unit arrives immediately after the patient declines the role, and it is opposed by the other two.
- **[p2] An indirect, badly executed emotional move still produced a favourable structural change.** The father made a "behind-the-scenes" scene by talking loudly so the daughter and her date could hear; the daughter told him in the next hour that he should have "been man enough to speak directly to the boy instead of yelling like an angry child." Bowen's verdict on it: "This series of events brought about a new equilibrium between father and daughter." Move quality and outcome come apart here.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

This is the section that converts the rules back into mechanism. Each rule → the claim about family functioning it presupposes.

1. **"All members present, every hour" assumes the pathology is a property of the relationship system, not of a person** — the thing to be treated only exists when the members are in contact, so it cannot be reached through any one of them alone. Corollary: symptoms in one person are not portable into a room without the others.
2. **"Two members minimum, one member is individual therapy" assumes the process requires at least a dyad to be visible**, and that a therapist plus one person constitutes a *different* system rather than a partial view of the same one.
3. **"With all three present the family soon encounters conflict; any two can avoid it" assumes the anxiety of the triad has no discharge route while the third member is watching.** Two people can collude to keep the anxiety out of the room; three cannot. This is the strongest structural claim the technique makes and it is directly testable: **anxiety in the triad is a function of who is present, not of what is discussed.**
   **[p2] — and this chapter's own case data splits on it.** Confirming instance: during the mother's three-week vacation, "Therapy hours were calm and uneventful." Disconfirming instance: during the father's two-week hospitalisation, "The mother made her greatest progress during the absence of the father" — the fusion insight, the somatic-mirror recollection and the "invisible wall" decision all occur with only two of the three present. Bowen reports both and reconciles neither. The rule survives as stated only if the *identity* of the absent member matters, which he does not say. Pass 1 called this the chapter's strongest structural claim and cited it as third corroboration of the same claim in Ch02; it is better described as a rule with one supporting and one contradicting instance inside a single case.
4. **"Family works, therapist observes from the sideline" assumes the family has and can exercise its own problem-solving capacity**, and that anxiety is what has disabled it — not deficit. Bowen states this as a premise: "the family can find its own answers if they work on it."
5. **"Therapist must not participate, even silently" assumes the system detects and reacts to an added participant.** A therapist who takes a side becomes a *member* of the emotional process, and the family can sense the alignment even unexpressed. Implication: the triad is complete at three; a fourth participant restructures it.
6. **"Analyze the relationships in situ; do not let transference form" assumes emotional investment is conserved and transferable** — investment that goes to the therapist is investment removed from the family relationships, where it is needed for change to happen. Same logic as "both were so much invested in each other that neither was overinvested in the patient": there is a fixed quantity being allocated.
7. **"Individual communications are read out to the group" assumes private channels create differential alliance**, and that the family's structure is defined by who knows what. A secret to the therapist is a coalition against the others.
8. **"Support the family leader, even if his actions look schizophrenogenic" assumes the family has a functional leadership office and that the office, not its occupant's technique, is what moves the family.** If the office is vacated, the family stalls regardless of everything else. It also assumes therapist criticism of the leader is enough to vacate the office.
9. **"Ask the leader to be spokesman, but let them designate another any time" assumes the leadership position is genuinely mobile** and that fixing it by fiat would be a therapist-imposed structure — precisely what the technique elsewhere forbids.
10. **"Don't try to persuade opposing members; the adequate parent can" assumes influence inside the family runs on channels the therapist has no access to,** and that the family's own coercive power (the mother leaving home until an appointment was made) is legitimate and effective where the therapist's persuasion is not.
11. **"Don't influence in a particular direction" assumes a change attributed to an outside authority is not a real change of function** — the assertiveness the father displayed while quoting the doctor was not his own position, and collapsed under challenge. Implication: **a functional position must be self-generated to hold.** This is the sharpest assumption in the chapter and the one that cost the project its most promising intervention strategy.
12. **"Don't evaluate the historical material" assumes the content of the accusations is epiphenomenal** — the "proof I am wonderful, proof you are terrible" pattern is the operative thing, and any content will serve it. Corollary: the fight is content-independent, so resolving any particular grievance changes nothing.
13. **"Avoid diagnostic labels; if you use one, apply it to everyone" assumes the label is causally active, not merely descriptive** — it is the mechanism that converts a shifting, distributed process into a fixed location. Symmetrizing it ("then the whole family is schizophrenic") destroys its ability to locate.
14. **"Refuse to arbitrate hospitalization" assumes the therapist's agreement would be recruited into the family's own fixing process.** Agreeing with the parents *is* participating in the scapegoating. Hospitalization is framed as a family/society decision, not a medical one: "People go to mental hospitals when the family or when society asks for it."
15. **"Ask whether she is treating her as if she really were helpless" assumes helplessness is co-produced and maintained by the response to it.** The vacation episode is the natural experiment: remove the overfunctioner and the underfunctioner functions within a day.
16. **"The father must deal with the mother before he can be a father to the patient" assumes the parent–child relationship is not independently available** — it is licensed by the parental dyad, and a father who bypasses the mother becomes a substitute mother rather than a father.
17. **"Expect physical illness when the inadequate one pulls up" assumes the position shift is costly to the person making it**, and that somatic symptoms are one of the exits from the cost.
18. **"Therapist absence matters less in family than individual therapy" assumes the therapeutic agent is the family's own contact**, not the relationship to the therapist. Corroborated by the family's therapy hours being "calm and uneventful" in the mother's absence rather than in his.
19. **"Avoid individual-oriented psychiatric vocabulary" assumes the observer's own categories change what he can see** — the terms themselves cause a shift back to individual perception even in a staff already committed to family thinking.
20. **"Families with the most active resistance and disagreement do best" assumes conflict is available energy**, and that the flat, agreeable family (the two-month impasse) is the harder case, not the easier one.
21. **[p2] "Say nothing they have not worked out themselves" assumes correct information delivered from outside does not act at all.** Not that it acts weakly — the mother "must have heard this hundreds of times" and reacted to her own version "as if she had just discovered a fresh new truth for herself." Combined with assumption 11 (a position quoted from the doctor collapses under challenge), the chapter's implicit rule is that the *source* of a conclusion determines whether it holds, independently of its content.
22. **[p2] "Route even the reply to a private letter through the family" assumes the channel, not the message, is what confers alliance.** He could have refused the letter's request in a sentence; instead he declined to answer her at all and offered the parents the option of relaying. The rule is about who is a party to the exchange, not about secrecy of content.
23. **[p2] "Analyse the dependence on the unit rather than preventing dependence" assumes dependence is not eliminable, only re-targetable.** The stated outcome of avoiding individual relationships is a dependent relationship with the family unit — a different address for the same quantity.
24. **[p2] "Reduce the areas of joint activity" (reported as the families' own solution, not the therapist's) assumes anxiety is generated per contested domain.** Withdrawing a domain from the joint set lowers anxiety while leaving the reciprocity in every remaining domain untouched — which is why the chapter treats the business/home division as a *symptom-free* form of the same pathology rather than as health.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Fixed characteristics → functioning in relation.** The project explicitly abandoned its own earlier research operation: "The research operation had first gone toward defining what we believed to be the fixed characteristics of each family member." Seeing characteristics change forced the shift to "the functioning of one person in relation to another." This supersedes the earlier working framing "the father is one kind of person, the mother is another kind of person, and the patient becomes another kind of person."
- **Mother–patient dyad → whole-family process.** The first year of the project treated mothers and patients as the unit, each in individual psychotherapy. Experience suggested "the mother-patient relationship was a dependent fragment of a larger family problem and that the father played an important part in it," and both the research plan and the psychotherapy plan were changed.
- **Focusing on the father's passivity — adopted then abandoned.** The staff reasoned that since the first change to be expected was activity from the passive father, therapy might be facilitated by focusing on his passivity. "Eventually this was abandoned." Yet the narrated case *is* an instance of the abandoned strategy still in use ("This phase of the therapy with this outpatient family occurred at the time that the therapists were focusing on the passivity of fathers"), and it went badly — daughter increasingly psychotic, hospitalization within weeks. Bowen presents the technique and its refutation in the same narrative without reconciling them.
- **The individual-relationship experiment.** The therapist deliberately broke his own core rule during the impasse, "This was an individual relationship between the therapist and one family member," found it produced exactly what the rule was designed to prevent, and abandoned it after one hour. A rule tested and confirmed by violation rather than argument.
- **Early formulation superseded:** "The problem in the family is as much an act of omission by the father as it is an act of commission by the mother" — described as how the project put it "in the first year or so," and implicitly replaced by the reciprocity concept, in which neither omission nor commission is primary.
- **"Victim" rejected.** The scapegoat language is kept but the victim framing explicitly refused: parents and patients all participate in the process.
- **[p2] The two-versus-three rule is contradicted by the chapter's own case.** Stated rule: any two of the threesome successfully avoid the anxiety issues and therapy becomes "more intellectual, more sterile, and less profitable." Reported outcome: "The mother made her greatest progress during the absence of the father." Unreconciled. (See B5.3 [p2].)
- **[p2] "Adequate outside, inadequate inside" is contradicted by the case father.** The general observation is that parents "might function adequately and successfully in outside business and social relationships while they are immature, indecisive, and inadequate within the family." The case father is inadequate in *both*: he loses his business within a year of the psychosis, is "at the most ineffective period in his life," his new business is in liquidation during the therapy, and he is re-employed only as he moves toward a more adequate position at hour 84. The chapter contains both the decoupling rule and a case in which the two track each other, and does not say which is the general case.
- **[p2] "The father must deal directly with the mother first" is marked as NOT project policy.** It carries "As I currently see it," and the chapter's closing footnote makes that marking a formal convention: "we"/"our" = ideas generally accepted by the staff and included in the operating policy of the project; "I"/"my" = "points in my thinking that are not part of the operating policy of the project." Pass 1 noted the hedge but not that the footnote converts it into an explicit statement of non-adoption. See PASS 2 P3 for the comparison with Ch04, where the same claim carries six families of evidence.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- **"As I see it" / "As I currently see it"** — used for the origin of the emotional divorce (early alternating overcloseness/overdistance) and for the claim that the father must deal directly with the mother before he can be a father to the patient. The footnote makes this marking deliberate: "I" and "my" refer to points in Bowen's thinking that are *not* part of the operating policy of the project; "we" and "our" refer to ideas generally accepted by the staff.
- **"In our experience"** — repeatedly used as the epistemic ceiling, e.g. the father who moves in still functions as a substitute mother; a therapist can lose his way evaluating historical material.
- **"We would postulate"** — the definition of a fairly normal family is offered as a postulate, not a finding.
- **"It is our opinion"** — the closing claim that the family-as-a-unit view can add to individual concepts and that family psychotherapy "may open up a whole new range of therapeutic possibilities." Deliberately weak.
- **"psychiatry has never found an answer to schizophrenia"** — stated by the therapist to the family, as the ground for refusing the answer-giving role.
- **"In my opinion"** — that a family diagnosis would be the main advantage over individual diagnoses.
- **"seemed a good prognostic sign"** — the daughter's vigorous stand against the mother; hedged.
- **"This in no way detracts from the value of the individual orientation"** — Bowen repeatedly protects the individual view, calling the two mutually enhancing rather than competing.
- **Genuine unknowns left open:** why the mother's own head hurt where the daughter's was injured — "She pondered the why of this"; the mechanism of the fusion is described but not explained, only that "her own life was connected with the daughter in some very complex way."
- **The case is presented as atypical in one direction:** "the change between the father and the mother was less marked, and the change between mother and patient was more dramatic than changes in other families."
- **[p2] A declared scope restriction that limits what absence from this chapter proves.** "I shall defocus, as much as possible, the more specific relationship characteristics which have been reported in other papers." Concepts missing from Ch03 may be missing because he chose to leave them out, not because he lacked them. This weakens any inference from Ch03's vocabulary to the state of the theory in 1960 — the safe reading is that Ch03 shows what he considered the *broad patterns*, not the whole of what he held.
- **[p2] "have tended to," not "must."** Staff emotional detachment is reported as a tendency that developed — "Staff members have tended to detach themselves emotionally to the point that they can work with the families without becoming overinvolved" — not prescribed as a rule. Pass 1 listed it under B1 (what the therapist does) at higher force than the text carries.
- **[p2] "seemed helpful," "seemed to be directed."** Two of the chapter's more interesting causal readings — the father's do-nothing attitude helping the daughter, and family speech being aimed at reactions rather than reality — are both carried on "seemed."

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The chapter's own epigraph is a mechanism claim.** Sterne's Shandy: a family is "a simple machine" of a few wheels, but those wheels "were set in motion by so many different springs, and acted one upon the other from such a variety of strange principles and impulses" that it has all the advantages of a complex one. Few components, many interactions, emergent complexity — Bowen chose an agent-based framing for the chapter's first paragraph.
- **The two viewing scales are explicitly complementary, not competing.** The microscope and stadium metaphors both argue that the wide view *enhances* the close view: "the high magnification of the oil-immersion lens is far more meaningful after it has been possible to see the larger area through the low-power lens."
- **A worked example of why single-observation descriptions fail:** "The father was seductive toward the daughter" might be accurate for an occasional or transient phase and "inaccurate and misleading if applied to their over-all relationship." A direct argument that state must be distinguished from disposition, and that a single timestep's observation cannot be read as a trait.
- **Each family member actively recruits the observer.** "The emotional situation is further complicated by the efforts of each family member to find a staff ally for his or her emotional point of view." Recruitment is a family behaviour directed at anyone in range, not just at the therapist.
- **Tears are a specific high-signal event.** "Few things disturb the parents as much as hostile psychotic expressions from the patient. Family members are distressed by tears, especially tears in the overadequate mother."
- **The daughter had a theory of the stigma that turned out to be wrong.** She had avoided her college friends for six years believing they would pity her; when she met them they accepted her as before, and she concluded that formerly "she had thought they were reacting to her but they were really reacting to their own fears about mental illness."
- **Tranquilizers as an anxiety floor, described as a loss.** "My spirit, my imagination, my fight is all gone... It is the tranquilizers. It is awful not to be anxious in situations in which anyone would be anxious." Anxiety is here framed as functionally necessary, and the mother insisted on continuing the drug while the daughter begged to stop.
- **The daughter can occupy the family leader position.** By hour 77–84 she holds it and refuses to take the mother's symptoms: "Don't try to dump your troubles on me. I have my own life to bother about." The office is not restricted to parents.
- **Success at work is graded by the same relationship skill.** She "won the admiration of fellow employees for her ability to get along with a dominating woman boss" — a functional position exercised outside the family, in the same shape as the one inside it.
- **A calm follows the discharge of the conflict.** "There was a marked calm between the parents the day the daughter was hospitalized." The parental anxiety and the patient's presence trade off directly.
- **Delusional content tracks the relationship system.** The daughter's paranoid threat was against a boyfriend who "had sided with the mother in one of the issues between the mother and daughter." The delusion is directed at a defector from her side of the triad.

**Positions: they shift, and Bowen states it as the general finding, not as case detail. This is the strongest evidence, from his own summary paragraph:**

The patient and the overadequate parent **exchange the two extreme positions**, and the chapter ends with the daughter still holding the adequate one and the father "moving toward a more adequate position in relation to the mother." - Voluntary transfer of the inside position: in hospital the mother "decided against visits 'for the good of the daughter,' but she encouraged the father to go regularly."
- The re-attachment cycle is an explicit oscillator on which parent is inside: mother tightens → blames the father → father approaches → "If the patient shows too much interest in the father, the mother moves to intensify the patient's attachment to her."

**Verdict for the timeline.** The mobile inside/outside position is present at ~1960, in a threesome of fixed membership. What is genuinely later is (a) the generalisation from this one clinical threesome to the triangle as the building block of *any* emotional system, (b) recruitment of a third from outside when a twosome's tension exceeds a threshold, and (c) interlocking triangles. ### Q-LADDER — the ranking is two deep. Verbatim, in order of appearance:

> "When the family attempts to follow this therapy structure, intense anxiety develops. There are several ways in which the family attempts to avoid the anxiety. **The most frequent is for the decision-making family member to engage the therapist in conversation.** In addition to avoiding the family problem, it tends to encourage an individual relationship with the therapist, and the decision-making family member thus becomes more dependent on the therapist. Some families have been able to keep this going with the therapists for weeks but when the therapist is able to structure himself out of the 'working-together effort' of the family, preferably by analyzing the family effort to avoid the structure, another series of anxiety-avoiding mechanisms is seen. **The next most frequent is for the parents to talk to the psychotic one about the psychosis.** There is the emotional divorce between the parents. It is extremely difficult for parents to discuss personal problems about themselves, but it is comparatively easy for either parent to talk directly to the patient, and **this frequently changes rapidly to criticism of the patient. Other ways the parents avoid anxiety between themselves is by small talk or by silence.**"

What the passage licenses, for use as a ranked fallback policy:
1. **Rank 1** — engage the therapist in conversation. Actor: **the decision-making family member specifically.** Named side effects: encourages an individual relationship, and increases that member's dependence on the therapist. Duration if unopposed: "weeks."
2. **Rank 2** — talk to the patient about the psychosis. Actor: **the parents.** Stated cause: personal talk between them is "extremely difficult," talk to the patient "comparatively easy." **Criticism of the patient is not rank 3** — it is where rank 2 goes on its own, "rapidly."
3. **Unranked residual** — small talk, silence. Introduced as "other ways," with no ordering between them and none relative to ranks 1–2. Actor: the parents.

Two structural features to carry into the policy: the ladder is **triggered** by the family attempting to comply with the structure, not by anxiety generally; and rank 2 is **unlocked by the closure of rank 1** — "another series… is seen" only once the therapist structures himself out. Blocking a channel produces the next one rather than reducing the anxiety, which is C3 appearing at move-selection level in the corpus's third-oldest chapter.

### Q-VALIDATION — no. Nothing in this chapter validates or measures anything.

There is no differentiation scale here to validate (P1.9), and no numeric level of any per-person property. What the chapter offers instead: an observation regime — "detailed around-the-clock observations on each family member," longitudinal follow-up of ten family groups, four of them in residence up to two and a half years. That is a data-collection procedure, not a measurement procedure. There is no rating instrument, no operational definition of any variable, no inter-observer check, no control or comparison group, and no test of any prediction against anything but the same clinical impressions that generated it. Every comparative claim is ungraded: families "have done the best," outpatients "have progressed more rapidly," changes "would usually last for a few weeks."

Two things point the other way and are worth recording. Bowen's epistemic markers are unusually careful for the period — the footnote convention separating project policy from personal opinion, and the ceilings "In our experience," "we would postulate," "It is our opinion." And the chapter contains an explicit argument **against** point measurement: "The father was seductive toward the daughter" may be "descriptively accurate in describing an occasional or transient phase in their relationship but inaccurate and misleading if applied to their over-all relationship." That is a stated reason why a single observation of a relationship cannot be treated as a measurement of it — the nearest thing to methodology in the chapter, and it argues that the quantity the model wants to read out is not readable from one timestep.

### Q-MATERIAL — no posited variable, one causal instance, and pervasive co-variation with a stated exception.

Bowen never names, defines, or theorises an economic or material variable. But the chapter is unusually full of material detail, in three distinct forms.

**(a) One instance where a material constraint causes an event.** "The family home was mortgaged to finance private treatment. Eventually the family finances were completely exhausted and she was transferred to a state institution." Money runs out → level of care drops → the patient is moved. This is the only place in the chapter where a resource is a binding constraint on an outcome, and the direction runs money → care, not position → money.

**(b) Sustained co-variation between economic standing and intrafamily position.** The father worries about financial security all his life; loses his business within a year of the psychosis and is "at the most ineffective period in his life"; the mother becomes "the decision-making breadwinner"; the father starts a new business as the daughter comes home; the possibility that it might fail surfaces in the hour *after* he is squelched; it is in liquidation during the therapy; the mother's attack on him is economic in content ("You remain an unemployed executive while your family starves"); and by hour 84 "The father had been employed for several weeks and he now was moving toward a more adequate position in relation to the mother." The daughter's course is the same shape: a file-clerk job the day after the mother leaves, lost the week she returns, another found immediately, then a resignation after three months for "a better job in the field of her college training" with the old employer offering to match the salary.

**(c) An explicit rule that decouples them, which the case then violates.** "The parents might function adequately and successfully in outside business and social relationships while they are immature, indecisive, and inadequate within the family." The case father is inadequate in both, throughout. Bowen states the decoupling as the general pattern and narrates a case that contradicts it, without comment.

So: no per-person material variable is posited. If one is built, the chapter supports **one** hard mechanism — a resource floor gating the care pathway — and warns explicitly against treating external economic functioning as a readout of internal position.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Conflict localizes in the weakest member through reciprocal functioning → `Family` + `Person` → the family needs a per-timestep routine that concentrates a distributed conflict/anxiety quantity onto the member with the lowest current adequacy, with all members contributing to the concentration.
- Diagnosis/labelling fixes the localization and raises family anxiety → `event-kind:LABEL` → an event that (a) pins the symptom-bearer role to one Person and (b) raises Family anxiety. Its removal (ambiguating the label) should re-fluidize and re-raise parental anxiety. Currently likely `unmodelled` as an event.
- Interdependent triad = father + mother + patient; siblings can exit → `Triangle` + `Family` → the triad must be a membership-bearing structure with a defined exit affordance for non-members. Note the chapter's triad is *fixed membership*, not the later general triangle; do not import the later semantics.
- Emotional divorce (fixed parental distance following early oscillation of closeness/distance) → `Relationship` → parental dyads need a distance state variable with a settling dynamic: early oscillation → fixed distance → lower anxiety. Two expressions (formal-positive vs. physical distance) suggest a style flag.
- Overadequate–inadequate reciprocity, no mid-ground, either can occupy either pole → `Relationship` → a zero-sum adequacy variable on the dyad with unstable middle: a bistable/anti-symmetric coupling, not two independent per-person traits.
- **[p2] Supersedes the bullet above — the coupling is per-DOMAIN and the switch has a stated rule.** Not one scalar per dyad but a set of joint-activity areas, each carrying a pole assignment; the summary position is a count of areas held. The switch fires when the inadequate one's assertion **exceeds** the other's aggression/domination (a comparison between the two parties' current values, not a fixed threshold), flips immediately, and reverts unless the mover holds through the other's anxiety reaction. Three consequences: `functioning_balance` becomes a vector over domains; `move:DISTANCE` operates by removing a domain from the joint set rather than by changing any pole; and the flip condition gives the bistable switch an implementable trigger the pass-1 note said it lacked.
- Change in one member → complementing change in others "almost immediately" → `Family` update rule → propagation latency of roughly one timestep, not a slow diffusion.
- Reciprocity is resolved by *reducing areas of joint activity* → `Relationship` → a `joint_activity_scope` variable; anxiety is a function of scope × distance. Reducing scope lowers anxiety without changing the underlying reciprocity. Probably `unmodelled`.
- Parents disagree about the patient but not about normal siblings; the disagreement can be latent for years → `Triangle` + `unmodelled` → a per-child disagreement variable, plus a distinction between *held* and *expressed* disagreement.
- Custody: either parent can be close to the patient only if the other permits → `Triangle` → the parent–child edge should be gated by the other parent's permission state, not freely settable.
- Re-attachment cycle (mother tightens → blames father → father approaches → mother re-intensifies) → `move:PURSUE_CHILD` / `move:RE_ATTACH` → an explicit oscillator on the mother–child and father–child edges with a negative-feedback coupling.
- Father moving in during mother's withdrawal still functions as substitute mother; mother wins back even after long absence → `Relationship` → position occupancy is not the same as relationship kind; needs a role-quality attribute distinct from proximity. Likely `unmodelled`.
- Father must deal directly with the mother before a real father–patient relationship is available → `move:` precondition → a move whose availability is gated on the state of a *different* edge. Important for the coach's move-legality logic.
- Patient as sponge absorbing family anxiety; help emerges as symptom → `Person` → a per-person absorption coefficient, and a transform that converts absorbed anxiety into symptomatic output rather than into functional action.
- Sequence: inadequate parent asserts → overadequate parent destabilizes (days) → settles into calm firmness (~2 weeks) → divorce resolves → investment shifts from patient to spouse → patient improves → whole thing reverts after ~1 month, but with a lowered barrier thereafter → `Family` + `test` → this is the single most simulable trajectory in the chapter. Model it as: barrier-to-shift decreases with each completed cycle even when the cycle reverts. Reversion must be *expected*, not a failure state.
- Investment appears conserved (invested in each other → not overinvested in patient) → `Person` → a fixed investment budget allocated across edges. Testable and cheap to implement.
- ~~Fusion of feeling: patient cannot generate own affect, adopts mother's; recovers away from home, loses it on return → `Relationship` → a fusion coefficient that suppresses independent affect generation, with a *distance-dependent* strength.~~ The "invisible wall" is the mother-side move that lowers it.
- **[p2] Correction — the fusion is not distance-dependent.** The affect-suppression is gated by whether *anyone* occupies the telling-you-how-to-feel position: at school she had her own feelings "unless a teacher or some such person suggested she must feel a certain way." Model as a **role slot** on the receiving person — occupied or vacant, with any sufficiently authoritative agent able to fill it — multiplying a suppression coefficient that does not itself vary with distance. Distance changes only the *probability the slot is filled*. This aligns Ch03 with C7 rather than against it.
- Physical illness as an exit from an attempted pull-up → `event-kind:SOMATIC` → a stochastic event triggered by attempted upward position change, returning the person to the prior position. Cheap and high-value.

**[p2] Added at the comparative pass:**

- **Triad positions are mobile on two independent axes; membership is not.** → `Triangle` → the structure needs a fixed member list plus two position vectors that update every tick: an **adequacy** rank over the three, and an **inside/outside** assignment (which two hold the close tie). Pass 1's note "do not import the later semantics" was right about the general triangle and wrong about mobility — the mobility is native to this chapter. What must **not** be imported: recruitment of a third under tension, and interlocking triangles. Neither exists here.
- **The inside position is permission-gated and revocable, including voluntarily.** → `Triangle` → the close parent–child tie needs an owner, a permission flag held by the excluded party, and three transition kinds: contested takeover, ejection by the patient's hostility, and *voluntary transfer* ("she encouraged the father to go regularly"). Reacquisition after long absence must be cheap — "the mother can usually win back the patient, even after long absences."
- **Investment in a child is set by the marital distance before the child exists.** → `Person`, initialisation → the projection-load on a child at birth should be a function of the parents' distance during gestation, not of anything about the child. Prenatal, so it is a clean initial condition with no feedback to disentangle.
- **Blocking a discharge channel produces substitution, at two scales.** → conservation invariant, `test` → move-level: closing rung 1 of the avoidance ladder produces rung 2. Content-level: the agreed ban on the delusional topic produced "another equally sensitive issue which created a scene." **Adversarial test:** a negotiated topic ban must leave the scene count unchanged and change only which topic carries it.
- **A relief channel that works, is administered by the overfunctioner, and reduces the symptom-bearer's capacity to change.** → `event-kind:SEDATE`, `coach` → the tranquilizer is C1's mechanism in explicit form and with a named agent. Model it as an action available to the overadequate member that lowers the symptom-bearer's expressed anxiety and simultaneously lowers her propensity to take any position. The overfunctioner's interest in administering it is structural, not malicious.
- **Information delivered from outside does not act; the same conclusion self-generated does.** → `coach`, appraisal → not a reduced effect but no effect: the mother "must have heard this hundreds of times" and reacted to her own version "as if she had just discovered a fresh new truth." Pairs with the father who quoted the doctor and collapsed. **Two coach move classes with different downstream dynamics: supplying a conclusion (zero or negative durable effect) and creating the conditions for one (durable).**
- **Dependence relocates rather than dissolving.** → `coach` → a coach that avoids individual ties does not produce an independent family; it produces a dependence on the *unit*, which then becomes the working object. A model where good coaching monotonically reduces dependence has no state for this.
- **Move quality and structural outcome come apart.** → `test` → the father's "behind-the-scenes" scene was indirect, angry and criticised by his daughter, and produced "a new equilibrium between father and daughter." A model in which better-executed moves reliably produce better structural outcomes contradicts this. The relevant variable is that he acted at all, from a position he had never occupied.
- **The peripheral member's non-reaction is an effective move.** → `move:HOLD_STEADY` → "the father's unexcited do-nothing attitude seemed helpful" while the daughter was acutely anxious about her job. A null move with a positive effect, which the repertoire currently lacks — note that later chapters (Ch22) deny a null move exists; this chapter reports one that worked. Record the tension, do not resolve it.
- **The removal event is family- and community-triggered, with the decision owned by the adequate member.** → `event-kind:INSTITUTIONALIZE` → fires off community disturbance plus the remaining members' tolerance, is executed by whoever holds the adequate position, and is followed immediately by calm between the parents. This is L08.1's trigger rule, available a decade earlier than the ledger currently dates it.
- **A resource floor gates the care pathway.** → `Family` → exhausted finances forced transfer from private treatment to a state institution. The only causal use of a material quantity in the chapter; everything else economic is co-variation, and the chapter explicitly warns that external functioning can be high while internal position is low.
- **The extrusion attempt as a response to a refused projection.** → `move:EXPEL` → when the symptom-bearer declines the role ("Don't try to dump your troubles on me"), the immediate next moves are "You can just move out on your own" and a proposal to move her to individual therapy. Removal pressure should rise when the projection target refuses, not only when she is symptomatic.
- **Reversion is a step, not a decay.** → `test` 5 → "the family **suddenly** reverted." Whatever curve the change-back reaction produces, the return to the prior configuration should be abrupt; the *barrier reduction* it leaves behind is the persistent part.
