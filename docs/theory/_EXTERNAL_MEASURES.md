# External measures of differentiation

## Why this file exists

`_LEDGER.md` records that **Q-VALIDATION is NO in all 22 chapters** — no instrument, no rater procedure, no comparison group, no number ever assigned to a person. That finding is about **Bowen's papers**, and it stands.

It has been used loosely in this project to mean something stronger and wrong: that nothing exists anywhere to calibrate against. **Instruments do exist.** They were built by other researchers decades later, they are the closest attempts anyone has made, and — on the assessment of the person directing this project, who supplied them — **there is still no good instrument for measuring level of differentiation.** Both halves of that matter, so this file records what the instruments give the model and what they do not.

## The Differentiation of Self Inventory (DSI)

**Skowron, E. A., & Friedlander, M. L. (1998).** The Differentiation of Self Inventory: Development and initial validation. *Journal of Counseling Psychology, 45*(3), 235–246.

**Skowron, E. A., & Schmitt, T. A. (2003).** Assessing interpersonal fusion: Reliability and validity of a new DSI Fusion With Others subscale. *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy, 29*(2), 209–222.

A multidimensional self-report measure for adults aged 25+, developed explicitly because "few programmatic attempts" had been made to test Bowen's constructs empirically. 609 adults across three studies; principal-components analysis on 313 in Study 1; 6-point Likert items.

### What it measures — and the decomposition matters to us

Four subscales: **Emotional Reactivity**, **I Position**, **Emotional Cutoff**, **Fusion With Others**. The last two began as Reactive Distancing and Fusion With Parents and were reconceptualised in Study 2.

Three of those four are objects the model already carries as separate constructs — reactivity, the I-position move, and cutoff. That an independent factor analysis of 313 people recovered a comparable decomposition is **weak but genuine external corroboration** of the model's split, and it is the only such corroboration in this project. It is a factor structure, not a validation of the theory.

### The one usable calibration target the corpus cannot supply

**DSI full-scale ↔ Trait Anxiety (STAI-T): r = .64.** Subscale correlations: Emotional Reactivity .58, Reactive Distancing .55, I Position .51, Fusion With Parents .16.

This is a published, numeric relationship between differentiation and chronic anxiety — Bowen's own proposition, and one of the model's core couplings, which the 22 chapters state directionally and never quantify. `M10.C.2` may treat it as a **calibration target of the same class as the corpus's stated durations**: a figure to check an ensemble against, never a parameter to set.

Two limits travel with it. The correlation is between a self-report of differentiation and a self-report of anxiety, so shared method variance inflates it. And it is a population-level association in a sample that is 82.7% White, largely educated, and drawn from the northeastern United States — it is not a norm.

### Reliability, which bounds how precise any calibration can be

Cronbach's alpha: full scale **.88**; Emotional Reactivity .83; Fusion With Parents .82; Reactive Distancing .80; I Position .80.

Nothing in this model should claim to resolve differentiation more finely than an instrument with α ≈ .88 can, and that is a ceiling on the instrument's *internal consistency*, not on its accuracy.

### Where it failed, and why that is the most informative part

The **Fusion With Others** subscale — measuring what is arguably the single most central construct in Bowen theory — did not work for five years. Skowron and Schmitt state it plainly:

- reliability "consistently demonstrated lower estimates, ranging from **.57–.74**", against .80+ for the other three;
- "**contrary to theoretical expectations, no significant relationships have been observed between FO scores and psychological adjustment**", problem-solving skills, or relationship satisfaction;
- "most research … conducted with the DSI FO subscale has **failed to corroborate** these observations", with only one study finding the theorised fusion–anxiety link;
- the diagnosis: "a **lack of conceptual clarity in its items**", mixing fusion with attachment insecurity and mature concern for others.

The subscale was rebuilt in 2003 on 225 adults into a 12-item revision. **The construct at the centre of the theory resisted operationalisation for half a decade by the researcher who had successfully operationalised the other three.**

### A tension the model should hold, not resolve

The DSI is a **self-report** instrument for a quantity Bowen says self-report cannot see. Ch17 · `L17.2`: pseudo-self is lent, borrowed and traded between people, which "**results in false readings when one attempts to estimate levels of differentiation**", recoverable only over a life course.

If that finding is right, an instrument asking people to rate their own differentiation is measuring functional level contaminated by borrowed self — which is close to what Bowen predicts would be hardest to measure. It is suggestive rather than proof, but the pattern is consistent: **the subscale that failed is the one most directly about self borrowed from others.**

This does not discredit the DSI. It means the model's own readout warnings (`model_explainer.md` §10.1 — estimates of differentiation are biased by construction) apply to the instrument too, and that agreement between the model and the DSI would be weak evidence for both.

## Where the research programme went next

**Skowron, E. A., et al. (2024).** Randomized trial of parent–child interaction therapy improves child-welfare parents' behavior, self-regulation, and self-perceptions. *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology, 92*(2), 75–92.

An NIH-funded RCT, N = 204 child-welfare-involved families, block-randomised, CONSORT-reported, pre-registered, with intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses. It measures **observed** parenting behaviour from coded dyadic interaction, inhibitory control on a stop-signal task, emotion regulation, and self-perceptions.

**It does not use the DSI.** Twenty-six years after building the instrument, its first author measures self-regulation behaviourally and physiologically rather than by self-reported differentiation. That is a different research question, not a repudiation — but it is worth recording, because it is what rigorous measurement in this lineage now looks like, and it is a long way from a self-report score.

For this model the relevance is the *shape* of the evidence: randomisation, a comparison group, observational coding, effect sizes. None of those exist anywhere in Bowen's 22 chapters, which is exactly what `Q-VALIDATION = NO` records.

## What this changes in the model

1. **`Q-VALIDATION = NO` stands, scoped correctly.** It is a finding about the corpus. It is not a claim that differentiation has never been measured.
2. **One external calibration target is admitted:** the DSI–anxiety association, as a target to check an ensemble against, never as a parameter. Everything else in `M10.C.1` remains invented.
3. **The four-way decomposition gains weak external support**, which is more than any other structural choice in the model has.
4. **The readout warnings extend to the instrument.** Agreement between this model and a DSI score is weak evidence, because both are estimating a quantity the theory says is systematically misread.
5. **No claim of the form "the model reproduces measured differentiation" may be made.** There is no good instrument to reproduce.
