## ⭐ KS02.1 — Autonomy is graded, and the grading **is** differentiation `[T]`

> "human beings have **far less autonomy in their emotional functioning than we generally assume**. People have **more control** over automatic reactions to their relationship environments than do ants and naked mole rats — and **better differentiated people have more control than do less differentiated people** — but, more control notwithstanding, the emotional system is **incredibly powerful** in human beings."

And again, as a limit on the metaphor:

> "people, **unlike smoke coming out of a chimney, have some choice** over the extent of their reaction to even a strong anxiety-driven togetherness."

*Model consequence:* **`M4.D.1a`'s mixing weight confirmed explicitly.** Agency exists, it is graded, and it grades on differentiation — the exact claim §G7 corrected G2 with. Kerr states it as a comparative continuum from ants upward, with humans never reaching full autonomy. So the self-directed channel's weight **MUST** have a ceiling below 1 at every level: no agent, however differentiated, becomes purely self-directed.

## ⭐ KS02.2 — Emotional systems predate emotions; the definition is *adaptive behaviour*, not affect `[T]`

> "Simple unicellular creatures such as bacteria do not have a brain, but they are capable of **adaptive behavior — the capacity to change a nonconstructive behavior to a constructive one**. Bowen theory considers the intracellular systems that **support and motivate adaptive behavior** to be part of a **bacterial emotional system**… in Bowen theory **emotional systems were part of living systems long before what people commonly consider emotions entered the picture.**"

*Model consequence:* the **cleanest definition of "emotional" in any of the five sources**, and it is functional rather than ostensive. `M1.A.0` currently defines the word by Bowen's instinct analogies (birds, salmon, amoeba). This gives it a criterion: *the systems that support and motivate adaptive behaviour.* Strengthens the B1 decision (define, do not rename) — the word has a definition, not just a disclaimer.

## ⭐ KS02.3 — The distancing reflex is older than vertebrates `[T]`

> "how far back in the phylogenetic history of our species must we go to discover the roots of our **automatic tendency to distance in face of emotional intensity**? **Single-celled organisms could not survive without a distancing mechanism to protect them from adverse stimuli.**"

*Model consequence:* bears on **`S2.15`** (the D9 question — is distance a neutral sink or itself a symptom?). Kerr's framing makes distance the **oldest and most automatic** of the moves — which supports neither "neutral" nor "symptom" but a third thing: `DISTANCE` is the **default automatic-channel move**, the one requiring least anxiety to trigger. `M4.D.3`'s propensity ordering should reflect that.

## KS02.4 — People select mates who are an **emotional fit**, and the past is re-created in the present `[T]`

> "People select mates for many reasons, but **one of the most important is that they are an emotional fit. The mates then re-create in the present the problems from the past.**"

The worked example is a reciprocal pair: a man "highly reactive" to feeling inadequately accepted, whose mate "**works very hard to make him feel accepted**", producing "an unsatisfactory interaction."

> "**the past does not cause the problem in the present.** The father, mother, and son **repeatedly re-create the problem in the present.** Correspondingly, **changing the process that unfolds in the present is the high road to resolving issues from the past.**"

*Model consequence:* two things.
1. Corroborates `M2.A.0c` (assortative pairing) and gives the mechanism a name — *emotional fit* — with the reciprocal pair as its signature. Note it pairs **complementary** postures, which is compatible with matched `basic_level` and worth stating: same level, opposite pole.
2. **A strong architectural licence.** "The past does not cause the problem in the present" means the model needs **no stored trauma history** driving current behaviour — only current state and current interaction. `M1.A.7`'s `chronic_anxiety`, fixed once in childhood from witnessed history, is the *only* legitimate past-dependence, and this confirms it should stay the only one.

## KS02.5 — Emotional process is inferred from its effects, never observed directly `[T]`

The Beaufort-scale analogy, quoted by Kerr from Huler: describing wind requires looking at *everything else* — "sails, the sea, trees, roof tiles."

> "The flow of emotional forces through a family system is an **invisible process, but you infer their presence by their impact on the emotional functioning of family members**… **You cannot see an emotional system, but you can see its effects on how people function.**"

And the limit of the analogy, which is the important half:

> "the trees, roof tiles, and flag on a flagpole **do not generate wind**; their movements are strictly reactions to the wind. In contrast, **people help to generate an 'emotional wind' by their reactions to one another's reactions.**"

*Model consequence:* the readout discipline the whole project has been asserting, from the source: every measure of the emotional system is **indirect**, taken from functioning. Confirms **A7** and `M1.A.4a`'s estimator form for a second time in two chapters. The generative half is `M4.D`'s feedback loop — agents are both sensors and sources, which forbids modelling the field as an exogenous input.

## KS02.6 — Relationship systems **are** the emotional system, extended `[T]` `[K-ext]`

> "**Only Bowen theorists think that, when they study human relationship systems, they are also studying the emotional system. Relationship systems are extensions of the many emotional systems of individual brains.**"

*Model consequence:* the theoretical warrant for modelling ties as carriers of the same substance as intra-agent state — `bound_anxiety` on a `Relationship` is not a metaphor. Marked `[K-ext]`: the framing against neuroscience is Kerr's.

## KS02.7 — The whole is greater than the sum of its parts, and the reciprocity is symmetric `[T]`

> "the changes in both partners create and sustain the process — **the whole is greater than the sum of its parts**."

> "**Each spouse is equally reactive to the other spouse — it takes two to tango.** Most people pay lip service to this being true, but **it is difficult to live it as if it is true.**"

> "She is **one of three equal participants** in an automatic emotional system process."

*Model consequence:* "equal" recurs in all three worked cases across Chs 1–2 (spouses, abuse dyad, airport threesome). This is a **symmetry constraint** on the model: a two-party interaction **MUST NOT** be implementable with an asymmetric reactivity term standing in for asymmetric behaviour. Different *postures* at equal *reactivity* is the required shape — which is exactly `M1.A.9a`'s two axes at equal magnitude, now stated four times.

## KS02.8 — The three-person field regulates all three `[T]`

> "**The threesome creates an emotional field that regulates the emotional functioning of each of them.** The process is triggered by the **high level of emotional reactivity each person has to the others.**"

The airport vignette runs the full sequence — anticipatory anxiety in all three before contact, a warm greeting carrying a criticism, the father siding with the mother, the son's demeanour subduing, the mother reading the subdued demeanour as illness, the father minimising, the mother reacting to the minimising, the son counting the days.

*Model consequence:* a complete, quotable **worked trace** usable as a validation fixture for `M1.C` (triangle) plus `M4.C`'s four cues plus `M5.E`. Every step is a typed move with a target and a witness. **Candidate for a named scenario test** — it is short, unambiguous, and every transition is stated.

Note the opening state: **all three primed before any event** — anticipatory anxiety with no trigger. `M4.A`'s standing load must be able to rise on an *approaching* nodal event, not only on one that has occurred.
