## ⭐⭐⭐ FE06.1 — The **four propositions**: a complete 2×2, and the spec tests one cell `[K]` `[T]`

Numbered in the text, verbatim:

> "**(1) A stable twosome can be destabilized by the addition of a third person.** For example, a
> harmonious marriage may become conflictual after the birth of a child…
> **(2) A stable twosome can be destabilized by the removal of a third person.** For example, marital
> disharmony may increase after a child leaves home…
> **(3) An unstable twosome can be stabilized by the addition of a third person.** For example, a
> conflictual marriage may become more harmonious after the birth of a child…
> **(4) An unstable twosome can be stabilized by the removal of a third person.** For example,
> conflict in a twosome can be reduced if the two people avoid a third person who has been
> consistently taking sides on issues in their relationship. **Side-taking foments conflict by
> emotionally polarizing the issues.**"

*Model consequence:* **the most important structural finding in the chapter, and a new acceptance
criterion.**

`M11.C.3` asserts only that *triangling relieves the pair and costs the third within the same tick*.
That is **cell (3)** and nothing else. The other three cells are untested, and each is a direction
the model could silently get wrong:

| | **Addition of a third** | **Removal of a third** |
|---|---|---|
| **Stable twosome** | destabilises — (1) | destabilises — (2) |
| **Unstable twosome** | stabilises — (3) `M11.C.3` | stabilises — (4) |

Note the shape: **the effect's sign depends on the twosome's prior state, not on the direction of
the change.** An implementation in which adding a third always relieves — the obvious reading of
`M11.C.3` — fails cells (1) and (2) and would pass every criterion the spec currently has.

**New criterion candidate — an exhaustive walk of all four cells.** This is precisely the shape the
project's own review discipline prefers: make the wrong state unrepresentable by walking every
combination, rather than asserting the one branch a reader happened to check. Notably, cells (2) and
(4) are both about *removal*, which means `M11.C.11`'s removal criterion also needs to be
conditioned on the remaining pair's prior state — as written it asserts a single three-phase shape
for all removals.

Cell (4)'s example is also `M8.6`'s alignment penalty from the other side: removing a **side-taker**
stabilises, because "**side-taking foments conflict by emotionally polarizing the issues.**"

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE06.2 — Position value has **three regimes**, not two `[K]` `[T]`

> **Calm:** "two people are comfortably close (the insiders) and **the third is a less comfortable
> outsider**… the insiders are trying to preserve what they have and the outsider is trying to break
> into it."

> **Moderate:** "**In a calm emotional field, the insiders work to exclude an outsider; in a moderate
> anxiety field, the outsider is actively recruited for more involvement.**"

> **High:** "During high stress periods… **Now the outside position is the most comfortable and
> desired. Each member of an overly intense two-person relationship works to get an outside position
> in a triangle to escape the tensions of that relationship.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.C.2` is confirmed and is one regime short.** The spec requires the outside
position to be unfavoured when calm and favoured under tension — the two ends. `FE06.2` inserts a
**middle regime in which the recruitment direction reverses**: the uncomfortable *insider* pulls the
outsider in, rather than the outsider pushing in (calm) or everyone pushing out (high).

That is not a smooth interpolation between the ends — it is a distinct behaviour with a distinct
initiator, and it is where the chapter says triangles are **most easily observed** ("Triangles are
most easily observed during periods of moderate tension"). A two-regime implementation would produce
no traffic at exactly the load where the real process is most visible.

The calm regime also has content the spec lacks: it is **not static**. "**Both insiders continually
make adjustments to preserve their comfortable togetherness… The outsider is not idly standing by,
but continually attempts to form a togetherness with one of the insiders.** … **The system is never
still.**" `M1.C.3` requires triangles to be *inoperative* when the system is calm; `FE06.2` says
inactive is not the same as still. Worth reconciling — probably "inoperative" should mean *routing
no net anxiety*, not *emitting no moves*.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE06.3 — Functional impairment **reads the transfer direction** — resolving `FE03.16` `[K]` `[T]`

> "It is a paradoxical situation in that the '**child**' may appear to have more control of what
> happens than his parents. Often the child or young adult is able to manipulate the parents into
> giving him what he wants. As a result of these manipulations, he is characterized as '**selfish and
> demanding.**' **Observing the pressure he puts on the parents, outsiders often feel sorry for them
> and blame the child.** Indeed, the parents may forsake many of their interests and goals to devote
> more time, energy, and money to the 'child.' **The parents do give up 'self' in relationship to the
> child, but they do not give up as much as the child does. It is the child's functioning that is
> most seriously impaired, which is evidence that he is exerting the least amount of control. He wins
> battles but loses wars.**"

*Model consequence:* **this answers the open question `FE03.16` raised, and it is directly
implementable.**

`FE03.16` recorded that the overfunctioner can lose more self than the underfunctioner, and
concluded that **transfer direction is not readable off the functioning balance** — leaving the model
with an invariant (`M6.I.4`) whose direction it could not compute. `FE06.3` supplies the rule:

> **The party whose *functioning* is most impaired has given up the most self — regardless of who
> appears to be exerting control.**

So `M6.I.4`'s transfer direction is computed from **functional impairment**, not from apparent
dominance and not from the `M1.B.5` pole. Those three come apart exactly in the cases that matter,
and this passage names all three coming apart at once: apparent control high, self given up highest,
functioning worst.

It is also `M11.F.6`'s readout-trap list gaining its most consequential entry — **outsiders blame
the party who has lost the most** — and that is the same error `FE01.1`'s asymmetric misattribution
predicts an *outside observer* will make, which the spec had only for participants.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.4 — Triangles **outlive their members**, and a dead corner can be reached through a proxy `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Triangles are forever, at least in families. Once the emotional circuitry of a triangle is in
> place, it usually outlives the people who participate in it. If one member of a triangle dies,
> another person usually replaces him. The actors come and go, but the play lives on through the
> generations. Children may act out a conflict that was never resolved between their
> great-grandparents.** … **nor does it form anew or completely dissolve with the ebb and flow of
> anxiety. Rather than dissolve, triangles become more or less active with fluctuations in the level
> of anxiety.**"

And the worked case, which is the operational half:

> "**The fact that A's father is dead is not an impediment to detriangling from the parental triangle.
> All that is required is for A to establish a relationship with her paternal first cousin. By making
> emotional contact with the cousin, A activates the original triangle with her parents.** … **The
> emotional field does not die with the deaths of individual people; it is carried down the
> generations through interlocking triangles. The family will respond to A's contact with her cousin
> as if she had made contact with her father.** … **While certain family emotional issues may become
> dormant after someone's death, the more important issues can be reactivated in this way.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.C.3` in 1988, plus two requirements the spec does not have.**

1. **Replacement on death.** "If one member of a triangle dies, another person usually replaces him."
   `M7.C.1`'s mortality and `M11.C.11`'s removal both treat a death as a subtraction. `FE06.4`
   requires the *position* to persist and be **refilled**. That is `M1.D.4`'s leadership-office logic
   — vacancy stalls the system until occupied — applied to every triangle corner, and it is why the
   topology is stored separately from the active set. **Requirement candidate.**
2. **Proxy activation.** Contact with someone in the same emotional field **activates the original
   triangle**, and the family responds *as if* the contact were with the dead member. This is
   `M4.A.2`'s `TRIGGER` (a spike with no contact at all with the cut-off person) generalised to a
   *third-party* route, and it is `M7.A.2b`'s substitution rule — which the spec sources to
   `KS13.11` (2019) — stated in 1988 **with a mechanism and a worked case**. Upgrade the citation
   and add the mechanism: the substitution works because the field is carried by the interlocking
   structure, not by the individual.

The worked case is also a ready `M11` fixture: activate a dormant triangle by adding one tie to a
peripheral member, and assert that the **mother's** reaction fires — "Those people are not worth
seeing", "If you continue to see your cousin, our relationship will never be the same" — which is
`M5.E.1`'s ladder arriving on a tie the actor did not touch.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.5 — Detriangling has an **operational rule**, and the model has no move for it `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A principle for guiding one's efforts to communicate a neutral position in a triangle is to
> respond to someone's efforts to triangle by saying and doing things that push that person together
> with the one with whom he is having a problem.**"

Worked, with two examples that show the register matters as much as the content — the daughter to her
father: "**Dad, I have listened for years to your complaints about mother and I am finally beginning
to believe you are right**", delivered with a hand on the arm and a smile "**in a way that lets him
know she is not agreeing with him at all**"; and to her mother, a note saying "**nobody can make
little sister feel more secure than you.**" Of the second: "**The remark highlights the togetherness
process between mother and baby sister, exaggerates it, and encourages it.**"

And the signature that it is genuine:

> "**The thought of pushing together a twosome that is in disequilibrium usually does not 'feel
> right.'**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.C.3c` distinguishes *detriangle self* from *detriangle another* and gives
the first no operational content. `FE06.5` supplies it, and it is an emitted act rather than a
withdrawal.**

The spec's `M5.B.1` `DETRIANGLE` returns an aligned third party to neutral — that is *detriangle
another*. There is **no move for detriangle-self**, and `M8`'s predicate counts the outside position
without saying how an agent gets there. `FE06.5` says the act is: **emit toward the triangling party
a message that pushes them toward the person they have the problem with.**

That is implementable, it is distinct from `WITHHOLD` (it is a positive emission), and it has a
computable target rule — the emission's content points the *sender* at the *third*, not at the
speaker. **Requirement candidate: a `PUSH_TOGETHER` outcome, or `M5.B.1` split into its two
operations per `M1.C.3c`.**

Note also `M1.F.4a`'s register constraint doing real work here: the same words with a different tone
land as agreement or as neutrality. The spec has register constraining the *reply's* register; this
requires it to constrain the *interpretation*.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.6 — The core tenet, and its **diagnostic inverse** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A basic tenet of systems therapy is that the tension in a two-person relationship will resolve
> automatically when contained within a three-person system, one of whom is emotionally detached.**
> In other words, despite togetherness urges to the contrary, **a problem between two people can be
> resolved without the 'well intentioned' efforts of a third person to 'fix' it. It only requires that
> the third person be in adequate emotional contact with the other two and able to remain emotionally
> separate from them.** … **If the twosome does not triangle in a fourth person who is not detached,
> instead continuing to relate primarily to the third person who is, the twosome will bring their
> relationship back into equilibrium.**"

The inverse, stated as an inference:

> "**If a twosome regains emotional equilibrium in the presence of a detached third person, the
> existence of symptoms in a relationship suggests that the relationship is linked to one or more
> other people who are not emotionally detached from it.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M8`'s live-position predicate and `M11.C.7`'s topology-not-skill in their
clearest statement, plus an inference rule that is testable in both directions.**

The forward claim is `M11.C.7` — vary who talks to whom in whose presence, hold the coach's
parameters fixed, and the topology arms must differ. The **inverse** is new and stronger: *symptom
presence implies an undetached third exists.* In a run that is a checkable invariant — no symptom
should be able to establish in a subsystem all of whose adjacent thirds are detached.

Footnote 8 supplies the honest limits and they must travel with it: **absence** of symptoms does not
imply a detached third, because anxiety can be low for three other reasons — little stress, the
tension triangled out elsewhere, or high differentiation. So the implication runs one way only.

Also here, and it belongs to `M1.E.2a`: the third person's job is **contact plus separateness**, not
fixing. "**without the 'well intentioned' efforts of a third person to 'fix' it.**"

## ⭐⭐ FE06.7 — Three fixed functioning positions: **generator, amplifier, dampener** `[K]` `[T]` `[M]`

> "There are triangles in which one person can be characterized as the anxiety '**generator**,' a
> second person as the anxiety '**amplifier**' and the third as the anxiety '**dampener**.' The
> 'generator' is typically accused of setting the emotional tone for the triangle (and family)…
> **While the 'generator' may be the first person to get nervous about potential problems, he is not
> the cause of the anxiety that circulates in the triangle.** The '**amplifier**' adds to the problem
> by his inability to stay calm when the 'generator' is anxious. **The 'amplifier' not only reacts but
> exaggerates the 'desperateness' of the situation.** The '**dampener**' uses emotional distance to
> control his reactivity to the others, **but at a certain level of tension he can be relied on to
> become overly responsible for the others in order to calm things down. By predictably serving this
> function, the 'dampener' may reduce symptoms, but he reinforces the relationship process (the
> triangle). As the pressure continually shifts, no one in the triangle assumes responsibility for
> managing his own anxiety.**"

*Model consequence:* **three distinct transfer functions on one triangle, each implementable, and the
model has none of them.**

| Role | Behaviour | Model form |
|---|---|---|
| **generator** | first to become anxious; **not the cause** | low threshold on `M4.C.3`'s integrator; blamed by the others (`M1.A.18a`) |
| **amplifier** | cannot stay calm; **exaggerates** | appraisal gain > 1 on this tie (`M4.C.2`) |
| **dampener** | distance until a threshold, **then overfunction** | a two-regime propensity: `DISTANCE` below, `OVERFUNCTION` above |

Three things follow. First, the **generator is blamed and is not the source**, which is `M1.A.18a`'s
blame readout with a named target and no causal event — the same emergent readout `FE01.4` predicted
from the closeness/distance oscillation. Second, the **dampener's paradox** is `M11.C.13` at the
triangle scale: reduces symptoms, reinforces the process. Third, and most useful, these are
**per-person-per-triangle** roles, which is a level of state the spec does not carry — `M1.C.1` holds
the inside pair and the outsider, and nothing else about who does what.

Recommendation: **derive rather than store.** All three are expressible as existing per-tie
quantities (threshold, gain, and a propensity switch) and should fall out of `M4.C`/`M4.D` rather
than being assigned. If they do not fall out, that is itself informative — it would mean the policy
is too uniform to produce the differentiated roles the source says are typical.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.8 — **Actions beat words**: the family's belief cannot be changed by assertion `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Actions have more impact than words in a detriangling effort. An individual can spend years
> attempting to get his family to accept him as a person in his own right and achieve very little.
> Although he repeatedly tells family members about what is important to him and about where his
> viewpoints differ from theirs, their basic perception of him is amazingly unaltered. Overcoming a
> family's ability to ignore or neutralize one's proclamations of being a separate and distinct
> 'self' requires that one learn about the nature of the interlocking triangles in one's family and
> act on the basis of that knowledge. These actions can define to the family a self that cannot be
> ignored or explained away.**"

> "**A self is sometimes communicated most effectively by what is not said or done.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M9.3`'s hysteresis becomes symmetric, and that is a genuine addition.**

`M9.3` requires institutional acts to write the who-is-sick belief with hysteresis **that verbal
denial cannot reverse**. `FE06.8` states the same resistance for the *other* direction: verbal
**assertion** cannot write it either. So the family-level belief store has one rule, not two:

> **Beliefs about a member are written by acts and are not writable by claims — in either
> direction.**

That is cheaper than two mechanisms (`M10.A.2`), it explains why `M5.F.2a`'s self-declarations score
as negative evidence rather than as no evidence, and it gives `M5.D.7`'s completed-exchange
requirement its reason: only a sequence that reaches `FOLLOW_UP` has produced enough *acted*
evidence to move the belief.

The second quote is `M4.D.1b`'s `WITHHOLD` as a **communicative** act rather than an absence, which
is the strongest form of the requirement that it change tie state.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.9 — The change-back reaction fires **even when the family approves** `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 11:

> "There are many ways people attempt to undermine someone's effort to define more of a self. **It is
> so automatic for people to work against someone's effort toward more differentiation that it will
> occur even though the differentiating person's effort may be viewed positively on an intellectual
> level (respected and sensed as constructive for the whole family) by the ones trying to undermine
> it.**"

*Model consequence:* **a hard constraint on `M5.E` that the spec does not state and that a natural
implementation would violate.**

The reaction ladder **MUST NOT** be gated on the reactor's evaluation of the move. An implementation
in which the family opposes because it judges the move harmful would be wrong: opposition fires
against a move the reactor **respects and believes constructive**. That is consistent with
`M5.E.7`'s account — the reaction is caused by the **life-energy debit**, not by an appraisal of
content — and `FE06.9` is the observation that forces it.

It also means the belief layer and the reaction are **decoupled**: `M9` can hold an accurate,
approving belief while `M5.E` runs. That is `M9.2`'s orthogonality applied to the model's central
sequence, and it is assertable — an arm in which the family's belief about the move is positive
must still produce the ladder.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.10 — The coach can be **surpassed**; there must be no ceiling `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 14:

> "**One member of the original twosome, based on a structured effort, can raise his functional and
> basic level of differentiation above the one who was originally more detached. This has important
> implications for therapy. Differentiation depends on knowledge more than on the skills of a
> therapist. A motivated family member can go beyond the level of differentiation of his therapist.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.E` must not cap a member's `basic_level` at the external agent's.**

`M2.A` gives Dr Halim `basic_level` 55, the highest value in the instance. A natural implementation
— the coach lifts members *toward* its own level — would silently bound every arm at 55 and make
`M11.C.17`'s coach arm untestable above it. `FE06.10` forbids that explicitly, and gives the reason,
which the spec already holds in another form: **`M11.C.7`'s topology-not-skill.** Differentiation
depends on knowledge and structured effort, not on the coach's level.

**Requirement candidate**, and it pairs with `M1.E.7c`'s four landed-contact forms: what the coach
supplies is a *frame*, and a frame has no ceiling.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.11 — Detriangling is **gated by anxiety, non-monotonically** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**In general, detriangling is most effective in low to moderate anxiety situations. In high anxiety
> emotional fields, people are usually too uptight to detriangle effectively and the family is too
> anxious to respond to it. When anxiety is that high, the goal is to stay in contact with people but
> not let the anxiety dictate one's actions. When the anxiety is reduced, detriangling comments can
> then be constructive.**"

> "**When an uptight person who is not neutral tries to 'detriangle' himself in a highly anxious
> family, there is a good chance he will make the family problems worse.**"

Footnote 10 adds the differentiation-dependent severity:

> "People usually attempt to detriangle **before** they are anywhere near being objective… **In
> moderately to well integrated families under average conditions, one's bumbling is not too
> disruptive. In less well differentiated families and in moderately well differentiated ones under
> high stress, it can be highly disruptive.** This is when a therapist or '**coach**' who is more
> objective than oneself is particularly important."

*Model consequence:* **a gate for `M5.C`'s table, and it is two-sided in the same way `M5.C.1a`
is.** Detriangling works in the **low-to-moderate** band and fails or backfires at high anxiety, and
the *cost of failure* scales inversely with the family's differentiation. That is the same structure
as `M5.C.1a`'s family-type term — peace-agree families take a raised issue, reactive families need
the actor calmed first — and it is a **safety** property for the same reason.

It also explains what the coach is for at high anxiety, which `M1.E` does not currently say: not to
supply a frame but to supply **objectivity the actor has temporarily lost** (`M4.C.4`,
`M1.A.18d`). Two different coach functions at two anxiety levels.

## ⭐⭐ FE06.12 — The professional system **amplifies and reflects back** `[K]` `[T]`

The clinic case: an adolescent's therapist sides with the adolescent, the parents' therapist with the
parents, the two therapists polarise, each triangles in a supervisor, the supervisors escalate — "**As
the clinic gets into more and more turmoil, the parents and son calm down and get along better!**"

> "**Anxiety in the central triangle may have been diminished initially by its diffusion into the
> system of interlocking triangles, but when this anxiety spreads into the larger system (the mental
> health center, service agencies, and the courts), it is often amplified (via the emotional
> reactivity of the helping professionals) and focused back on the family. This anxiety in the
> 'helpers' can increase symptoms in the family. In most situations, helpers are primarily helpful;
> their involvement reduces anxiety and symptoms. There are these instances, however, when the
> anxiety-induced loss of differentiation and the process of triangling result in the helpers'
> becoming a major component of the family's problem.**"

*Model consequence:* **a gain > 1 return path the spec does not carry.** `M1.E.5` gives proximity a
negative-signed burden-transfer term and `M1.E.6` forbids a costless observer, but the anxiety that
leaves the family is not modelled as **coming back amplified**. `FE06.12` requires it, and it needs
`M1.E` to hold more than one external agent with ties **to each other** — which `M1.E.1` permits
(externals are `Person`s with real ties) and `M2.A` does not instantiate.

Note the two-sidedness, which must travel with it: "**In most situations, helpers are primarily
helpful.**" A model in which the coach is always iatrogenic is as wrong as one in which it is
always beneficial — `M11.F.6` again, and it is the honest framing for `M11.C.17`'s coach arm.

The passage is also `L05.2` (function absorbed by whoever will carry it, including the professional)
with the escalation made explicit, and it is `FE05.11`'s "outsiders must own the problem too."

## ⭐ FE06.13 — Neutrality's three betrayals, and the rule-making trap again `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Emotional neutrality is unrelated to approving or disapproving of particular aspects of human
> behavior and unrelated to making rules for oneself about not passing judgment on people's actions.
> Adherence to rules usually results in the appearance of being more neutral than is actually the
> case.** … **Dogmatic positions, the inability to define where one stands on important issues, and
> efforts directed at changing others all betray the absence of emotional neutrality. In essence,
> neutrality is reflected in the ability to define self without being emotionally invested in one's
> own viewpoint or in changing the viewpoints of others.**"

The two triangle-specific components:

> "**first, the ability to see both sides of a relationship process between two others, and second,
> the ability not to have one's thinking about that process clouded with notions about what 'should'
> be.**"

And the readout: "**Intolerance of aspects of the human process is a manifestation of being triangled
into it.**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE04.13`'s rule-invocation detector confirmed in a second chapter** — a
standing rule produces *the appearance* of neutrality, not neutrality. Two instances is enough to
add it to `M5.F.2a`'s form list.

The three betrayals map cleanly and exhaust `M1.A.9a`'s space: **dogmatic position** = outward
impingement; **inability to define where one stands** = inward; **efforts to change others** =
outward again, and it is the one `M1.E.2a` already carries. That the *inability to take a position*
is a betrayal of neutrality is the half most implementations would miss — silence reads as neutral
and is not.

The counterfeit form appears here too, for the third time in this book: "**When people are anxious,
their efforts to detriangle are usually attempts to 'pry' themselves free of triangles. Such
attempts are reflected in vociferous proclamations such as, 'I am not getting involved in the problem
between you two!' This either does not work or achieves some comfort for one person at someone else's
expense.**"

## ⭐ FE06.14 — Defending against the accusation puts you **back in the triangle** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A common response to a detriangling comment or action is, 'If you are not with me, then you are
> against me.' A pitfall people frequently fall into is attempting to defend or explain their actions
> in response to being accused of having turned against someone. These defensive and explanatory
> remarks get a person right back into the triangle.**"

> "If the detriangling person is fairly detached, **it is not necessary to respond to such
> accusations. His neutrality is conveyed in so many nonverbal ways that it automatically dilutes the
> force of any criticism.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M5.E.2`'s feeding rule generalises beyond `I-POSITION`.** The spec requires
the change-back reaction to decay unless fed by the mover defending or counterattacking. `FE06.14`
applies the identical rule to a detriangling move, with the same two feeding actions (defend,
explain) and the same decay in their absence.

So `M5.E.2` should be stated as a property of **any** differentiating outcome, not of the
`I-POSITION` state machine alone — which matters because `FE06.5`'s push-together move and
`M4.D.1b`'s `WITHHOLD` would otherwise have no ladder attached.

The escalation content is also here, and it is `M5.E.1`'s third rung with teeth: "**People might be
told that they will never be spoken to again, that they will be 'cut out of the will,' or that their
disloyalty will kill someone.**" *Cut out of the will* is a third independent source for
`M1.A.15`'s financial-dependence gate being the condition under which the threat is enforceable.

## ⭐ FE06.15 — Sibling conflict is **one side of a triangle** — and the interlocking trace `[K]` `[T]`

> "**This sibling conflict, while often explained on the basis of 'sibling rivalry,' is actually just
> one side of a triangle, the side where the triangle's tensions are usually acted out.**"

Figure 9's six-step trace is the corpus's most complete interlocking sequence: all triangles inactive
→ mother–son tension → father triangled in → tension shifts to father–son → mother withdraws, the
original triangle goes inactive, the daughter is triangled into father–son → **conflict erupts
between the two siblings.**

*Model consequence:* **`M1.C.6` in 1988** — a sibling-conflict event must instantiate the
parent-level triangle, and an intervention on the sibling pair alone must fail. The spec has this;
`FE06.15` supplies the six-step mechanism by which the tension *arrives* at the sibling pair, which
`M1.C.4`'s `activation_memory` and `M6.I.6`'s conservation should reproduce without being scripted.

The second-child case gives the same outcome from a different start: a mother anxious about treating
two children equally communicates that **she is responsible** for making them feel equally loved; each
child grows up holding her responsible for correcting perceived deficits; "**This can result in
siblings' continually fighting and not liking one another, an outcome the mother had dedicated

## ⭐ FE06.16 — Triangle count is **combinatorial** — a scale fact `[K]` `[#]`

> "**In a nuclear family unit of two parents and two children there are four 'uncomplicated' (one
> person at each corner) triangles. With the addition of just one more child, the number of triangles
> jumps to ten!** Some of these triangles are barely active; others are very active. **The active ones
> strongly influence one another.**"

*Model consequence:* C(4,3) = 4 and C(5,3) = 10, so the count is the plain combinatorial one. For
`M2.A`'s **eleven** family members that is **C(11,3) = 165** triangles, against 55 possible ties.

`M2.B.1` already requires that only ties carrying traffic be declared rather than the full 55-pair
matrix. **There is no triangle analogue, and 165 is where it starts to matter.** `M1.C.3`'s
separation of persistent topology from the active set is the right structure; what is missing is the
instruction to instantiate lazily — triangles come into existence when tension activates them, and
the declared set is the ones with history. **Requirement candidate for `M2.B`.**

## ⭐ FE06.17 — Function, not motive — the theory's own methodological rule `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The theory is an attempt to define the facts of functioning in human relationships — facts which
> can be observed to repeat over and over so consistently that they become knowable and predictable.
> What and how and when and where are facts about a relationship that can be observed. Conjecture
> about why something happens is not fact** and so the inclusion of such conjecture in the
> theoretical concepts was avoided as much as possible… **The triangle describes the what, how, when,
> and where of relationships, not the why.** … **The assignment of motive is necessarily subjective
> and not verifiable; the assignment of function can be objective and potentially verifiable.**"

Footnote 1, on beliefs specifically:

> "**While it may not be possible to assign the content of a belief to the realm of fact, the
> function of that belief for the individual and the group can be factually defined. The content of
> paranoid ideation, for example, is not factual, but paranoid ideation does have a function.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.F.1`'s event record and `M9.2` justified from the source's own method.**
The `Event` carries sender, targets, witnesses, move type, intensity, timestamp — *what, who, when* —
and **no motive field**. That is not an omission; it is the theory's rule. Worth saying in `M1.F`,
because "why did this agent act" is exactly the field a modeller adds by reflex.

Footnote 1 is `M9.2` operationalised: the model stores a belief's **function** (what it binds, per
`FE05.3`) and its **strength** (`FE02.6`), and never needs its truth value. That closes the loop on
`M9.2`'s requirement that truth and emotional function be orthogonal — they are orthogonal because
the model only ever reads one of them.

## ⭐ FE06.18 — Reciprocity inversion, worked, in 1988 `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The nature of this functional reciprocity becomes evident in situations where one or both parents
> become dysfunctional and the child's functioning improves dramatically. A chronic schizophrenic

## FE06.19 — Non-live corners, and why the model restricts to persons `[K]` `[T]`

Footnote 3:

> "**A live third person is not required for a triangle. A fantasied relationship, objects,
> activities, and pets can all function as a corner of a triangle. For all the facets of a triangle to
> be played out, however, three live people are usually required.**"

*Model consequence:* a **scope note** for `M1.C.1`, which requires a `Triangle` to hold three
members. That restriction is a deliberate simplification and the source says what it costs: work,
substances and fantasied relationships can occupy a corner, and the model cannot express them.

`M1.B.8`'s `investment` is the nearest existing quantity — it is share of thought occupied by a
target, and nothing in its definition requires the target to be a person. Record as `unmodelled`
with a note that the second clause limits the damage: **all** the facets need three live people, so
a person-only model captures the full process and loses the partial cases.

## FE06.20 — Well-differentiated people still triangle `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Some degree of triangling is always present in human groups. Three people, together for a brief
> period of time, will invariably gravitate to a process of two insiders and one outsider. Well
> differentiated people do not make a 'federal case' out of being an outsider, nor does their
> emotional security depend on being one of the insiders. This keeps whatever triangling that goes on
> among well differentiated people low-key and flexible.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.C.3a` exactly — routing capacity is a function of the members'
`functional_level`, not of the triangle — with the additional requirement that the **topology forms
regardless**. An implementation in which high-`basic_level` agents form no triangles is wrong; they
form them and route little. That is the difference between `M1.C.3`'s persistent topology and its
active set, and `FE06.20` is the reason both are needed.

Footnote 2 adds that triangle formation is **concurrent with**, not alternative to, the other
adaptations: "**The formation of a triangle is an anxiety-binding process that occurs simultaneously
with the other relationship adjustments people make in response to anxiety.**" So `M1.D.1`'s sinks
and `M1.C`'s triangling run together on the same tick, which `M3.D.1`'s ordering already implies and
should state.
