# Ch08 — Family Psychotherapy with Schizophrenia in the Hospital and Private Practice

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** *not stated.* No presentation venue or publication date appears in the chapter. Internal dating evidence only:
  - Notes 1 and 2 date the two data sources: an informal five-year clinical study at "the Menninger Clinic and Shawnee Guidance Center, Topeka, Kansas, 1949–1954," and a formal family research study at "the Clinical Center, National Institute of Mental Health, Bethesda, Maryland, 1954–1959."
  - "Since 1959, the author's work in family psychotherapy has been confined to out-patient work and to private practice."
  - "This method of family psychotherapy has now been used for eight years with over 300 families" — eight years from the 1954 shift places the writing around 1962.
  - Latest citations are Bowen (1961) and Sonne and Speck (1961).
  - "In the past three years there has been an effort to involve certain key members of the extended family."
- **Clinical setting and data source:** Three distinct settings, explicitly contrasted.
  1. **Informal individual-psychotherapy study (1949–1954, Topeka).** Patients *and* various family members each seen in individual psychotherapy. Yielded the mother-patient symbiosis hypothesis.
  2. **Live-in hospital research study (1954–1959, NIMH Clinical Center).** Schizophrenic patients and their families lived in residence on a psychiatric ward. Year 1: three mothers and three patients, mothers rooming with or visiting the patient, individual psychotherapy for each. From the end of year 1: whole families (both parents, the psychotic offspring, and normal siblings) resident on the ward, all individual psychotherapy discontinued, family psychotherapy substituted. Run concurrently with a growing out-patient family caseload.
  3. **Out-patient work and private practice (1959 onward).**
  - Totals stated: over 300 families over eight years; 63 families with a schizophrenic member, in family psychotherapy for periods from seven weeks to over seven years, comprising 51 families with one severely impaired adult schizophrenic offspring, ten families with a psychotic spouse, and two families with severely impaired young children diagnosed autistic or childhood schizophrenic. Separately, "some 250 families" with problems ranging from simple neuroses to near-psychotic degree.
- **Genre:** Both, theory-leading. Bowen states outright that "Considerable emphasis is placed on the theoretical orientation," and structures the chapter as four theory sections followed by three clinical ones. **Roughly 60% theory / 40% technique.** The technique material is unusually rich on the institutional/administrative surround rather than on in-session moves.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **The mother-patient tie oscillates between extremes.** Each mother-patient pair could at times be so close as to be "emotional Siamese twins," and at other times so distant and hostile that they repelled each other. Observed first in the 1949–1954 individual-therapy study.
- **The observed symbiosis was larger and more extensive than the hypothesis predicted, once families lived together.** Same hypothesis, same kind of families, but the close-living ward made "relationship actions and responses more intense" and new characteristics appeared. Bowen names two factors and **ranks them**: "One factor was the close living situation in which the relationship actions and responses were more intense, but the most important factor seemed to be 'observational blindness' in the investigators." `[p2 — pass 1 wrote "as much as", flattening a stated ranking; the observer's frame is the larger term.]` He explicitly compares it to men looking at prehistoric bones for centuries without seeing them.
- **Cross-person ego functioning in the closeness phases** (live-in ward families, then confirmed out-patient at lower intensity):
  - One ego could function for that of another.
  - One family member could accurately know the thoughts, fantasies, feelings, and dreams of another.
  - One family member could become **physically ill** in response to an emotional stress in another.
  - "Every detail of a patient's psychosis could have its mirror image in the mother."
  - Cases in which the patient's psychosis acted out the mother's unconscious.
- **In the angry-distance phases, the fusion recruits outsiders** — other family members, or nonrelatives "such as members of the hospital staff" — and the recruited person "would also 'fuse' into the family problem."
- **Strengths and weaknesses shift fluidly between members.** Part of a "pathology" could sit in one member and other parts in others — a "family jigsaw puzzle of strengths and weaknesses, with each family member holding pieces of the same puzzle and with considerable trading of pieces."
- **Treating one member can quiet the whole family.** In some families, individual psychotherapy with a *single* family member "would tone down the emotional process in the entire family for varying periods of time."
- **Fathers turned out to be as involved as mothers.** Concurrent out-patient observation contradicted the mother-centred hypothesis.
- **All the dynamisms of schizophrenia appear in mild and even asymptomatic families.** "It was surprising to find that all the family dynamisms so striking in schizophrenia were also present in families with the least severe problems and even in 'normal' or asymptomatic families." Basis for the single-scale claim.
- **Neurotic families change fast; schizophrenic families change slowly or indefinitely.** The rapid changes in neurotic families gave a range of observation impossible in schizophrenia.
- **Live-in mothers behaved irresponsibly when the staff held responsibility.** Under the year-1 arrangement, mothers "could be irresponsible in upsetting the patient and return home, telling the nurse, 'Your patient is upset.'"
- **When staff stopped absorbing emotional responsibility, family problems re-emerged as physical illness.** "An increasing number of family problems became manifested as physical illness, and families overused the ward physician and medical structure to make the staff responsible." The projection process "has an amazing capacity to utilize existing structure."
- **Community complaints did not fall when staff gave more help and instruction.** "The frequency of incidents remained the same except that they occurred in unusual places or in circumstances that 'you did not tell us about.'" `[p2 — correction: pass 1 wrote "Frequency dropped to zero only when responsibility was assigned to the parents." The text does not say that. What Bowen writes after the responsibility requirement is "Complaints from the hospital and the community stopped." The outcome variable changes between the two phases — **incidents** in phase one, **complaints** in phase two — and the stated goal of the requirement was "the prevention of complaints, whether justified or unjustified," i.e. the intervention targeted the complaint channel by name. Nothing in the chapter says the incidents stopped. No counts of any kind are given anywhere in this sequence: "frequent complaints", "remained the same", "stopped".]`
- **Normal siblings leave the family therapy effort.** In the live-in family psychotherapy "the normal siblings soon found reason to separate themselves from the family effort," leaving father, mother and psychotic one interlocked.
- **Improvement in the triadic one runs in sequential cycles, not in parallel.** One parent pulls up to a more confident level of functioning; then the other parent goes through the same process; then the triadic one "grows up a little." Each cycle could take as long as a year.
- **The recovered triadic one's move toward independence re-fires the whole process.** Detailed case: daughter in her late twenties, six of the previous years in institutions; rapid progress; working within six months; at one year functioning at the highest level Bowen had seen in a schizophrenia recovery, free of guilt and defensiveness. Parents calm, the parental pattern (aggressive anxious mother, compliant father) receded. **On the day she announced definite plans to move out, the formerly calm parents became "anxious, pleading, attacking, and helpless," and the family projection process returned at full intensity.** She gave up her own life goals "to help her parents," her functioning failed, within six months she had lost her job and was in a severe regression, and after another ten months she was returned to an institution.
- **Physical separation does not dissolve the triad.** With the triadic one living away from home in dependent attachments not involving the parents, all three are more comfortable — but "if the threesome is reunited, the old emotional fusion of the triad is immediately operative again."
- **The triad is not autonomous.** Bowen had expected the father-mother-patient triad to be "an autonomous, contained unit within which the problem could be resolved." Observation showed it is not.
- **Acting out relocates rather than disappears.** In the seventeen-year-old case, symptoms moved from the community/school into the family, then between family members as each parent took up psychotherapy in turn.
- **Parental conflict and projection to the child trade off directly.** Same case: a period of overt conflict between the parents ended when "the parents quickly joined forces and began another period of projection of the family problem to the son. The parental conflict subsided and the son went into another period of regression."
- `[p2]` **Fusion has a stated ceiling and a stated floor.** "The ego fusion is most intense in the least mature families. In a family with a schizophrenic family member, the fusion between father, mother, and child approaches maximum intensity." And: "Theoretically, the fusion is present to some degree in all families except those in which family members have attained complete emotional maturity." So fusion is monotone decreasing in maturity, saturates at the schizophrenia end, and reaches zero only at the theoretical top.
- `[p2]` **Year 1 produced good conventional results and no structural movement.** "The treatment results were rather good by usual individual standards but individual psychotherapy kept the family problems divided. Critical issues could be avoided by each expecting the other to deal with the issue, and no family went beyond the passive attitude of cooperating while waiting to be changed." Two things pass 1 missed: separate individual channels are themselves an **avoidance device** (each defers to the other), and there is a named inert engagement state — compliant attendance with no work.
- `[p2]` **The son improved within two weeks while the parents were making no progress.** In the seventeen-year-old case: "The parents made little progress in their first family psychotherapy hours together. Their anxiety was high and thoughts went only to inadequacies in the son. In spite of this, the son's anxiety subsided and within two weeks there were no further complaints from the school or the community." The parents' *taking up* the problem, not their success at it, moved the son. "The son expressed pleasure that the parents were finally working on their problem."
- `[p2]` **When one parent vacates, the other parent's inflow is not stable — it passes through closeness and inverts.** "While the mother was more occupied with her own problem and was less involved in family problems, the father began to 'feel with' the son's problems. There was a brief period of closeness between father and son, followed by conflict. The father became a strict 'disciplinarian,' to which the son reacted with aggressive acting out directed specifically at the father."
- `[p2]` **The trigger for the projection's return is the acting parent redirecting attention to the spouse.** The father, working alone, "made more rapid progress than the mother. After a brief period, his thoughts, feelings, and actions were directed more to the mother than the son. About three months after he started, there was a period of overt conflict between the parents. The parents quickly joined forces and began another period of projection." Pass 1 recorded the conflict/projection trade-off but not what set it off.
- `[p2]` **The son's symptom was the enabling condition for the parents' first joint work.** "The parents, with the tangible problem provided by the son's acting out, were able to focus on a family problem and then to begin the first working together on their own problem."
- `[p2]` **The responsibility response overshoots.** "The formerly irresponsible parents became **overly** responsible." Pass 1 recorded only that they became responsible.
- `[p2]` **Symptom escalation as a response to the other's anxious solicitude.** Psychotic-wife case: "The husband was anxious and solicitous in relating to the wife's overactive and bizarre thinking and behavior. The wife reacted to his reactions with an increase in psychotic symptoms." The eventual resolution replaced solicitude with contingency — "the wife's 'privileges' were determined by her ability to control her own behavior" — and symptoms disappeared within ten days.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **Undifferentiated family ego mass** → the family emotional oneness; the unit of illness → acts on all family members, with variable membership. "A fused cluster of egos of individual family members with a common ego boundary." Some egos more completely fused than others; some intensely involved during emotional stress and relatively detached at other times; father and mother "always maximally involved." Membership can extend to the extended family network, nonrelatives, and pets; "live-in servants are often more 'fused' into the family ego mass than certain blood relatives who are more differentiated." Clinically it "is considered equivalent to a single ego."
- **Emotional fusion / ego fusion / emotional stuck-togetherness** → obliteration of ego boundaries between two people, so that differentiation of one from the other is impossible → acts on the whole range of ego functioning, not a sector of it.
- **Differentiation of self from the family ego mass** → the developmental process by which a child achieves a defined self with well-defined ego boundaries → yields a "contained emotional unit" who can be emotionally close to anyone "without fusing into new emotional oneness."
- **Pseudo-self and pseudo-separation** → the substitute for differentiation available to the poorly differentiated → the person, unable to grow away gradually, after adolescence "tears himself away" and establishes a pseudo-self. Achievable by denial while still at home, or reinforced by physical distance. Physical distance is not evidence of separation: "the young adult who runs away never to see his parents again may have more basic attachment to his parents than siblings who continue to live with the parents."
- **Assortative mating on differentiation level** → "People tend to marry spouses who have identical levels of differentiation of self" → sets the starting level of the new family ego mass.
- **Marital fusion into a new family ego mass** → on marriage, two pseudo-selfs fuse into a "common self," ego boundaries between them are obliterated, individuality is lost to the common self → most intense in low-differentiation spouses; "a replica of their former emotional fusions with their parents."
- **Emotional divorce** → a distance-keeping device → both spouses long for closeness, but closeness produces fusion and its anxiety, so they hold "sufficient emotional distance ... for each to maintain as much 'pseudo-self' as possible."
- **Ego-strength trading within the marital dyad** → determines the internal pattern of the ego mass → "the way the spouses fight for, or share, the ego strength available to them. One spouse usually functions with a dominant share." Three positions on a scale (see A6).
- **Outside relationships as pressure relief** → determine "the intensity and extensiveness of the emotional phenomenon within the family ego mass" → when outside relationships are not too intense, intensity inside is toned down; when they become intense, the problem is transmitted outward and "the outside person ... becomes fused into the family ego mass." The outside relationship may be to the past generation (parents), the present generation (siblings), or the future generation (children).
- **The child as triadic stabilizer** → converts an unstable father-mother ego mass into a more stable triad → parental stability depends on the child continuing to function as "the triadic one"; the child's existence is so involved in "being for the parents" that he has no self of his own. Parents are made anxious by anything that threatens to remove the child from that function — "growing away" or "moving away."
- **Family projection process** → the mechanism transmitting the parental problem to the child → a three-step episode, repeated (see below). Bowen insists both sides are active: parents may force the projection against resistance until the child accepts it; most often parents initiate and the child accepts; or *the child may initiate and coerce the parents to agree that he is the cause of the family problem.*
- **The three steps of each projection episode** (which the clinical program is built to interrupt):
  1. **Feeling-thinking** — a feeling in the mother merges into thinking about defects in the child.
  2. **Examining-labeling** — she searches for and diagnoses the defect in the child that best fits her feeling state; "the 'clinical examination-diagnosis' step."
  3. **Treating** — she acts toward and treats the child as though the diagnosis is accurate.
- **Projection creates the defect it names.** "The mother feels and thinks about the child as a baby ... she calls him a baby and she treats him as though he is a baby. When the child accepts the projection, he becomes more infantile." Each accepted projection "adds to his increasing state of functional inadequacy."
- **Anxiety is the fuel, and externalisation is the discharge.** "The projection is fed by the mother's anxiety. When the cause for her anxiety is located outside of the mother, the anxiety subsides." The child's incentive: "For the child, accepting the projection as a reality is a small price to pay for a calmer mother."
- **Blamer / self-blamer axis** → the two complementary perceptual failures that make projection possible → the blamer's perceptual system is attuned to finding causes in the other or the environment and "he is incapable of looking inside of self"; the self-blamer accurately perceives causes in self "but he is as impaired at looking outside himself as the blamer is at looking inside of self." Both are equally irresponsible for self. The roles can invert: an overload of self-blaming "can erupt into blaming," and a self-blamer can reach the point of feeling himself the cause of "floods, storms, and earthquakes."
- **The wordless completion of a projection.** Bowen's two-person illustration: A and B are equally responsible for an embarrassment; A thinks "if I had not been so awkward…" while B simultaneously thinks "look at the mess caused by A's awkwardness." "The process is completed without a word being exchanged. Both blame A, both are blind to the part played by B, and both act as if their diagnosis is accurate."
- **The projection recruits external experts to certify the defect.** It can utilise an existing minor defect, and "parents can go from one physician to another until the 'feared' defect is finally confirmed by diagnosis." Physical examinations, laboratory tests, and psychological tests all facilitate it; the projection "can pour into a newly discovered innocuous congenital anomaly and convert it to a disability."
- **The medical/psychiatric consultation as the completing move of the projection.** "The psychiatric consultation fits, step by step, with the family projection process" — examine, confirm with a diagnosis, recommend treatment. So "a time-proven principle of good medical practice serves to support the parental projection process in the family, to crystallize and fix emotional illness in the patient, and to help make the illness chronic and irreversible."
- **The therapist's acceptance of responsibility as a load-bearing part of the mechanism.** When the psychiatrist takes responsibility for treating the illness in the patient he "condones the externalization of the parental problem," takes on "the family problem separated from the family," and "permits the parents to continue projecting onto the patient, without holding the parents responsible."
- **Multigenerational descent of differentiation** → schizophrenia as the product of a family process in which a child in each generation is more impaired than the parents, repeating until an offspring is low enough that his marriage produces enough parental impairment for schizophrenia in a child. Explicitly: "children less involved in the process may have higher levels of differentiation of self than the parents."
- **Spread vs. concentration of the parental problem** → the second variable governing severity → the parental impairment may be transmitted to a single child or "spread" to multiple children or to other relationships in the extended family.
- `[p2]` **The course of a new family ego mass has exactly two ordered determinants.** "In general, the course of the new family ego mass is determined by (1) the pattern of dynamics within the ego mass and by (2) relationships to those outside the ego mass." Pass 1 recorded both as separate mechanisms and missed that Bowen states them as *the* two, numbered.
- `[p2]` **Functional capacity is relationship-context-dependent, not a global trait.** "The Achilles' heel of these people is a close emotional relationship. They may function successfully in business or a profession as long as they keep relationships casual and brief, but in a marriage to a spouse with equally poor differentiation of self, they become overly involved emotionally." A low-differentiation person is not uniformly impaired; the impairment is expressed only on close ties.
- `[p2]` **Long-term separation is achieved only by substituting a new ego mass.** For those at the lowest level: "Some achieve so little self that they collapse into psychosis during their first efforts to function independently of the parents. Some achieve sufficient 'pseudo-self' to function for brief periods alone, but any long-term separation from parents is accomplished only by finding a new family ego mass to which they can append themselves. Their life adjustment depends on the all-important attachment to others who will guide and advise them and from whom they can borrow enough self to function." This is the mechanism behind the non-decay of coupling, and pass 1 never stated it: the person is never *uncoupled* during separation, he is **re-attached elsewhere**. The same wording recurs in the reunion passage — the triadic one lives away "in dependent attachments that do not involve the parents", and meanwhile "the parents function by 'borrowing self' from outside themselves and by 'projecting their inadequacies' to others." Both ends of the severed tie are substituting, not decaying.
- `[p2]` **A three-state impairment variable held across a marriage's whole history.** "From the beginning of their marriage, the husband had been either a potential 'patient,' an actual 'patient,' or a compensated 'patient,' and the wife had been a 'mother' to his varying degrees of incapacity." *Potential / actual / compensated* is a discrete state machine, with the reciprocal position ("mother") held constant across all three.
- `[p2]` **The external absorber is selected by expedience.** "On a practical level, it is easier and more expedient for medicine and society to assume responsibility for the projected family problem (the psychotic patient) than to attempt to hold the parents responsible." The sink is chosen because it is the path of least resistance, not because it is effective.
- `[p2]` **Responsibility for self is available only in sporadic periods.** "Since the parents of a schizophrenic person grew up as triadic recipients of similar but less intense family projection processes in their own families in which responsibility for self was not possible, it is difficult for such parents to assume more than sporadic periods of responsibility for self." Note also the explicit intergenerational intensity gradient — the parents' own childhood projection was "similar but less intense."

## A3. Predictive claims

*(E = evidence offered in this chapter, A = asserted.)*

- **If parents have a low level of differentiation of self AND a high proportion of the parental impairment is transmitted to one or more children, then schizophrenia develops.** Two named variables: severity of the problem in the parental ego mass, and degree of concentration vs. spread. [E — the 63-family series, but no data table.]
- **Concentration dominates severity.** "A less severe parental problem transmitted to a single child would produce more severe schizophrenia than a very severe parental problem 'spread' to multiple children." [A — no supporting data.]
- **People *tend to* marry spouses with identical levels of differentiation of self.** [A.] `[p2 — correction: pass 1 stated this deterministically ("will be identical"). Bowen's wording is "People tend to marry spouses who have identical levels of differentiation of self." It is a tendency claim, and the model's marriage-seeding rule should carry a spread, not an equality constraint.]`
- **The lower the level of differentiation of two spouses, the more completely closeness produces fusion, and the more emotional distance ("emotional divorce") they must keep.** [A.]
- **If a spouse habitually "gives in," he loses strength to the other and can reach a "no-self" state, at which point he becomes incapacitated with (a) physical illness, (b) emotional illness, or (c) social dysfunction — work inefficiency, drinking, social irresponsibility.** A marriage may be "permanently stabilized with the 'no-self' spouse disabled with a chronic illness." [A, with illustrative cases.]
- **If outside relationships intensify, intensity inside the ego mass falls (and the outside person is drawn in); if outside relationships are weak, intensity inside rises.** [A.]
- **If the full parental problem is transmitted to a single maximally impaired child, the parental relationship is left calm and harmonious.** [E — asserted as an observed marriage type, and dramatised in the seventeen-year-old case where parental conflict subsided as projection resumed.]
- **If the triadic one attempts to separate self from the parents, parental anxiety rises sharply and the triadic one's budding self returns to the ego mass "to save the parents," and regression follows.** [E — the daughter case, with a stated timeline: job lost within six months, institutionalised ten months later.]
- **The lower a person's differentiation, the more vulnerable he is to loss of the important other**; some "collapse into psychotic nothingness in the face of life events that threaten or disrupt their dependent attachments." [A.]
- **The more immature the people, the more intense the blaming and self-blaming.** [A.]
- **When the diagnosis is confirmed and the triadic one becomes "a patient," parental anxiety subsides.** [A/E.]
- **If the psychiatrist supports the parental side of the projection at the point of first diagnosis, prognosis for modifying the family process is worse; if he avoids it, prognosis is better.** Specifically: where the triadic one *opposes* the diagnosis and the designation "patient," and the psychiatrist declines to support the parents, prognosis is better; where the triadic one readily accepts, "the chances of modifying the family process are remote." [A — presented as a theoretical proposition.]
- **Individual psychotherapy with a patient still living with his parents may make him a *more* effective absorber of the parental projection.** [A — offered as the out-patient case where the psychiatrist is "even more effective in facilitating the family projection process."]
- **Family anxiety at the psychotic collapse is caused not by the psychosis but by the loss of the triadic one's function.** Corollaries: anxiety is *less* when the psychosis develops slowly, or when the psychotic one is quiet and cooperative and continues to serve the projection — and parents may then not seek help at all unless urged by an outside person or agency. Anxiety is *most* intense when the collapse is sudden and the psychotic one "not only rebels against accepting the projection but turns into a vehement 'blamer' who denies the existence of a problem, while, in addition, causing problems with irresponsible behavior." [A/E.]
- **If a person is hospitalised or restricted for "behavior" rather than "sickness," his progress is more rapid and his hospital stay briefer.** [E — the psychotic son who agreed to sign himself in; the bus-stop patient discharged back to his job within weeks.]
- **If the reason given is "sickness," the resisting person is disadvantaged either way**: insisting on his own perception makes him uncooperative and in conflict with his environment; accepting it makes him "dependent on the environment to teach him about 'sickness.'" [A, with mechanism.]
- **The more severe the family problem, the more likely the family is to terminate psychotherapy after symptomatic improvement.** [E — stated as a pattern across the caseload.]
- **When the family achieves a higher level of differentiation, the projection process disappears permanently.** In schizophrenia, by contrast, "when the family is calm, the process can disappear from view, but if anxiety builds up again, the process appears again." [A/E.]
- **When acting out shifts from the community back into the family, that is a hopeful sign.** [A — "Generally, it is a hopeful sign."]
- **When one parent differentiates, the other parent's involvement with the child increases.** [E — while the mother "was more occupied with her own problem and was less involved in family problems, the father began to 'feel with' the son's problems."]
- **When the triadic one takes up the therapy work, parental motivation subsides.** "In these situations, it is common for parental motivation to subside and for parents to depend on the triadic one to solve the entire problem." [A/E.]
- **Parents of a schizophrenic offspring, who "commonly function as helpless and irresponsible people, have the capacity to function responsibly when it is required."** Conditional on the surrounding system requiring it. [E — the community-complaints sequence.]
- **Family progress correlates with parental responsibility.** "It was during the periods when the parents were functioning responsibly that families made the most progress in the daily family psychotherapy meetings." [E.]
- **The therapist's/administrator's own level of responsible functioning gates what he can require of the family.** "When I functioned more responsibly, it was possible to require families to be responsible." [E, first-person.]
- **Institutional resources substitute for family resources.** Live-in parents "became overdependent on the hospital resources available to them, which precluded development of their own resources." [E.]
- **The schizophrenic process can be reversed in the family ego mass in which the parents grew up, if members of the family of origin are available for therapy** — while it is "as fixed and rigid in the father-mother-patient triad as in the patient." [A — "there is evidence to indicate," evidence not shown; the extended-family effort is "too new to report here."]
- `[p2]` **Ego fusion intensity is monotone in immaturity, saturating at the bottom and vanishing only at the theoretical top.** "The ego fusion is most intense in the least mature families"; in a schizophrenic family it "approaches maximum intensity"; it is "present to some degree in all families except those in which family members have attained complete emotional maturity." [A — a stated functional shape with two endpoints, and the only shape statement about any variable in the chapter.]
- `[p2]` **The rate at which a family can work on itself scales with its differentiation level.** The seventeen-year-old family's parents "proceed much more cautiously and slowly at investigation of their own intrapsychic problems than would be characteristic of parents in less impaired families." Also stated at the top of the scale: "The differentiation process is most rapid with problems of neurotic degree." [E/A — a per-family rate parameter, not just a per-family ceiling.]
- `[p2]` **The symptom-bearer's ability to oppose the projection, and the parents' ability to reach effective self in acute situations, index the family's level.** "The ability of the son to oppose the family projection process and the ability of the parents to achieve a reasonable effective level of 'self' in acute situations is more 'self' than is found with severe schizophrenia." [E — Bowen uses these two behavioural observables to rank a family relative to severe schizophrenia. See Q-VALIDATION: it is the nearest thing in the chapter to a level-estimation procedure, and it is ordinal, behavioural, and clinician-judged.]

- Bus-stop patient: "Within a week he had another town pass and shortly thereafter he was discharged to return to his job."
- Neurotic-husband case: "A husband in his mid-thirties had four years of fairly successful psychoanalysis." "About two months later she said…" "he … went into a week of individualistic, 'I do not need you.'"
- Seventeen-year-old case: son is "seventeen-year-old"; "within two weeks there were no further complaints from the school or the community"; "During the next six months, the mother was the only family member to attend"; "Within a few months the son's behavior was 'almost normal'"; "some eight months after the start of the family psychotherapy when the exasperated father demanded the family psychotherapy time for himself"; "About three months after he started, there was a period of overt conflict between the parents"; the son "asked for psychotherapy time for himself, the first time he had been seen since the evaluation almost a year before"; "After two months the son stopped his regular appointments"; "This family has now had seventy-two hours of family psychotherapy over a period of eighteen months"; "he still has at least another year of high school."
- Scale positions named (ordinal, top to bottom): highest levels of maturity → the person who later develops a neurosis, "somewhere in the middle of the scale" → the person who later becomes the parent of a schizophrenic offspring, "toward the lower end" → the person who later becomes schizophrenic, "at the lower end as a 'no self.'"

## A5. Terminology

- **Undifferentiated family ego mass** — Bowen's chosen name for the family emotional oneness. He is candid that "the term has certain inaccuracies but it aptly describes the over-all family dynamics, and no other term has been as effective in communicating the concept to others." Clinically treated as equivalent to a single ego.
- **Emotional fusion / emotional connectedness / emotional stuck-togetherness / ego fusion** — offered as four interchangeable descriptions of the same phenomenon.
- **Symbiosis / symbiotic relationship** — the *superseded* term. Explicitly retired here: "The 'symbiotic' hypothesis was discarded." It was accurate about one area of the phenomenon (mother-patient) and was "a compatible extension of individual theory," which is why it was reachable and why it blinded him to the rest.
- **The family as the unit of illness** — the concept the jigsaw observations suggested.
- **Differentiation of self** — the achieving of a defined self with well-defined ego boundaries out of the family ego mass; equated with "our familiar concept of a mature person."
- **Pseudo-self / pseudo-separation** — the substitute for real differentiation; a self "torn away" rather than grown away.
- **Common self** — what two pseudo-selfs fuse into on marriage.
- **Emotional divorce** — the sustained emotional distance spouses keep to preserve pseudo-self against fusion anxiety.
- **No-self / "ego nothing"** — the lowest position. The schizophrenic person "functions on a 'self' borrowed from others"; in the triad he is "an 'ego nothing' which permits the parents to be an 'ego something.'"
- **Triadic one** — Bowen's deliberate replacement for "the psychotic one," "the schizophrenic one," "the patient." Note 3: "I prefer to use the term 'triadic one' because it designates one component part of the family ego mass."
- **Interdependent triad** — his name for the father-mother-psychotic-one unit left after normal siblings withdraw from the therapy.
- **Family projection process** — the transmission mechanism; explicitly offered as the family-theory replacement for the individual-theory pair "projection" (by parents) plus "introjection" (by the child).
- **Blamer / self-blamer** — the two poles of an axis of functioning; both equally irresponsible for self.
- **Undifferentiatable** — coined here for the schizophrenic family ego mass, which is not merely undifferentiated: "in my experience it is 'undifferentiatable.'"
- **Functioning positions** — used in place of "role." Note 4 is explicit that Spiegel's (1957) "role" "might be more accurate," but he could not adapt role concepts consistently and preferred "other descriptive terms" over loose use of role theory.
- **Vocabulary substitutions imposed on the ward** — a deliberate lexical program, not incidental: replacing "sick," "illness," "schizophrenia" with **"dysfunction," "incapacity," "functional collapse"**; replacing "patient" with **"impaired one," "disabled one," "collapsed one," "triadic one."**
- **Family psychotherapy with a single family member** — used here as a term of art: the therapist relates to the family ego mass *through* one member, analogous to relating to "the intact portion of the patient's ego" in individual psychotherapy. Not the same as individual psychotherapy.
- `[p2]` **"Compensated neurotic" / "compensated 'patient'"** — a named third state between well and symptomatic, held stably for years with the spouse acting as protector: "he was a 'compensated neurotic' with his sickness under control. The wife was the adequate one who protected him from upsetting situations."
- `[p2]` **State of the vocabulary at ~1962 — what is present and what is not.** Present and load-bearing: *undifferentiated family ego mass* · *emotional fusion* · *differentiation of self* (as both a process and a single scale) · *pseudo-self / pseudo-separation* · *common self* · *emotional divorce* · *family projection process* with three named steps · *interdependent triad* · *triadic one* · *no-self* · *blamer / self-blamer* · *functioning positions* · multigenerational descent with a generation count. **Absent:** the word *triangle* (Bowen writes *triad*) · *emotional cutoff* (though "the young adult who runs away never to see his parents again" describes it exactly, unnamed) · *sibling position* as a concept (siblings appear only as "more differentiated siblings") · *nuclear family emotional system* · *societal emotional process* · any numeric scale or quartile scheme.
- `[p2]` **What this chapter discards is the *symbiotic hypothesis*, not the ego mass.** "The 'symbiotic' hypothesis was discarded and thinking was directed to a larger family phenomenon." By contrast the ego-mass term is actively retained and defended in the same chapter: "The term has certain inaccuracies but it aptly describes the over-all family dynamics, and no other term has been as effective in communicating the concept to others." This conflicts with the terminology-timeline row in `_CONVERGENCES.md` that has "undifferentiated family ego mass **already discarded**" at Ch05 / ~1961 — a chapter this one post-dates. Recorded, not resolved: it is a question for Ch05, not for Ch08.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **Parents are structurally fixed at maximum involvement.** "The father and mother are always maximally involved" in the ego mass. Other members' involvement fluctuates with stress.
- **Membership of the ego mass is variable and can include non-kin.** Extended family, nonrelatives, pets; live-in servants often more fused than differentiated blood relatives.
- **The spouses are the highest-leverage position.** "Since the two spouses (or parents) are the two family members most involved in the family ego mass, the most rapid family change occurs when the spouses are able to work as a team." The final stage of successful family psychotherapy is "the two spouses working together on their individual and joint problems."
- **The marital ego-strength scale — three positions:**
  1. Both spouses "fight for their rights" → a conflictual marriage; conflict subsides when either gives in.
  2. Middle group: differences resolved by one spouse who "reluctantly gives in"; that spouse loses strength, the other gains it.
  3. One spouse "works actively to be the dependent one who gives in," becoming a no-self supporting the strengthened self of the other → can end in chronic disability that permanently stabilises the marriage.
- **The symptom-bearer is the child who absorbs the projection.** Selection is by concentration: some marriages contain the problem between the spouses with little or no involvement of the children; others transmit "the full weight of the parental problem ... to a single, maximally impaired child, leaving the parental relationship calm and harmonious"; in "the average family" the problem is spread over more than one outside relationship.
- **Sibling differentiation is unequal and can exceed the parents'.** Less-involved children may reach higher differentiation than their parents. In the parents' own childhoods, "their more differentiated siblings" began the steady process of growing away while the future parent of a schizophrenic did not.
- **Normal siblings exit the therapy.** In live-in family psychotherapy they "soon found reason to separate themselves from the family effort."
- **The mother is the usual projector, the father the usual endorser.** "The parental problem is most often projected to the child by the mother, with the father supporting her viewpoint."
- **Who moves first.** In the cyclical live-in course: one parent, then the other parent, then the triadic one. In the seventeen-year-old case Bowen made the choice explicit and delegated it — "It was suggested that one parent try the psychotherapy alone and that they decide which one it should be"; the mother went first (six months), the father took over at eight months and "made more rapid progress than the mother," and the son asked for his own time only at about one year.
- **Generational position of the outside relationship.** Spouses may anchor to the past generation (their parents), the present generation (their siblings), or the future generation (their children).
- **Hospital staff as occupants of family positions.** Staff members are named as people who can be fused into the family ego mass during the angry-distance phases; ward staff "feel with the patient and be angry at the parents" — i.e. take up the projection's assigned positions.
- `[p2]` **The triad's fixed membership is a selection effect, not a premise.** The live-in family psychotherapy began with both parents, the schizophrenic offspring *and* the normal siblings. "The normal siblings soon found reason to separate themselves from the family effort, leaving the father, mother, and psychotic one interlocked in an intense emotional interdependence, which I have called the 'interdependent triad.'" The three-person unit is what remains after the less-involved members leave, so its membership is derived from involvement rather than assigned by kinship role.
- `[p2]` **The triad is derived from dyadic instability.** "The child functions as a stabilizer for the parents, converting the unstable father-mother ego mass into a more stable triad." Two-person instability is the stated cause of the third position.
- `[p2]` **Who moves first is not specified, and Bowen delegated the choice.** In the cycles he gives the *order* (one parent → the other parent → the triadic one) but never says which parent. In the seventeen-year-old case he handed the selection to the family: "It was suggested that one parent try the psychotherapy alone and that they decide which one it should be." The mother went first for six months; the father, who took over at eight months, "made more rapid progress than the mother." In the neurotic-couple case the mover was the **adequate** spouse, working alone. This chapter therefore supplies no rule about which member moves first, and contains a counter-instance to the peripheral/inadequate-first pattern (see P5).
- `[p2]` **Reason for the single-member format.** "The parents were in so much basic disagreement about the son that their 'selfs' neutralized each other. Their effort ended in the same 'selfless' lack of direction that characterized the home environment." Two present selfs can cancel; the format change is a response to cancellation, not a preference.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

- **Direct the therapy at the family ego mass, not at individuals.** "The family psychotherapy is directed to the family ego mass, without specific regard for the individuals involved in it at the time." Relate to it as one would relate to an individual ego in individual psychotherapy.
- **Choose who is in the room by dynamics and immediate goal, not by rule.** "All involved family members present, with any combination of family members present, or with only one family member present."
- **Work through a single member when the combination is unproductive.** When the parents' selfs "neutralized each other," Bowen had them pick one to attend alone.
- **Coach the attending parent to define a self:** "become clear about her own beliefs and convictions, and especially to maintain a stand on important family issues without losing 'self' in the family emotional field." The target behaviour is stated exactly: "deal with distraught family situations by controlling her own 'self' instead of trying to change the 'self' of others."
- **Aim the therapy at the parental anxiety that feeds the projection, not at the projection's content.** "The most profitable approach has been to avoid becoming entangled in the issues of the family projection process and to direct attention to the parental anxiety that feeds the projection. A few hours spent with one or both parents is often enough to relieve the immediate anxiety and convert the situation into a calm, productive, family psychotherapeutic effort."
- **When anxiety is too high to dodge the sickness-diagnosis issues, state the theoretical position clearly and refuse.** Two escalating refusals, in Bowen's own order: "I state my theoretical position as clearly as possible and quietly refuse to participate in action designed to fix the problem in the patient, or I refuse to continue working with the family unless parents are willing to assume some responsibility for their part in the problem."
- **Hold the family responsible for the hospitalisation decision and for the reason given.** Give hospitalisation as "we are fed up with your behavior and the problems you create at home," never as sickness. The worked line: when the mother said "Will you go for us, to give us some relief?" the son answered "That's the first fair proposition I've heard. I am ready to sign myself in any time."
- **Substitute "behavior" for "sickness" as the basis of every restriction, inside and outside the family.** The bus-stop patient was restricted for behaviour and told he could try again when "he was able to behave in a way that did not call attention to himself or interfere with others." Bowen's reason: behaviour "provided him with something he could understand, that he could work on, and that he could change."
- **Instruct the surrounding institutions to do the same.** The school was "reminded that the therapist did not recommend that the boy remain in school and was urged to make decisions on the basis of behavior instead of 'sickness,' sending the boy home if his behavior did not meet school rules."
- **Ask a family member to stop thinking, calling, and treating the other as sick — all three steps.** To the wife: could she "stop thinking of him as a neurotic, stop calling him neurotic, and stop treating him as an impaired person"? When she objected that she had no replacement word, he suggested she "play like" he was not sick, weak, or neurotic. (She reported later she could change calling and treating but not thinking.)
- **Function as catalyst and participant observer.** In the live-in family hours: "a catalyst to facilitate the working together and as a participant observer who was sufficiently detached to observe objectively and interpret the family process."
- **In the separation crisis, split the therapy deliberately.** "As soon as parents and triadic one are aware of the part that each plays in the family process, the parents are seen together with the explicit goal of helping them to separate their lives from the triadic one, and the triadic one is seen alone with the explicit goal of helping him to function without the parents and to resist his automatic emotional 'reflex' to 'save the parents' ... when they are anxious."
- **Direct part of the "support" to the parental side, not to the patient alone.** Bowen's revised approach to schizophrenia.
- **Take responsibility for one's own function first, then require it of the family.** As project director he assumed responsibility with the hospital administration for stopping the community incidents, and only then could require the families to be responsible.
- **Require the parents to be responsible for the impaired member; staff help but do not take over.** On the ward: "The doctors and nursing staff would be available to help the parents but not to assume direct responsibility for the 'patients.' One parent was required to be with the 'patient' at all times."
- **Where structure forces a treating role, rebuild it as a request-driven service.** The ward "clinic" with regular office hours: families made appointments, went for consultation, returned to their ward "homes" holding their own drug supplies and responsible for carrying out the recommended treatment.
- **State requirements as explicit rules with an explicit target.** The families "were told that they were held responsible for learning the rules of the community and the hospital and that there be no complaints about disturbed behavior off the ward," with the goal stated as "the prevention of complaints, whether justified or unjustified."
- **Consider bringing in key extended-family members** to add "strength" to the family ego mass (new, results not reported).
- `[p2]` **The single-member format is explicitly *temporary*.** "The technique of conducting family therapy by **temporarily** seeing only one family member." Pass 1 recorded the move without its stated time-boundedness.
- `[p2]` **Return the hospitalisation decision to whoever holds the tolerance, rather than deciding it.** Psychotic-wife case: "I maintained the position that home was best for the wife but that he could go toward hospitalization if he had reached his tolerance for the stress at home." The hospitalisation did not happen. Ownership of the decision, not the decision's content, is the operative variable.
- `[p2]` **Two different therapist stances, for two settings.** Live-in family psychotherapy was "a nondirective psychotherapy in which the family members 'worked together on the family problem'", the therapist acting as catalyst and participant observer. The out-patient technique in the same chapter is heavily directive — stated refusals, explicit requirements, instructions issued to schools, a stated goal ("the prevention of complaints"). Bowen reports the out-patient results as the better ones. Pass 1 recorded both stances and never noticed they are opposite.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not think of, diagnose, or treat any family member as sick.** All three steps are prohibited, because they are the three steps of the projection process. Avoid the concepts "sick," "illness," "patient," and avoid diagnoses.
- **Do not accept responsibility for treating the illness in the patient.** Doing so condones the externalisation, takes on the family problem separated from the family, and lets the parents keep projecting unaccountably.
- **Do not perform the standard psychiatric consultation on a family in a projection crisis.** Examine → diagnose → recommend treatment "fits, step by step, with the family projection process."
- **Do not supply the diagnostic confirmation the family is shopping for.** Parents will go from physician to physician until the feared defect is confirmed; tests and examinations all feed the process.
- **Do not conduct individual psychotherapy with a patient still living with his parents** — it may make him "an unusually effective absorber of the parental projection."
- **Do not run any individual psychotherapy alongside family work.** In the research decision: "experience suggested that any individual psychotherapy might obscure 'family' observations," and all individual psychotherapy was discontinued.
- **Do not oppose the projection process head-on.** "In the early clinical efforts I too often found myself opposing the projection process" — named as an error, replaced by attending to the anxiety behind it.
- **Do not hospitalise for "sickness" if any alternative exists**, and do not let the psychiatrist be the one who owns the hospitalisation decision.
- **Do not agree to somatic treatment demanded as an anxiety discharge.** He refused to give the wife "shock treatment," and turned the request into "an exchange about what kind of 'shock' he meant."
- **Do not be over-helpful.** The staff's period of "being overhelpful ... in specifying off-limits areas and in helping them to understand and deal with disturbed behavior" left the incident rate unchanged, because "in providing help and instruction, the staff doubted the parents' ability to find their own solutions."
- **Do not scold without stating the requirement.** "The families had been scolded for their mistakes but they had not been told exactly what was required."
- **Do not merely change words.** The head nurse "protested the emptiness of changing words" when staff began avoiding "patient"; early efforts to change concepts and terms "were no more than 'play acting.'"
- **Do not let the triadic one substitute for the parents' work.** When the son took up his own hours, "it soon became clear that he was 'going through the motions' of psychotherapy because the parents insisted," and parental motivation subsided.
- **Do not encourage the triadic one toward individual psychotherapy when it would end the parents' participation.** In the daughter case the therapist discouraged her request on exactly this ground. (See X1 — the outcome does not obviously vindicate this.)
- `[p2]` **Do not respond to symptoms with anxious solicitude.** Not stated as a rule, but it is the failing behaviour in the psychotic-wife case: "The husband was anxious and solicitous in relating to the wife's overactive and bizarre thinking and behavior. The wife reacted to his reactions with an increase in psychotic symptoms." What replaced it — privileges contingent on the wife's own behaviour control — was followed by remission in ten days.
- `[p2]` **Do not run family members in parallel separate channels.** The year-1 arrangement is diagnosed exactly this way: "individual psychotherapy kept the family problems divided. Critical issues could be avoided by each expecting the other to deal with the issue." Separate channels supply the avoidance route.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition for the whole method: total commitment to the family orientation.** The shift "constituted a turning point that might not have been possible without the 'total commitment' to the family." Combination plans were considered and rejected: "any combination plan contained drawbacks."
- **Precondition for the therapist: his own responsible functioning.** He could only require responsibility of the families after taking responsibility himself with the administration.
- **Precondition for the split therapy in the separation crisis:** "As soon as parents and triadic one are aware of the part that each plays in the family process."
- **The best interception point is before the first hospitalisation.** "Much is gained if a diagnosis and hospitalization can be avoided and the projection process can be modified before the family takes this additional major step in confirming emotional illness." "The step into mental illness is probably the most critical in the long series of family projection crises."
- **Prognosis is set at that step by which way the triadic one goes.** Where the groundwork is complete, family members are fixed in their functioning positions, and the triadic one readily accepts the designation "patient," "the chances of modifying the family process are remote." Where the step is bigger and the triadic one opposes it, prognosis is better — provided the psychiatrist declines to take the parents' side.
- **Order in the acute out-patient crisis:** decline immediate hospitalisation → offer family psychotherapy evaluation interviews → work with whichever parents are willing, leaving the disturbed member out "until he expressed a positive wish for psychotherapy" → hold the surrounding institutions to behaviour-based decisions.
- **Bowen's stated go/no-go for that gamble:** "The son's acting out at school and in the community did not appear too great to risk a family psychotherapy effort."
- **Order in the seventeen-year-old case, with timings:** both parents together (unproductive, anxiety high, thoughts only to the son's inadequacies) → one parent alone, the parents choosing which (mother, months 0–8) → other parent alone on his own demand (father, months 8–11) → parental conflict, then re-fused projection → son requests his own time (~month 12) → son seen alone plus weekly parental appointments (2 months) → son quits → projection resumes → parents finally "begin the first working together on their own problem." 72 hours over 18 months.
- **Order in the psychotic-wife case:** wife stays at home → focus on the husband's anxiety, refuse direct responsibility for the wife's illness, hold the husband responsible → at 2 weeks he requests hospitalisation ("too sick"), therapist maintains home is best but concedes he "could go toward hospitalization if he had reached his tolerance for the stress at home" → husband recruits a relative → relative requests shock treatment, refused → husband on his own initiative institutes a firm program in which "the wife's 'privileges' were determined by her ability to control her own behavior" → symptoms gone in ten days.
- **The three-step projection sequence is itself the sequence to interrupt** — Bowen organises the clinical section around clinical management of exactly those three steps: thinking of the triadic one as sick; diagnosing him and designating him "patient"; treating him as a sick person.
- **The three reasons for hospitalisation**, in Bowen's order: (1) family tolerance for disturbance at home has been reached; (2) the community demands it because of offences; (3) the disturbed person requests it. He treats (1) as the important one.
- **Willingness to spend real time on the reason for hospitalisation:** "days or weeks on this one point."
- **Institutional change takes about two years.** "It was almost two years before the staff achieved a reasonable working version of the clinical program."
- **The end state.** "The final stage of successful family psychotherapy includes the two spouses working together on their individual and joint problems."
- `[p2]` **The responsibility response has a four-stage profile, not one state.** Latent ("have the capacity to function responsibly when it is required") → called out by an explicit stated requirement, *not* by scolding ("The families had been scolded for their mistakes but they had not been told exactly what was required") → **overshoot** ("The formerly irresponsible parents became overly responsible") → **not sustained** ("it is difficult for such parents to assume more than sporadic periods of responsibility for self"). Pass 1 collapsed all four into "the capacity is present but unexercised."
- `[p2]` **A live symptom can be the precondition for the parents' first joint work.** "The parents, with the tangible problem provided by the son's acting out, were able to focus on a family problem and then to begin the first working together on their own problem." The sequence is projection resumes → son acts out → parents get a shared object → first joint work. This cuts across the naive reading of "symptom relief opposes progress": here symptom *presence* is the entry condition.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **The staff cannot be made to think family by instruction.** "It was second nature for staff members to think in terms of the individual, to 'assume responsibility for the patient,' and to 'feel with' the patient and be angry at the parents. It required time and intimate exposure to the families for the staff to know the family orientation."
- **The hospital's individual orientation cannot be repealed, only interpreted.** "A medical administrator can 'interpret' but not change medical structure." The project ran under routine medical structure; short cuts in administrative steps were possible, elimination of steps was not; the maintenance of the project required "innumerable admissions, discharges, physical examinations, and routine laboratory studies."
- **Two contradictory orientations coexisting, which the families learn to exploit.** The hospital designated every family member a patient and required physicians to be responsible for them; within the ward there were "family members" and responsible parents. "The families understood the two orientations and they became students of the fine points of difference between them."
- **The projection re-routes through whatever structure exists.** When the staff stopped absorbing emotional responsibility, family problems reappeared as physical illness through the one channel still open — the physician's responsibility for physical health.
- **Parents become students of psychiatry and use it to project better.** "The parents often become students of emotional illness and use psychiatric terms and concepts to better facilitate the projection to the patient."
- **Overhelpfulness leaves incident rates flat and relocates the incidents.** The families' answers to complaints were themselves projection moves: explaining them away as "fusses or disagreements"; blaming the public's lack of understanding of emotional illness; diagnosing the complainant as "an upset, touchy, neurotic person"; or debating whether the complaint was justified.
- **The live-in environment produces institutional dependence.** "The parents became overdependent on the hospital resources available to them, which precluded development of their own resources. Family psychotherapy has been more successful with families responsible for the care of the psychotic one in the home."
- **A live-in environment is stated outright to be "not the most favorable for change in family psychotherapy."**
- **Families stop when symptoms stop.** "Some families would continue through a few cycles and stop the psychotherapy during an asymptomatic period or use an angry family incident as a reason to stop. Some families would progress very rapidly for a few months and stop suddenly when symptoms were less troublesome." And: "The more severe the family problem, the more likely the family to terminate the psychotherapy after symptomatic improvement."
- **The differentiation cycles do not run to completion in schizophrenia.** "It was hoped the cyclical course would continue until all three emerged as well-differentiated individuals. This did not occur."
- **The separation crisis reverses everything gained.** The daughter case is the canonical failure, and the failure was *at the point of best functioning*.
- **The split therapy that fixes the separation crisis costs something.** "Results have been better with this approach but it involves a more 'supportive' relationship to each side of the family and the basic level of differentiation of self does not change."
- **Parents may control their side and the triadic one restarts the process.** "Occasionally, parents can be successful in controlling their side of the process, only to have the triadic one begin acting inadequate and impaired in a way that forces them to recognize his 'petition for sickness.' The schizophrenic person is not oriented to becoming 'normal' and he plays an important part in perpetuating the status quo."
- **The method does not resolve severe schizophrenia at all.** "I have not yet seen a family projection process completely resolved in a family with severe schizophrenia." "I have not yet seen a reasonable resolution of the basic problem in a family with severe schizophrenia." Five to seven years of therapy can produce symptom-free adjustment "but no change in the basic problem."
- **When the triadic one lives away in dependent attachments, the parents lose motivation.** "The parents perceive no problem within themselves and they have no motivation for family psychotherapy."
- **A therapist refusing hospitalisation exposes himself legally.** "Dangerous acting out by one psychotic family member can threaten the therapist with medical-legal breach of responsibility; the charge may be made that the psychotic should have been considered a patient requiring hospitalization."
- **Institutions mishear the instruction.** The school, told the boy should not be hospitalised, misread that "to mean that he should remain in school."
- **Some teachers refuse the behaviour frame outright**, fearing "it would 'hurt' the son to make him deal with reality; they preferred excusing his behavior on the basis that it was 'sick.'" Others took firmer reality stands "with considerable benefit."
- **The improved spouse is attacked by the impaired one.** The husband responded to his wife's change "with a campaign to act helpless, to call himself neurotic, and to plead with her to mother his helplessness" and, when refused, "became angry, accused her of not loving him, and went into a week of individualistic, 'I do not need you.'" Then he joined the therapy.
  - `[p2]` **This is a five-rung reaction ladder with a duration and a terminal branch, and pass 1 recorded it as one undifferentiated bullet.** In order: (1) escalate helplessness; (2) self-label — "call himself neurotic"; (3) demand caretaking — "plead with her to mother his helplessness"; (4) on refusal, attack the tie — "accused her of not loving him"; (5) withdraw — "a week of individualistic, 'I do not need you.'" Then capitulation: he joins. Two features distinguish it from the ladders recorded elsewhere in the corpus: the **first rung is symptomatic, not verbal**, and the mover here is the **adequate** spouse. Note also the partial-compliance detail on the mover's side — asked to stop thinking, calling and treating him as neurotic, she reported two months later: "I no longer call him neurotic or treat him like a neurotic, but for the life of me I cannot stop thinking of him as neurotic." Treating and calling are changeable; thinking is not, at least not on that timescale.
- `[p2]` **The schizophrenia-level form of the same first rung is the "petition for sickness."** "Occasionally, parents can be successful in controlling their side of the process, only to have the triadic one begin acting inadequate and impaired in a way that forces them to recognize his 'petition for sickness.'" Same opening move, from the lower end of the scale, and here it succeeds in restoring the projection rather than ending in capitulation.
- `[p2]` **Year 1's failure mode has a name: compliant inertia.** "No family went beyond the passive attitude of cooperating while waiting to be changed." A family can present full attendance and cooperation with zero structural movement, and the conventional outcome measure will read it as success — "the treatment results were rather good by usual individual standards."
- `[p2]` **The workaround that fixed the physical-illness rerouting is not generalisable.** Of the ward "clinic" with office hours and family-held drug supplies: "This kind of an arrangement would not have been possible in any but a research setting."
- `[p2]` **The institutional constraint is not an artifact of working in a hospital.** Bowen answers the objection directly: "the very same issues are involved even in the private practice of family psychotherapy through a similar attitude to illness on the part of the legal and medical institutions of society." Pass 1 framed this as a hospital problem; Bowen states it as setting-invariant.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

This is the section where each rule is converted back into a claim about mechanism.

1. **"Direct the therapy at the family ego mass, not the individual" assumes the family is a single coupled system whose state is not decomposable into per-person states.** A per-person intervention is a partial view of one variable. This is why he says a part of the total problem "was not possible from examination of the parts separately."
2. **"You may work through a single member" assumes any member is a valid channel into the whole system's state** — that the coupling is strong enough that a change at one node propagates. Evidence he offers for the assumption: individual psychotherapy with a single member "would tone down the emotional process in the entire family."
3. **"Change is fastest when the spouses work as a team" assumes the parental dyad is the highest-degree node in the system.** The two spouses are the members most involved in the ego mass; therefore intervening there has the largest downstream effect.
4. **"Aim at the parental anxiety, not the projection's content" assumes anxiety is a scalar drive with a conserved quantity, and the projection is only its discharge route.** Reduce the drive and the process idles; block a route without reducing the drive and it finds another. Everything in the ward experience — physical illness replacing emotional demand, incidents relocating to unspecified places — is this assumption's evidence.
5. **"Do not think, name, or treat anyone as sick" assumes the label is causally productive, not merely descriptive.** The projection "can create its own defects." A defect exists more after being confirmed than before; therefore a therapist's diagnosis is an *act on the family*, not an observation of it.
6. **"The therapist's acceptance of responsibility for the patient perpetuates the illness" assumes responsibility is a conserved, transferable quantity in the system.** If the therapist holds it, the parents cannot; if the staff holds it, the parents will not. Corollary the ward experience makes explicit: an available external absorber displaces the family's own capacity ("the parents became overdependent on the hospital resources available to them, which precluded development of their own resources").
7. **"Require the parents to be responsible" assumes the capacity is present but unexercised, and is called out by the surrounding system's demand.** Bowen states this as a finding: helpless, irresponsible-seeming parents "have the capacity to function responsibly when it is required." Responsibility is therefore not a trait but a function of the demand the environment places.
8. **"The therapist must function responsibly first" assumes the therapist is inside the same coupled system, at the same kind of node as any family member.** He can only require of others the level he is himself supplying. This makes the professional system part of the family's emotional field by construction, not by accident.
9. **"Give behavior, not sickness, as the reason for any restriction" assumes a person acts on what his own perceptual system can verify, and that an unverifiable reason forces him into either conflict or dependence.** Behaviour is a controllable variable the person owns; sickness is an attribute assigned from outside over which "he had no control." So the frame determines whether there is any lever the person can pull.
10. **"Hospitalisation is more for the family than the patient" assumes removal is triggered by a family-tolerance threshold, not by a severity threshold.** Bowen states the mechanism plainly: "Families keep severe degrees of 'sickness' at home as long as the person behaves himself. The real reason is that the family wants the disturbing behavior removed from the home." "When you are well" really means "when you no longer disturb the family." **The simulation implication is that removal events are governed by a tolerance parameter on the *remaining* members, not by a symptom score on the removed one.**
11. **"Split the therapy at the separation crisis" assumes the triadic one's separation and the parents' anxiety are locked in a negative feedback loop that will otherwise abort the separation.** The two goals are stated as complements: help the parents to separate their lives from the triadic one, and help the triadic one resist his "automatic emotional 'reflex' to 'save the parents'." The word "reflex" is doing work — the return is modelled as involuntary and fast.
12. **"Do not encourage individual psychotherapy for the triadic one while he lives with the parents" assumes support given to the absorber increases his absorbing capacity, i.e. supporting the symptom-bearer stabilises the pathological equilibrium.** Strengthening one node in this system is not neutral.
13. **"Do not oppose the projection" assumes the process is anxiety-driven and self-restoring, so direct opposition raises anxiety and therefore intensity.** The projection is "adaptive and it can reestablish itself after most threats."
14. **"Bring in extended family to add strength" assumes the ego mass has a total quantity of available self that can be raised by widening membership** — the same quantity that the marital dyad fights over or shares. It also assumes the ties to the family of origin remain live enough to be worked with in adulthood.
15. **"Prognosis depends on whether the triadic one accepts the 'patient' designation" assumes the symptom-bearer's consent is a required input to the projection, not merely its output.** He can be coerced, but the process needs his acceptance; his opposition is therefore a resource, not just resistance. Bowen goes further and lets the child *initiate*.
16. **The vocabulary substitutions ("dysfunction," "impaired one," "triadic one") assume the therapist's own perceptual system is a mechanism in the loop.** "Man can fail to see what is before his eyes unless it fits into his theoretical frame of reference." Individual theory is not merely wrong, it is *blinding*: the observer's frame determines which data exist for him. The head nurse's protest sets the limit — the words alone are inert until the thinking changes, so this is a claim about representation, not about phrasing.
17. **"Instruct the school to decide on behaviour" assumes the surrounding institutions run the same three-step projection process as the mother.** Bowen says this literally: "The school had long followed the usual steps in the family projection process of thinking about, diagnosing, and treating the son as 'sick.'" So schools, hospitals, courts, and merchants are not context — they are additional nodes running the same rules.
18. **"If the medical structure did not exist, the families could find other means to make the environment responsible"** — assumes the demand for an external absorber originates in the family and is invariant; the institutions are interchangeable sinks. This is the strongest form of the assumption and the one Bowen ends on.
19. **The whole clinical program assumes the family projection process "does not have to be"** — that it is "a natural phenomenon that develops as any phenomenon in nature when conditions are favorable for it," and that the favourable conditions "can be controlled and modified by man." Deterministic mechanism, modifiable boundary conditions.
20. `[p2]` **"Give the person a reason he can perceive in himself" assumes the frame determines whether a control loop exists at all.** Bowen's mechanism is explicit: a resisting person's ego "does not perceive 'sickness' with his own perceptual system"; restriction for behaviour "provided him with something he could understand, that he could work on, and that he could change," whereas restriction for sickness gives him something "over which he had no control." So a frame is not a description of state — it selects which of the target's own variables is actuable. This is a stronger claim than pass 1's item 9 and it generalises past hospitalisation: the same logic drives the school instruction and the wife's privileges-for-behaviour program.
21. `[p2]` **"Hospitalisation belongs to whoever holds the tolerance" assumes the trigger is a decision, and that decision ownership is movable.** The three reasons name three different tolerance-holders — the family, the community, the disturbed person. In the psychotic-wife case Bowen declined to own it and handed it back ("he could go toward hospitalization if he had reached his tolerance"), and it did not fire. So the removal event is not a threshold crossing evaluated by the model; it is a threshold crossing *evaluated by a named agent*, and reassigning the evaluator changes the outcome.
22. `[p2]` **"Separate channels let members defer to each other" assumes avoidance capacity is a property of the communication topology, not of the members.** Year 1: "individual psychotherapy kept the family problems divided. Critical issues could be avoided by each expecting the other to deal with the issue." The same families, put in one room, could not avoid the issue. The variable is who is present in which channel.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **The symbiosis hypothesis is explicitly discarded**, and Bowen goes out of his way to say it was reachable *because* it was compatible with individual theory, not because it was accurate: "I believe the specific shift was determined more by limitations in theoretical thinking than by the accuracy of the concept."
- **The mother-centred etiology is superseded by a whole-family one** — fathers "were as involved as the mothers in the emotional oneness." Yet the family projection process is still described in this chapter as running through the mother, "with the father supporting her viewpoint." The chapter carries both.
- **The three-generation hypothesis of Bowen (1960) is softened here.** He restates it, then adds that "in most situations there are varying degrees of 'spread' in the transmission process, which requires more than three generations for the development of schizophrenia."
- **The triad was expected to be an autonomous unit; it is not.** Stated as an explicit reversal in the final section: he had expected the problem could be resolved inside the triad "without giving support to each separate individual. But the triad did not prove to be autonomous."
- **"Undifferentiated" is upgraded to "undifferentiatable" for severe schizophrenia** — a substantive retraction of the theory's own optimism at the severe end, made in the same chapter that argues all severity is one scale.
- **The original goal of family psychotherapy is abandoned for schizophrenia.** "I no longer approach schizophrenia with the expectation that the basic process will be changed by the present techniques."
- **Support, previously avoided, is now accepted as necessary in schizophrenia** — "some emotional 'support' of the family ego mass by the therapist is necessary" — with the caveat that under the split-therapy approach "the basic level of differentiation of self does not change." This sits against the anti-support logic of B5 item 12.
- **Tension around the daughter case.** The therapist discouraged her request for individual psychotherapy because the plan implied the parents would stop participating; her collapse followed. Bowen does not draw the inference either way, but the case is presented immediately before the modification that splits the therapy — i.e. the modification is, in effect, a partial concession to what she asked for.
- **The chapter's founding claim that avoiding "sick-patient-diagnosis" is central sits beside the admission that it is of "direct benefit" only below schizophrenia.** "It is with problems less severe than schizophrenia that avoiding 'sick-patient-diagnoses' is of direct benefit."
- **Live-in research: the setting that produced the theory is the setting that works worst therapeutically.** The intensity that made the phenomenon visible is the same condition that made change harder.
- `[p2]` **The therapist's stance is opposite in the two settings, and Bowen does not reconcile them.** Live-in: "a nondirective psychotherapy," therapist as catalyst and participant observer. Out-patient: refusals, stated requirements, instructions to third-party institutions, an explicit goal statement. The directive setting is the one he reports as producing the better results ("Clinical results in dealing with the family projection process have been best in outpatient work and private practice"), yet the theory section gives no account of directiveness as a variable.
- `[p2]` **The "symbiotic hypothesis" is discarded; "undifferentiated family ego mass" is kept and defended.** Two different fates for two terms in the same chapter — see A5 `[p2]` for the conflict this raises with the corpus terminology timeline's Ch05 row.
- `[p2]` **The mover who succeeds in the one fully-resolved case is the *adequate* spouse.** The wife, described as "the adequate one who protected him from upsetting situations," takes the stand alone and the couple goes "on to a good resolution of their problem." In the seventeen-year-old case the choice of first mover was handed to the parents, and the second mover progressed faster than the first. Nothing in this chapter privileges the inadequate or peripheral member as the one who moves first.
- **A duplicated line in the source text** ("with the highest range of human adjustment to be on a single scale, with the highest levels of maturity…") appears to be an OCR/typesetting artifact, not a substantive statement.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- On the ego mass term: "The term has certain inaccuracies but it aptly describes the over-all family dynamics."
- On role theory: Note 4 — "I have not been able consistently to adapt role concepts to this work."
- On the original decision: it was made "in spite of doubts that it could be successful," and "the most serious doubts concerned the family psychotherapy." He expected at the time that family psychotherapy could be "no more than preparation for eventual individual psychotherapy for each family member," and framed the trial as giving it a fair run "before returning to the individual orientation."
- On the origin of the projection: "We can wonder if the pattern of the mother's thinking and fantasies came from her own mother!" — explicitly speculative.
- On mixed-orientation plans: "Various combinations ... were considered but any combination plan contained drawbacks" — a judgement, not a result.
- On the extended-family work: "This effort is too new to report here but initial reports appear promising."
- On the multigenerational explanation: "the experience with families adds increasing conviction to the belief that schizophrenia will eventually be explained as an emotional phenomenon."
- On reversal in the family of origin: "there is evidence to indicate that the process can be reversed" — evidence not presented.
- On scope: "In this presentation it is not possible to consider variables in families with multiple psychotic offspring, nor the variables operative in divorce, death, and other serious family disruptions." An explicit gap.
- On the medical-structure problem: "a monumental problem for which there are no easy answers."
- On the validity question, asked of himself and answered honestly: "How valid is the effort and what are the results? I have not yet seen a family projection process completely resolved in a family with severe schizophrenia."
- On the seventeen-year-old family's future: "The current motivation of the parents to continue suggests that they may be among those to go on to a reasonable resolution."
- On the validity of "self-blaming" as an explanation of the whole: "The real cause of any situation is probably a combination of internal and external factors."
- On the *contingency* of his own discovery: the whole "observational blindness" passage is a hedge on the reliability of clinical observation itself.
- `[p2]` **On the impossibility of differentiation in the severe triad: "it *appears* impossible."** "These three people can be physically separated but it appears impossible for any one to differentiate a 'self' from the other two." The hedge matters — this is the sentence immediately preceding the reunion claim that the corpus is treating as decisive (see P5 / Q-NODECAY).
- `[p2]` **On assortative mating: "People *tend to* marry spouses who have identical levels."** A tendency, not an identity.
- `[p2]` **On the ward "clinic" workaround: "This kind of an arrangement would not have been possible in any but a research setting."** An explicit generalisability limit on the one measure that solved the physical-illness rerouting.
- `[p2]` **On the seventeen-year-old case as evidence: it is unfinished at the time of writing.** The son "still has at least another year of high school"; the parents "may be among those to go on to a reasonable resolution." The chapter's most detailed trajectory is reported mid-course.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The observer is part of the model.** The chapter's opening argument is that "the psychotherapist establishes and controls the milieu in which the psychotherapy takes place, and his theoretical thinking about the nature of the problem to be treated determines his approach to the problem, the procedures he will use, the observations he will make and the way he will respond and react as the therapy proceeds." A simulation that includes a therapist agent should include his frame as a state variable.
- **Increasing coupling intensity increases observability.** The live-in design worked as an amplifier: relationship actions and responses were "more intense," and once seen at high intensity the phenomenon could be recognised in out-patient families "in which the phenomenon was less intense in its manifestations." A useful dual for a simulation: raise the coupling coefficient to make a weak effect measurable.
- **A projection can complete with zero communication** (the A/B awkwardness example). The exchange is not a message-passing event; it is two simultaneous private attributions that agree. This is hard to model as messaging and easy to model as a shared attribution variable.
- **Bowen names an involuntary, low-latency return move**: the triadic one's "automatic emotional 'reflex' to 'save the parents'." Distinct from deliberate action.
- **Locking the ward as a family-initiated intervention.** "The family might ask permission of the other families to lock the ward for a few days until the upset subsided" — one family requesting a system-wide constraint from its peer families. Multi-family coupling on a shared ward.
- **The "petition for sickness"** — the impaired member actively acting inadequate to restore the projection when the parents stop projecting. A demand-side symptom.
- **Physical illness as a substitutable channel for emotional demand.** Both at the individual level (one member becomes physically ill in response to another's emotional stress; the no-self spouse becomes chronically ill) and at the system level (ward families' problems reappearing as physical complaints when the emotional channel closed).
- **Mendell's (1958) alternative** — psychological tests on all key family members, used as authoritative evidence to diagnose everyone, then each family member treated in a *different* group. Recorded as a contrasting solution to the same dilemma.
- **Sonne and Speck (1961) have included pets in family psychotherapy**; Bowen includes pets in ego-mass membership.
- **The out-patient family psychotherapy caseload ran concurrently with the live-in research** and provided the corrective observations (fathers involved, phenomenon present at lower intensity). Two data streams, cross-validating.
- **The head nurse who protested that changing words was empty later wrote "The Patient Is the Family"** (Kvarnes 1959) — a three-year conversion of a staff member, i.e. the professional node itself has a slow-changing state.
- **The recovering bus-stop patient spent days practising ways to behave normally in spite of the voices**, and "became so successful at this that he returned to work while the voices were still present." Symptom and function are separable variables.
- **Terminating events are as varied as improving ones.** Families stop "during an asymptomatic period" *or* "use an angry family incident as a reason to stop."
- `[p2]` **A latent process reads as zero.** "When the family is calm, the process can disappear from view, but if anxiety builds up again, the process appears again." *Disappear from view*, not disappear. Any readout of "the projection process" is an observation of an anxiety-gated expression, and a zero reading is ambiguous between absence and latency.
- `[p2]` **The symptom-bearer's *opposition* is used as an index of the family's level.** Not merely as resistance to be managed, and not as an outcome — as evidence of how much self is present: "The ability of the son to oppose the family projection process ... is more 'self' than is found with severe schizophrenia."
- `[p2]` **A family can be fully compliant and completely inert.** "No family went beyond the passive attitude of cooperating while waiting to be changed," under an arrangement whose "treatment results were rather good by usual individual standards."
- `[p2]` **The improvement in the seventeen-year-old preceded any parental progress.** Two weeks of complaint-free behaviour while "the parents made little progress" and "thoughts went only to inadequacies in the son." The active ingredient in that window can only have been the redirection of the parents' effort, not its content or its success.
- `[p2]` **Capacity is present at the top and bottom of the scale simultaneously.** The same parents who "commonly function as helpless and irresponsible people" both (a) exercise full responsibility under an explicit requirement and (b) overshoot into being "overly responsible" and (c) cannot sustain it beyond "sporadic periods." Three different readings of the same underlying capacity depending on when it is sampled.

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

**1. The reroute evidence was described as "measured." None of it is measured.**
The pass-1 ledger entry L08.3 says "Three measured instances." The chapter contains **no counts
anywhere in that sequence** — "frequent complaints", "an increasing number of family problems",
"The frequency of incidents remained the same", "Complaints ... stopped." Every quantity is a
qualitative comparison. Calling this "measured" is exactly the kind of upgrade that turns a
clinical impression into a calibration target.

**2. The reroute claim changes its outcome variable halfway through, and pass 1 papered over it.**
Pass 1's A1 wrote "Frequency dropped to zero only when responsibility was assigned to the
parents." The text does not say that. Phase one measures **incidents** ("The frequency of
incidents remained the same"); phase two measures **complaints** ("Complaints from the hospital
and the community stopped"), and the requirement Bowen imposed named complaints as its target:
"the goal was the prevention of complaints, whether justified or unjustified." The chapter is
consistent with incidents continuing while complaints ceased. The adversarial test built on this
must not assume the two phases share an endpoint.

**3. The third "instance" of rerouting is not an instance of rerouting.**
L08.3's third item — the no-self spouse presenting as physical illness, emotional illness, or
social dysfunction — is a **typology of presentations**, listed in the theory section as
alternatives available to one underlying state. No channel is blocked and nothing is observed to
relocate. It supports substitutability; it is not an observation of rerouting. Pass 1 padded a
count of three from two.

**4. The no-decay finding was read as "coupling persists with zero interaction." Bowen's
mechanism is the opposite: the person is never uncoupled, he is re-attached elsewhere.**
Pass 1 (and `_CONVERGENCES.md` C7) treat the reunion sentence as evidence for a bond-energy term
that survives an interaction-free interval. What the chapter actually describes on both sides of
the severed tie is **substitution**: the triadic one lives away "in dependent attachments that do
not involve the parents"; the parents meanwhile "function by 'borrowing self' from outside
themselves and by 'projecting their inadequacies' to others." And the general rule for that
population is stated flatly: "any long-term separation from parents is accomplished only by
finding a new family ego mass to which they can append themselves." A decay rate near zero is
still the right parameter, but the justification is not persistence-in-vacuum, and a simulation
that models separation as an isolated node with a slowly-decaying edge is modelling something
Bowen never describes. Separation is **edge-rewiring with the old edge dormant**, and the
substitute attachment is load-bearing.

**5. The no-decay passage is scoped to severe schizophrenia, and pass 1 dropped the scope.**
The reunion sentence sits inside the paragraph that opens "The family ego mass in severe
schizophrenia is not only an 'undifferentiated' family ego mass but in my experience it is
'undifferentiatable.'" The *next* paragraph states the contrast explicitly: "The orderly
'differentiation of self' from the family ego mass **does occur** in a wide range of problems less
severe than schizophrenia." So the chapter's decisive no-decay claim is a claim about the bottom
of the scale. Applying a near-zero decay rate uniformly across all differentiation levels is not
sourced from this chapter.

**6. The reunion passage and the institutionalisation passage are two different passages, and
pass 1's ledger entry welded them together.** L08.2 reads "the triad survives intact with the
member institutionalised as a permanent ward of the state" *and* the reunion quote as one claim;
`_CONVERGENCES.md` C7 compresses it further into "Reunite an institutionalised triad." The
institutionalisation sentence is in the theory section, about the *projection process* surviving
the psychotic collapse ("the process can even survive this threat and continue with the parents
living at home and the triadic one institutionalized as a permanent ward of the state"). The
reunion sentence is nine pages later, about a triadic one "living away from home in dependent
attachments." No institutionalised triad is described as reuniting anywhere in this chapter.

**7. Pass 1 flattened a ranking Bowen stated.** On why the phenomenon was missed for five years:
pass 1 wrote "observational blindness ... as much as ... reduced intensity." Bowen ranks them —
close living was "one factor," but "the most important factor seemed to be 'observational
blindness'." Since the chapter's headline methodological claim is that the observer's frame
determines what data exist, losing the ranking loses the point.

**8. Pass 1 rendered a tendency as a law.** A3 had "If two spouses marry, their levels of
differentiation of self **will be** identical." Bowen: "People **tend to** marry spouses who have
identical levels of differentiation of self."

**9. The change-back reaction in the neurotic couple was recorded as one bullet. It is a
five-rung ladder with a duration, a refusal gate and a terminal branch** — helplessness →
self-labelling → demand for caretaking → attack on the tie ("accused her of not loving him") →
withdrawal ("a week of individualistic, 'I do not need you'") → capitulation and joining. Given
that three later chapters are being used to source a differentiation state machine, missing an
instance of it here, in a chapter written around 1962, was the largest omission of pass 1.

**10. Pass 1 recorded the conflict/projection trade-off without its trigger.** It is not
spontaneous. It fires when the working parent redirects attention from child to spouse: "his
thoughts, feelings, and actions were directed more to the mother than the son. About three months
after he started, there was a period of overt conflict between the parents. The parents quickly
joined forces and began another period of projection." Redirection → marital conflict surfaces →
conflict aborted by resuming projection. The mechanism, not just the correlation.

**11. Pass 1 missed that the son improved while the parents were failing.** "The parents made
little progress ... In spite of this, the son's anxiety subsided and within two weeks there were
no further complaints." Pass 1 filed the two weeks in A4 as a duration and never noticed the
oddity, which is the sharpest support in the chapter for the load variable being **where parental
attention is pointed**, independent of what the parents do with it.

**12. Pass 1 flattened the responsibility finding into a single latent capacity.** The chapter
gives four stages — latent, called out by an *explicit stated requirement* (not by scolding),
**overshooting** into "overly responsible", and then **not sustained** ("no more than sporadic
periods of responsibility for self"). Pass 1's B5 item 7 keeps only the first two.

**13. Pass 1 recorded both therapist stances and never noticed they contradict.** The live-in
work is explicitly "nondirective"; the out-patient work is the most directive material in the
corpus so far. Bowen reports the directive setting as the one with the better results and offers
no account of the difference.

**14. Provenance caution pass 1 should have raised and did not: this chapter shares its data with
other chapters.** Notes 1 and 2 name Topeka 1949–1954 and the NIMH live-in study 1954–1959.
Agreement between Ch08 and any other chapter reporting that ward is not independent replication,
and the convergence counts in `_CONVERGENCES.md` (C3 counts Ch01 and Ch08 as two) should be
audited for this.

**No anachronism found in pass 1.** The pass-1 file consistently writes *triad*, not *triangle*,
and does not import cutoff, sibling position, societal process or the numeric scale. The one place
it leans forward is the phrase "bond energy" in the ledger, which is model vocabulary rather than
Bowen's — flagged, not a reading error.

## P2. What is NEW here relative to the preceding chapters

- **The family projection process, named, with three steps.** *Feeling-thinking → examining-labeling
  → treating*, presented as the repeating episode and as the thing the whole clinical program is
  built to interrupt. Nothing in Ch01–Ch07 has the episodic three-step form.
- **The projection is bidirectional and requires the child's acceptance.** "The child may initiate
  the projection and coerce the parents to agree that he is the cause of the family problem"; and
  the "petition for sickness" — the impaired one acting inadequate to force the projection's
  restoration when the parents stop.
- **Projection as productive of the defect it names.** "The projection system can create its own
  defects" — thinks him a baby, calls him a baby, treats him as a baby, "he becomes more infantile."
- **The blamer / self-blamer axis, with wordless completion.** Two simultaneous private
  attributions that agree, with no message exchanged.
- **The professional and institutional system as additional nodes running the same three steps.**
  The psychiatric consultation "fits, step by step, with the family projection process"; the school
  "had long followed the usual steps in the family projection process." Ch05's finding that a
  professional absorbs function is adjacent but far weaker than this structural isomorphism.
- **The behaviour-vs-sickness frame**, with the mechanism (a frame selects which of the target's own
  variables is actuable) and two worked cases.
- **Removal triggered by family tolerance rather than severity**, with three named tolerance-holders.
- **Multigenerational descent with a generation count** — "three or more generations", more where
  the transmission is spread — and the pair of severity variables (severity in the parental ego mass
  × concentration vs. spread), with concentration stated as dominant.
- **"Undifferentiatable"** — a coined term marking the retraction of the method's goal at the severe
  end, in the same chapter that argues all severity lies on one scale.
- **The separation crisis and the involuntary return.** Collapse from the best functioning state
  Bowen had ever seen, on the day of the announcement, with the return named a "reflex."
- **The single-scale claim stated as such**, with four ordinal positions and the finding that drove
  it: all the schizophrenia dynamisms are present in mild and asymptomatic families.

## P3. What CHANGED from earlier chapters

- **Ch01 (1957).** Ch01's causal vocabulary is symbiosis + transfer of anxiety + projection. Ch08
  **retires the symbiotic hypothesis by name** and gives an unusually candid reason: it was reachable
  because "it was a compatible extension of individual theory," not because it was accurate. Ch08 is
  the chapter that closes Ch01's frame.
- **Ch03 — the interdependent triad's fixed membership.** Ch03 has the triad with membership fixed by
  premise. Ch08 uses the same term and **derives** the membership: the normal siblings withdraw,
  "leaving the father, mother, and psychotic one interlocked." The fixity is a selection effect of
  involvement, not an axiom.
- **Ch02 — why the third position exists.** Ch02 derives triangulation from avoidance capacity (two
  can avoid, three cannot). Ch08 derives it from **dyadic instability**: "The child functions as a
  stabilizer for the parents, converting the unstable father-mother ego mass into a more stable
  triad." Two different derivations of the same structure; they are compatible but not the same claim.
- **Ch02/Ch03's avoidance finding, relocated to the channel level.** Ch08 shows the same families
  avoiding through **separate therapy channels** rather than through group size: "individual
  psychotherapy kept the family problems divided. Critical issues could be avoided by each expecting
  the other to deal with the issue." The variable generalises from *who is in the room* to *who
  shares a channel*.
- **Ch03/Ch05's reciprocity — a third regime, and a named middle band.** Ch03 and Ch05 give a
  bistable pair with no stable midpoint. Ch08 gives a **three-position marital scale**: both fight
  for their rights; a middle group where one *reluctantly* gives in; and a third where one "works
  **actively** to be the dependent one who gives in." The third — sought, not conceded — is new, and
  it terminates in a chronic disability that "permanently stabilized" the marriage. Note the middle
  band is a classification of *marriages*, not a claim that a dyad can rest at a midpoint, so it does
  not straightforwardly contradict Ch03/Ch05.
- **Ch05 / the live-in ward — same data, restated with the causal claim attached.** Ch08 states
  outright that "a live-in environment is not the most favorable for change in family
  psychotherapy," and gives the mechanism: "the parents became overdependent on the hospital
  resources available to them, which precluded development of their own resources." This is the same
  study Ch05 reports, not an independent confirmation of it.
- **Ch06's termination-on-relief finding gains a gradient.** Ch06 has symptom relief reducing
  motivation. Ch08 makes it **severity-graded**: "The more severe the family problem, the more likely
  the family to terminate the psychotherapy after symptomatic improvement." A functional form, not
  just a sign.
- **Ch06's distance finding gains a scope restriction and a mechanism.** See P1 items 4 and 5.
- **Ch07's stress-dependent ego-mass radius is corroborated and extended.** Ch08 adds the verbatim
  membership list (extended family, nonrelatives, pets, live-in servants "often more 'fused' ... than
  certain blood relatives who are more differentiated") and adds an invariant Ch07 does not state:
  "The father and mother are always maximally involved."
- **Ch07's ordered sensitivity ranking is replaced by a different, shorter one.** Ch07 ranks three
  determinants; Ch08 states exactly two, numbered: "(1) the pattern of dynamics within the ego mass
  and (2) relationships to those outside the ego mass."

## P4. What was DROPPED

- **Who moves first.** Ch01/Ch02/Ch03/Ch04 all assert the peripheral or inadequate member moves
  first, Ch04 in its hardest form. Ch08 gives the *order* of the cycles (one parent → the other
  parent → the triadic one) and never says which parent, **delegates the choice to the family**
  ("they decide which one it should be"), and reports the second mover progressing faster than the
  first. In the neurotic couple the mover is the *adequate* spouse. The precondition claim is gone.
- **Ch07's stabiliser-efficacy as an independent third variable.** Ch08 names exactly two variables
  governing severity — parental severity and concentration/spread. There is no term for how well the
  binding mechanism works.
- **Ch01's per-edge transfer latency and its "almost quantitative transfer" language.** Ch08 has
  anxiety feeding the projection and subsiding on externalisation, but no latency claims and no
  quantitative-transfer framing.
- **The investment seesaw's improving direction.** Ch02/Ch03/Ch04/Ch05/Ch06 state the rule in both
  directions (parents more invested in each other → the patient improves). Ch08 states only the
  structural version — full weight to a single child "leaving the parental relationship calm and
  harmonious" — and observes the trade-off firing in one direction only: projection resumes,
  parental conflict subsides. It never states that increasing marital investment improves the child.
- **Ch05's outcome tallies.** Ch05 supplies leadership-type counts with rank-ordered outcomes. Ch08
  counts 63 schizophrenia families and some 250 others and tabulates nothing.
- **Ch03's emotional-divorce resolution sequence with durations.** Ch08 has emotional divorce as a
  mechanism but no resolution trajectory for it.

## P5. Assigned open questions

### Q-NODECAY — verified, with three qualifications that matter

**Exact wording.** "These three people can be physically separated but it appears impossible for any
one to differentiate a 'self' from the other two. With the triadic one living away from home in
dependent attachments that do not involve the parents, all three members of the triad are more
comfortable. The parents function by 'borrowing self' from outside themselves and by 'projecting
their inadequacies' to others. In this situation, the parents perceive no problem within themselves
and they have no motivation for family psychotherapy. **If the threesome is reunited, the old
emotional fusion of the triad is immediately operative again.**"

**How long had the separation been? Bowen does not say.** No duration, no range, no example. The
claim is scale-free: it asserts a latency on reunion ("immediately"), not a bound on how long
coupling survives. There is exactly one duration-bearing statement anywhere in the chapter that
bears on the question, and it is a different, hedged one: "The young adult who runs away never to
see his parents again **may have more basic attachment** to his parents than siblings who continue
to live with the parents." That is a lifetime separation and a *comparative* claim — the separated
one may be coupled **more**, not merely as much. Pass 1 recorded this sentence under pseudo-self and
never connected it to the question; it is the stronger of the two pieces of evidence on duration and
the weaker on certainty ("may").

**Does Bowen qualify it anywhere? Yes, twice.**
1. **Scope.** The passage sits inside the severe-schizophrenia paragraph — the ego mass that is
   "undifferentiatable" — and the immediately following paragraph states the contrast: "The orderly
   'differentiation of self' from the family ego mass **does occur** in a wide range of problems less
   severe than schizophrenia ... they can attain high levels of identity." The no-decay claim is
   made about the bottom of the scale and explicitly not extended upward.
2. **Hedge.** The sentence that sets it up is "it **appears** impossible for any one to differentiate
   a 'self' from the other two."

**And the mechanism is substitution, not persistence.** Both parties are described as attached
elsewhere during the separation, and the general rule for the population is: "any long-term
separation from parents is accomplished **only by finding a new family ego mass** to which they can
append themselves."

**Verdict for the simulation.** The near-zero coupling-decay rate is defensible, but not as
currently justified. Three amendments: (a) do not model separation as an isolated node — the
separated member must acquire a substitute attachment, and the parents must acquire substitute
projection targets, or the configuration Bowen describes is not being reproduced; (b) the parameter
is sourced only for the lowest differentiation band, so make it level-dependent rather than global,
or state plainly that it is an extrapolation; (c) what "immediately operative again" licenses is a
**zero re-activation latency on reunion**, which is a separate parameter from the decay rate, and it
is the one the sentence actually supports.

### Q-REROUTE — the claim is accurate; there are no counts, and the outcome variable is not constant

**Verbatim, phase one:** "The staff went through a period of being overhelpful to the families in
specifying off-limits areas and in helping them to understand and deal with disturbed behavior.
**The frequency of incidents remained the same** except that they occurred in unusual places or in
circumstances that 'you did not tell us about.'" The diagnosis follows: "In providing help and
instruction, the staff doubted the parents' ability to find their own solutions. The families had
been scolded for their mistakes but they had not been told exactly what was required."

**Verbatim, phase two:** the families were told the project required them to assume responsibility,
"that we had a responsibility to the community, and that the goal was **the prevention of
complaints, whether justified or unjustified** ... they were held responsible for learning the rules
of the community and the hospital and that there be no complaints about disturbed behavior off the
ward. **Complaints from the hospital and the community stopped.**"

**Counts: none.** Not one number appears in the sequence. "Frequent complaints", "an increasing
number of family problems", "remained the same", "stopped" — all qualitative. Whoever calibrates
against this must not treat "the rate stayed flat" as a measurement.

**A caution pass 1 missed.** Phase one's endpoint is **incidents**; phase two's endpoint is
**complaints**, and complaints are what the intervention explicitly targeted. The chapter does not
claim incidents stopped. The adversarial test as drafted ("same incident count in different places,
not fewer") is faithful to phase one; the phase-two arm cannot be scored on the same variable.

**The second reroute instance is sound and better-motivated than pass 1 made it.** "When the staff
no longer accepted as much responsibility for emotional functioning in the families, the family
process found new ways to make the staff responsible. Since the physician was responsible for the
physical health of his 'patients,' an increasing number of family problems became manifested as
physical illness." The routing rule is stated generally — the process "has an amazing capacity to
utilize existing structure" — i.e. the flow goes to whatever channel remains structurally open, and
the channel set is a property of the environment, not of the family.

**The third instance should be withdrawn** (P1 item 3), and a genuine fourth added: in the
seventeen-year-old case the acting out relocated from the community into the family, and Bowen
attaches a **sign** to the direction: "Generally, it is a hopeful sign when acting out in the
community shifts back within the family." So rerouting is not valence-neutral — destination
matters, and inward relocation counts as improvement. That is a second, independent constraint on
the adversarial test: the coach must not be scored on incident count alone, since the same count in
a different location can be a better state.

### Q-TRIGGER — verified verbatim, with a refinement

"The degree of 'sickness' is not the real reason for hospitalization. Families keep severe degrees
of 'sickness' at home as long as the person behaves himself. The real reason is that the family
wants the disturbing behavior removed from the home. **Hospitalization is more for the family than
the patient.** Another distortion is, 'When you are well.' This really means, '**When you no longer
disturb the family.**'"

Preceded by: "There are three main reasons for hospitalization: (1) The family demands it because
the family has reached a tolerance for the disturbance in the home. (2) The community demands it
because of offenses to the community. (3) The disturbed person requests it for himself. The first
reason is the important one for this presentation."

Second verbatim instance, from the psychotic-wife case, using the same word: "I maintained the
position that home was best for the wife but that he could go toward hospitalization **if he had
reached his tolerance for the stress at home**."

**Refinement pass 1 did not make.** The trigger is not a state of the remaining members alone. It
is a **comparison of two quantities** — the disturbing *behaviour* the member produces against the
family's tolerance — and Bowen decouples behaviour from severity in the same breath ("families keep
severe degrees of 'sickness' at home as long as the person behaves himself"). So the model needs
disturbing-behaviour as a variable **distinct from symptom severity**, and the removal condition
compares it to a per-family tolerance. Two further points: the three reasons name **three different
tolerance-holders**, one of them outside the family; and in the one case where the threshold was
reached, the event did not fire, because Bowen returned ownership of the decision to the
tolerance-holder instead of executing it. Removal is a decision by a named agent, and reassigning
the agent changes the outcome.

### Q-VALIDATION — no external validation, no instrument, and measurement is treated as an intervention

There is no validation procedure and no measurement procedure for a differentiation level or any
other quantity in this chapter. What there is:

- **An ordinal mapping from diagnostic category to scale position** — neurosis "somewhere in the
  middle", parent of a schizophrenic "toward the lower end", the schizophrenic person "at the lower
  end as a 'no self'." This is circular for validation: the categories are defined by the outcome the
  scale is meant to predict. No numbers, no bounds, no zero point, no threshold.
- **An informal behavioural ranking rule**, the nearest thing to a procedure: "The ability of the son
  to oppose the family projection process and the ability of the parents to achieve a reasonable
  effective level of 'self' in acute situations is more 'self' than is found with severe
  schizophrenia." Two named observables, used to rank one family against a reference class, by
  clinician judgement, with no scale and no inter-rater anything.
- **Outcome language that is explicitly personal or family-reported**: "as high a level of confident,
  adequate functioning as I have ever seen"; the "'perfect result,' which the family praises highly"
  — Bowen's scare quotes.

And the chapter takes an actively hostile position on external instruments: physical examinations,
laboratory tests and psychological tests are described as things that **feed the projection
process** ("Any defect discovered in physical examinations, laboratory tests, and psychological
tests can also facilitate the projection process"), and parents "can go from one physician to
another until the 'feared' defect is finally confirmed by diagnosis." Mendell's (1958) use of
psychological tests on all key family members is reported as a contrasting solution and not
endorsed. In this chapter, measuring a family member is an act on the family.

The only hard numbers are dosage and duration: 63 families, seven weeks to over seven years, 72
hours over 18 months, 14 hours over 7 weeks with a 5-year follow-up, five to seven years with "no
change in the basic problem." Bowen asks the validation question himself — "How valid is the effort
and what are the results?" — and answers with a clinical impression, not data.

### Q-MATERIAL — no

The chapter posits no per-person material, economic or financial resource variable.

- **Employment appears constantly but only as a readout of functioning**: the daughter "was
  working", then "lost her job"; the bus-stop patient "returned to work"; the neurotic husband had
  been "forced ... to give up his work" and "returned to work"; the son "was motivated to get a
  part-time job." Never as a resource that feeds back into anything.
- **Work failure is a symptom channel, not an economic state**: "social dysfunction, such as work
  inefficiency, drinking, or social irresponsibility."
- **"Resources" appears once and is functional, not material**: "the parents became overdependent on
  the hospital resources available to them, which precluded development of their **own resources**."
  The context is capacity to manage the impaired member, not money.
- **The nearest thing to an economic argument is about institutions, not persons**: "it is easier and
  more expedient for medicine and society to assume responsibility for the projected family problem
  (the psychotic patient) than to attempt to hold the parents responsible", and the triadic one as "a
  permanent ward of the state." That is a cost-of-effort claim about why society absorbs the load; it
  attaches to the institution, and no cost is quantified.

Merchants appear only as complainants. Drug supplies and hospital passes are administrative
details. There is nothing here to source a per-person material variable from.

## M. Model implications (first cut)

- Undifferentiated family ego mass with variable membership (extended family, non-relatives, servants, pets) → `Family` → membership is a per-tick set with per-member fusion weight, not a fixed roster; father and mother pinned at maximum weight.
- "Clinically equivalent to a single ego" → `Family` → the family needs an aggregate state (available self / anxiety) that individual states are drawn from, not merely derived.
- Jigsaw trading of strengths and weaknesses → `Relationship` / `Family` → ego strength is conserved and transferable between members within the mass; one member's gain is another's loss.
- Assortative mating on differentiation level → `event-kind:MARRIAGE` → new dyads pair equal differentiation; this is the seeding rule for a new `Family`.
- Marital ego-strength scale (both fight / one reluctantly gives in / one actively becomes no-self) → `Relationship` → three-regime marital dynamic, with regime determined by differentiation level; the third regime terminates in chronic disability that *stabilises* the marriage.
- No-self spouse incapacitated as (a) physical illness, (b) emotional illness, (c) social dysfunction → `Person` → three interchangeable symptom channels off one underlying variable; blocking one should route to another.
- Outside relationships as pressure relief, with three generational targets (parents / siblings / children) → `Triangle` / `Relationship` → intensity inside the dyad is inversely coupled to the intensity of active outside ties, and the outside party is drawn into the mass.
- Concentration vs. spread of the parental problem → `Family` → a distribution parameter over children/outside relationships; concentration matters more than parental severity for the symptom-bearer's outcome.
- Child as triadic stabiliser; parents' stability depends on the child's continued function → `Triangle` → the triangle has a stabilising *utility* to the parents; threats to it (growing away, moving away, psychotic collapse) raise parental anxiety.
- Family projection process as a three-step episode (feeling-thinking → examining-labeling → treating) → `move:PROJECT` → each episode consumes parental anxiety and increments the child's functional inadequacy; acceptance by the child is a required input.
- The child can initiate the projection and coerce the parents → `move:PROJECT` → the move must be bidirectional; also `move:PETITION_FOR_SICKNESS` for the impaired member restoring a lapsed projection.
- Blamer / self-blamer axis, with inversion under overload → `Person` → a per-person attribution orientation, plus a threshold at which a self-blamer "erupts into blaming."
- Wordless completion of a projection (the A/B example) → `Relationship` → projection resolves as agreement between two private attributions; no message-passing required.
- Anxiety subsides when the cause is located outside the self → `Person` → externalisation is the anxiety-discharge operator; this is the drive behind every projection move.
- Triadic one's separation attempt → parental anxiety spike → reflex return → regression → `move:SEPARATE` + `move:RESCUE_PARENTS` → the highest-value dynamic in the chapter; the reflex return should be involuntary and fast, and the collapse should occur *from the best-functioning state*.
- Physical separation ≠ differentiation; reunion instantly restores fusion → `Triangle` → distance suppresses expression without changing the underlying coupling; a `distance` state variable distinct from `differentiation`.
- Removal into an institution is triggered by the family's tolerance threshold, not by the removed member's severity → `event-kind:HOSPITALIZATION` → **the trigger is a state of the remaining members.** Three named reasons: family tolerance reached, community offence, self-request.
- Effect on remaining members after removal: parental anxiety subsides once the diagnosis is confirmed and the triadic one becomes "a patient"; the triad survives removal, "with the parents living at home and the triadic one institutionalized as a permanent ward of the state" → `Family` / `Triangle` → removal *completes* the projection rather than dissolving the triangle; it stabilises the remaining dyad at a low level.
- Effect on return: "If the threesome is reunited, the old emotional fusion of the triad is immediately operative again" → `event-kind:RETURN` → re-entry restores the pre-removal triangle state with no decay; the parents' comfort while the member is away should not be read as change.
- With the triadic one away in dependent attachments not involving the parents, all three are more comfortable and the parents have no motivation for therapy → `coach` → a stable low-motivation absorbing state the coach cannot enter.
- Cyclical differentiation order: parent A → parent B → triadic one, cycles "as long as a year" → `Family` → change is sequential and slow, not simultaneous; a cycle-length parameter.
- Multigenerational descent: each generation's most-involved child is more impaired than the parents; less-involved siblings may exceed them → `Person` / `Family` → per-child inheritance is a function of involvement, and can go up as well as down.
- Three-or-more generations to schizophrenia, more with spread → `test` → a multigenerational run should reproduce descent to the lowest level in ≥3 generations under full concentration and more under spread.
- Anxiety as a conserved drive with substitutable discharge channels (emotional demand → physical illness → community incidents → institutional demand) → `Family` → blocking a channel must reroute rather than reduce; this is the single most testable systemic claim in the chapter.
- Professional/institutional system as additional nodes running the same three-step process (psychiatrist, hospital, school, courts, merchants) → `unmodelled` → the model currently has no place for a professional agent that can *absorb* responsibility, *certify* a defect, or *decline* to. This is the chapter's biggest gap relative to the model.
- Responsibility as a conserved transferable quantity: if the therapist/staff holds it, the parents cannot → `unmodelled` → needs a responsibility variable that sums across family and professional nodes.
- Capacity for responsible functioning is present but latent and called out by environmental demand → `Person` / `coach` → responsibility is a function of demand, not a fixed trait; the ward experiment is the natural test case.
- The therapist's own responsible functioning gates what he can require → `coach` → the coach agent needs its own state that bounds its available moves.
- Overhelpfulness leaves incident rate flat and relocates incidents → `coach` / `test` → an adversarial test: a coach that supplies help and instruction should produce the same incident count in different locations, not fewer incidents.
- Behaviour-framed vs. sickness-framed restriction → `move:RESTRICT_FOR_BEHAVIOR` vs `move:DIAGNOSE` → the frame determines whether the target has a controllable lever; measurable as recovery speed and stay length.
- Diagnosis as a productive act ("the projection system can create its own defects") → `move:DIAGNOSE` → labelling increments the labelled member's impairment; expert confirmation is a sought-after resource the family shops for.
- Prognosis set at the first "step into mental illness," by whether the triadic one accepts the designation and whether the professional takes the parents' side → `event-kind:FIRST_DIAGNOSIS` → a branch point with a durable effect on all later trajectories.
- Individual therapy for a symptom-bearer still at home increases his absorbing capacity → `coach` → supporting the symptom-bearer stabilises the pathological equilibrium; the coach should have a move that *makes things worse* in exactly this way.
- Family terminates therapy on symptomatic improvement, more likely the more severe the problem → `Family` / `test` → an engagement-termination rule inversely coupled to differentiation.
- Symptom relief without change in basic differentiation level (5–7 years, "no change in the basic problem") → `Person` → symptom level and differentiation must be separate variables, and a run must be able to flatten one while the other is fixed.
- Function separable from symptom (the bus-stop patient working while the voices persisted) → `Person` → same separation, at the individual level.
- Severe schizophrenia as "undifferentiatable" → `test` → below some differentiation floor, no coaching intervention should raise the basic level; only symptom level should move.
- Extended-family involvement adds "strength" to the ego mass → `Family` / `move:ENGAGE_EXTENDED_FAMILY` → widening membership raises available self; flagged by Bowen as unproven.
- Reversal possible in the parents' family of origin while impossible in the nuclear triad → `unmodelled` → implies the family-of-origin tie remains an operable relationship in adulthood, distinct from the nuclear family.
- Acting out shifting from community back into family as a hopeful sign → `test` → a location-of-symptom variable, with inward movement scored as improvement.
- Improved spouse attacked by the impaired one before he joins → `Relationship` → a transient escalation phase should follow one member's differentiation, before either resolution or resumed projection.
- One parent differentiating increases the other parent's involvement with the child → `Triangle` → conservation across the three legs of the triangle.
- Parental conflict and projection to the child are alternatives, and the conflict subsides when the projection resumes → `Triangle` → direct trade-off, and a strong candidate for a regression test.
- Parental motivation subsides when the triadic one takes up the work → `coach` → engaging the symptom-bearer de-motivates the parents; a cost on that coach move.
- Multi-family coupling on a shared ward (one family asking the others' permission to lock the ward) → `unmodelled` → no place for family-to-family interaction in a shared institutional space.
- Normal siblings withdrawing from the family effort → `Person` → a disengagement move available to less-involved members.
- Observer's theoretical frame determines what is observable → `unmodelled` → if a therapist agent is modelled, his frame is a state variable that gates which family features he can perceive at all.

### `[p2]` Added or amended at the second read

- **Amended — coupling decay.** Keep the rate near zero, but split the parameter and scope it.
  (a) `Relationship` needs a **re-activation latency of zero on reunion** — that is what "immediately
  operative again" licenses, and it is not the same parameter as a decay rate.
  (b) Separation must be modelled as **edge-rewiring, not isolation**: the departing member acquires a
  substitute attachment ("a new family ego mass to which they can append themselves") and the
  remaining members acquire substitute projection targets ("borrowing self from outside themselves ...
  projecting their inadequacies to others"). A separated node with no substitute is a configuration
  this chapter never describes.
  (c) The claim is sourced only for the lowest band; make the decay rate **level-dependent**, or
  record the uniform application as an extrapolation. See P5 / Q-NODECAY.
- **Amended — the reroute adversarial test.** Two constraints pass 1 did not carry.
  (a) The two ward phases do not share an endpoint (incidents → complaints), so the test must score
  one variable across both arms or acknowledge the switch.
  (b) Relocation has a **sign**: "it is a hopeful sign when acting out in the community shifts back
  within the family." So the test cannot be "same count, different place" alone — the destination
  carries valence, and an inward move at constant count is an improvement. `readout` needs a
  symptom-location dimension with an ordering on locations.
- **Amended — the hospitalisation trigger.** `event-kind:HOSPITALIZATION` fires on a comparison of
  **disturbing behaviour** (a variable distinct from symptom severity) against a **per-agent
  tolerance**, evaluated by a named agent. Three tolerance-holders — family, community, self — and
  the community is outside the family. Reassigning ownership of the decision changes whether it
  fires: in the one case where tolerance was reached, returning the decision to the husband prevented
  the event. This is a `coach` move (`RETURN_DECISION`) with a measurable effect.
- **New — the change-back ladder has a symptom-mediated variant.** `move:I-POSITION` reaction
  sequence: escalate helplessness → self-label → demand caretaking → attack the tie → withdraw →
  capitulate. The first rung is a **symptom**, not a verbal challenge, and the same opening move
  appears at the severe end as the "petition for sickness", where it succeeds instead of failing.
  Whether the ladder terminates in capitulation or in restored projection should depend on the level.
- **New — partial compliance on the three-step interruption.** Asked to stop *thinking*, *calling*
  and *treating*, the wife could change calling and treating within two months and could not change
  thinking. The three steps are not equally actuable; `move:STOP_PROJECTING` should be decomposed,
  with the feeling-thinking step slowest and possibly unreachable by instruction.
- **New — the responsibility variable has four stages, not two.** Latent → released by an **explicit
  stated requirement** (scolding does nothing: "they had not been told exactly what was required") →
  **overshoots** ("overly responsible") → **decays to sporadic**. `Person` needs a
  responsibility-capacity with a demand-driven release, an overshoot, and no persistence.
- **New — functioning is per-tie, not global.** "They may function successfully in business or a
  profession as long as they keep relationships casual and brief" — impairment is expressed only on
  close ties. `Person.functional_level` should be evaluated against the tie in question, not once
  globally.
- **New — a three-state impairment machine on the symptom-bearer.** *potential patient / actual
  patient / compensated patient*, with the reciprocal caretaker position held constant across all
  three. `Person` state, `Relationship` position unchanged by the transition.
- **New — fusion has a stated shape.** Monotone decreasing in maturity, saturating near maximum at
  the schizophrenia end, reaching zero only at complete maturity. This is the only functional-form
  statement in the chapter and it should constrain whatever maps `C` to coupling strength.
- **New — work rate scales with level.** Low-differentiation families "proceed much more cautiously
  and slowly"; "the differentiation process is most rapid with problems of neurotic degree." Level
  sets a **rate**, not only a ceiling.
- **New — avoidance is a property of channel topology.** Separate individual channels supplied the
  avoidance route ("each expecting the other to deal with the issue"); the same families in one room
  could not avoid. Generalises the group-size rule from other chapters to **channel membership**,
  and makes the coach's choice of who-shares-a-session mechanically consequential.
- **New — a compliant-inert engagement state.** "Cooperating while waiting to be changed", scoring
  well on the conventional outcome measure with zero structural movement. `Family` needs an
  engagement mode that is neither refusal nor work, and the readout must be able to distinguish it.
- **New — latent vs. absent.** "When the family is calm, the process can disappear from view, but if
  anxiety builds up again, the process appears again." A zero reading on projection activity is
  ambiguous. `readout` should separate expressed activity from the underlying propensity.
- **New — the projection-return trigger.** The working parent redirecting attention from child to
  spouse surfaces marital conflict, and the conflict is aborted by resuming projection. This is a
  concrete regression test with an order: redirect → conflict → re-fusion → child regresses.
- **New — attention direction, not attention quality, is the load.** The son improved within two
  weeks while the parents "made little progress" and their "thoughts went only to inadequacies in the
  son." Corroborates the directed-attention finding from elsewhere in the corpus and sharpens it:
  the parents' *success* is not in the loop.
- **New — a live symptom can be the entry condition for joint work.** "With the tangible problem
  provided by the son's acting out, [the parents] were able to focus on a family problem and then to
  begin the first working together on their own problem." A coach objective defined purely on
  symptom reduction removes the object the work needs.
- **New — the triad is derived, twice over.** From dyadic instability (the child converts an unstable
  pair into a stable triad) and from differential involvement (the less-involved siblings leave). The
  three-person unit should be an emergent selection over involvement, not a kinship-typed object.
- **New — no rule for who moves first.** This chapter delegates the choice and reports the second
  mover progressing faster. Whatever gating rule the model adopts for the first differentiating move,
  it is not sourced here, and one case here runs the other way.
- **Amended — evidence weight.** Ch08's data are Topeka 1949–1954 and the NIMH live-in ward
  1954–1959. Findings shared with other chapters reporting that ward are the same observations, not
  independent replications; the convergence counts should be discounted accordingly.
