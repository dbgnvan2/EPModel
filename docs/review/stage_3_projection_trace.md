## Reading

Written by Claude, 2026-10-24, from the tables above. D18's condition is whether the parents' term is what lifts the
child under projection. It is not, so P1 is not built as decided and D18 goes to the owner again before revision 39.

- **The parents' term carries none of the lift.** Summed over the fifteen steps, the parents' term contributes
  −0.0072 ± 0.0296 to the treatment − baseline difference, and the child's own term contributes +0.0951 ± 0.0362;
  together they make the criterion's +0.0886 ± 0.0439 (the band bound 6 of 2250 steps in each arm, so the two
  contributions add to the step almost everywhere).
- **Why §3.1's reading did not hold.** §3.1 said an `OVERFUNCTION` lowers the child's own term and raises the
  parents'. But the step reads the estimator's functional level, which is `functional_level − pseudo_self −
  max(0, adjust_swing)` (`slow_tick.py::record_week`, revision 23), so a conserved exchange moves neither term. The
  exchange does happen: in the treatment the child lends about 2.3 to 3.1 points of pseudo-self at ages 6–12 and
  borrows up to 6.4 by age 20 (the by-age table). The estimator nets it out on both sides.
- **Where the lift is.** The child's own term is higher under projection from age 8 and grows to +0.24 to +0.26 at ages
  16–20. The child's stress swing, the functioning that `M1.A.5e`'s coping raises and soothing lowers, is higher by
  0 before age 11 and by +0.09 to +0.16 from age 14. Summed over the ages it is +0.94 against the own term's +1.90:
  about half. From age 16 the treated child copes more weeks a year (+1.0 to +3.3) and soothes fewer (−0.6 to −3.2).
  The child's load is higher under projection at ages 6–12 (+1.7 to +3.7) and lower from 16. This trace does not
  attribute the other half of the own-term difference.
- **What P1 as worded would do.** Read on these runs' weeks, the parents' functional level less their borrowed
  pseudo-self, against their basic level, is about −5 in both arms (−5.05 and −5.28), against the present term's
  −0.30 and −0.31. P1 would lower every child's development in both arms, projection or not, and its treatment −
  baseline difference is −0.17 ± 0.56, too wide to show a direction. This is a first-order estimate with no re-run,
  so it leaves out the feedback of a changed basic level.
