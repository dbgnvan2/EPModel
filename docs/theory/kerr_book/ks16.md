# KS16 — Personal Vignettes of the Process of Differentiation

**Citation rule:** cite `KS16`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

Eight worked first-person cases. This is the densest source of `M5.D`/`M5.E` fixtures in the corpus.

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ KS16.1 — **The three-phase trajectory** — `M5.D`'s state machine, observed over three years `[T]` `[M]`

The adoption dispute, 1975–1978, and Kerr labels the phases himself.

**Phase 1 (~1 year) — counter-argument.** "I was **very reactive to Mother's criticism** of the decision and would **counter her arguments with my arguments. It accomplished nothing** other than my realizing that **getting so reactive to her was pointless.**"

**Phase 2 — non-reaction.** "I was **less defensive and did not try to change her mind.**" Tested when he overhears her "**bad-mouthing my wife and me to my cousin and her family**" — "**it helped me to think of Mother as triangling her anxiety into the family system rather than just feeling angry.**"

**Phase 3 — the position.** In the car: she "**launched into a diatribe.**" He says, calmly, "**I understand your point of view.**" She replies: "**Well, if you understood me, you wouldn't do what you are doing!**"

> "**I saw that trying not to react was an insufficient response to her.** I did not feel angry with her, but after five or ten minutes **I pulled the car over and in a very firm voice said, 'Mother, I am not willing to listen to your attacks on my decision making and blaming my wife for my decision any longer. This will stop now!'** If I came across as angry, it was a **controlled anger** that I was using to make my point."

**The follow-through.** "**I remember being concerned that Mother would dive into the scotch and be a mess the next day, but I walked up the stairs to the bedroom and fell asleep fairly quickly.** I recall thinking at the time, '**This woman can bloody well take care of her own emotions.**'"

**The next morning.** She speaks first: "**Michael, are you still mad at me?**" — "**Do I have reason to be?**" — "**Finally, you told me what you think.**"

**The result**, days later: a package in the mail, an infant sweater reading "**Grandma Loves You.**"

*Model consequence:* **the single most important fixture for `M5.D` in the corpus**, because it distinguishes three states the spec collapses into two.

| Phase | Move class | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Counter-argument** — defending the position | "accomplished nothing"; sustains the exchange |
| 2 | **Non-reaction** — declining to engage | **"an insufficient response"** — no change |
| 3 | **Position** — stating what self will and will not do | lands; reaction; resolution |

`M5.E.2` already says the reaction decays unless fed by the mover **defending or counterattacking** — that is phase 1, confirmed. What the spec does **not** have is that **phase 2 is also insufficient.** Non-reaction is not a weak I-position; it is a *different and inadequate* move. That is `KS00.5`/`KS04.6`'s "not taking the bait" a third time, and here it appears as a **necessary developmental stage that must be passed through and exceeded.**

**Requirement:** `M5` needs a `WITHHOLD` move distinct from both `I-POSITION` and the reactive set (see also `KS08.1`'s staircase), and `M11.C` should assert that a `WITHHOLD`-only arm **MUST NOT** produce the `M5.E` resolution that an `I-POSITION` arm produces.

Two more details worth encoding. The position's content is **exactly `KS06.11`'s form** — what the actor will not do, not what the other should do. And **"controlled anger"** is admissible: `M5.F.4`'s assertion-form failure is not about affect intensity but about impingement. Note the contrast with `KS05.5`'s "not taken in anger" — reconcile in pass 2; the distinction appears to be *anger used* versus *anger acting*.

## ⭐⭐ KS16.2 — **The follow-through is the move.** Going to bed while she may fall apart `[T]`

> "I was concerned that Mother would **dive into the scotch and be a mess the next day**, but I walked up the stairs… **This woman can bloody well take care of her own emotions.**"

And the earlier, near-identical marital instance:

> "I turned to my wife and said, '**I realize that you are upset, and I would like to be able to say or do something to relieve it… but I have something I need to do upstairs and am going to do that now.**' I got up from the couch and climbed the stairs. **My legs felt like they weighed a hundred pounds each**, but I made it to the top of the stairs."

> His wife, later: "**As I watched you walk up those stairs, I did not like it, but thought you were finally being your own person.**"

*Model consequence:* `M5.D.6` requires `FOLLOW_UP` and makes skipping it revert the gain. These two cases show what the follow-through **costs** and what it **is** — not a subsequent contact, but **sustaining the position through the window in which the other is expected to deteriorate.**

"Legs felt like they weighed a hundred pounds" is the short-horizon anxiety spike (`M11.C.5`) reported from inside. And the receiver's own account — *did not like it, but thought you were finally being your own person* — is `KS15.5`'s simultaneous attraction-and-resistance, stated by the recipient.

**This is also the model's clearest case of a differentiating move being an omission.** With `KS08.1`, that is two of the book's canonical instances where the differentiating act is *not doing the automatic thing.* `M4.D.1`'s exactly-one-move-per-tick cannot represent either.

## ⭐⭐ KS16.3 — **Detriangling by action, not statement** — the funeral `[T]` `[M]`

The triangle: Kerr's mother opposed his oldest half-brother's marriage on religious grounds, "**threatened to boycott the wedding and to cut my brother out of my parents' will**", then backed down — after which "**it was not overtly conflicted… but more about negative comments behind her back**", with the father siding with the mother and "**the three of us younger boys… drawn into seeing it from Mother's point of view.**"

**The observation that changed it:** "the many **subtle negative comments my oldest brother made about his wife. I had never noticed that before. Much of it had been conveyed nonverbally. I also observed that no one in the family challenged his comments.** … **I had not seen until then how my oldest brother helped create and sustain this triangle.**"

**The explicit rejection of explanation:** "**Experience has shown that trying to explain emotional process to one's own family on a rational level does not work — people tend to get defensive. Trying to explain it to the family usually reflects one's own anxiety.**"

**The actions:** better personal contact with the sister-in-law; visiting her mother; and travelling east with wife and two daughters to attend that mother's funeral.

> "**This was a clear statement to my family that I was not buying into all the negativity**… **Being present at such times in one's family goes a very long way to opening up the relationship system.**"

**The result:** "**My effort changed my relationships not just with my oldest brother and his wife but between mother and his wife as well. Things softened on both sides, and my sister-in-law eventually became Mother's right arm in her later declining years.**"

> "**This is a case where I did not make detriangling statements but took detriangling actions.**"

*Model consequence:* **a detriangling that consists entirely of `STAY-IN-CONTACT` moves directed at the outsider**, sustained over years, with a measurable structural outcome. Three things:

1. **The move type is ordinary.** Attending a funeral, visiting a relative. `M5.A.1`'s `STAY-IN-CONTACT` carries this, and its effect here comes from **who it is directed at** relative to the triangle's alignment — `M8.6`'s alignment-not-knowledge rule doing real work in the positive direction.
2. **The outcome propagated to a tie the actor was not on** — mother and sister-in-law softened toward each other. `M1.C.5`'s triangle decrement should produce this.
3. **"Trying to explain… usually reflects one's own anxiety"** — a *diagnostic*, and a fourth confirmation that the model loses nothing by excluding utterance content (`KS15.2` said the same).

Also note the **third-party effect on the mover's own perception**: the brother's covert negativity was invisible until Kerr had the triangle concept. Mindware changes what is *observable*, not only what is *doable*.

## ⭐⭐ KS16.4 — Bowen's smile: a coach contact that transmitted **nothing but state** `[T]` `[M]`

1969, the Monday after a weekend visit home. Kerr opens the session with "**a long monologue… the better part of a half-hour. It ended when I finally looked at Bowen and noticed a slight smile on his face.** My reaction… was, '**What the hell is he smiling at — this isn't funny!**' **Well, bingo, my head cleared.**"

> "I did not realize until that moment **how anxious I was in describing my visit and how anxious my family and I were when I was there.** I also realized that **I had been more chronically anxious in anticipating the visit than I had realized.**"

> "**Something I have never forgotten is that Bowen did not need to tell me that I had been caught in the system; he simply did not get caught in my anxiety, and that was enough to get me out of it. He was emotionally neutral and present to me during the session.**"

*Model consequence:* **the purest available instance of `M1.E`'s external agent, and it contains no information transfer at all.** The coach emitted nothing but a facial expression, and the effect was the recipient detecting his own chronic anxiety — `KS15.1`'s ingredient 3, which `KS15.3` says is the hard one because chronic anxiety is poorly felt.

So `M1.E.7`'s landed contact has a **third form**, beside `KS10.11`'s specifics and `KS13.4`'s category: **non-participation.** The agent's *failure to be recruited* is itself the signal, and it works because the recipient's expectation (`KS08.9`'s emotional logic) is violated.

That is directly implementable and it is cheap: the coach's move is `M4.D.5`'s fused default **not** being played. It also explains why `M1.E.2` requires the coach to hold a drifting reactive state — the mechanism *is* the coach's state, so a stateless coach transmits nothing.

Note the failure of the pre-visit coaching: "**I recall expressing confidence that I could deal with the situation**" — then "**I had no clue how much I had fused into the emotional system.**" **Anticipatory confidence is not predictive of performance**, which is a readout warning and consistent with `KS08.3`.

## ⭐⭐ KS16.5 — The Sidman sequence: a system-level intervention with a stated counterfactual `[T]` `[M]`

The most consequential case in the book. After his brother's suicide (Nov 1969), Kerr's mother stayed in her own apartment for three months and "**seemed to know that the apartment was the place she needed to be.**" In February 1970 her sister and brother-in-law pressured her to move to their town.

> "**when Mother got to Sidman, her functioning declined markedly. She became childlike**… Kitty, Dick, and the three of their five children that lived near them were **all anxiously focused on Mother.** … '**Your mother is not good, Michael, not good.**' **The anxiety was palpable.**"

> "A family physician… **prescribed tranquilizers, antidepressants, and sleep medications**… **Mother was pretty drugged up and lethargic. The doctor and all of Kitty's family were operating on the assumption that Mother's emotional state was a depression caused by Billy's death.**"

> "**I saw the decline in her functioning as related to living in the midst of such an anxious emotional field**… these were people who **meant well but… were overfunctioning and Mother was underfunctioning.**"

**A prior natural experiment, stated as consistent:** during an earlier period in Maryland, taking her *to* Sidman reliably restored her, and returning her to Maryland reliably crashed her "**within a matter of months.**" Same person, same two locations, **opposite sign** — because which environment was anxious had changed.

**The plan and the moves**, Memorial Day 1970:
- Direct statement about the medication: "**You are on so much medication, I can't talk with you. Please do something about that.**"
- Listening without siding: "**I listened but did not take a side on what decision she should make. I did say, 'It sounds like you want to go home.'**"
- Visiting all three cousins individually — "**I had gained a reputation in Kitty's family for not taking Mother's problems seriously enough**" — to communicate presence and concern.
- The car ride with Kitty: "**I drove and vowed to myself to keep driving until the climate of uneasiness between Kitty and me broke.**" Broken by a joke: "**when this is over, maybe you could write a book about it!**"
- **Bringing the private content into the open group.** At dinner with ~10 at table: "**Kitty was telling me how worried she was about Mother and that she is at her wit's end.**" Mother left the table, circled the living room, and returned. Two cousins spoke at length. "**The conversation was not tense, which was striking because these sorts of things tended to get murmured about behind closed doors, out of earshot of the parties most directly involved.**"

**The outcome**, days later: "**she had flushed all of her pills down the toilet and that she was going back home. Her voice was clear and firm.**" She lived in that apartment another **sixteen years**.

**And the interlocking case:** the half-brother Bob, panicking at the prospect of her return, watched a mole darken from early March, was diagnosed in May with **stage II malignant melanoma**, and was operated on the week after the family tension broke. Clean margins; he died 38 years later of unrelated causes. Bowen's comment: "**Predictable!**"

> "**It is possible that my efforts to maintain 'self' during that period saved my mother's life.**"

*Model consequence:* four distinct model claims, all assertable.

1. **The removal was the stressor.** Relocation into a higher-anxiety field, with better intentions and more care, **degraded** functioning. That is `M11.C.13` (help relocates, does not reduce) at its starkest, and the Maryland↔Sidman reversal is a **within-subject control**: the same move helped in one direction and harmed in the other, so the effect is in the *field*, not the person or the place.
2. **Overfunctioning was universal and well-meant**, including the physician's. `M1.E.3`'s responsibility-absorption is here performed by a *medical* external agent, with sedation as the mechanism.
3. **The intervention was a single move type — bringing covert content into the open group** — and it worked by changing the *route* (`M1.F.3`) rather than the content. "Murmured about behind closed doors" versus said at a table of ten. **`M1.F` already carries `route` and witness sets, and this is the strongest demonstration of why.**
4. **Symptom in a third person**, per `M5.E.5`, and its **remission** on resolution. Kerr's own reading is explicitly stated as a belief, and Bowen's "Predictable!" is an unfalsifiable clinical judgement — grade the melanoma claim `[K-ext]` and **do not implement a cancer mechanism** (`M11.F.4` already forbids it, per `KB11`).

Bowen's line, recorded: "**We all grow up on the backs of schizophrenia!**" — "**Billy had inherited the weaknesses of the family, and siblings like me were thus somewhat freer to inherit the strengths.**" That is `KS11.4`'s sibling reciprocity and `KS11.8`'s compartmentalisation stated as one sentence.

## ⭐⭐ KS16.6 — Seeing the 50/50: "I was a 50 percent contributor" `[T]`

Mid-to-late 1990s, after his wife returned from six weeks at Gombe.

> "**I saw very clearly that I was shutting down and starting to distance in the face of what I perceived as her intensity.** This part was all very familiar… **This time it was different — I suddenly thought of her intensity as her reaction to my distancing and associated disdain.** Up to that time, I realized, **I had justified my distancing by viewing her intensity as causing it. I had not realized until then that I was a 50 percent contributor to her intensity and she was a 50 percent contributor to my withdrawal.**"

> "**At that point I had been teaching this cocreation idea to students of Bowen theory for over twenty-five years, but I had not observed it fully in my own marriage until that day.**"

> "**The action part was simply calming down, not withdrawing, and watching the emotional intensity in my wife diminish in an amazingly short time.**"

*Model consequence:* the reciprocity is confirmed as **exactly symmetric** ("50 percent… 50 percent"), which is `KS02.7`'s equality constraint. And the intervention is again an **omission plus a non-withdrawal** — "calming down, not withdrawing" — with a **fast** receiver response ("amazingly short time"), contrasting with the multi-day and multi-year timescales elsewhere. `M5.D`'s reaction window is not one duration; it varies with what was withheld.

The twenty-five-year gap between **teaching** the idea and **seeing** it in his own marriage is the most direct evidence in the corpus that **knowing the theory is not having the mindware available in the loaded tie** — which is `KS05.11`/`KS06.8`'s context dependence applied to the coach himself, and a caution against any implementation where `systems_perspective` is a single global scalar. **Candidate: `systems_perspective` may need to be per-tie.**

## ⭐ KS16.7 — Multigenerational research as an operation, with named effects `[T]`

The method: "**genealogical records, birth certificates, death certificates, obituaries, census records, newspaper articles, real estate records, old letters, family bibles**", plus "**remembrances of deceased relatives from a wide range of living relatives.**" Target: "**at least four generations.**"

The named effects:

- **Comparison across branches.** "**Being able to compare your family unit with units from other branches**… provides clues as to how multigenerational emotional process shaped your own immediate family."
- **Opinion/fact separation.** Getting many versions, "**I could develop my own view of the family history by keeping the distinction between fact and opinion at the forefront of my mind.**" Result: "**what one family member said to me about another less easily swayed my view of the person being described.**"
- **Being seen as an individual.** "**it contributed to the family beginning to think of me as an individual in my own right, not just my parents' youngest son.**"
- **Distant branches are calmer and more available.** "**Because you are related to a somewhat distant branch… but were not caught up in their emotional system as you grew up, they typically welcome your interest**… **It is easier to be objective about more distant branches.**"
- **Interest displaces threat.** "**Being interesting is an antidote to feeling threatened. Threat triggers negative (critical) or overly idealized (defensive) emotions about one's family.**"

*Model consequence:* `M1.A.12`'s involvement weight and `M1.C`'s interlocking triangles already support all of this, and it identifies a **cheap source of mindware the spec has not represented**: distant, low-load branches of the same system. That is `KS13.3`'s observability asymmetry generalised — the model should let `systems_perspective` rise from contact with **structurally similar, low-conductance** ties.

The two-sided reactivity signature — **critical or overly idealized** — is `M1.A.18a` again, fifth occurrence.

## ⭐ KS16.8 — Two discoveries that dissolved blame, both from a third party `[T]` `[M]`

**The grandmother's drinking.** Nobody mentioned it — "**including Mother**" — until an aunt by marriage did. Confronted, the mother "**finally fessed up**": "**She was a saint but did drink a lot!**" Kerr's reading: protectiveness. The **model-relevant** part is the hypothesis it generated: the grandmother's decline "**may have been related to feeling overwhelmed by the birth of a fourth child**", at a point when Kerr's mother "**was beginning to enter adolescence**" — which "**helped me better understand how Mother's sensitivity to the unhappiness and distress of others might have been intensified by responding to her mother's distress. That dynamic was most strongly replicated in her relationship with my oldest full brother.**"

The pinball analogy, stated as a general mechanism:

> "**A family may adapt to having a certain number of kids but have its functioning drop dramatically when one more child is added — it is one more than the family organism can manage without a significant increase in symptoms.**"

**The abortion.** Kerr's mother confessed an abortion in early 1939 and "**apologized to me passionately**" — he was conceived at her insistence immediately after. Years later his aunt, unprompted: "**I told your mother in no uncertain terms that my baby brother already had too much responsibility on his shoulders with three children and that she should abort that baby!**"

> "**What struck me even more powerfully was that Mother had given in to that pressure.** … **Dad's functioning position in his family of origin was a bit of a doted-on prince surrounded by three women who were fiercely protective of him**… **Dad married a woman with fierce instincts to protect the male from being overloaded with responsibility.**"

> "**I realized that to some degree I blamed Dad for being an underfunctioner**… **I finally understood that Mother and Dad were a perfect emotional fit: each played an equal part in perpetuating their pattern, and each had been programmed as kids to do just that.**"

*Model consequence:* two things.

1. **The pinball threshold is a capacity claim**, and it is implementable: family functioning is not linear in member count — there is a **step** at the point where the system's absorption capacity is exceeded. `KS03.5`'s triangle overload is the same shape one level down. `M6.I` should carry a family-level capacity with a threshold, not just per-tie budgets.
2. **`KS02.4`'s "emotional fit" demonstrated across three generations** — the father programmed by three protective women, the mother by a distressed mother, meeting at complementary poles. This is `M2.A.0c` pairing *plus* `KS12.3` complementarity *plus* `KS09.1`'s programming, all in one case, and the resolution of blame followed from seeing the fit rather than the fault.

Also: **the decisive information came from a non-participant in the triangle** (an aunt by marriage; a paternal aunt). `M1.F.5`'s witness path is the model's mechanism for this, and it means the estimator (`M1.A.4a`) has access to information the participants do not.

## ⭐ KS16.9 — Family-of-origin work does **not** transfer automatically — with two reasons `[T]`

> "**Progress in the family of origin does not transfer automatically to nuclear family relationships, but it helps considerably.**"

> **Reason 1:** "**many important aspects of the relationships with one's parents and siblings replicate in the relationships with one's spouse and children, but one's spouse is not one's parent. Spouses bring aspects of their own unresolved attachment to their family of origin that must be observed and addressed.**"

> **Reason 2:** "**a person lives day in and day out with them, which makes the emotional process more intense and more difficult to observe objectively.**"

> "**it is more accurate to refer to the process as increasing solid self, which is one component of basic self.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M7.A.2` needs qualifying.** It requires that differentiation gained in a peripheral system "**MUST** transfer automatically to the nuclear family." Kerr says explicitly it does **not** transfer automatically, and gives two mechanisms — the second party has their own unresolved attachment, and the nuclear tie is higher-load and therefore harder to observe.

`KS16.6`'s twenty-five-year gap is the evidence. Reconciling with `KS13.11`'s "as people made progress in their family of origin they also started making progress in their current family systems" — the transfer is **real but requires separate observation in the new context**, which is precisely `KS16.6`'s per-tie mindware point.

**Amend `M7.A.2`:** the *capacity* transfers; the *application* must be re-earned per tie. That is a materially different requirement and it is well supported.

## KS16.10 — "Keep going home until you don't get diabetes no more" `[T]`

A man told Bowen: "**I went to see my family and was diagnosed with diabetes soon thereafter!**" Bowen: "**Good, good! Keep going home until you don't get diabetes no more!**"

> "**people can learn to get less reactive to their families once they quit trying to change their families and, with the aid of theory and coaching, focus on changing self rather than others. People can get so focused on what is wrong with others that they are barely aware of how much they want to change them.**"

And the resistance: "**The only reason I would go to see my family is to rehabilitate them!**"

*Model consequence:* **exposure-with-changed-objective is the mechanism, and symptom on early exposure is expected rather than disconfirming.** That is a real requirement for `M11.C`: a `REDUCE_CUTOFF` arm **MUST** show a symptom or anxiety *increase* in the early window before any improvement. An implementation in which bridging is monotonically beneficial is wrong, and would also contradict `M5.B.3a`'s floor from the other direction.

## KS16.11 — Expectations, and "it is what it is" `[T]`

> "**I believe that the amount of chronic-anxiety-driven unrealistic expectations that people have of themselves and others usually exceeds realistic expectations, particularly in intimate relationships.**"

> "**Family research revealing the universality of family difficulties gives credence to the adage 'it is what it is.' That recognition can reduce counterproductive anxiety-driven urges to push to make things how they 'should be.'** That alone can reduce family anxiety and help each family member be a little more thoughtful. **This is not about giving up but about growing up.**"

> "**being more of a 'self' usually brings out more of the best in others.**"

> "**Awareness of multigenerational emotional process provides solid evidence for going beyond forgiveness as a feeling to knowing objectively that one's parents did the best they could**… **based on recognition of the limits multigenerational emotional process imposes on human emotional autonomy. Nor, of course, does this attitude absolve people of responsibility for their actions.**"

*Model consequence:* **expectations** — one of `KS10.1`'s four cues — are stated here as *predominantly unrealistic* in intimate ties, which sets the channel's default. And the last quote is `KS01.5`'s no-culpability rule reached from the participant's side for the second time (`KS10.11` was the first): reduced blame **without** absolution. `M11.F` should state it that way, since the pairing is what makes it defensible.
