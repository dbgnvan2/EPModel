# Ch11 — Principles and Techniques of Multiple Family Therapy

## 0. Provenance
- **Stated date / venue (from the text itself):** No presentation date or venue is stated for the
  paper. The *method* is said to have been "developed as part of a clinical research study at
  Georgetown University Medical Center"; the multiple-family pilot is described as beginning at
  "Georgetown University Hospital." Internal dating anchors: the live-in research project ran
  1954–1959 (terminated 1959); the practice change excluding the problem child came "early in
  1960"; the present two-spouse method "started about 1962" and settled "about 1964"; the
  multiple-family group started in 1965. Latest citation is Bowen 1971. So the chapter is written
  no earlier than 1971.
- **Clinical setting and data source (who was observed, how many, over what period):** Two data
  sources are braided together.
  (a) The **live-in research ward**: entire families lived on the ward with their schizophrenic son
  or daughter, some for as long as thirty months; three to five families resident at any one time;
  five-year project, 1954–1959. Staff of four therapists functioning as co-therapists, plus ward
  staff and research observers.
  (b) A **simultaneous outpatient clinical practice** for less severe problems, later private
  practice and teaching/supervision programs: parents and a preadolescent-to-midteen child
  referred by Juvenile Court or by the school; ~25 such children treated ~1957–1960. Then the
  two-spouse method from ~1962. Then the **multiple family therapy pilot** begun 1965 with three
  families found by an admissions social worker, with research observers seated at the back and
  sessions recorded, plus a later videotaped multiple-family group at another medical school. He
  also reports "over twenty-five families" in which a togetherness-oriented husband abruptly
  terminated therapy by taking an out-of-town assignment.
- **Genre: theory / technique — and roughly what proportion.** Both, but decidedly
  technique-dominant: roughly **25% theory / 75% technique**. The theory content is compressed
  into two places — the triangle as the building block of an emotional system, and the alternating
  differentiation sequence between spouses. Everything else is a history of successive changes in
  method and a detailed operational account of how a session is run. Notably, the *evolution of
  technique* is itself presented as evidence about how families work: each structural change to
  the meetings is justified by the family behaviour it removed.

# STREAM A — How families function

## A1. Phenomena requiring explanation

- **Anxiety gets externalised to whatever channel is available.** On the research ward, when ward
  staff participated in the family meetings, family members used the meetings to externalise
  intrafamily anxiety into conflict with the ward staff. When staff became silent observers, that
  route closed — and the families then began "externalizing intrafamily anxiety into differences
  between the therapists." The same anxiety kept finding a new outlet as each outlet was shut.
  Observed: research ward, 1954–1959, families with a schizophrenic son or daughter.
- **Cross-family interruption at exactly the moment of definition.** With multiple families present
  and anyone free to speak, "just as the therapist was successfully working toward a definition of
  an issue in one family, some anxious member from another family would interrupt and change the
  subject to another issue in another family." Observed: research ward group meetings.
- **Auditing beats participating.** When the structure changed so that each session was designated
  for a single family with the other families as silent auditors, families reported they "often
  obtained more benefit from auditing, when they were free to listen and to really 'hear,' than
  when they spent time preparing their next comment." Both the families and the staff rated that
  final year the most productive of the whole project.
- **A first-year "therapeutic honeymoon" that ends.** Converting the small closed system into an
  open one produced decreased symptoms in most families and "near exhilaration" in some; the
  honeymoon ended after about a year and the meetings became repetitive and less productive.
- **The child's symptoms absorb the session.** With parents and child seen together weekly, parents
  focused so much on the detail of the child's problem that the parental relationship could not be
  kept in focus. The "good" result was a year of attendance ending with the child's symptoms down,
  the mother's "domination" subsided and the father's "passivity" decreased — families left with
  glowing praise while Bowen judged them to have attained "little or none of the basic change of
  which they were capable."
- **Removing the child improved outcomes for the child.** When parents were seen alone, sessions
  became lively and profitable, most of the children became symptom-free, and the average "bad"
  result was better than the former "good" results.
- **Marriage progressively closes.** Spouses have their most open relationships in courtship,
  common-law, or other intimate but non-permanent relationships. After marriage each learns which
  subjects make the other anxious, avoids them to avoid discomfort in self, and "an increasing
  number of subjects thus become taboo for discussion." Attempts to "talk it out" typically stir up
  emotional reactiveness and drive them farther apart.
- **Spouses do not know each other.** After ten sessions one wife said she had learned more about
  her husband from listening to him talk to the therapist than in ten years of marriage; a husband
  after twelve monthly sessions said the greatest value was learning what went on inside his wife
  "after having been in the dark for twenty years"; a wife said she was fascinated by how her
  husband's mind worked and "never had any idea he thought like that."
- **Minor stimulus, major response.** Every marriage contains a system of automatic emotional
  responses operating largely out of awareness: "minor emotional stimuli in one that trigger major
  emotional responses in the other," mostly visual or auditory. Negative examples given verbatim:
  "that look of hatred in her eyes," "that cold icy stare," "that sneer of contempt," "that awful
  snarl in his voice." A positive example: a husband so dependent on a certain smile that he spent
  "a sizable segment of his life" trying to evoke it, while she was generally turned off by his
  efforts.
- **A stranger inserted into a triangle is either absorbed or expelled.** Put a stranger into the
  father-mother-child triangle in the child's place: "after a brief time he will either become
  programmed into the familiar patterns of the triangle, or he will withdraw — also a predictable
  response to triangles."
- **Families learn by watching other families.** In the multiple-family setting, "if one family made
  a breakthrough in an area, within a week or two other spouses would be trying some version of
  that in their own families."
- **Multiple-family families progressed faster.** Estimated at about 50 percent faster than
  comparable clinical problems in other families.
- **Monthly appointments did not slow families down.** Families seen monthly made as much, "and
  possibly even more," progress than equivalent families in multiple family groups meeting weekly.
- **Mental health professionals resist knowing their extended families.** "Almost all people in the
  mental health professions" have such deep resistance to learning about their extended families
  that "they literally have to force themselves to work at it" — and the resistance keeps operating
  even while they are searching out data and visiting distant relatives. Bowen calls it an
  "emotional revulsion" that "operates to deny them meaningful contact with the past."
- **[p2] Parallel individual therapists make a family problem undefinable.** First year of the ward
  project: "with parents and patients in individual psychotherapy with separate therapists, family
  problems were diffuse and compartmentalized, and difficult to define for research or therapy."
  All individual psychotherapy was then discontinued. Two professional channels into one family did
  not add information; they destroyed the definition of the problem. Pass 1 missed this entirely.
- **[p2] An open channel does its work without being used.** Family members were free to read any
  record about them and to attend any clinical, administrative or research meeting. "Initially there
  was a large family attendance, but the families did not have the time or the interest to continue
  the meetings, or to consistently send family delegates… **What was important was that the system
  was open and they could attend if they wished.**" Availability, not traffic, is the operative
  variable — and he credits the open policy with the project surviving its early months.
- **[p2] Urgency arrives in the family that is not in focus.** The planned flexible time allocation
  collapsed within weeks: "Too often a silent family would have problems of an urgent nature with
  too little time." Events are exogenous and unforecastable, so the schedule cannot be allocated by
  predicted need.
- **[p2] Attendance regime varies within one course of treatment.** "in some of these families both
  spouses attended sessions regularly; in some large blocks of sessions were with one spouse alone;
  in others the total course was with a single family member." Participation is a per-interval
  property, not a fixed configuration set at intake.

## A2. Mechanisms proposed

- **The triangle** → the basic building block of an emotional system; a system of four or more
  people consists of a series of interlocking triangles. Acts on: any emotional system —
  "whether in a family system, an emotional system at work, a social system, or elsewhere," the
  characteristics are the same. A triangle is "in constant motion with moves that operate
  automatically like emotional reflexes," so predictable "that one can precisely predict the next
  move in the system." Gross behaviour may look random; "beneath the gross behavior is the
  constant, predictable microfunctioning of triangles."
- **Triangle modification via an unabsorbed third person** → the first of two stated ways to change
  a triangle: put two people from a familiar emotional system into contact with a third who knows
  and understands triangles and "who does not play into the emotional moves of the familiar
  twosome." If that third person stays in contact without playing the twosome's familiar game, "the
  functioning in the twosome will automatically be modified." Acts on: the twosome's relationship.
- **Triangle modification via one member** → the second way: if one member of a triangle changes,
  the triangle predictably changes; if one triangle changes, an entire extended family can change.
  Acts on: the whole extended family, through one motivated person, provided that person has
  "sufficient dedication and life energy to work toward his goal in spite of all obstacles." The
  change worked for is "to change and improve self, which then secondarily affects the others."
- **Contact requirement on propagation** → change in a triangle propagates to the larger system only
  if "the members of that triangle stay in emotional contact with the larger system" / "if that
  triangle can stay in meaningful emotional contact with the others." Without contact, no
  propagation.
- **Triangling of the therapist** → each spouse will predictably use the mechanisms each is most
  adept at to involve the therapist in the family emotional system; "the first move is usually to
  cause the therapist to take sides with one or the other." Critically: "the therapist is as
  effectively triangled when angry as he is if charmed." Being pulled to either valence is the same
  capture.
- **Automatic resolution in the presence of a detriangled third** → stated as a basic principle:
  "the emotional problem between two people will resolve automatically if they remain in contact
  with a third person who can remain free of the emotional field between them, while actively
  relating to each." Two conditions, both required: free of the field, *and* actively relating.
- **Taboo-topic accumulation** → each spouse learns what makes the other anxious and avoids it to
  reduce discomfort in self; the avoided set grows monotonically over the marriage. Acts on: the
  couple's communication surface.
- **Emotional responsiveness / trigger-stimulus circuit** → a low-amplitude cue in one (a look, a
  tone, a gesture) drives a high-amplitude response in the other, out of awareness, on any of the
  five senses but mostly visual and auditory. Described as "part of the emotional interdependence,
  and also as part of the family emotional process." Mechanism of change: "often the careful
  definition of the mechanism is enough to defuse it."
- **Defining defuses.** Making a mechanism explicit reduces its power — the wife-slugging husband
  never hit her again after the stimulus ("that look of hatred in her eyes") was identified, and
  *both* parties then gained control over their end of it (he avoided looking at her face when
  tension was high; "she also had some control over 'the look'").
- **Alternating differentiation** → one spouse begins to focus on self while the other pleads for
  togetherness; the differentiating one commonly yields to the togetherness pressure at least once
  before proceeding on a self-determined course despite opposition; this produces "a brief emotional
  reaction in the other," after which "they both arrive at a new and slightly higher basic level of
  differentiation"; a calm period follows; then the roles reverse and the other spouse
  differentiates while the former applies togetherness pressure. "Thus, differentiation proceeds in
  small alternating steps."
- **Extended-family spillover** → each new differentiation step "stirs emotional disharmony in
  extended families and other interlocking emotional systems, which is generally easier to handle
  than the disharmony between the spouses." Displacing the reaction outward is a relief valve, and
  one that is *easier* to manage.
- **Family-of-origin work bypasses the alternation** → when a motivated spouse succeeds at
  differentiating a self in his family of origin, "the total process proceeds more rapidly without
  the alternating pattern that occurs when there is less attention to families of origin."
- **Undifferentiated family ego mass / merging** → separate families placed in one room can
  "emotionally merg[e] into one large undifferentiated family ego mass which could inundate the
  family process in each family." Emotional separateness between families is a precondition for
  work on the nuances of emotional interdependence *within* a marriage.
- **Observational learning across families** → "Apparently, it is easier to really see and know your
  own problem when you watch it in other people than when you only know about it in relation to
  yourself. Families learn from each other." This is the chapter's distinctive transmission path:
  it acts family-to-family, without any relationship tie between the families, and it moves both
  *problem recognition* and *solution technique* (a breakthrough in one family reappears as a
  variant in others within a week or two).
- **Dependence-suppression by appointment spacing** → the stated best explanation for the success
  of monthly sessions: "families are more on their own, made to be more resourceful and less
  dependent on therapy to provide working solutions."
- **Calendar-time-limited change** → "it takes a certain amount of time on the calendar for families
  to change, and the length of time necessary for change is not decreased by increasing the
  frequency of appointments." Change is rate-limited by elapsed time, not by session count.
  **[p2] Note the epistemic status:** this is introduced as "my *conviction*", which the monthly
  result "also fits with" — a prior confirmed, not a finding derived. And he brackets the term
  immediately: "(Change here refers to change as it is considered at this Center, and not to
  superficial manifestations of change.)"
- **[p2] Triangling moves are drawn from a per-person practised repertoire.** "each spouse will use
  mechanisms with which each is most familiar and at which each is most adept to involve the
  therapist." Not a common move set with a shared propensity — a per-person distribution over
  capture techniques, weighted by practice.
- **[p2] Channels are always present; flow selects the open one.** Correction to pass 1's causal
  wording. The four co-therapists were present from the start of the second year, so the
  therapist-difference channel was not *created* by silencing the ward staff; it became the used
  one. The structure does not build channels, it closes them.
- **[p2] Permanence, not marriage, is the named variable behind relationship closing.** Bowen's
  contrast class is "courtship, common-law relationships, or other fairly intimate relationships
  **that are not permanent**." Taboo accumulation is indexed to permanence of the tie.

## A3. Predictive claims

Stated as conditionals; evidence status flagged.

1. **If the third person in a triangle stays in contact with the twosome without playing their
   familiar game, then the twosome's functioning will automatically be modified.** — Asserted as
   principle; supported by the general clinical record, no controlled evidence.
2. **If a stranger is placed in a triangle's third position, then he will either be programmed into
   the triangle's patterns or withdraw.** — Asserted as predictable; presented as observation.
3. **If the parents can define and modify their relationship, then the children's problems will
   automatically disappear.** — Stated as Bowen's opening conviction to new parents; evidence
   offered: after removing the child, "most of the children became free of symptoms" and the average
   bad result exceeded the former good results (~25 prior cases as the comparison).
4. **If one member of a triangle changes, then the triangle will predictably change; and if one
   triangle changes, then an entire extended family can change** — conditional on the changing
   member having "sufficient dedication and life energy" and on the triangle staying in meaningful
   emotional contact with the rest. — Asserted; no counted evidence in this chapter.
5. **If two people in conflict remain in contact with a third who stays free of the emotional field
   while actively relating to each, then the emotional problem between them will resolve
   automatically.** — Asserted as "a basic principle."
6. **If the therapist becomes silent and cannot think of a response, then he is too emotionally
   involved.** — Asserted as a diagnostic rule; note it is a *test of the therapist's state*
   inferred from an observable behaviour.
7. **If the therapist is already emotionally involved, then his attempt at a reversal will be heard
   as sarcastic and mean.** — Asserted from experience.
8. **If the therapist tries to instruct or direct a family away from an unprofitable direction while
   family tension is moderately high, then he is vulnerable to being triangled** — specifically, each
   spouse interprets the communication differently, they debate it at home, and return to ask the
   therapist for the correct interpretation. — Asserted from experience; the mechanism is spelled
   out step by step.
9. **If one family member can calmly state his own convictions and act on them without criticising
   others' beliefs and without entering emotional debate, then other family members will start the
   same process of becoming more sure of self and more accepting of others.** — Asserted.
10. **If the therapist defines himself more clearly in relation to the families, then it becomes
    easier for family members to define themselves to each other.** — Asserted (a monotone claim,
    "the more … the easier").
11. **If one spouse moves toward differentiation, then the other will apply togetherness pressure,
    and the mover will typically yield at least once before proceeding.** — Asserted as
    characteristic; described as "common."
12. **If a differentiation step is completed against togetherness opposition, then there is a brief
    emotional reaction in the other, followed by a new and slightly higher basic level of
    differentiation for both, followed by a fairly calm period.** — Asserted as the standard
    sequence.
13. **If attention is paid to families of origin, then the total process proceeds more rapidly and
    without the alternating pattern.** — Asserted from experience.
14. **If the opposing, togetherness-demanding spouse can generate enough momentum at a nodal point
    to overcome the positive forces in the other, then abrupt termination occurs.** — Evidence
    offered: "over twenty-five families" in which a togetherness-oriented husband suddenly took a
    business assignment in another city or overseas at such a point, ending therapy.
15. **If therapy terminates abruptly after fairly brief work, then the cause is often the
    therapist's emotional involvement in the family emotional system.** — Asserted; makes the
    therapist, not the family, the default suspect for early dropout.
16. **If the family is well motivated and the therapist has stayed relatively out of the family
    emotional system, then the family will find more and more to work on and to resolve.** —
    Asserted.
17. **If families in the same room are not kept emotionally separate, then they will merge into one
    large undifferentiated family ego mass that inundates each family's own process.** — Asserted
    as the risk the design guards against; the elaborate precautions are the evidence of how
    seriously it was taken.
18. **If one family makes a breakthrough, then within a week or two other spouses will try some
    version of it in their own families.** — Reported observation from the multiple-family group.
19. **If appointment frequency is increased, then the calendar time required for change is not
    decreased.** — Asserted as conviction, supported by the monthly-vs-weekly comparison.
20. **If a family's ingrained life patterns are intense enough and its initial basic level of
    differentiation low enough, then it "will never be able to change significantly."** — Asserted;
    an explicit ceiling claim, no numbers attached to the boundary.
21. **If a person does well at searching out and getting to know people in his extended family, then
    he tends to do well at differentiation of a self in family psychotherapy.** — Explicitly hedged:
    "there is some indirect evidence."
22. **If the therapist positions himself as a healer and the family waits passively for magic, then
    something subtly worse happens than if the family is put in position to accept responsibility
    for its own change.** — Asserted; the operational corollary is that research families did better
    than families seen only for psychotherapy.
23. **[p2] If a family is well motivated *and* the therapist has stayed relatively out of the family
    emotional system, then the family will find more and more to work on.** — Recorded separately
    from #16 because the conjunction is load-bearing: both conditions sit in one sentence, so coach
    outside-ness is a *gate* on family motivation, not an additive contribution and not a cap on
    attainment (pass 1's M said "cap").
24. **[p2] If two professional channels operate on one family in parallel, then the family problem
    becomes "diffuse and compartmentalized, and difficult to define."** — Reported as the reason all
    individual psychotherapy was discontinued at the start of the ward project's second year.
25. **[p2] If the therapist has the right degree of emotional distance–emotional contact, then "it is
    almost automatic for him to say and do the right thing."** — The positive form of #6, stated in
    the same sentence pair; the right position makes the right action automatic, which is why no one
    "can tell a therapist what to say."

## A5. Terminology

- **"family psychotherapy"** — used in the original research to name the modality *and* to
  "emphasize and complement the companion theoretical concept which considered 'the family as the
  unit of illness'," and to mark a "clear delineation between this and conventional group therapy."
  He notes the underlying method in that early period actually *was* group therapy; the name was
  doing conceptual work the technique had not yet caught up to.
- **"multiple family-therapeutic milieu network therapy"** — his retrospective, more accurate name
  for the early open-system ward meetings that included all families and all available staff.
- **"the family as the unit of illness"** — the theoretical concept the modality name was chosen to
  complement.
- **"triangle"** — "the basic building block of an emotional system"; a system of four or more
  consists of "a series of interlocking triangles"; in constant motion, moves operating
  "automatically like emotional reflexes," precisely predictable. Not restricted to families —
  identical characteristics in work, social, and other systems.
- **"triangled"** — being drawn into the emotional system; explicitly symmetrical in valence ("as
  effectively triangled when angry as … if charmed").
- **"detriangled" / "keeping self detriangled"** — the therapist's second main function; staying
  relatively outside the family emotional system while remaining in contact.
- **"family"** (operational, for therapy purposes) — redefined here as "the two most responsible
  family members (both spouses) with the therapist as a potential triangle person." This is a
  *technical* definition of family, not a kinship one, and it is the load-bearing move of the whole
  method.
- **"potential triangle person"** — the therapist's structural position: the third seat of the
  triangle, occupied but deliberately not activated.
- **"family psychotherapy with one family member"** — teaching the motivated person the predictable
  characteristics of triangles and emotional systems, then "coaching and supervising his efforts"
  as he returns to his family to observe, learn, and gain control over his own emotional
  reactiveness. Usable when one spouse is motivated and the other antagonistic, or with young
  unmarried adults.
- **"I position"** — calmly stating one's own convictions and beliefs and acting on them, without
  criticising others' beliefs and without becoming involved in emotional debate. Used by the
  therapist as an operating position, and named as one of his four functions
  ("Demonstrating differentiation by taking 'I position' stands").
- **"reversal"** — a named technical move: "a comment that focuses on the unobvious or the opposite
  side of an issue, or that picks up the casual or slightly humorous aspect," used to decompress an
  overserious situation.
- **"differentiation of self"** — the goal state; measured against a family's "initial basic level
  of differentiation of self," which can be raised in steps to "a new and slightly higher basic
  level."
- **"ego fusion or undifferentiation"** — the starting condition: "the average family starts with a
  significant degree of ego fusion or undifferentiation."
- **"undifferentiated family ego mass"** — used here across *unrelated* families: the risk that
  several families in one room merge into "one large undifferentiated family ego mass." The term is
  doing a job here (inter-family merging) that it normally does within a single family.
- **"emotional interdependence"** — the frame under which the minor-stimulus/major-response circuits
  are filed; also called "part of the family emotional process."
- **"emotional system"** — the general object; includes families, workplaces, social systems.
- **"nodal points" / "nodal anxiety periods"** — the points of change at which the
  togetherness-oriented spouse becomes negative and disenchanted, and at which abrupt termination
  can occur.
- **"togetherness"** — the opposing force to differentiation, exerted by the non-moving spouse
  ("pleads for togetherness," "togetherness pressure," "togetherness-oriented spouse").
- **"silent auditors"** — the other families' role while one family is in focus. Distinguished from
  participants and from silent *observers* (the ward staff's role).
  **[p2] CORRECTION — the distinction is not maintained.** In the multiple-family section the other
  families are called "silent **observers**": "The focus was to be on the emotional interdependence
  between the spouses in each family, with the other families as silent observers." Bowen uses the
  two words interchangeably for the non-focal role; pass 1 built a terminological contrast that is
  not in the text.
- **[p2] "clean" session** — a named mode, in scare quotes: "There are occasional 'clean' sessions,
  in which the therapist does nothing more than direct questions from one to the other." The
  degenerate case of the method, where the coach emits only routing and no content.
- **[p2] "thinking-feeling systems"** — worth re-flagging as the chapter's only compound name for the
  per-person internal state, and it is the object said to drift unobserved between sessions.
- **[p2] "undifferentiated family ego mass" — a chronological anomaly.** Ch05 (~1961) already
  reports the term discarded, and Ch17 abandons it outright as conceptually inaccurate. Here, at
  ≥1971, it is used without apology — and used for *unrelated* families merging, not for one family.
- **"research family"** — a family positioned to study itself rather than be healed; "I have worked
  to make every family into a research family."
- **"psychotherapy"** — explicitly disavowed at the close: "it is inaccurate to refer to the clinical
  method described here as 'psychotherapy.' I would like to drop the entire concept … but there is
  no accurate and acceptable word to replace it."
- **"thinking-feeling systems"** — used once, of what can change in a family between sessions
  without the therapist's awareness.
- Note on a term doing another term's job: **"defining and clarifying the relationship"** is the
  chapter's name for what is elsewhere called making the emotional process visible; and
  **"defocus the content"** paired with **"stay focused on process"** is the operational form of the
  therapist's neutrality.

## A6. Structure and roles

- **The two spouses are the operative unit.** "Practically, the two spouses are usually the only
  ones who are important enough to the rest of the family and who have the motivation and dedication
  for this kind of an effort." Theory allows any triangle; practice selects this one.
- **The therapist occupies the child's structural seat.** Explicitly: "Put a family therapist with
  knowledge of triangles into the triangle **in the place of the child**." The therapist is not an
  outside observer of the triangle; he is a substitute for a family position.
- **The child is removed from the room.** Since 1960 children are not part of family psychotherapy
  sessions, though occasionally seen for special reasons. The symptom-bearer is deliberately absent
  from the treatment of his own symptom.
- **The symptom-bearer is variable.** The starting undifferentiation "has evolved over the years to
  the point of acute dysfunction in a spouse (usually emotional illness, somatic illness, or social
  dysfunction such as drinking), to marital disharmony and conflict, or to a behavior problem or
  life failure problem in a child." Three destinations for the same underlying quantity.
- **One spouse moves first, and they alternate.** "Characteristically, one spouse begins to focus on
  self while the other pleads for togetherness"; later the roles reverse. Bowen does not say which
  spouse moves first or what determines it.
- **The togetherness-opposing spouse is the termination risk.** He is the one who becomes negative
  and disenchanted at nodal points; in the >25 abrupt terminations the opposer was specifically the
  **husband**, and the exit mechanism was geographic (a business assignment in another city or
  overseas).
- **One person can carry the whole family.** "An entire family can be changed through the effort of
  one person" — the motivated member, coached, returning to his own family.
- **Parental roles in the old child-focused format:** the tracked variables were the mother's
  "domination" and the father's "passivity" — both of which decreased in the "good" outcome that
  Bowen nonetheless judged shallow.
- **Seating encodes structure.** Families sit in a semicircle facing the therapist; research
  observers at the back; "each husband and wife sat beside each other, slightly separated from the
  next two spouses." Physical separation between couples enacts the required emotional separation.
- **Roles among therapists.** Four co-therapists with equal voice produced interruption and a new
  externalisation channel; the fix was "a single therapist … in charge of each session," able to ask
  another for an opinion, with others intervening only for compelling reason.
- **Extended family and family of origin are a distinct arena.** Each differentiation step stirs
  disharmony there; the therapist coaches spouses into that arena "as early as possible."
- **[p2] The group setting adds no technique.** Stated flatly: "the principles and techniques of
  working with families in a multiple family setting are **exactly the same** as working with a
  single family." The group contributes an observation channel and a raised triangling hazard —
  nothing in the therapist's repertoire changes.
- **[p2] Which spouse moves first is left open — a deliberate non-answer against C5.** Four earlier
  chapters (01, 02, 03, 04) say the peripheral or inadequate member moves first, and Ch04 makes it a
  hard precondition. Ch11 says only "**Characteristically, one spouse begins to focus on self while
  the other pleads for togetherness**" and never names the determinant. It neither corroborates nor
  contradicts C5; it drops the question.
- **[p2] The professional field is a structural position too.** Two individual therapists on one
  family diffuse the problem; four co-therapists with equal voice open a channel; one therapist in
  charge closes it. The professional side of the system has its own topology with its own effects,
  independent of what any professional intends.

# STREAM B — How to do therapy  (→ the coaching agent)

## B1. What the therapist does

**The four stated main functions**, verbatim: (1) Defining and clarifying the relationship between
the spouses; (2) Keeping self detriangled from the family emotional system; (3) Teaching the
functioning of emotional systems; (4) Demonstrating differentiation by taking "I position" stands.

Operational actions:

- **See the parents alone in the first interview and state the premise.** State the conviction that
  the basic problem lies in the relationship between the parents, and that if the parents can define
  and modify their relationship the children's problems will automatically disappear. Get agreement
  to that working premise before proceeding.
- **Control the interchange.** All talk routes through the therapist: "Each spouse talks directly to
  me in the calmest, low-keyed, most objective possible way."
- **Open with a progress question to the husband.** "I open by asking the husband what kind of
  progress he has made since the last session, and ask him to give me his most objective report."
- **Then ask the wife for her thoughts while he was talking.** If his report has reasonable content,
  turn to her: "What was going on in your thoughts while she was talking?" is the mirrored form when
  turning back to him.
- **Grade the intimacy of the question by phase.** Early: ask for the intellectual process —
  thoughts, opinions, ideas. Middle: ask for "response or reaction," which is "a little less
  intellectual." Only "much later in therapy, and in special situations" ask for a reading from
  subjective inner feelings.
- **Elaborate a minimal answer before switching speakers.** If the husband's comments are too minimal
  for the wife to respond to, ask enough questions to get him to elaborate first; same in reverse.
- **Raise question tempo when affect rises.** "If feelings build up and one responds emotionally and
  directly to the other without waiting for my questions, I increase the directness and tempo of the
  questions so as to return the process to me."
- **Keep a deep question queue.** "I am always dozens of questions ahead of them. There is always a
  backlog of theoretical assumptions about the family about which I have questions." During a lull,
  make notes about new areas for questions.
- **Convert expression into description.** "When obvious feelings are stirred up during a session,
  the goal is to get them talking *about* the feeling, rather than expressing it." Concretely: if
  the wife becomes tearful, ask the husband whether he noticed the tears, or what was going on in
  his thoughts when he saw them.
- **Target emotionally important areas but require calm answers.** "An over-all goal for the
  questions is to touch on areas known to be emotionally important for them, and to get calm,
  low-keyed responses."
- **Hunt trigger stimuli explicitly.** Define the automatic emotional response mechanisms "in as
  much detail as possible," and train the spouses to become better observers so they can define more
  of them. Persist across sessions — the wife-slugging stimulus was found only after several failed
  attempts.
- **Calibrate emotional distance deliberately.** "I attempt to back out emotionally to the point
  where I can watch the ebb and flow of the emotional process while always 'thinking process,' and
  without getting caught in the flow." The stated calibration target: "The 'right' point for me is
  one between seriousness and humor, when I can make either a serious or a humorous response."
- **Keep talking, especially in response to a triangling move.** Silence is the failure signal.
- **Use casual comments as signalling.** "Casual comments are effective messages that he is not
  overinvolved."
- **Use the reversal.** Comment on the unobvious or opposite side, or pick up the casual/humorous
  aspect. Worked example: to a wife tightening up about her dominating, nagging mother, he made "a
  casual comment about her lack of appreciation for her mother's lifelong effort to make her be a
  good daughter"; the spouses laughed, tension decompressed, and he had communicated that there was
  another side.
- **Stay on process, defocus content.** Stated as essential.
- **Time teaching to the anxiety level, and change its form by phase.** Communicate "in a neutral way
  that is not perceived as authoritative, and at a time when family anxiety is lowest." Early, when
  anxiety is high → put instruction in "I position" terms. Later, when anxiety is lower → teach "by
  parables, illustrated by successful clinical solutions of similar problems in other families."
  Still later, when there is little anxiety → didactic teaching works.
- **Take I positions.** Define self clearly in relation to the families; the therapist is also free
  to exercise his I position "to define his part in the effort."
- **Challenge to maximum effort, and carry them through predictable anxiety.** "The therapist makes a
  continuing effort to challenge them to maximum effort, and to help them through the predictable
  episodes of anxiety that occur."
- **Coach family-of-origin differentiation as early as possible.**
- **Make every family a research family.** Get the therapist out of the healing/helping position and
  the family into the position of accepting responsibility for its own change.
- **Work continuously on his own differentiation** — from his own family's emotional system and from
  the emotional system in which he works. Stated as *essential*, not optional, for clinical
  competence.

For the multiple-family setting specifically:

- **Designate the session's focal family; the others audit in silence.**
- **Check every family every week — do not skip any family in any session.** "Too much could take
  place in their thinking-feeling systems in that period of time without the therapist's awareness."
- **Divide available time about equally between families**, despite an original plan to allocate
  flexibly.
- **Cap the group at four families**; five makes the schedule "too rushed and pressured."
- **Cap session length near two hours** — the observed attention limit before fatigue. (One and a
  half hours of therapy plus a half-hour research summary.)
- **Explain the purpose and technique to each family and obtain agreement** to the separation rules
  before starting.
- **Recruit for matched intensity**: about three or four new families "each with neurotic level
  problems of about the same intensity," motivated, not severely impaired or fragmented, and unknown
  to each other beforehand.
- **Seat couples adjacently but separated from the next couple**; families in a semicircle facing
  the therapist; observers at the back.
- **Space appointments widely.** Bowen moved from weekly to monthly, then settled his whole practice
  at once every two weeks, with an increasing number monthly and a small experimental group at once
  every three months.
- **[p2] Gate the hand-off on content adequacy.** The turn only passes to the other spouse "**if his
  report has reasonable content**"; otherwise elaborate first. The routing rule has a quality test on
  the payload, not just a turn counter.
- **[p2] Divide time equally *because* urgency is unforecastable.** The stated reason the flexible
  plan was abandoned is that "too often a silent family would have problems of an urgent nature with
  too little time" — not fairness.
- **[p2] Keep the channel open even if it goes unused.** The ward's open-records / attend-any-meeting
  policy kept working after the families stopped attending. The coach action is to make a channel
  available, and availability is the effective quantity.

## B2. What the therapist must NOT do

- **Do not let spouses talk to each other.** "After about 1962, I stopped suggesting that they talk
  directly to each other in the family therapy sessions." And earlier: "Very early in my family
  psychotherapy I discouraged spouses from attempting to talk more at home."
- **Do not direct therapy at feelings.** Do not aim at the "therapeutic" expression of feelings —
  results are worse than with the calm, intellectual, conceptual approach, and free expression comes
  faster as a by-product than when pursued directly.
- **Do not include the problem child in the sessions** (since 1960; occasional special-reason
  exceptions only).
- **Do not take sides** — and note that anger is not a safe alternative to being charmed: "the
  therapist is as effectively triangled when angry as he is if charmed."
- **Do not go silent.** Silence in response to a triangling move is itself the symptom of
  overinvolvement.
- **Do not answer the request to adjudicate an interpretation.** When spouses have debated a teaching
  point at home and return to ask the therapist for the correct interpretation, "the therapist's goal
  is to detriangle himself rather than explain, because that would involve him more deeply in the
  family system."
- **Do not instruct or direct while family tension is moderately high** — that is precisely when the
  attempt gets him triangled. Teach at the anxiety minimum.
- **Do not deliver teaching in an authoritative register.** Communication must be "in a neutral way
  that is not perceived as authoritative."
- **Do not attempt a reversal while already emotionally involved** — "his effort to reverse the
  emotional process will be heard as sarcastic and mean."
- **Do not focus on content.** "It is essential that he always stay focused on process, and that he
  defocus the content of what is being said."
- **Do not occupy the healer position.** Do not let the family function passively "waiting for the
  therapist to work his magic."
- **Do not criticise others' beliefs or enter emotional debate when taking an I position.**
- **Do not sit too close or too far emotionally.** Too close → caught in the seriousness; too distant
  → not effectively in contact.
- **Do not let families in a multiple group have social contact outside sessions**, and if they later
  discover mutual friends, they are not to mention the other families in any social contact.
- **Do not skip a family in a session.**
- **Do not run five or more families in this format.**
- **Do not exceed roughly two hours.**
- **Do not let multiple co-therapists share the floor.** (Historical lesson: multiple active
  therapists produced interruptions, therapist-to-therapist definitional wrangling, and a new channel
  for the families to externalise anxiety.)
- **Do not let non-therapist staff participate in the sessions.** (Historical lesson: it gave families
  a route to externalise intrafamily anxiety into conflict with ward staff.)
- **Do not let any family member interrupt another family's work.** (Historical lesson: unrestricted
  cross-family speech made it "extremely difficult to keep the issue in a single family in focus.")
- **Do not assume more frequent appointments help.** Increasing frequency does not shorten the
  calendar time required for change.
- **[p2] Do not run two professional channels into one family in parallel.** Individual
  psychotherapy for parents and patients with separate therapists made family problems "diffuse and
  compartmentalized"; it was discontinued and never reinstated.
- **[p2] Do not let the group format change the technique.** The multiple-family principles and
  techniques are "exactly the same"; the temptation to teach the group as a group is what finding
  (1) refuted.

## B3. Sequencing and preconditions

- **Precondition on the therapist:** he must be working continuously on differentiating self from
  his own family's emotional system and from his work system. Without it, staying outside the
  family's system in clinical work is not available to him. Knowledge of triangles is named as "the
  most effective way I know" of keeping self in meaningful contact without becoming overinvolved.
- **Precondition on entry:** parents seen alone first; premise stated; agreement obtained. Only then
  does the two-spouse work begin.
- **Question depth is gated by phase:** thoughts/opinions/ideas → response/reaction → subjective inner
  feelings (only much later, and in special situations).
- **Teaching mode is gated by anxiety level:** high anxiety (early) → I-position statements only;
  lower anxiety → parables drawn from other families' successful solutions; little anxiety → didactic
  instruction.
- **[p2] CORRECTION on the phase scheme below.** Bowen writes only "The therapy proceeds through
  several distinct phases" and names exactly **one**: "One of the significant early phases is the
  stage through which they each come to 'know' each other better." The numbering below is pass 1's
  construction, and item (3) is positively wrong as an *ordering* — family-of-origin coaching begins
  "as early as possible," concurrent with (1) and (2), not after them. Read (1)–(3) as three
  concurrent processes with one stated start-order constraint, not as stages.
- **Phases of therapy:** (1) an early phase in which the spouses come to "know" each other better —
  "slow and gradual" in some, "rapid and almost exhilarating" in others, and sufficient that some
  want to terminate on symptom relief and increased togetherness alone. (2) Gradual differentiation
  of a self from the other spouse, proceeding in small alternating steps with a togetherness-pressure
  yield, a self-determined step, a brief emotional reaction in the other, a new slightly higher basic
  level, and a calm period — then role reversal. (3) Family-of-origin differentiation work, begun by
  the therapist "as early as possible," which speeds the whole process and removes the alternation.
- **Termination criteria (orderly):** both have achieved a reasonable level of differentiation of
  self from each other *and* from their families of origin; they know enough about family systems
  that at least one of them "has developed the capacity to handle crises"; and they have "some kind
  of reasonable plan and motivation to continue working toward differentiation in the years ahead."
  Note that only *one* of them needs the crisis capacity.
- **The family may stop at symptom relief** — that is an available and legitimate stopping point,
  distinct from the deeper resolution. "They are free to stop at any point."
- **Multiple-family setup order:** develop the method for two years → find enough families of matched
  intensity applying at about the same time → interview them → obtain agreement to participate and to
  the social-separation rules → agree a research plan for observation and recording → start.
- **Time course:** the average motivated upper-middle-class family continues about four years, at
  either monthly or twice-monthly frequency. Change is rate-limited by calendar time.

## B4. Stated failure modes

- **The unrestricted open group loses focus.** With everyone free to speak, the therapist could not
  work through to a defined intrafamily issue. Cause of the drift: anxious members of other families
  interrupting at the moment of definition. Fix: designated family + silent auditors.
- **Any open channel becomes an anxiety drain.** Ward staff participation → conflict with staff.
  Multiple active therapists → differences between therapists. Each channel closed produced the next
  displacement. Implication: the therapy structure determines *where* the anxiety goes, not whether it
  exists.
- **The honeymoon.** Opening a closed system produces symptom decrease and enthusiasm that ends after
  about a year, leaving meetings "repetitive and less productive." Early improvement is not evidence
  of change.
- **The child-focused session.** The parents' detail about the child's problem crowds out the parental
  relationship; the family terminates delighted with a result Bowen judges shallow.
- **Talking it out.** Spouses' attempts to communicate more "only stirs up emotional reactiveness and
  drives them farther apart."
- **The therapist gets triangled** — first move usually a bid to take sides; anger and charm are
  equally effective captures. Bowen states that in the multiple-family setting it was *even easier*
  to be triangled than with single families — one of the two findings he had not accurately
  predicted.
- **The therapist goes silent** — the marker of being too emotionally involved.
- **The reversal misfires** — heard as sarcastic and mean when the therapist is already involved.
- **Teaching at high tension backfires** — divergent interpretation, home debate, return demand for
  adjudication, deeper triangling.
- **The togetherness spouse becomes negative and disenchanted at nodal points.** Usually manageable
  ("usually fairly easy to help them through these nodal anxiety periods") — but can escalate.
- **Abrupt termination at a nodal point** when the opposing spouse's momentum overcomes the mover's:
  documented in over twenty-five families via a sudden out-of-town or overseas business assignment.
- **Abrupt termination after brief therapy is often the therapist's fault** — his emotional
  involvement in the family emotional system.
- **Families merging** into one large undifferentiated family ego mass, inundating each family's own
  process — the explicit risk the separation rules exist to prevent.
- **Recruitment failure** for multiple family therapy: the good candidates were referred for
  individual psychotherapy, and those referred to Bowen were "too severely impaired and fragmented
  and too poorly motivated"; saving families up in private practice failed because referrals did not
  arrive close enough together in time. Several years of unsuccessful attempts.
- **The teaching-efficiency premise failed.** The stated motive for multiple family therapy — saving
  time by teaching the same principles to many families at once — did not hold: "The larger group did
  not facilitate advantageous use of teaching time."
- **Skipping a family** lets too much happen in their thinking-feeling systems without the therapist's
  awareness.
- **Resistance to extended-family contact** in mental health professionals — an "emotional revulsion"
  that keeps working against them even while they are doing the work, denying them "meaningful contact
  with the past."
- **A hard ceiling.** Some families "will never be able to change significantly," because of the
  intensity of their ingrained life patterns and their initial basic level of differentiation.
- **[p2] Parallel individual therapy destroys problem definition** — year one of the ward project.
  The failure mode is not that the treatment was weak but that the *problem became undefinable*
  ("diffuse and compartmentalized… difficult to define for research or therapy").
- **[p2] Three distinct termination mechanisms, which pass 1 did not separate.** (a) *Satisfaction* —
  "so pleased with the decrease in symptoms and increase in togetherness in the marriage that they
  are ready to terminate," early, legitimate, and explicitly permitted ("They are free to stop at any
  point"). (b) *Opposer momentum* — the togetherness spouse overcomes the mover at a nodal point;
  >25 cases, exit by out-of-town or overseas assignment. (c) *Coach involvement* — "abrupt
  termination after fairly brief therapy is often the result of the therapist's emotional
  involvement." Different triggers, different timing, different predictors. Only (b) and (c) are
  failures.

## B5. What the technique assumes about the family

*Each rule of technique, converted into the claim about family functioning it presupposes.*

- **Routing all speech through the therapist assumes that direct spouse-to-spouse speech is a carrier
  of emotional reactiveness, not of information.** The content is transmissible; the reactiveness is
  what direct address adds. Implication: a message's effect depends on its *path*, not only its
  content. The same words the wife could not hear from her husband, she can hear when he addresses
  the therapist. → A model needs a channel with a routing attribute, where reactivity is a property of
  the edge, not the payload.
- **Asking each spouse what they *thought* while the other spoke assumes the listener is always
  processing, even while silent, and that the processing is retrievable.** Silence is not absence of
  participation. → Non-speaking members must still be updating state each tick.
- **Grading questions thoughts → reaction → feelings assumes a family's tolerance for interiority
  rises over time and can be overrun.** Asking for feeling too early costs something. → A per-person
  "depth tolerance" that rises with elapsed work.
- **Timing teaching to the anxiety minimum assumes anxiety gates the *reception* of information,
  independently of the information's truth.** The same content is absorbed at low anxiety and becomes
  a triangling weapon at high anxiety. → Information transfer must be multiplied by an anxiety gate.
- **The three teaching registers (I-position → parable → didactic) assume that authority itself is a
  triangling invitation** at moderate anxiety, and only becomes safe once anxiety falls. → Message
  *form* matters as much as message content.
- **"Get them talking about the feeling rather than expressing it" assumes description and expression
  have opposite effects on system tension** — description defuses, expression amplifies. → Two
  distinct move types with opposite sign on the same variable.
- **"Often the careful definition of the mechanism is enough to defuse it" assumes automatic emotional
  circuits depend on being out of awareness to operate.** Naming the trigger removes the reflex — and
  removes it for *both* parties (the husband gained control over his response, and she "also had some
  control over 'the look'"). → Awareness is a state variable that damps a specific stimulus-response
  edge; naming an edge weakens it bilaterally.
- **The taboo-accumulation account assumes the closing of a marriage is driven by discomfort *in the
  avoider*, not by concern for the other.** Each avoids the subject to protect self from the other's
  anxiety. → The avoidance rule is self-referential, so the taboo set only grows.
- **The therapist sitting in the child's seat assumes the third position of the triangle is a slot
  that can be occupied by anyone**, and that the occupant's *behaviour*, not identity, determines
  whether the triangle runs its usual circuit. → The triangle is a structure with a role, not a
  fixed set of persons.
- **"Either become programmed into the familiar patterns or withdraw" assumes a triangle applies
  strong pressure to any occupant of its third seat, with only two stable outcomes for the naive.**
  A third stable outcome exists only for someone who knows triangles. → Occupying a triangle position
  without a "knows-triangles" trait forces one of two absorbing states.
- **"The emotional problem between two people will resolve automatically if they remain in contact
  with a third who can remain free of the field while actively relating to each" assumes conflict in
  a dyad is *sustained by* the availability of a captured third**, and dissipates when no capture is
  available but contact persists. Both conditions are load-bearing: free-of-field alone (withdrawal)
  does not work; relating alone (capture) does not work. → Detriangling requires simultaneously high
  contact and low reactivity — a two-dimensional therapist state, not a scalar.
- **"Keep talking, especially in response to a triangling move" assumes silence is read by the family
  as a position** — it is not neutral, it is a message, and one that signals involvement. → No null
  action; every therapist tick emits a signal.
- **Casual comments and reversals as tension-decompressors assume the family continuously monitors the
  therapist's involvement level and infers it from register.** "The spouses are continually
  misperceiving the therapist's involvement, or lack of it, or misperceiving him to either be for or
  against them." → Each member holds an estimate of therapist alignment, which is noisy and needs
  active correction.
- **"If already involved, the reversal is heard as sarcastic" assumes the family can detect the
  therapist's true internal state through the same utterance** — the same words carry opposite meaning
  depending on the speaker's state. → The receiver decodes sender-state, not just the message.
- **Removing the child assumes the child's symptom is downstream of the parental relationship and has
  no independent maintenance requirement.** Treating the parents alone is not a compromise; it is the
  causally correct site, and the child improves without being present. → A symptom variable on a
  child, driven entirely by a parental-dyad variable, with no direct-intervention pathway.
- **Defining "family" as the two most responsible members plus the therapist assumes change enters a
  family through its highest-functioning, most-committed members** — not through the symptom-bearer,
  and not through the whole group. Two selection criteria are named: importance to the rest of the
  family, and motivation/dedication. → The change agent is selected by (influence × motivation), not
  by distress.
- **"An entire family can be changed through the effort of one person" assumes influence propagates
  through the whole extended network from any node**, subject only to maintained emotional contact and
  the mover's persistence. → Change propagates along contact edges; severed contact blocks propagation
  entirely.
- **Coaching a single member — "change and improve self, which then secondarily affects the others" —
  assumes others' change cannot be a target, only a consequence.** Direct attempts to change others are
  outside the causal model. → A move that acts only on self, whose effect on others is emergent.
- **The alternating differentiation sequence assumes the couple has a shared, conserved togetherness
  budget.** When one moves toward self, the other must supply the pressure back; only after the
  reaction resolves can the other take his turn. Both end at a higher level — so the resolution is not
  zero-sum, but the *transition* is. → A coupled pair of levels with an opposing-force term, a
  yield-then-proceed sequence, and a joint ratchet upward.
- **"It is common for the differentiating one to yield to the togetherness pressure at least once
  before proceeding" assumes a first attempt at differentiation predictably fails,** and that the
  failure is part of the process rather than a defeat. → Model the first attempt as a scripted
  non-success, not a probabilistic one.
- **Family-of-origin work removing the alternation assumes the togetherness pressure is *sourced*
  outside the marriage** and can be discharged there instead of at the spouse. → Differentiation
  pressure has a destination; if the extended-family destination is open, the spouse is spared.
- **"Each new step stirs emotional disharmony in extended families … which is generally easier to
  handle" assumes the extended family is a lower-intensity sink for the same disturbance.** → A
  conserved disturbance quantity that can be routed to lower-intensity edges.
- **The whole history of structural changes to the meetings assumes intrafamily anxiety is conserved
  and will find *whatever* channel the structure leaves open** — staff, therapist disagreements,
  another family's issue. It does not evaporate when a channel closes; it migrates. → Anxiety as a
  routed flow, with the structure defining the available edges.
- **Silent auditing producing more benefit than participating assumes recognition of one's own pattern
  is easier at a remove.** "It is easier to really see and know your own problem when you watch it in
  other people than when you only know about it in relation to yourself." Preparing one's next comment
  actively *blocks* hearing. → An observation channel with higher information gain than the
  participation channel; participation consumes the capacity that observation would use.
- **Cross-family imitation within a week or two assumes solution techniques transmit between families
  with no relationship tie**, purely by observation. → A transmission path with no kinship or
  emotional edge — currently unrepresented.
- **The elaborate separation rules assume that emotional merging between unrelated families is the
  *default* if not actively prevented**, and that the merge destroys the very intra-marital resolution
  the setting is for. Note the asymmetry Bowen designed for: *observation across families is wanted;
  emotional connection across families is not.* → Two distinct inter-family edge types, one
  beneficial and one destructive, which must be separately switchable.
- **Physical seating (couples adjacent, separated from the next couple) assumes proximity itself
  carries emotional coupling.** → Spatial arrangement as a modifier on edge strength.
- **The four-family cap and two-hour limit assume attention is a finite resource that gates the
  observational learning channel.** Beyond it, nothing is absorbed. → An attention budget per session,
  divided across families.
- **"It did not work well to skip any family at any session" assumes families drift unobserved between
  contacts at a rate that makes a week already risky.** → State evolves autonomously between sessions;
  observation must sample often enough to track it.
- **Monthly appointments working as well or better assumes therapy contact substitutes for the
  family's own problem-solving.** More contact → more dependence → less resourcefulness. → Session
  frequency has a *negative* term (dependence) as well as a positive one.
- **"It takes a certain amount of time on the calendar for families to change" assumes change is
  driven by lived experience between sessions, not by session content.** → Change rate is bounded by
  wall-clock time, not by intervention count.
- **Making every family a "research family" assumes the family's stance toward its own process is
  itself a determinant of outcome** — observing self is therapeutic; waiting to be healed is not. →
  A per-family "self-observing" trait affecting change rate.
- **The therapist's own family-of-origin differentiation being *essential* assumes the therapist's
  reactivity is a property he brings in, not one the family generates.** He is a node with his own
  level, and it caps what he can do. → The coach agent needs its own differentiation level, and the
  ceiling on the family's progress is partly the coach's.
- **"The more the therapist can define himself in relation to the families, the easier it is for
  family members to define themselves to each other" assumes differentiation is contagious by
  demonstration.** → An I-position move by any node raises the probability of I-position moves by
  observing nodes — which is the same mechanism as the cross-family imitation, generalised.
- **Orderly termination requiring only *one* of the two to have crisis capacity assumes the couple
  functions as a unit for crisis handling** — one competent node suffices for the pair. → Crisis
  response is a max over the pair, not a mean.
- **"Some families will never be able to change significantly" assumes an immovable floor set by
  initial differentiation level and pattern intensity.** → A per-family change-capacity parameter that
  can be zero.
- **[p2] WITHDRAWN — "the couple has a shared, conserved togetherness budget."** Nothing in the
  alternating-differentiation passage states or implies a conserved quantity. The text says one
  focuses on self while the other pleads for togetherness, then the roles reverse. Pass 1 imported
  the conservation frame from Ch08/Ch09/Ch16. What survives: an opposing-force term, a mandatory
  first-attempt yield, a bilateral ratchet, and role symmetry. Not a budget.
- **[p2] WITHDRAWN — "the extended family is a lower-intensity *sink* for the same disturbance"** and
  "differentiation pressure has a destination; if the extended-family destination is open, the spouse
  is spared." Bowen says only that each step "stirs emotional disharmony in extended families and
  other interlocking emotional systems, which is generally easier to handle than the disharmony
  between the spouses," and separately that attention to families of origin makes the process go
  faster without the alternation. Two bare statements, no displacement claim, no routing, no
  conservation. The mechanism was pass 1's invention.
- **[p2] Discontinuing parallel individual therapy assumes a problem's *definability* is a property
  of the observation topology, not of the problem.** Two independent professional channels made the
  same family's problem "diffuse and compartmentalized." → Whatever the model reads out as the
  family's problem state must depend on how many channels observe it, not only on the underlying
  state. Converges with Ch01's per-hop fidelity finding from the opposite direction.
- **[p2] Keeping records open while nobody reads them assumes an unexercised channel still lowers
  system tension.** "What was important was that the system was open and they could attend if they
  wished." → Channel *availability* is a state variable separate from channel *traffic*, and it is
  the availability that carries the effect. Nothing in the model distinguishes the two.
- **[p2] Abandoning flexible time allocation assumes urgency is exogenous and unforecastable.** "Too
  often a silent family would have problems of an urgent nature with too little time." → Events
  arrive on their own schedule in unobserved units; a need-predicting allocator is provably worse
  than an equal split.
- **[p2] "Exactly the same principles and techniques" assumes the group setting adds no operator.**
  → Whatever the multiple-family configuration buys must come from the topology (observation, coach
  load), never from a new move in the repertoire.

# CROSS-CUTTING

## X1. Contradictions and revisions

- **Feelings: from target to by-product.** From ~1956–1960 he analysed intrapsychic process and
  dreams in each spouse in the other's presence. By 1960 the emphasis shifted to the relationship
  system "with far less emphasis on the intrapsychic process in each."
- **Direct spouse-to-spouse speech: from goal to prohibition.** Around 1960 the goal was that each
  spouse could communicate anything they thought or felt about the other or about self; spouses were
  *encouraged to speak directly to each other rather than to the therapist*, with emphasis on direct
  expression of feeling. After about 1962 he stopped suggesting they talk to each other at all, and
  routed everything through himself. This is a direct reversal of his own prior technique, made
  explicit in the text.
- **"Family psychotherapy" was a misnomer at the start.** He says the basic method during the early
  ward period "was group therapy, although the meetings were called 'family psychotherapy'" to mark a
  delineation from conventional group therapy. He retrospectively renames the period "multiple family
  therapeutic milieu network therapy."
- **The child: from central to excluded.** Originally "all available family members attended the
  psychotherapy sessions." Since 1960, children are not part of sessions.
- **The premise of multiple family therapy was wrong.** He undertook it to save teaching time; finding
  (1) was that "the larger group did not facilitate advantageous use of teaching time," and that
  triangling was *easier* in the group setting. The method survived on a benefit he had not predicted
  (rate of family progress), not on the one he designed for.
- **Session frequency reversed twice.** Two or three appointments per week (late 1950s) → weekly (over
  the following ten years) → monthly forced by scheduling → settled at fortnightly, with monthly
  increasing and a quarterly experiment. The monthly change was adopted only because an external
  scheduling constraint forced the experiment, and he records having had "many reservations."
- **Open-system participation reversed.** The early policy was maximal openness — family members free
  to read any records about them and attend any clinical, administrative or research meeting; all
  staff in the psychotherapy meetings. The successive fixes each *closed* something: staff to silent
  observers, then one therapist in charge, then one family in focus with the rest as silent auditors.
  He credits the initial openness with the project's survival while dismantling most of it.
- **"Psychotherapy" itself is disavowed** in the closing paragraph — the name for the whole enterprise
  is declared inaccurate, with no replacement available.
- **Multiple therapists abandoned.** Four co-therapists with equal voice gave way to a single
  therapist in charge per session.
- **[p2] The paper's opening advertises the one benefit its own research refuted.** Sentence two:
  the method "takes less of the therapist's time than more conventional methods." Finding (1) of the
  research: "The larger group did not facilitate advantageous use of teaching time." He never
  reconciles the two, and the claim in the opening is about the therapist's *total* time across
  families while the finding is about *teaching* time — but the tension stands unaddressed.
- **[p2] Individual psychotherapy abandoned as a parallel modality.** Year one had every mother and
  every patient in individual psychotherapy with separate therapists; after the hypothesis was
  revised to treat schizophrenia as a whole-family process, "all individual psychotherapy was
  discontinued and family psychotherapy became the only treatment modality."
- **[p2] The research hypothesis itself was revised mid-project** — during the first year, "the
  research hypothesis was modified to consider schizophrenia a process that involved the entire
  family." The unit of illness changed while the study was running.

## X2. Hedges and uncertainty

- On the cross-family effect: "**Apparently**, it is easier to really see and know your own problem
  when you watch it in other people…" — and the families' own explanation is quoted rather than
  endorsed ("It is reassuring to know that other people have the same kinds of problems").
- On the rate of progress: "This was **estimated** to be about 50 percent faster."
- On the monthly sessions: "the **best explanation** … **seems to be** that families are more on their
  own" — offered after "considering all the ideas and the explanations offered by the families and
  the observers."
- On the honeymoon: "The conversion of the small closed system operation into an open system
  **probably** accounted for much of this."
- On the extended-family/differentiation correlation: "There is **some indirect evidence** that the
  people who do best at the differentiation of a self … have some of the same qualities of people who
  do well in searching out and getting to know people in their extended families."
- On defining the automatic emotional responses: "They are so numerous that one could **probably spend
  a lifetime and never define all of them**."
- On openness: "Most people **probably** have the most open relationships in their adult lives during
  courtship…"
- On what to say in a triangling moment: "**No one can tell a therapist what to say** in such
  situations."
- On co-therapy: "This is **not a place to evaluate** co-therapists or multiple therapists" — an
  explicit refusal to adjudicate.
- On duration: "the often asked, **impossible question**, 'How long does family therapy take?'"
- On measurement: the research staff "has tried hard to define change in a way that can be measured
  and quantified" — stated as an ongoing problem, not a solved one; and he notes that in professional
  practice "change or improvement is applied to things as elusive as feeling better, or disappearance
  of presenting symptom."
- He twice parenthesises his own meaning of change to distinguish it from the ordinary one: "(Change
  here refers to change as it is considered at this Center, and not to superficial manifestations of
  change.)" and "The 'change' mentioned here is not some superficial change in role or posture."
- On the closing terminology: "there is **no accurate and acceptable word** to replace it."
- **[p2]** On the auditing benefit: "**Some families said** they often obtained more benefit from
  auditing" — attributed self-report from a subset, not a measurement and not Bowen's own finding.
  Pass 1 promoted it to a phenomenon ("Auditing beats participating") and built a capacity-competition
  mechanism on top of it.
- **[p2]** On the fast results: "There have been a number of striking early '**cures**'" — the scare
  quotes are Bowen's, on both occurrences, and they carry the disclaimer.
- **[p2]** On the two "major findings … **had not been accurately predicted**" — his own words, and
  they apply to *both* the failure of the teaching-time premise and the 50 percent result. The
  chapter's most-cited finding is explicitly an accident.

## X3. Unprompted observations

- **The families' own explanation was rejected in favour of a different mechanism.** Asked why they
  progressed faster, families gave the standard group-therapy answer (reassurance that others share
  the problem). Bowen keeps the observation but substitutes his own explanation — recognition of
  one's own pattern is easier at a remove. Two candidate mechanisms for the same effect: *normalising
  comfort* vs. *externalised self-recognition*. They predict different things and could be
  distinguished in a simulation.
- **A therapy structure is an anxiety-routing topology.** The whole history section reads as an
  experiment in closing channels one at a time and watching where the flow goes next. This is the
  chapter's strongest structural idea and it is never stated as theory — only enacted.
- **Symptom relief and structural change are decoupled, and symptom relief can *terminate* structural
  change.** The child-focused format's "good" result was symptom relief plus family satisfaction plus
  no basic change; families in the two-spouse method who are "so pleased with the decrease in
  symptoms and increase in togetherness … are ready to terminate." Being pleased is a stopping
  condition, not a progress signal.
- **Increased togetherness is listed alongside symptom decrease as a *reason families quit* —**
  i.e. the reward the system produces early is the very quantity the therapy is trying to reduce.
- **The exit route in the >25 abrupt terminations was geographic and pre-existing** (a business
  assignment), not an explicit refusal. The system found a face-saving physical distance mechanism.
  Distance is a move available to a member under differentiation pressure.
- **Bowen tracks his *own* emotional position with an explicit calibration heuristic** — a target band
  between seriousness and humour, testable by whether he can produce either kind of response at will,
  and with a hard failure indicator (going silent). This is a self-monitoring loop with an observable
  proxy, and it is directly implementable.
- **Videotape is mentioned as a setting in which he may sit physically close** — physical and
  emotional distance are explicitly decoupled.
- **The research role itself is therapeutic.** "Research families did better in psychotherapy than
  families seen only for psychotherapy" — the observation stance is an intervention, and the whole
  later method is built to induce it in every family.
- **Both first-move directions of the triangle theory are stated but only one is practised.** Theory:
  *any* triangle can be the lever. Practice: the spouses, because of importance-plus-motivation. The
  gap between the theoretical and practical statement is where the model's agent-selection rule lives.
- **A single-session cure is reported without embarrassment alongside a four-year average** — a
  seven-session "cure" of severe frigidity. The distribution of outcomes is extremely wide, and
  Bowen's framing is that fast symptom results and deep change are different variables.
- **The resistance to extended-family contact is described as strongest in mental health
  professionals** — the population most of the research was done on. This is a sampling caveat he
  states without flagging it as one.
- **He names the population for his duration figure: "upper middle class families."** The four-year
  average is explicitly class-scoped.
- **[p2] Availability beats use.** The open-records policy kept its effect after the families stopped
  attending anything. This is the sharpest idea in the chapter that Bowen states in one clause and
  never returns to, and it has no analogue anywhere else in the corpus.
- **[p2] The 50-percent result arrived despite a stated handicap.** The same two-item list reports
  that the therapist was *more* easily triangled in the group setting than with single families.
  Whatever produced the speed-up did so while the coach was functioning worse. Pass 1 recorded both
  findings and never connected them.
- **[p2] The whole method is a sequence of subtractions.** Every structural change he reports removes
  a participant or a degree of freedom: individual therapists out, ward staff silenced, three
  co-therapists silenced, the problem child out, cross-family speech out, spouse-to-spouse speech
  out, appointment frequency down. The only thing ever *added* is other families as observers. A
  model of this coach's repertoire is mostly a set of channel closures.
- **[p2] He records the measurement problem as unsolved in the same paragraph as the 50 percent
  figure.** "The research staff on this project has tried hard to define change in a way that can be
  measured and quantified" — an effort, with no procedure, no instrument and no result reported.

# PASS 2 — COMPARATIVE

## P1. Corrections to pass 1

**1. The headline design claim is half wrong — Bowen did not engineer the setting to maximise
observation.** Pass 1 (A2, B5, M, ledger L11.2) states he built the multiple-family format to
maximise cross-family observation while suppressing emotional connection. The suppression half is
verbatim and stands: "Elaborate precautions were taken to keep the families emotionally isolated
from each other, and to prevent the families from emotionally merging into one large undifferentiated
family ego mass which could inundate the family process in each family." The observation half is
false. The stated motive for putting families together was **teaching-time economy** — "I began
thinking of the time that could be saved, and the advantages of covering the material in more detail
to many families at the same time" — and that motive is the *first* of "two major findings … that had
not been accurately predicted": "(1) The larger group did not facilitate advantageous use of teaching
time." Cross-family learning is finding (2), "**the surprise**," discovered afterwards and explained
post hoc under a hedge ("**Apparently**, it is easier to really see and know your own problem when you
watch it in other people"). What he actually designed for, in his own words, was a structure "that
would **avoid** the social and emotional togetherness of groups, and **preserve** the emotional
separateness between families." Observation was not a design objective; it was a by-product of a
seating plan borrowed from the 1958–59 ward structure, which itself had been adopted to fix a
*focus* problem. The model may still implement two edge types — but it cannot cite Bowen as having
deliberately engineered the pair, and a mechanism justified on that basis is resting on a
misattribution.

**2. The 50 percent figure does not measure the observation channel.** Verbatim: "The surprise was
the unusually rapid progress of the families. This was **estimated** to be about 50 percent faster
than comparable clinical problems in other families." It is (a) an estimate with no stated procedure,
(b) attributed to the multiple-family *setting*, not decomposed into a per-mechanism gain, and (c)
confounded in the same two-item list by the report that the therapist was **more** easily triangled
there than with single families. Pass 1's M treats it as a calibration target for an `observes` edge.
It is not one.

**3. "A transmission path with no emotional tie" is an aspiration in the text, not an achieved
state.** Bowen reports *precautions*, never that they worked. The families sat in one room weekly for
months hearing each other's most intimate material, and had "contact with each other outside the
sessions … meeting in the hallways and elevators." "Kept emotionally isolated" is his effort, not his
measurement.

**4. Pass 1 counted the seating as an anti-merging precaution; the text does not.** The precautions
Bowen enumerates are: the families were unknown to each other beforehand; purpose and technique
explained and agreement obtained; avoid social contact outside sessions; if mutual friends later turn
up, do not mention the other families in any social contact. The seating sentence — "each husband and
wife sat beside each other, slightly separated from the next two spouses" — appears afterwards as
description of the arrangement and is never labelled a precaution. A6 and M assert it enacts the
emotional separation. That is a reasonable reading; it is pass 1's, not Bowen's.

**5. B3's three-phase scheme is a construction, and its ordering contradicts the chapter.** Bowen
writes "The therapy proceeds through several distinct phases" and names exactly one: the early phase
of coming to "know" each other. Pass 1's phase (3), family-of-origin work, is explicitly begun "**as
early as possible**" — concurrent, not terminal. Corrected in place.

**6. "Silent auditors" vs "silent observers" is not a distinction Bowen maintains.** In the
multiple-family section the non-focal *families* are "silent observers." A5 corrected.

**7. Conservation language was imported from Ch08/Ch09 and does not belong here.** Three places.
(a) A2/B5/M call the extended family a "relief valve", a "lower-intensity sink", "a conserved
disturbance quantity that can be routed to lower-intensity edges." Bowen says only that each step
"stirs emotional disharmony in extended families … which is generally easier to handle than the
disharmony between the spouses." No displacement, no conservation, no routing. (b) B5 gives
family-of-origin work a mechanism — "togetherness pressure is *sourced* outside the marriage and can
be discharged there instead of at the spouse." The text states a bare correlation with no mechanism.
(c) B5 posits "a shared, conserved togetherness budget" in the alternating sequence. Invented.
All three withdrawn in place. This chapter does not state anxiety conservation anywhere.

**8. The third step of the ward sequence is pass 1's framing, not Bowen's.** He writes "externalize
intrafamily anxiety" twice — into conflict with ward staff, and into differences between the
therapists. For the third displacement he writes only that "some **anxious** member from another
family would interrupt and change the subject," and treats it as a loss of focus. Two of the three
transitions are his displacement language; the third is pass 1's extension. See Q-REROUTE.

**9. Auditing-beats-participating is an attributed self-report, not a finding.** "**Some families
said** they often obtained more benefit from auditing." Pass 1's A1 states it as a phenomenon and
B5/M build an attention-capacity competition on it ("participation consumes the capacity observation
would use"). That mechanism has one clause of family self-report behind it.

**10. Two errors in one X3 bullet.** It says "A **single**-session cure is reported … a
**seven**-session 'cure'" — internally contradictory; the text says seven. And "reported without
embarrassment" is wrong: Bowen scare-quotes "cure" on both occurrences, which is the embarrassment.

**11. Coach differentiation is a conjunctive gate, not a cap.** M says "Coach's own differentiation
level caps what the family can achieve." The text gives a conjunction: "If the family is well
motivated, **and** the therapist has been relatively successful at keeping himself out of the
emotional system with the family, it is usual for the family to find more and more to work on." Both
required; no ceiling on attainment is stated. Added as A3 #23.

**12. M over-reads the group-size claim about coach capture.** "Coach capture probability should rise
with the number of families present" generalises a single two-point comparison — group setting vs
single families — into a monotone function of N. The text supports only the two-point comparison.

**13. Missed entirely: parallel professional channels destroy problem definition.** Year one of the
ward project ran individual psychotherapy for every mother and every patient with separate
therapists; the result was that "family problems were diffuse and compartmentalized, and difficult to
define for research or therapy," and all individual psychotherapy was discontinued. Pass 1 has
nothing on this. It is a second instance of Ch01's finding that observation topology, not
professional skill, is the effective variable — arriving from the opposite direction.

**14. Missed entirely: an open channel does its work without being used.** Families could read any
record and attend any meeting; attendance collapsed; "**What was important was that the system was
open and they could attend if they wished**," and he credits the policy with the project surviving.
Availability is a state variable distinct from traffic. Nothing else in the corpus says this.

**15. Missed: the reason the equal time split replaced flexible allocation** — "too often a silent
family would have problems of an urgent nature with too little time." Urgency is exogenous and
unforecastable, which is an argument against any need-predicting scheduler.

**16. Missed: attendance regime varies within one course** — "in some large blocks of sessions were
with one spouse alone; in others the total course was with a single family member."

**17. Missed, smaller.** The opening sentence advertises that the method "takes less of the
therapist's time," the exact benefit finding (1) refuted. "Clean" sessions as a named mode. "A
significant number of the families have had long exposure to other forms of psychiatric treatment."
The claim that multiple-family principles and techniques are "**exactly the same**" as single-family
ones — which bounds what the group format can contribute. And the mid-project revision of the
research hypothesis itself.

**18. A1's marriage-closing bullet names the wrong variable.** Bowen's contrast class is intimate
relationships "**that are not permanent**." Permanence drives taboo accumulation; marriage is the
instance, not the cause.

**19. Reading later theory back into the chapter — one confirmed case.** The conservation frame of
item 7 is the model's own invariant and Ch08/Ch09's, applied here to text that does not carry it.
Otherwise pass 1 is clean on this: no scale numbers, no cutoff, no sibling position, no
multigenerational language was imported, and A5's terminology list matches the text.

## P2. What is NEW here, relative to the chapters preceding it

- **The triangle is promoted to core theory.** Ch09 (~1965) explicitly *excluded* the triangle from
  the major theoretical concepts as "more to do with therapy than the basic theory." Ch11 opens the
  theory section with "**The triangle is the basic building block of an emotional system**," makes
  systems of four or more "a series of interlocking triangles," and asserts domain-independence
  ("whether in a family system, an emotional system at work, a social system, or elsewhere"). This
  chapter is where L09.5's flagged promotion happens, and it is dated inside the text: "In the early
  1960s I began defining my theoretical concept of 'triangles'."
- **Two named ways to modify a triangle, as a pair.** (i) An unabsorbed knowledgeable third in
  contact with the twosome; (ii) one member changing. Earlier chapters have the neutral third
  (Ch10 L10.4) and the single-member lever (Ch06 L06.4) separately; nothing earlier presents them as
  the two exhaustive operators on one object.
- **The third seat is a slot, and a naive occupant has exactly two outcomes.** "Put a stranger into
  the system in place of the child; after a brief time he will either become programmed into the
  familiar patterns of the triangle, or he will withdraw — also a predictable response to triangles."
  A third outcome exists only for someone who "knows and understands triangles." New: earlier
  chapters describe the outside position, none describe what the position does to an arbitrary
  occupant.
- **Capture is unsigned.** "The therapist is as effectively triangled when angry as he is if
  charmed." New and directly testable.
- **A self-calibration heuristic for the coach with an observable proxy.** Target band "between
  seriousness and humor, when I can make either a serious or a humorous response"; hard failure
  signal is going silent and being unable to think of a response. Ch20 later gives the coach a
  *detector* (his own urge to be critical); Ch11 gives him a *set-point test*.
- **A cross-family transmission path with no kinship edge** — however arrived at, this is the only
  place in the corpus where change propagates between systems that have no relationship.
- **The inter-family merge hazard.** "Undifferentiated family ego mass" applied across unrelated
  families.
- **Anxiety displacement into the professional structure, stated twice as such** — into conflict with
  ward staff, and into differences between therapists. Ch08 has substitutable discharge channels
  inside a family; this locates two of them in the treatment apparatus.
- **Parallel professional channels degrade problem definition** (P1 item 13). New.
- **Channel availability without traffic** (P1 item 14). New, and unique in the corpus.
- **Calendar time as the rate limit, stated as a general conviction.** Earliest full statement in the
  corpus: "it takes a certain amount of time on the calendar for families to change, and the length
  of time necessary for change is not decreased by increasing the frequency of appointments."
- **The trigger-stimulus circuit as a first-class object, with worked cases.** Minor stimulus / major
  response, mostly visual or auditory, and "often the careful definition of the mechanism is enough to
  defuse it" — with the wife-slugging case showing the defusal is **bilateral** (he stopped hitting
  her; "she also had some control over 'the look'"). Ch10 has the growing taboo set; nothing earlier
  has a named, defusable stimulus–response edge.
- **The three-register teaching ladder gated by anxiety** — I-position → parable → didactic. New.
- **The four stated therapist functions**, enumerated. New as an enumeration.
- **Termination criteria stated as a checklist**, including that only *one* of the two needs crisis
  capacity. New.
- **A ceiling claim.** Some families "will never be able to change significantly." Earlier chapters
  report failures; this states an inherent floor.

## P3. What CHANGED from earlier chapters

- **Ch09 → Ch11, the triangle's status.** Ch09 excludes it from the major concepts while calling it
  the building block; Ch11 drops the exclusion and makes it the theoretical foundation of the method.
  Delta: from a therapy device to the unit of theory.
- **Ch03 / Ch05 → Ch11, the third position.** Ch03's "interdependent triad" has *fixed* membership
  (father, mother, patient). Ch11's triangle has an **occupiable slot**: the therapist is placed
  "into the triangle in the place of the child." Delta: the position is separated from the person,
  which is the change that makes the whole two-spouse method possible.
- **Ch05 / Ch17 → Ch11, "undifferentiated family ego mass."** Reported discarded in Ch05 (~1961) and
  abandoned in Ch17; used here without apology at ≥1971, and applied to *unrelated* families rather
  than one family. Delta: a term's retirement is not monotone, and its referent widened before it
  went.
- **Ch01/02/03/04 (C5) → Ch11, who moves first.** Four chapters say the peripheral or inadequate
  member moves first, and Ch04 makes it a hard precondition (six families, six failures). Ch11 says
  only "one spouse begins to focus on self while the other pleads for togetherness" and names no
  determinant. Delta: the determinant is dropped, not revised. C5 gets no support from this chapter.
- **Ch14 → Ch11, the child-focused format.** Same abandoned format, same dataset era, different
  reports. Ch11: ~25 children, 1957–1960, weekly, the "good" result about a year, mother's
  domination down, father's passivity down, glowing praise, "little or none of the basic change of
  which they were capable"; after removing the child, "most of the children became free of symptoms"

**Q-CROSSFAMILY — verified, and pass 1 is half wrong.** Confirmed verbatim: cross-family imitation

finding (2), "the surprise," which he says was "not accurately predicted" and explains post hoc under

social and emotional togetherness of groups, and preserve the emotional separateness between
families." **Also not confirmed:** the seating as an anti-merge device (described, never labelled a
precaution); and "no emotional tie" as an achieved state (precautions taken, no claim they worked;
they met in hallways and elevators). **What survives for the model:** the two edge types are
*separable in practice* — his rules govern outside-session contact while observation happens inside
the session, so the setting does demonstrate one being suppressed while the other operates. That is
enough to justify two independently switchable inter-family edges. It is not enough to justify
calibrating the observation edge to 50 percent, and the design-intent story must be removed from the
justification. One further constraint pass 1 missed: whatever produced the speed-up did so while the
coach was *more* easily triangled than usual.

**Q-REROUTE — sequence verified; the framing is half Bowen's and half pass 1's.** The sequence is
exactly as pass 1 has it, in this order: (1) open system, all families and all staff, four
co-therapists, honeymoon ends after about a year, focus "lost in a group therapy type of feeling

members could no longer use the meetings to **externalize intrafamily anxiety into conflict with the
ward staff**"; (3) families plus four therapists, anyone speaking — "the families began to use this
channel for **externalizing intrafamily anxiety into differences between the therapists**"; (4) one
therapist in charge — "just as the therapist was successfully working toward a definition of an issue
in one family, some **anxious** member from another family would interrupt"; (5) one family in focus,
the rest silent auditors. Bowen uses displacement language himself at steps 2 and 3, twice, and the
word "channel" once. At step 4 he does not: he attributes anxiety to the interrupting member but
frames the problem as loss of focus, not as an anxiety outlet. He nowhere states conservation,
nowhere says the anxiety was undiminished, and nowhere presents the history as an experiment — he
presents it as an evolution of technique driven by focus problems. So: **the two-step displacement
chain is his; the third link, the "controlled experiment" framing, and the conservation claim are
pass 1's.** One mechanical correction: the co-therapists were present from the start, so silencing
the staff did not *create* the therapist channel — it was always available and became the used one.
Channels are present; the structure closes them; flow selects among what is left.

**Q-FREQUENCY — all five verified verbatim, with three qualifications.** (a) "families have made as
much, and possibly even more, progress than equivalent families seen in other multiple family groups

**Q-VALIDATION — no.** The chapter offers no external validation and no measurement procedure for
anything. It records the problem as open, in the same paragraph as the 50 percent figure: "In
professional practice, the concept of change or improvement is applied to things as elusive as
feeling better, or disappearance of presenting symptom. The research staff on this project **has tried

**Q-MATERIAL — no per-person material, economic or financial variable.** Four things in the chapter

attributes the exit to the togetherness momentum, not to the job. (iii) **Room space** — the number

the whole method was meant to economise, and the economy was the finding that failed. Nothing about
income, employment, wealth, cost of treatment, or any per-person material state.
