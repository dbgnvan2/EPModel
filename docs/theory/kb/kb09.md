# KB09 — Background to Systems Thinking, and Discipleship

**Cite `KB09`. ASR source — no verbatim quotation.**

## ⚠⚠ K09.1 — Bowen's explicit instruction not to take the theory on his authority

> "one of my principal goals is to develop an **open theoretical system**, where the basis for new knowledge is **research and science rather than anything I said** or I defined or my theory."

> "Discipleship is characteristic of philosophies, religions, dogmas… insofar as they **personalise** it, then it becomes a religion or a dogma, and then that person becomes a disciple. There is **no way to get out of that box** and in contact with the research and science."

> "I would like people to be **grounded in the natural sciences rather than something I said**… the more people that treat it like that, the more **my theory will perish** as being a dogma."

*Model consequence:* **this is a mandate for exactly the discipline this project has adopted** — grading every claim, refusing to present invented constants as sourced, checking extractions against the source, and recording what he got wrong. It also settles how the model should be presented: **as something to be tested, not as an implementation of an authority.** It belongs in the framing of the proposal and the spec.

Kerr describes breaking his own discipleship through the cancer research: finding out "how much of the theory I had **borrowed**", then "for a while there, **I thought you were wrong. It was the greatest period of my whole development**", and only afterwards coming to agree.

## K09.2 — Small-point disagreement is a symptom `[T]`

> "when people start doing that I [am] aware that they are **real caught up in it** and this is the best level of disagreement they can find."

Valid disagreement is at a broad level — his example being general systems versus natural systems with Scheflin.

## K09.3 — Natural versus general systems: compatible within limits `[T]`

> "I never really understood general systems and he never really understood what I meant by natural systems, but **the two are compatible within limits**."

*Model consequence:* softer than `KB13` · K13.2's rejection, and both are his. The honest framing is: he preferred a foundation rooted in nature, considered general systems a different thing, and thought they overlapped without being the same. **Record both statements; do not collapse them.**

## K09.4 — The inclusion criterion for a concept `[T]`

> "I didn't put in any concepts until I was **reasonably sure that they applied to schizophrenia and to the less severe things**."

Theory as "a **conceptual abstraction** of what you see", built from patterns that "kept repeating, repeating, repeating."

*Model consequence:* a stated admission test for the model's own mechanisms — a concept must hold across the severity range, not only at the extreme where it was observed.

## K09.5 — Psychiatry's swing, and the residual category `[T]`

Twenty-five years attributing schizophrenia to psychological causes, then rejection of the whole area as unscientific and a move to biology — "I would see this as a **regressive move**." And: "when a researcher didn't know what it was, he would say it's **constitutional or genetic**. That is a **bag that you put all the stuff in that you didn't know what to do with**."

## K09.6 — Timescales for a paradigm `[#]`

Bowen: ~75 years, or three generations. Scheflin: 50 to 200 years for a paradigm to be "woven into the fabric" of a society.
