# FE10 — Family Evaluation

**Citation rule:** cite `FE10`. Written primary text — verbatim quotation permitted.

**Authorship, verified from the text.** Third person; cites Toman as another researcher; refers to
"**Bowen has referred to them as 'interlocking' concepts**". `[K]`.

> **The readout chapter.** It is the book's clinical-method chapter, and for this project that makes
> it the closest thing the corpus has to a **specification for what the model should emit**. The spec
> has acceptance criteria and no readout schema; `FE10` supplies one, and every component is
> computable from state the model already carries.

---

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE10.1 — The **ten-component family diagnosis** — a readout schema the spec does not have `[K]` `[T]`

> "Interpretation of the data… are broken down into the following ten areas: **(1) the symptomatic
> person, (2) sibling position, (3) nuclear family emotional process, (4) stressors, (5) emotional
> reactivity, (6) nuclear family adaptiveness, (7) extended family stability and intactness, (8)
> emotional cutoff, (9) therapeutic focus, and (10) prognosis.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M9.5` requires readouts to report ground truth and belief separately and that
is the whole of the spec's readout requirement. `FE10.1` is a complete schema from the source.**

| # | Component | Model state it reads | Scale given |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Symptomatic person | `M1.A.11` `symptom_load` — who, category, severity | 0–4 / 1–10 |
| 2 | Sibling position | `M1.A.14` static + `M1.A.14a` derived | Toman notation |
| 3 | Nuclear family emotional process | `M1.D.1`'s sink allocation, **primary and secondary** | named pattern |
| 4 | Stressors | `M1.F.6` spells — **events only** | 0–4 / 1–10 |
| 5 | Emotional reactivity | `M1.A.8` + `M1.A.7a` | 1–10 |
| 6 | **Adaptiveness** | **derived: (5) against (4)** | 1–10 |
| 7 | Extended family stability **and** intactness | — **not in the spec** | 1–5, **per spouse separately** |
| 8 | Emotional cutoff | `M1.B.3` tie states | 0–5 |
| 9 | Therapeutic focus | — | — |
| 10 | Prognosis | derived — see `FE10.6` | — |

Component (3) requires the **primary and secondary** patterns to be reported, not just the dominant
one: "the pattern… that contributes to the eruption of the clinical dysfunction… is usually the
easiest to define. Mechanisms that have contributed to significant symptoms in the past and/or that
contribute to less serious symptoms now may be more difficult to define." `M1.D.1` holds three sink
allocations and nothing requires them to be *reported* as a ranked pair.

**Recommendation: adopt this as `M11`'s readout schema** (or a new `M9.6`). It costs nothing — every
component is already computed — and it is the one place the corpus tells the project what a
family-level output should contain.

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE10.2 — **Adaptiveness is derived**: reactivity *against* stress `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Adaptiveness of a nuclear family is assessed by comparing a family's level of emotional reactivity
> with the level of stress it is experiencing. A high level of emotional reactivity in response to a
> low level of stress is consistent with a low level of adaptiveness. A low level of emotional
> reactivity in response to a high level of stress is consistent with a high level of adaptiveness.**
> … **Level of adaptiveness parallels level of differentiation.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.4a`'s estimator in its simplest and most computable form, and it is
complementary to what the spec already has.**

| | Reads | Timescale |
|---|---|---|
| `FE10.2`'s ratio | reactivity ÷ stress, per episode | one stress period |
| `M1.A.4a`'s estimator | elevated mean `functional_level`, low variance, sustained, across situations | years |

The ratio is the **per-episode reading**; the estimator **aggregates** the readings. `M1.A.4b` already
requires the window's situations to be weighted by the load they imposed — `FE10.2` says why: an
unweighted reading is meaningless because **adaptiveness is only defined relative to the stress that
elicited it.** That is a stronger justification for `M1.A.4b` than the spec currently gives, and it
makes the load weighting a *definitional* requirement rather than a corrective.

`FE10` is also explicit that one episode is not enough: "**Assessment… is evaluated most accurately by
examining the entire history of a nuclear family and not just recent events… An impression formed
from the assessment of several periods in a family's history is more reliable than an impression
formed from just the recent period.**" That is `M1.A.4c`'s breadth count, stated as method.

And the precision limit, which belongs with `M10.C.2b`: "**Attempting to assign a specific level of
differentiation to a nuclear family, such as '38' or '46,' is often not very productive. There are
too many variables to allow a clinician to be that precise.**"

## ⭐⭐⭐ FE10.3 — The **two-family comparison**: same adaptiveness, opposite symptom histories `[K]` `[T]`

**Family A.** An 18-year-old has an acute psychotic reaction two months after leaving for college;
long hospitalisation, never returns, "**became chronically dysfunctional and totally dependent on his
parents**". The parents report "**few problems or symptoms in the family prior**"; both parents and both
older children had done well.

> "**If the magnitude of the symptom and associated functional impairment is compared with the fairly
> benign nature of the stress, it can be concluded that the family's level of adaptiveness is fairly
> low. Absence of serious symptoms in the past was not related to the family's adaptiveness but to the
> way anxiety had been bound in the system.**"

**Family B.** Two sons with behaviour and drug problems as teenagers (both improved on leaving home);
the father with chronic back problems and two operations that interfered with his career; the mother
with recurrent depressions, three courses of treatment. Same average stress.

> "**So one family had 20 years without significant symptoms and the other family had many years with
> symptoms, but the levels of adaptiveness were similar.**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE07.6`'s sink-mobility claim as a measured pair, and a correction to
`FE07.12`.**

`FE07.12` gives the count ladder — the number of symptomatic members scales inversely with level.
`FE10.3` shows two families **at the same level** with opposite counts, distinguished only by whether
the allocation was **fixed** (one target, catastrophic outcome) or **mobile** (many targets, moderate
outcomes). Both claims hold; the count depends on level **and** on allocation mobility, and neither
alone is a readout.

That makes `FE07.6`'s proposed criterion sharper: the two arms must be matched on `basic_level` and
on total budget, and must differ only in `M1.C.4`'s rerouting persistence. **Symptom count is not a
differentiation readout** — `M11.F.6`'s trap list, and this is the clearest instance in the corpus
because both arms are given.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.4 — Three reactivity readouts that **invert** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**One spouse may appear calm because of his conviction that it is the other spouse who is at fault.
> His conviction is a form of reactivity. A parent may appear calm because he does not allow himself
> to believe that his son or daughter has problems. The parent's denial is a form of reactivity. In
> contrast, a spouse may appear quite reactive on the basis of sending out numerous signals about how
> anxious he is. Sending out such signals may be his way of keeping himself calm internally.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.F.6`'s trap list gains three, and the third is a mechanism, not just a
misreading.**

- **Conviction is reactivity.** This connects directly to `FE07.2`: the three sink-identifying belief
  configurations are themselves reactivity measures. So `M1.A.18a`'s blame readout and the reactivity
  readout are **the same quantity**, which is a genuine simplification.
- **Denial is reactivity.** `M9`'s belief store carrying a false negative about a family member is a
  positive reactivity signal, not an absent one.
- **Visible distress can be the calming mechanism.** That is `L16.4`'s *overt emotionality peaks
  mid-scale* with the mechanism supplied — signalling anxiety **is how the signaller stays calm** —
  and it means the model should be able to produce an agent whose emitted distress is inversely
  related to its internal load.

Footnote 23 adds a scope boundary worth recording against `M1.A.1`: "**Emotional reactivity is not
always linked to anxiety… Hunger and sexual attraction, for example, are emotional reactions that can
be influenced by anxiety but also occur independently of anxiety.**" The model derives reactivity
from level and anxiety (`M1.A.1`) and therefore covers only the anxiety-linked subset. That is a
deliberate scope choice and should be stated as one.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.5 — Stress is the **event**, and **spacing** counts `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Stress refers to an event, not to a family's reactions to the event.** The emotionally driven
> chain reactions that can be set in motion… are often a much greater source of stress to a family
> than the event itself. **This fourth component of family diagnosis, however, is confined to
> describing the events themselves.**"

> "**The magnitude of the events, the number of events, and the time spacing between events are used to
> determine the level of stress a family is under.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.F.7`'s `exogenous` flag doing precisely this job — keeping event and
reaction countable separately — and one parameter the spec lacks.**

`M1.F.6` requires stressors to be spells with a **start and a duration**; nothing weights
**clustering**. `FE10.5` makes spacing a third determinant alongside magnitude and count, and the
worked contrast is stark: six events in a few months (relocation, re-entry into a difficult extended
family, pregnancy, the father's parents separating, a job given up, new debts) scores
"moderate to severe", while one or two with recovery time between scores "mild".

**Requirement candidate:** the stress readout **MUST** weight inter-event interval, not only magnitude
and count. It is cheap — the event log has timestamps — and it is what makes `FE07.9`'s colitis case
and `FE09.14`'s Smith case legible as *convergences* rather than as sums.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.6 — The **prognosis rules**, and four of the five are computable `[K]` `[T]`

**(a) Symptom magnitude against adaptiveness.**

> "**When a fairly adaptive family develops major symptoms in face of a high level of stress, the
> prognosis is better than when a poorly adaptive family develops major symptoms in face of a low
> level of stress.**" At one end, "**The symptom is out of proportion to the basic level of adaptiveness
> in the system. Given time, the system will usually recover and the symptom will not become chronic**";
> at the other, "**The symptom is not out of proportion to the basic level of adaptiveness… It is
> unlikely that the system will recover.**"

**(b) Pattern fixity.**

> "**If one pattern of emotional functioning has been the predominant binder of anxiety through much of
> a nuclear family's existence, the chances of significant shifts in that pattern occurring are less
> than if several patterns have been used by a family over the years… the chance that the presenting
> symptom will be intransigent is fairly high. In contrast, if it has been characteristic of the family
> to have symptoms shift around from person to person over the years, there is more chance the
> presenting symptom will fade into the background.**"

**(c) Extended-family stability and intactness** — "The lower the level of differentiation, the greater
the likelihood a symptom will be chronic and/or severe."

**(d) Emotional cutoff — the strongest single variable.**

> "**The degree of emotional cutoff may be the most significant family variable that influences
> prognosis. When a major symptom occurs in a highly cutoff nuclear family emotional system, the
> prognosis is less favorable than when a major symptom occurs in a nuclear family that is in good
> emotional contact with extended family.**"

*Model consequence:* **(a) is `FE09.13`'s acute/chronic fork restated as a ratio test and it is
directly implementable** — chronicity follows from *symptom magnitude ÷ adaptiveness*, not from
magnitude alone. **(b) is `FE07.6`/`FE10.3`'s mobility claim for the third time**, now as a
prediction about the *future* rather than the past. **(d) is a rankable claim the model can test**:
across an ensemble, cutoff should dominate the other family variables in predicting chronicity.

That last is unusual and valuable — the corpus almost never ranks its variables against each other,
and `M1.D.8`'s requirement that the family not be the boundary now has a *predictive* justification
as well as a mechanistic one.

The chapter's closing caution belongs with them: "**No one set of variables is an absolute predictor
of outcome. Even when all the known variables are taken into consideration, a clinician's predictions
will often be wrong.**"

## ⭐⭐ FE10.7 — Sibling position: **level selects the expression** — 1988 `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Toman's research described characteristics of functioning, but not levels of functioning. All older
> brothers of brothers, for example, are not the same. A mature older brother accepts leadership and
> responsibility easily, but he does not attempt to control or to intrude on others. He can let others
> be responsible for themselves. An immature older brother, in contrast, may be a dogmatic and
> overbearing leader who fails to respect the rights of others.** … **An immature younger brother may go
> through life accepting no responsibility, but just demanding from and rebelling against
> authority.**"

> "**There is no such thing as a best sibling position. Each position has its positive and negative
> aspects.**" … "**sibling position accounts for only part of an individual's personality.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.A.14c` in 1988** — the spec cites `KS12.5`/`KS12.7` (2019) for position
being **gating** rather than additive, with `basic_level` selecting the expression. Here it is
thirty-one years earlier, and the two immature profiles map onto `M1.A.9a`'s two axes: the **overbearing
older brother** fails outward, the **demanding, rebelling younger brother** fails inward. Same
position class, opposite counterfeits — which is why the effect peaks mid-scale (`M11.C.9`).

`M2.A` also gains a `[#]`: "**Five or more years difference between siblings usually reduces the
predictability of the characteristics associated with each position.**" The reference family's third
generation is Leo 22 / Nadia 17 / Pia 14 — a five-year gap and a three-year gap — so **Leo sits exactly
at the threshold** and the instance is, unintentionally, a good test of the rule.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.8 — Functions are **allocated exclusively**, and traits partition across siblings `[K]` `[T]`

> "**every family emotional system generates certain functions. These functions are performed by
> specific individuals in the system. When one individual performs certain functions, other
> individuals will not perform them.**"

Footnote 20 extends it well past sibling position:

> "**One child may acquire one aspect of a parent's personality and another child may acquire another
> aspect of that same parent's personality. In a family of two daughters, one daughter may acquire
> traits and functions that are considered 'masculine' and the other daughter may acquire traits and
> functions that are considered 'feminine.'** … **Much of the generational transmission appears to be
> based simply on prolonged association. People in the same family often talk the same, walk the same,
> think the same, and act the same.**"

*Model consequence:* **an exclusivity constraint the spec does not have, and a third mechanism for
sibling divergence.**

`M1.A.14b` requires positional attributes to be **derived from observed function** rather than read
from labels. `FE10.8` adds a **conservation** property: functions are generated by the system and
their occupancy is **exclusive** — one member performing a function prevents others from performing
it. That is `M1.D.4`'s leadership-office logic (vacancy stalls; occupancy excludes) generalised to
every family function, and `FE06.4`'s refill-on-death is the same rule seen at a triangle corner.

The footnote is stronger still: **traits partition across siblings**, so two children of the same
parent acquire complementary halves of that parent. With `M7.E.1b`'s different-triangles and
`FE07.14`'s birth-cohort effect, that is a **third independent mechanism** for why siblings differ,
and it is the only one that predicts *complementarity* rather than mere difference. Testable, and
`M11.C.6(b)`'s variance requirement should distinguish which mechanism produced the spread.

"Prolonged association" as the transmission route is `FE02`'s R-complex imitative behaviours, and it
is deliberately unglamorous — no special mechanism needed.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.9 — Extended family: **stability** and **intactness**, scored **per spouse** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**Stability refers to the average level of functioning of the members of an extended family.
> Intactness refers to who is alive and available in the extended system. These parameters parallel,
> but are not equivalent to, basic level of differentiation.** … **The stability and intactness of each
> spouse's extended family are evaluated separately.**"

> "**A nuclear family cannot be understood adequately as a closed system. It is part of a larger
> multigenerational emotional matrix and failure to consider the nature of that matrix leaves a
> clinician with a narrow perspective on a family's problems.**"

*Model consequence:* **two distinct quantities, neither of which the spec measures.**

`M1.D.8` requires at least one family-of-origin tie per adult and `M2.3` forbids a closed nuclear
four — both are *structural* requirements with no corresponding readout. `FE10.9` supplies two:

- **Stability** — the mean functioning of the extended system, which the model computes trivially.
- **Intactness** — **who is alive and available**, which the model also has (`M7.C.1` mortality,
  `M1.B` tie existence) and which is what `FE08.1`'s quantum-jump condition (2) and `FE08.4`'s "the
  castle crumbled" case both turn on.

**Scored per spouse, separately** is the part that matters most: it is exactly what `FE09.12`'s
asymmetric-cutoff prediction needs, and a family-level average would erase it.

## ⭐⭐ FE10.10 — The coach needs only to be **less reactive than the family** `[K]` `[T]`

> "**It is not necessary for a therapist to have complete emotional autonomy to be effective. He just
> needs to be more autonomous or less reactive than the family.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M1.E`'s efficacy is a *difference*, not a level — second source.**

`M5.C.1a` already carries this from `KB06` ("just be **less reactive than the others**, and you've made
a step toward calming a family"), scoped there to the reactive-family technique. `FE10.10` states it
generally and for the external agent.

Read with `FE06.10` — the coach can be **surpassed**, and `M1.E` must not cap a member's level — the
two are consistent and together they specify the relation precisely: **efficacy is relative;
the ceiling is not.** `M2.A`'s Dr Halim at 55 is effective against a family in the 29–44 range
because of the *gap*, and that gap is what a run should manipulate.

## ⭐ FE10.11 — Two therapeutic principles, with a **base-rate** claim `[K]` `[T]`

> "Therapy based on family systems theory, no matter what the nature of the clinical problem, is
> always governed by two basic principles: **(1) a reduction of anxiety will relieve symptoms, and (2)
> an increase in basic level of differentiation will improve adaptiveness.** The early period of most
> therapy is concerned with the reduction of anxiety… **Most families that undergo therapy will
> experience a reduction of anxiety and symptoms. A smaller percentage of families will make some
> change in basic level of differentiation.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M11.C.17`'s central distinction stated as the treatment model, plus an
ensemble prediction.** The coach arm must show **most** runs producing anxiety and symptom relief and
**a minority** producing any `basic_level` movement. That is a distributional claim, testable over
seeds, and it is a much better shape for `M11.C.17` than a flat no-drift assertion — it says what
*should* happen as well as what should not.

The ordering matters too: anxiety first, differentiation later. That is `FE06.11`'s anxiety gate on
detriangling and `M1.E`'s two functions at two anxiety levels, stated as the sequence of a course of
therapy.

## ⭐ FE10.12 — Cutoff blocks progress — three graded costs and one hard block `[K]` `[T]`

> "**People who do not bridge cutoffs with the past can get some improvement in the present, but they
> usually get less improvement and it generally occurs more slowly and is less durable. At times,
> failure of at least one spouse to focus on family of origin can block progress all together.**"

Footnote 26 names the durability mechanism:

> "**The person whose efforts towards more differentiation of self shift back and forth between focus on
> the nuclear and extended families seems to learn more**… **Conviction is necessary for one's 'self' to
> endure the emotional pressure of an anxious relationship system.** So if a person's efforts extend
> beyond the nuclear family into his family of origin, **he is more likely to develop enough of a 'self'
> to assure that changes made now will survive emotional turmoil in the future.**"

*Model consequence:* `M7.A.2b` (the family of origin is the privileged peripheral system) with a
**three-way graded cost** — less, slower, less durable — and a hard-block case. All three are
distinguishable in a run, which makes this a better criterion shape than a single direction.

And **conviction as the durability mechanism** ties three things the spec keeps separate:
`M1.A.18b`'s coupling of the `systems_perspective` ceiling to `basic_level`, `FE04.10`'s solid self as
the internally-consistent belief subset, and `M5.D.6`'s mandatory `FOLLOW_UP` whose absence reverts
the gain. If conviction is what makes a gain survive later turmoil, then **durability is a property
of the belief store**, and `M9` is where `M5.D.7`'s completed-exchange gain should be checked.

## ⭐ FE10.13 — The family becomes a resource only when you stop wanting it to change `[K]` `[T]`

> "**The family of origin becomes a resource when a person goes back to it, not to get something from
> the family such as support, approval, or acceptance, but to learn more about himself or herself in
> that context. When people stop wanting their families to change, their families become resources.**"

Footnote 28:

> "**A universal stalemate in relationships is, 'Either you must change or I must change.' The lower the
> level of differentiation, the more intense this stalemate.** The 'change' refers to change under
> pressure from the relationship system. **Differentiation of self provides the flexibility of not
> having to change the other and not having to change oneself in reaction to the other. This can occur
> without disrupting the relationship.**"

*Model consequence:* **the clearest statement in the corpus of *why* `M1.A.9a` must be
two-dimensional.**

The stalemate is a forced binary — change the other, or be changed — and the two horns are exactly
`M1.A.9a`'s two axes. The differentiated position is not a midpoint between them; it is the **exit
from the binary**, available only in two dimensions. A scalar `outside_ness` cannot represent an exit
from a one-dimensional trade-off, which is the geometric reason `M5.F.2b`'s scalar detector fails.

`FE09.6`'s "two options without the frame, three with" is the same claim in the repertoire, and
`FE10.13` supplies the *reason* the third option exists.

The first quote is `M1.E.2a`'s **understand, not help** pointed at the family member rather than at
the coach — the same objective, one role over.

## ⭐ FE10.14 — Parental sibling positions predict the **children's** functioning `[K]` `[T]`

> "**A marriage of two youngest siblings may result, particularly under stress, in neither spouse
> wanting decision-making responsibility. A marriage of two oldest siblings, in contrast, may result,
> particularly under stress, in battles over who is in charge.** The lower the level of differentiation
> of self, the more prone people are to these type of stalemates. **When both parents are youngest
> children, their firstborn son or daughter may be highly prone to develop parenting qualities in
> relationship to his or her parents.**"

> "**The sibling positions of the parents' parents are also important. A mother who grew up as a
> younger sister of a sister, and whose mother also grew up as a younger sister of a sister, often has
> exaggerated qualities of a younger sister of a sister.**"

*Model consequence:* two structural predictions the spec has no room for and both are computable from
`M2.A`'s data:

- **A parentified child produced from position data alone** — two youngest-child parents produce a
  firstborn who takes the parenting function. That is `FE10.8`'s exclusivity rule firing across a
  generation: the function exists, the parents do not occupy it, so the child does. Directly
  testable, and it needs no new mechanism.
- **Position effects compound across generations.** A youngest of a youngest shows *exaggerated*
  position characteristics. `M1.A.14c` makes `basic_level` the gate on expression; `FE10.14` adds a
  second multiplier — repetition of the position in the parental line. Worth recording; whether to
  implement is a judgement, since it adds a parameter.

## ⭐ FE10.15 — Toman's divorce-rate correlation — the only external number on sibling position `[K]` `[#]`

> "**Toman (1962) showed that divorce rate correlated with the fit of sibling positions. If an older
> brother with a younger sister married a younger sister with an older brother, the chance of divorce
> was significantly less than if an older brother with a younger brother married an older sister with
> a younger sister.**"

With the limit stated in the same breath: "**The usefulness of such data in predicting the outcome of
physical, emotional, or social dysfunctions is still unknown.**"

*Model consequence:* **`M2.A.0f`'s rank-and-sex complementarity with an external correlate.** The spec
cites `KS12.3`/`KS07.9` for "complementary pairs form more readily and carry lower baseline conflict";
Toman gives a *measured outcome* — divorce rate — on the same variable. That makes it a candidate for
`M10.C.2`'s calibration-target list in the same restricted sense as `M10.C.2a`'s DSI correlation:
usable as an **ensemble check**, never to set a parameter, and carrying its own limits.

The complementary and non-complementary pairs are also fully specified, so `M2.A.0f`'s pairing rule
has a concrete test case: `b(s)`+`(b)s` should show lower conflict and lower dissolution than
`b(b)`+`s(s)`.

## ⭐ FE10.16 — The **ninth concept was subjectivity, and it was never published** `[K]` `[T]`

> "Family systems theory is comprised of the following **eight concepts: scale of differentiation,
> triangles, nuclear family emotional process, family projection process, multigenerational
> transmission process, sibling position, emotional cutoff, and societal emotional process.**"

> "**Preliminary work has been done on another concept in family systems theory. This ninth concept
> incorporates subjectivity into systems theory by defining its function in the emotional process of
> an individual, of a family, and/or of a society. Since nothing on this concept has actually been
> published, family systems theory is still considered to consist of eight concepts.**"

*Model consequence:* **a real and quotable limitation, and it should go into `M11.F`.**

The model's `M9` belief layer and `M1.A.18`'s `systems_perspective` are both attempts at exactly the
thing the ninth concept was to formalise — the **function** of subjectivity in the emotional process
of an individual, a family and a society. `FE06.17` states the method (assign function, never
motive) but there is no published concept behind it.

So `M9` and `M1.A.18` are the parts of the model with the **least** theoretical backing, and the
reason is not that the project chose badly — it is that the concept was never finished. That is worth
saying plainly wherever `M9`'s or `systems_perspective`'s outputs are reported, and it argues for
grading them conservatively: `M1.A.18` is already `[M]`, and `M9`'s content rules should be too.

## ⭐ FE10.17 — Societal regression: dated, with the **same exit condition** as a family regression `[K]` `[T]`

> "the concept of societal emotional process describes how a prolonged increase in societal anxiety
> can result in **a gradual lowering of the functional level of differentiation of a society.** The lower
> the functional level of a society, the greater the incidence of '**social symptoms**' such as a high
> crime rate, a high divorce rate, an incessant clamor for '**rights**,' and a notable neglect of
> responsibilities. During the course of history, **the emotional functioning of society has risen and
> fallen many times. Since about the mid-1960s, society has been in an emotional regression. The
> regression is anticipated to continue until the discomfort associated with implementing short-term
> solutions designed to relieve immediate anxiety becomes greater than the discomfort associated with
> implementing solutions that tolerate immediate anxiety and encompass a long-range view.**"

> "**The lower the level of differentiation, the more a family's emotional process is influenced by
> societal emotional process.**"

*Model consequence:* **`FE05.5`'s regression floor at societal scale, with an identical exit
condition — one mechanism, two scales.** The family-level nadir and the societal-level nadir are the
same crossing: **binder cost exceeds change cost.** That is `M11.F.2`'s analogy requirement satisfied
in the strongest available way — not a resemblance, but the same stated threshold — and it is worth
noting that the corpus itself makes the two-level claim in one sentence.

Two cautions:

- **The societal level moves `functional_level`, not basic.** "a gradual lowering of the **functional**
  level of differentiation of a society" — consistent with `M1.D.7` acting on the symptom threshold
  and with `M11.C.17`'s no-basic-drift requirement.
- **The symptom list is value-laden and dated.** "an incessant clamor for 'rights'" is a 1988
  editorial judgement, not an observation, and `M1.D.7`'s readouts **MUST NOT** inherit it.

## FE10.18 — Diagrams mislead, and theories yield to facts `[K]` `[T]`

> "**All diagrams and symbols of family emotional process are at risk for being simplistic or

corpus's own standing instruction rather than an aside.
